# Era Style: The Settled Reach

## Setting

Far-future interstellar civilization. Humanity has spread across multiple star systems via generation ships and FTL. The frontier is decades old — not raw wilderness anymore, but not fully institutional either. Corporate-colonial heritage meets post-Earth pragmatism. Institutions exist but they're young, still finding their shape. The galaxy is mapped but not tamed.

## Tier Identity (visual/tonal)

- **Tier 1 — Frontier:** Boxy, seamed, matte. Bolted-together hardware. Names feel functional, rough-edged, born from necessity.
- **Tier 2 — Establishment:** Smooth, integrated, polished. Professional navy. Names feel institutional, confident, proper.
- **Tier 3 — Zenith:** Flowing, luminous, abstract. Something from the next age. Names feel transcendent, old-becoming-new, mythic.

## Naming Philosophy

Names in the Settled Reach feel like they emerged from a culture that remembers Earth but has moved beyond it. Stellar cartography survey designations became place names. The surnames of explorers and founders stuck to the systems they charted. Corporate-colonial naming conventions (functional compound labels) coexist with older cultural traditions that people brought from Earth and adapted over generations.

There's a formality to institutional names — these are people who write charters and sign treaties — but the frontier undermines it. The further from core systems, the more names shed their institutional polish.

## Per-Category Philosophy

### People
Names reflect a post-Earth internationalism that has had centuries to blend. Family names from distinct Earth traditions survive but are worn smooth by time — abbreviated, combined, phonetically shifted. Given names tend toward the short and functional on the frontier, longer and more ceremonial in core systems. Some families carry survey-mission surnames (the family name IS the system they charted). Mononyms are common among frontier populations.

### Places
Named by the people who found them: stellar cartography designations that stuck, survey team leaders whose surnames became system names, descriptive labels from first-contact reports ("Pale Reach", "The Breaks"), corporate development names that outlived the corporations, and indigenous names from the first generation born there. The best place names feel like they have a *story* behind them — you can imagine someone naming it in the moment.

### Units
Military designations from a professional spacefaring navy. Formation names reference operational heritage — battles won, systems defended, notable commanders. Ship class names draw from the era's mythology (which is itself a blend of Earth traditions). Operational codenames feel bureaucratic but evocative. No fantasy-word + English-noun constructions.

### Factions
Political entities in a multi-system civilization: colonial administrations, trade compacts, defense pacts, breakaway movements, corporate successor states, cultural blocs. Names should feel like real political entities that appear on star charts and in diplomatic communiques.

## Anti-Patterns for This Era

- No medieval titles (Sir, Lord, Duke) — these are post-Earth institutions
- No modern military ranks used as name components (Captain Smith, General Lee)
- No "cyber-" or "neo-" prefixes — this isn't cyberpunk
- No fantasy apostrophes (Kal'thar) — this is grounded sci-fi
- No single-culture dominance — the post-Earth blend should be visible
- No "USS" or "HMS" ship prefixes — naval tradition exists but isn't copy-pasted from Earth
