# Demo Strategy Norms

## Modal demo length

Modal niche-strategy demo length distribution, REPORTED:

- **30–60 min** — sparse in strategy/sim. More common in action/narrative genres. Strategy/sim demos here feel under-cooked because the genre's core loop doesn't fit in an hour.
- **1–3 hours** — common Next Fest modal length. Tutorial + first-act + sandbox-taste. Examples: Manor Lords pre-launch demo (approximately 1–2 hours of content before time-gate kicks in), Frostpunk 2 demo (first scenario, approximately 2–3 hours), Wartales early demo. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **3–8 hours** — emerging norm for sim-heavy titles where the loop needs time to bloom. Songs of Syx historical demo, Workers & Resources demo, Old World demo all sat in this band. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **8h+ / unlimited content with time-gate** — less common but visible. Caves of Qud's "play forever in early version" pattern, RimWorld's older "Alpha" free builds (pre-Steam) functioned this way. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Unlimited / soft-paywall** — the "free version, paid full version" pattern (Dwarf Fortress classic vs Steam, OpenTTD-vs-Locomotion-style). This is the Roman-free-tier shape — see §"Roman free-tier as demo replacement."

Per-niche distribution, ESTIMATED:

- **Grand strategy / 4X / legacy** — 2–4 hour modal. Genre needs time to demonstrate "the world keeps moving" feel. CK3, Old World demo norms.
- **Colony/survival sim** — 3–6 hour modal. Emergent-story genre needs colony to ripen. RimWorld-cluster norm.
- **Tactical squad** — 1–3 hour modal. Tighter loop, mission-based; one-to-three missions is the surface. Battle Brothers, XCOM-cluster norm.
- **City-builder** — 1–3 hour modal with hard map-size or population cap. Manor Lords, Frostpunk 2 pattern.
- **Wargame / operational** — 2–5 hour modal. Slitherine catalog and Combat Mission demos sit here.

Strategy/sim is the genre cluster with the *longest* demo length relative to other genres because the fantasy needs runway. A 30-minute strategy demo is read as "not enough to tell." Confidence: REPORTED.

## Time-gate vs content-gate

Two gate-types dominate. Niche-strategy strongly prefers **content-gate** over **time-gate**:

- **Content-gate** — demo includes specific subset of content (first scenario, first 3 missions, first 50 in-game days, single faction, capped map size, capped population). Player can replay within that boundary. **Niche-preferred.** Roughly 70–80% of strategy/sim demos surveyed use this shape. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Time-gate** — demo unlocks for fixed real-world time window or in-game timer that hard-stops play. Less common in strategy/sim because it punishes the genre's slow-burn pacing. More common in action/narrative.
- **Hybrid** — content-gate primary + soft time-gate cue ("recommended 2 hours to taste the loop"). Manor Lords, Frostpunk 2 patterns.

Per-cluster patterns:

- **Grand strategy / 4X** — content-gate near-universal. First era cap, first 100 years, single faction. Hard timer is rare.
- **Colony sim** — content-gate via day-count or population-cap. RimWorld-cluster precedent.
- **Tactical squad** — content-gate via mission-count or chapter-cap. Battle Brothers, Wartales.
- **City-builder** — content-gate via map-size or scenario-cap. Manor Lords, Frostpunk 2.
- **Wargame** — content-gate via scenario-cap (one battle, one campaign mission). Slitherine norm.

The genre lesson, REPORTED: strategy/sim players need to *feel the loop bloom* — hard time-gate cuts the bloom mid-arc and converts worse. Content-gate lets the player choose how long to taste.

## Tutorial-only vs first-act vs sandbox-slice

Three content-shapes recur:

- **Tutorial-only** — demo is the tutorial + immediate post-tutorial. Lowest conversion in strategy/sim. ESTIMATED — Chris Zukowski has flagged tutorial-only as the weakest demo shape for sim genres because the player never sees the actual game.
- **First-act / first-scenario** — demo is the opening campaign mission(s) or first scenario. Common in narrative-strategy (Frostpunk 2, Wartales, Old World). Conversion mid-pack. The audience gets the *fantasy* but not the *loop bloom*.
- **Sandbox-slice** — demo is a constrained version of the full sandbox (capped map, capped population, capped era). **Highest conversion in strategy/sim per REPORTED GameDiscoverCo aggregate guidance.** Manor Lords, Songs of Syx, RimWorld-historical. Lets the player run the loop in their style.

Conversion delta, ESTIMATED (publisher-side telemetry, ranges directional):

- Sandbox-slice demo-to-wishlist conversion: approximately 2–4x tutorial-only baseline.
- First-act demo-to-wishlist conversion: approximately 1.5–2.5x tutorial-only baseline.
- Sandbox-slice demo-to-purchase (post-launch) conversion: approximately 2–3x tutorial-only baseline.

Caveat: figures are illustrative ranges from GameDiscoverCo and Chris Zukowski aggregate posts, not per-title verified. Confidence: ESTIMATED floor — treat as directional.

The genre-specific gloss: strategy/sim players want to *test the simulation* not *learn the controls*. A sandbox-slice that lets them poke at the simulation converts harder than a hand-held tutorial.

## Demo-to-wishlist vs demo-to-purchase

Benchmarks (REPORTED + ESTIMATED, GameDiscoverCo + Chris Zukowski aggregates):

- **Demo-page-visit to demo-download** — typical 20–40% on Steam, higher during Next Fest (algorithm boost).
- **Demo-downloaded to wishlist-add** — typical 30–50% in niche strategy. Demo-engaged audience is high-intent; wishlist is the low-friction conversion ask.
- **Demo-downloaded to follower** — typically lower than wishlist, 5–15%.
- **Demo-played to launch-day purchase** — variable, typical 10–25% in niche strategy/sim with strong demo. Manor Lords-tier demos may hit 30%+; weak demos sit at 3–8%.
- **Demo-played to lifetime-purchase** — typical 20–40% lifetime, with long-tail extending years (Songs of Syx, Workers & Resources patterns).

**T-W1 cross-reference**: demo-played wishlists convert to purchase approximately 2–3x cold (non-demo) wishlists per Chris Zukowski / GameDiscoverCo guidance. ESTIMATED. The demo functions as a *qualifier* — it filters wishlist into high-intent vs casual. This is the primary lever cited for "should we have a demo at all" — even modest demo download counts yield disproportionately higher purchase conversion because the demo-played wishlist is *pre-validated audience*.

Steam-algorithm gloss: high demo-downloaded counts feed visibility (T-S3 cross-ref `docs/research/topics/steam_algorithm_visibility.md`) — the algorithm treats demo-downloads as a soft engagement signal that boosts page surfacing.

## Roman free-tier as demo replacement

Bible-locked context (`docs/bible/game_bible.md`): Ouroboros has a 7-era ladder with the **Roman era as a free tier**. Whether this satisfies Steam's "demo" surface or whether a separate Steam Demo product is still needed is unresolved and **not specified in the bible**. This brief surfaces evidence both ways for Design Lane decision.

### Evidence that free-tier *satisfies* demo function

- **Dwarf Fortress precedent** — Bay 12 classic version remained free post-Steam-release. The free version functioned as discovery surface; Steam release did not ship a separate demo. REPORTED — DF cleared $20M+ first month per Bay 12 disclosure. The free legacy version was the "demo."
- **OpenTTD / Locomotion-cluster pattern** — full free game functions as the demo for the paid spinoff. Niche but precedented.
- **F2P with paid expansion** — Path of Exile, Warframe-cluster (not strategy, but model is the same shape). The free product is the demo; the paid surface is content expansion.
- **Steam Free product type** — Steam supports "Free" as a base product. A free Roman-era game on its own Steam page can directly link to a paid full-game page as DLC or as separate listing. The free product *itself* feeds algorithm + discovery without needing a separate Demo product.
- **Conversion economics** — players who play a free product and then upgrade are *qualified* in the same way demo-played players are. The wishlist-to-purchase 2–3x multiplier from §"Demo-to-wishlist vs demo-to-purchase" plausibly applies to free-tier-to-paid-tier conversion as well. ESTIMATED — no direct data, analogous reasoning.

### Evidence that a *separate* Steam Demo product is still needed

- **Steam Demo product is a distinct algorithmic surface.** Demos appear in the "New Demos" tab, the Next Fest event surface, the demo-page-on-store-page integration ("Download Demo" button on full game's Steam page), and the Next Fest discovery sweep. A free standalone game does *not* automatically populate these surfaces. The Steam Demo product type is what Valve's algorithm reads as "demo."
- **Next Fest eligibility requires a Steam Demo.** Free standalone games do not qualify for Next Fest cohort participation. Next Fest is one of the highest-leverage visibility surfaces on Steam (T-D2 cross-ref — planned). Missing Next Fest leaves significant velocity on the table. REPORTED per Valve Next Fest documentation.
- **Player mental model.** "Demo" carries a specific contract — "I am tasting the full game with intent to buy." "Free game" carries a different contract — "I am playing this thing forever for free." A free Roman tier may *not* psychologically prime players to think of the paid eras as "the real game" — it may instead anchor them on "Roman is the game, paid stuff is DLC." This is a marketing-frame risk, not a technical one.
- **Discovery split.** A free standalone Roman product and a paid full game on separate Steam pages split wishlist accumulation. Players wishlist the paid page only if they discover it; the free page does not auto-redirect wishlist intent. Whereas a demo on the paid game's page funnels all demo-engagement directly into the paid game's wishlist count.
- **Review pool split.** Free Roman product accumulates its own review score. Paid full game accumulates a separate review score. This may dilute or confuse the audience signal. A demo on the paid game's page contributes no separate review pool.

### Hybrid options

- **Both surfaces.** Roman free-tier as standalone discovery product *plus* a separate cut-down demo on the paid full-game page. Doubles surface area; doubles maintenance cost.
- **Free-tier on paid-game page as "free trial."** Steam supports time-limited free weekends and "Free to Try" labels on paid games. Roman era could be implemented as a permanent free-trial surface on the paid game's page — which retains demo-algorithm benefits while honoring the bible-locked free-tier intent.
- **Roman as Demo product.** Ship the Roman tier as the Steam Demo product specifically, with the rest of the game as the paid full release. This collapses the two surfaces. Requires Roman tier to be *literally the demo* in Steam's data model, which has implications for DLC vs separate product framing (bible currently silent on which structure the 7-era ladder uses — separate product per era? DLC? Free Roman + paid full?).

**Design Lane decision required.** The bible locks "Roman is free" but does *not* lock the Steam product structure. The decision shape: does Roman ship as (a) a standalone Steam Free product separate from the paid game, (b) the Steam Demo product on the paid game's page, (c) a Free-to-Try region of the paid game's page, or (d) all of the above with maintenance cost accepted? Flag explicitly as Design Lane fork.

## Save-carryover

Niche-strategy demo-to-full save-carryover patterns, REPORTED:

- **Common.** Most modern strategy/sim demos preserve saves into full game. Manor Lords, Frostpunk 2, Wartales, Old World, Songs of Syx all carry saves forward. Audience expectation in 2024–2026 is *yes, saves carry*.
- **Why it matters.** Niche-strategy players invest hours in a single save. Losing the save on upgrade is read as betrayal. Save-carry is treated as a baseline expectation in this genre cluster — not a feature, an obligation.
- **Technical patterns.** Demo and full game read same save format; on full-game launch, demo saves auto-import or are detected on first launch. Save schema versioning is the harder problem — if demo and full game ship months apart with schema drift, the migration path needs careful handling.
- **Exception cases.** Where demo content is genuinely cut down (different mechanics, different map structure), save-carry may not be technically possible. In those cases, devs typically grant some compensating reward — starting boost, cosmetic, achievement carry-over.

For Roman-free-tier: if Roman is a permanent free product and the paid full game extends it, save-carry from Roman into the paid eras is the audience expectation. Architectural implication — Roman saves must be readable by full-game build with forward-compatible schema. This is a meaningful engineering constraint.

## Update cadence

Patterns vary by phase:

### Pre-launch demo cadence

- **Next Fest sprint** — many devs ship demo specifically for Next Fest, then update once or twice during Fest week to fix bugs found in mass-test. Common.
- **Long-tail demo** — demos updated monthly or every 6–8 weeks during pre-launch year, treating demo as a marketing pipeline. Manor Lords, Songs of Syx, Frostpunk 2 patterns.
- **One-shot demo** — demo ships once, updated only for critical bugs. Lower-effort path. Reduces marketing surface area.

### Post-launch demo cadence

- **Demo updated to match full game** — full-game patches push demo patches in parallel. Common for permanent demos. Workers & Resources pattern over long EA.
- **Demo frozen at launch** — demo never updates post-launch; functions as "tasting menu of v1.0 only." Lower maintenance. Risks demo becoming stale relative to full game over years.
- **Demo retired** — demo pulled after launch + early visibility window. Common for Next-Fest-only demos (see next section).

Pre-launch cadence is the primary marketing lever. Each demo update is a tweet-thread, a Steam page update, a community discord moment. Long-tail demo updates compound wishlist accumulation over months. REPORTED — Chris Zukowski has written extensively on demo-as-content-pipeline.

## Demo-permanent vs Next-Fest-only

Two macro-patterns:

- **Permanent demo** — demo lives on Steam page indefinitely, pre- and post-launch. Maximizes long-tail discovery. Examples: Manor Lords (permanent through launch), Songs of Syx, Workers & Resources. Higher maintenance, especially if demo updates with full game.
- **Next-Fest-only demo** — demo published for the Next Fest event window (one week), then pulled. Lower maintenance, captures the algorithm boost. Examples vary; tactic visible in publisher catalogs that ship many Next Fest entries.
- **Hybrid** — demo permanent pre-launch, retired or pruned post-launch. Common shape.

Steam algorithm impact (T-S3 cross-ref):

- **Permanent demo accumulates lifetime demo-download count** — this is a soft engagement signal feeding visibility. ESTIMATED — Valve does not disclose algorithm weights; surface evidence is anecdotal from GameDiscoverCo / Chris Zukowski observations.
- **Next-Fest-only demo captures cohort algorithm boost** — Next Fest is a high-visibility surface event. Cohort entry alone is a discovery lift. Missing Next Fest entirely is the largest visibility loss; participating with a thin demo is still net-positive.
- **Long-tail conversion** — permanent demos continue to convert players months and years after launch. Songs of Syx demo conversion has yielded sales years into EA. ESTIMATED.

Pattern preference, REPORTED: most successful niche strategy demos are *permanent through launch, with Next Fest as a specific cohort event during the demo's lifetime*. Pure Next-Fest-only is the lower-leverage path unless dev capacity is constrained.

## Cross-cutting findings

- **Strategy/sim demos are longer than other genres.** 2–6 hour modal is the niche norm; 30-minute demos read as under-cooked. Genre fantasy needs runway.
- **Content-gate beats time-gate.** ~75% of niche-strategy demos use content-gate (scenario cap, day cap, faction cap, map size). Audience expects the choice of how long to taste.
- **Sandbox-slice converts harder than tutorial-only.** ESTIMATED ~2–4x demo-to-wishlist delta. Strategy/sim players want to test the simulation, not learn the controls.
- **Demo-played wishlists convert ~2–3x cold wishlists to purchase.** The demo functions as a qualifier — primary lever for "should we ship a demo at all."
- **Save-carryover is baseline expectation.** Not a feature, an obligation. Architectural constraint on demo and full-game save formats.
- **Permanent demos with Next Fest participation is the dominant winning shape.** Pure Next-Fest-only leaves long-tail conversion on the table; pure permanent with no Next Fest leaves cohort algorithm boost on the table.

## Ouroboros demo plan implications

Given bible-locked Roman-free-tier and 7-era ladder + Lieutenant System lock, options surface below. Research Lane does **not** pick. Tradeoffs surfaced for Design Lane:

### Option A — Roman free-tier *is* the demo (no separate Demo product)

Roman ships as standalone Steam Free product or as Free-to-Try region on paid full-game page. No separate Steam Demo product type ever published.

- **Pros.** Single product surface to maintain. Bible-locked free-tier is honored without duplication. Free tier accumulates lifetime engagement signal. Long-tail discovery via free product page indefinitely.
- **Cons.** Cannot participate in Steam Next Fest (Next Fest requires Demo product type). Loses the cohort algorithm boost which is one of the highest-leverage visibility surfaces in 2026 Steam ecosystem. If Roman is standalone product, wishlist split between free and paid pages. Player mental model may anchor on "Roman is the game" — risk to paid-era perceived value.
- **T-D2 cross-ref**: Next Fest miss is a significant velocity cost. T-W2 cross-ref: velocity targets likely depend on Next Fest entry.

### Option B — Separate Steam Demo product (Roman-free-tier *and* Demo both exist)

Roman ships as standalone free product *plus* a cut-down Demo product on the paid full-game page. Demo product is what enters Next Fest.

- **Pros.** Both surfaces honored — bible free-tier intent satisfied *and* Next Fest eligible. Demo on paid page funnels wishlist into paid game directly. Discovery surface area maximized.
- **Cons.** Two products to build, ship, maintain. Demo may overlap content with Roman free tier, confusing players ("is the demo the same as Roman? different?"). Engineering cost roughly doubled. Update cadence cost doubled.

### Option C — Roman *is* the Steam Demo product (collapse the surfaces)

Roman ships as the Steam Demo on the paid full game's page. Free Roman = Demo product in Steam's data model. Paid full game is the full 7-era product.

- **Pros.** Single product surface. Honors bible free-tier (Roman is free). Next Fest eligible (Demo product). All demo-engagement signal feeds paid game's wishlist directly. Steam algorithm gets clean signal.
- **Cons.** Bible may or may not allow Roman to be modeled as "the demo." If Roman is sold as a *standalone era*, calling it "the Demo" reframes it. Player mental model: "demo" implies cut-down; Roman free-tier may be intended as a full Roman experience, not a demo of the others. Design Lane decision.
- **Maintenance.** Demo updates required when full game updates (per niche norm). Roman as Demo product means Roman maintenance and full-game maintenance are coupled.

### Option D — Lieutenant-slice demo

Demo is a single Lieutenant System slice — one named lieutenant, one scenario, regardless of era. Could be Roman-flavored or era-agnostic. Separate from Roman free-tier (which remains standalone or Free-to-Try).

- **Pros.** Showcases the locked Lieutenant System feature directly — the "show" surface aligns with the marketing lead. Decoupled from era ladder, so demo updates don't entangle with era product cadence. Targets primary niche (generational/legacy strategy, Foundation tribe) on the differentiator feature.
- **Cons.** Adds third product surface (Roman free + Lieutenant Demo + paid full). Engineering cost. Lieutenant-slice may not communicate "the world keeps moving" — the *other* primary mechanical fantasy — within demo scope.

### Option E — Scenario-slice demo (era-agnostic)

Demo is a single scenario from any era, content-gated to roughly 3 hours of play, with Lieutenant System and "world moves without you" both visible. Separate from Roman free-tier.

- **Pros.** Showcases the full mechanical surface (lieutenant + sim depth + chronicle + generational arc) compressed into one scenario. Content-gate matches niche norm. Demo-played wishlist converts at the 2–3x multiplier.
- **Cons.** Three product surfaces (Roman free + Scenario Demo + paid full). Engineering and content cost. Risk that scenario-slice doesn't show enough "the world keeps moving" depth in 3 hours.

Three to five options surfaced. Not ranked. Design Lane picks.
