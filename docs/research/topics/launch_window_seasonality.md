## Month-by-month release density (strategy/sim)

Steam's overall new-release count grew from approximately 10,000 titles in 2020 to approximately 18,000 to 20,000 titles per year by 2024-2025 per GameDiscoverCo recaps. Strategy and simulation tags collectively run roughly 8 to 14 percent of monthly releases depending on month. Density patterns recur year over year.

**January.** Light volume month overall. AAA pipeline empty (Q4 push spent). Strategy/sim count typically 200 to 400 Steam new releases per month. Indie discoverability relatively high. REPORTED.

**February.** Picks up. First Next Fest cohort typically lands in February. Steam release count rises toward 1,500 to 2,000 across all tags. Strategy/sim 300 to 500. REPORTED.

**March.** Heavier. Spring Sale mid-month. Volume 1,800 to 2,200. Strategy/sim 350 to 550. REPORTED.

**April.** Heavy. Manor Lords (April 2024) and other notable launches set precedent for this window. Volume similar to March. REPORTED.

**May.** Peaks pre-summer. Volume 1,800 to 2,300. Heavy AAA marketing churn pre-E3-replacement events (Summer Game Fest in June). REPORTED.

**June.** Difficult. Summer Sale late June absorbs discovery attention. Summer Game Fest dominates press cycle first half. Second Next Fest cohort lands mid-June. Indie launches in late June compete with steep sale discounts on established titles. REPORTED.

**July.** Recovers. Post-Summer-Sale window. Press cycle quiet. Volume 1,500 to 1,900. GameDiscoverCo has flagged July repeatedly as an underrated indie window. REPORTED.

**August.** Quiet end, busy mid-to-late. Gamescom in mid-to-late August dominates press second half. Indie launches early August often noted as decent windows. REPORTED.

**September.** Heavy. AAA fall season starts. Volume rises. Strategy/sim launches face more attention competition. REPORTED.

**October.** Heaviest. AAA Q4 push, Steam Scream Fest, third Next Fest cohort, pre-holiday wishlist conversion attempts. Volume 2,200 to 2,800. AAA blackout starts in earnest. REPORTED.

**November.** Heavy and dangerous. Black Friday and Autumn Sale (late November) dominate. AAA blockbuster launches concentrate here. Indie discoverability worst-in-year per multiple GameDiscoverCo posts. REPORTED.

**December.** Front-half busy with AAA tail and Winter Sale ramp-up. Back-half (after Winter Sale starts late December) is dead for new launches — players are buying discounted backlog, not new full-price releases. Front-half early December can work for niche titles seeking holiday-gift-card conversion. REPORTED.

## AAA blackout windows

The "AAA blackout" pattern is the observation that AAA titles concentrate launches in specific windows, and indie launches in those windows lose press oxygen and Steam front-page real estate.

**Q4 blackout (mid-October through early December).** The single hardest window for indie strategy/sim launches. AAA publishers concentrate fall blockbusters here for holiday gift-purchase capture and fiscal-Q3 calendar. Press cycle is saturated. Steam front page is occupied by AAA new-release banners. Recurrent advice from GameDiscoverCo and Chris Zukowski is to avoid mid-October through early December for indie launches unless title is intentionally targeting the gift-card window with a sub-$20 price and high-conversion hook. REPORTED.

**May-June pre-summer secondary blackout.** Summer Game Fest in early June pulls press attention to AAA reveals and console announcements. AAA shadow-drops occasionally land in this window. Indie launches in late May through late June face press cycle saturation plus Summer Sale proximity. Weaker blackout than Q4 but real. REPORTED.

**Late August Gamescom blackout.** Smaller blackout. Gamescom press cycle dominates mid-to-late August. Indie launches in the Gamescom week itself lose press visibility. The week before and the week after can be acceptable. REPORTED.

**March GDC quiet zone.** Game Developers Conference in mid-March pulls developer-press attention but does not blackout consumer-facing launches significantly. Often noted as an acceptable window. REPORTED.

The blackout pattern matters less for niche audiences that do not rely on broad press coverage. A Crusader Kings 3 / Wildermyth audience reaches via subreddits, niche YouTube channels (SsethTzeentach, Quill18, Many a True Nerd), and word of mouth — channels less affected by AAA blackouts than mainstream press. ESTIMATED.

## Steam-sale proximity risk

Steam's seasonal sale calendar (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter) creates predictable proximity risk for full-price launches.

**Approximate Steam sale dates (recurring year-to-year, ±1 week).**

- Spring Sale — mid-March, approximately 1 week. REPORTED.
- Summer Sale — late June (typically last Thursday of June through second Thursday of July), approximately 2 weeks. REPORTED.
- Autumn Sale — late November (typically the Tuesday before Thanksgiving through the Tuesday after), approximately 1 week. REPORTED.
- Winter Sale — late December (typically December 19 through January 5), approximately 2-plus weeks. REPORTED.

**Proximity risk patterns.**

Launching 2 to 4 weeks before a major sale is the most-cited risk window. The pattern: wishlist conversions stall as buyers wait for the imminent sale; reviews accumulate slowly because purchases are deferred; the title misses the "new and trending" Steam algorithm window. GameDiscoverCo has flagged this specifically for early-June launches (Summer Sale in 3-4 weeks) and mid-November launches (Autumn Sale in 1-2 weeks). REPORTED.

Launching during a sale is generally worse — the title's launch discount competes with deep discounts on the entire catalog, and Steam's algorithm prioritizes sale-tagged titles. ESTIMATED.

Launching 4 to 8 weeks after a major sale is generally the best window — wishlist conversion has reset, the discount-cycle expectation is dormant, and the next sale is far enough out that buyers do not defer. This puts the optimum windows at: mid-to-late February (4-8 weeks after Winter Sale ends), late April through mid May (4-8 weeks after Spring Sale), late August through mid September (4-8 weeks after Summer Sale), and mid January (4-8 weeks after Autumn Sale, though Winter Sale proximity disqualifies most of December and early January). REPORTED.

## Best-observed niche-launch months 2020-2026

Strong launches in the strategy / sim / generational / colony-sim space, by month, with what the case adds to the pattern.

**February.** Songs of Syx 1.0 (early 2024) cleared its biggest revenue spike with the v1.0 release and February timing avoided Q4 noise. Multiple Hooded Horse catalog titles have launched into late-February / early-March windows successfully. Pattern: Q4 noise has cleared, Winter Sale is over, Spring Sale is 4-6 weeks out, Next Fest visibility halo from February cohort can extend into late-February launches. REPORTED.

**March.** Stardew Valley 1.6 (March 2024) update drove a re-spike. Spring Sale presents proximity risk in mid-March, but late-March windows post-sale have been noted favorably. ESTIMATED.

**April.** Manor Lords (April 23, 2024) is the headline case. Approximately 100,000 peak CCU within hours, 3 million-plus units by mid-2024. April timing missed Summer Sale proximity entirely and benefited from a quiet press cycle pre-Summer-Game-Fest. The Manor Lords case is over-cited — it is more of an "exceptional title in clear water" data point than a "April is magic" data point. REPORTED.

**July.** Repeatedly flagged by GameDiscoverCo and Chris Zukowski as an underrated indie window. Post-Summer-Sale clear water, pre-Gamescom press quiet, AAA pipeline dormant. Specific case-study titles less well-known but the calendar logic is consistent. REPORTED.

**September.** Frostpunk 2 (September 20, 2024) cleared approximately 600,000 units in the first three months and approximately 35,500 peak CCU at $45. September dodged the worst of Q4 blackout (October-November are heavier) and captured back-to-school discretionary spend. REPORTED.

**Early October.** Sea Power (November 12, 2024 — early access) is borderline-October timing and cleared top-seller status with 5,700 reviews at 90% positive at launch. Old World launched in early access in 2021 and into 1.0 in 2022 with October-adjacent windows; approximately 300,000 units by 2024. October-before-AAA-blackout is acceptable for genuinely-niche titles where the audience reaches through non-mainstream channels. REPORTED.

**December (front half, version-1.0 push).** Caves of Qud 1.0 (December 5, 2024) hit universal acclaim at $30 with a 15-year cult audience. The December front-half window worked because the audience was pre-converted (decade-plus of early access) and the launch was a release-from-early-access event rather than a cold launch. Not generalizable to cold launches. REPORTED.

## Worst-observed months

**Late November.** Repeatedly worst-observed in GameDiscoverCo annual recaps. Autumn Sale absorbs purchase attention, AAA holiday push dominates press, Steam algorithm prioritizes Black-Friday-tagged titles. Specific flop case studies tend to involve mid-tier titles launching the week before Thanksgiving with effectively zero discovery reach. REPORTED.

**Late December.** Dead zone post-Winter-Sale-start. The Winter Sale begins late December and runs through early January. Players spend the back half of December buying discounted backlog. New full-price launches lose to $5-$15 discounted blockbusters. ESTIMATED.

**Mid-June.** Summer Sale proximity plus Summer Game Fest press absorption. Multiple titles have postmortemed mid-June launches negatively — wishlist conversion stalls in anticipation of imminent Summer Sale. REPORTED.

**Mid-October during AAA crunch weeks.** When a Call of Duty / Battlefield / Madden / FIFA / major Bethesda or Bioware title lands the same week, indie launches are erased from the press cycle. Pattern-recognition rather than specific title case studies. ESTIMATED.

**Any week containing a major AAA shadow-drop.** Unpredictable but historically has flattened indie launches that happened to share the same week. The Stormgate open-beta launch in August 2024 also coincided with multiple competing strategy launches and absorbed discovery oxygen in its sub-genre. REPORTED.

## Regional calendar adjustments

Most Ouroboros' likely buyer base concentrates in North America, Western Europe, and English-speaking commonwealth markets. Regional calendar effects matter mostly for secondary markets and for press cycle timing.

**Lunar New Year (late January through mid February, varies by year).** Affects China and Southeast Asia primarily. Chinese Steam buyers are a real and growing segment for strategy/sim titles per multiple GameDiscoverCo posts on Chinese-market success stories. Launching during Lunar New Year week itself reaches less of that audience; launching 2-4 weeks after captures post-holiday discretionary spend. ESTIMATED.

**Golden Week (late April through early May, Japan).** Affects Japanese Steam buyers, a smaller segment for niche strategy titles but real for hard-SF audiences with anime/Japanese-sci-fi overlap. Launching during Golden Week itself can either help (vacation playtime) or hurt (press cycle quiet in Japan). REPORTED.

**Ramadan (varies by year, lunar calendar).** Affects Middle Eastern and South Asian Muslim-majority markets. Smaller direct Steam-segment effect; press cycle effects in those markets. ESTIMATED.

**Thanksgiving (US, late November).** US press cycle goes quiet for approximately 4 days. Black Friday / Cyber Monday dominate consumer attention. Thanksgiving-week launches are universally not recommended. REPORTED.

**Christmas / Boxing Day (late December).** Western markets. Winter Sale dominates. Gift-card spend happens December 25 through January 3 but goes to discounted titles. ESTIMATED.

**Northern Hemisphere summer vacation (late June through August).** Higher casual playtime in vacation periods; broader-appeal indie titles benefit. Hardcore-strategy titles whose audience is 25-45 demographic may see neutral or slightly negative effect (the target demographic vacations less than students). ESTIMATED.

For Ouroboros — given the targeted niches (Crusader Kings / Wildermyth / Foundation-literary, all heavily English-speaking and Western-European) — regional calendar effects are second-order. Lunar New Year proximity matters for China-segment wishlist conversion if the title has Chinese localization; if not, regional effects mostly compress into "avoid Thanksgiving week, avoid Christmas week."

## Day-of-week patterns

Steam's launch-day convention has shifted across the survey window.

**Tuesday norm.** Through approximately 2018-2020, Tuesday was the dominant launch day, inherited from US retail-cycle conventions. Many indie launches still default to Tuesday. REPORTED.

**Thursday rise.** Since approximately 2020-2021, Thursday has been increasingly cited (Chris Zukowski, GameDiscoverCo) as preferred for indie launches. Reasoning: Thursday gives 4 days of weekend playtime before the algorithm's "new and trending" window closes; Tuesday launches lose 2 days of momentum before the weekend. REPORTED.

**Friday and Monday discouraged.** Friday launches risk press cycle missing the title (Friday-afternoon news cycles bury announcements). Monday launches collide with US Monday-morning email volume and press calendar resets. REPORTED.

**Wednesday acceptable.** Wednesday is a quieter day for game launches but not actively discouraged. Some indie postmortems prefer Wednesday for the "less competition on launch day" effect. ESTIMATED.

For Ouroboros, the day-of-week pick is downstream of the month and week pick. Once a launch week is chosen, Thursday is the modal indie recommendation.

## Launch-vs-quarter-end fiscal cycle (publisher) — flag indie N/A

Publisher fiscal-cycle pressure shapes AAA launch timing but does not apply to solo / micro-team indie launches.

**The AAA pattern.** Public-company publishers (EA, Take-Two, Activision-Blizzard pre-Microsoft, Ubisoft) push launches to align with fiscal-quarter-end revenue recognition. Q4 calendar (October through December) maps to publisher fiscal-Q3 reporting for many; Q1 calendar (January through March) often holds delayed Q4 titles. The pattern drives the October-November AAA concentration. REPORTED.

**Indie N/A flag.** Ouroboros is a solo / micro-team indie launch. There is no quarter-end revenue recognition pressure, no shareholder reporting cadence, and no marketing-budget burn-down deadline. The fiscal-cycle frame is informational only — it explains why the AAA blackout windows form, not a direct input to Ouroboros' pick. The pre-Zip gate (friends-ZIP → Steam $100 → 30-day Steam page → launch) sets Ouroboros' calendar from readiness, not from fiscal cycle.

## Cross-cutting findings

- The single strongest pattern: **avoid mid-October through early December (Q4 AAA blackout plus Autumn Sale plus Black Friday)** unless the title is intentionally targeting holiday gift-card conversion. REPORTED. High confidence.
- The single strongest opportunity pattern: **4 to 8 weeks after a major Steam sale ends, before the next one starts**, gives the cleanest wishlist-conversion window. This points at late February, late April through mid May, late August through mid September, and mid January as the cleanest weeks. REPORTED. High confidence.
- **Next Fest cohort fit matters.** A title that participates in a Next Fest cohort (Feb / Jun / Oct) and launches 2-4 months after the cohort captures the demo-to-purchase conversion window. Launches more than 6 months after a Next Fest appearance lose the conversion halo. REPORTED. Cross-references T-D2.
- **Niche audiences are less calendar-sensitive than broad audiences** for press-driven discovery but equally calendar-sensitive for Steam-algorithm-driven discovery. The Steam-algorithm "new and trending" window operates the same regardless of the audience tribe. REPORTED.
- **Thursday is the modal indie launch day** in the 2022-2026 window per recurrent Zukowski / GameDiscoverCo guidance. REPORTED.
- **Day-of-week is downstream** of week, which is downstream of month, which is downstream of pre-Zip-gate readiness. The Ouroboros calendar pick is gate-out, not gate-in.

## Ouroboros launch window options

Surfaced for Westley to choose from. Not ranked, not picked.

**Option A — Late February.** 4-8 weeks after Winter Sale ends. Next Fest February cohort halo if Ouroboros participates. Q4 noise fully cleared. Spring Sale risk in 3-4 weeks (mid-March), so launch in the first half of February or wait until late-February with awareness the Spring Sale window approaches. T-D2 implication: requires Next Fest February cohort participation for the demo-to-launch halo, which means the demo build must be ready approximately 1-2 months before February. Strategy/sim density moderate. Niche reach via SsethTzeentach / Quill18 audience is calendar-independent. REPORTED.

**Option B — Late April through mid May.** 4-8 weeks after Spring Sale, well before Summer Sale (late June). Manor Lords precedent in this window. Press cycle building toward Summer Game Fest but pre-saturation. T-D2 implication: gap from February Next Fest to late-April / mid-May launch is 2-3 months — within the Next Fest conversion halo window. Strategy/sim density moderate to heavy. REPORTED.

**Option C — July (post-Summer-Sale, pre-Gamescom).** 4-8 weeks after Summer Sale ends. GameDiscoverCo-flagged underrated window. AAA pipeline dormant. Press cycle quiet. T-D2 implication: gap from June Next Fest to July launch is approximately 1 month — strong demo-to-purchase halo. Strategy/sim density light to moderate. Lowest competition window per recurrent commentary. REPORTED.

**Option D — Late August through mid September.** 4-8 weeks after Summer Sale, before Q4 blackout. Frostpunk 2 precedent in this window. Press cycle picks up post-Gamescom. T-D2 implication: gap from June Next Fest to late August / mid September is 2-3 months — within the halo window. Strategy/sim density moderate. REPORTED.

**Option E — Early October (pre-AAA-blackout).** Captures Steam Scream Fest and October Next Fest cohort if applicable. Risk: mid-to-late October is when the Q4 blackout intensifies, so launching before October 15 is roughly the cutoff. T-D2 implication: October Next Fest cohort fit is tight — cohort and launch nearly overlap. Strategy/sim density heavy. REPORTED.

**Option F — Mid January.** 4-6 weeks after Autumn Sale, Winter Sale just ended. Quiet press cycle. Risk: post-Winter-Sale buyer fatigue, gift-card spend has been exercised on discounted backlog. T-D2 implication: gap from October Next Fest to mid-January launch is approximately 3 months — within halo window. Strategy/sim density light. REPORTED.

Tradeoff axes Westley may want to weigh: pre-Zip gate readiness date (calendar pick is downstream of when the gate clears), Next Fest cohort participation requirement (sets demo-build deadline), AAA-blackout tolerance for the targeted niches (lower for press-driven discovery, neutral for subreddit / YouTube-niche driven discovery), and Steam-sale proximity preference.
