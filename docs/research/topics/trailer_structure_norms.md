# Trailer Structure Norms

## What this brief is

Brief surfaces niche-strategy trailer structural norms: modal length distribution, first-3-second hook patterns, gameplay-vs-cinematic ratio, captioning conventions (Steam auto-mutes capsule autoplay), music register by audience cluster, era-cut technique validation against multi-era games, pre-launch trailer cadence, voice-over vs voice-over-free patterns. Feeds trailer-build spec, T-S4 Steam page top-slot pick, T-S2 capsule key-art alignment, T-D2 Next Fest trailer plan, T-F2 festival trailer slot fit.

**Does NOT recommend a pick.** Research Lane surfaces inputs; Design Lane + Marketing Lane decide. No "Ouroboros should cut like X" statements. Conflict section surfaces bible-vs-niche collisions only.

## Modal length

Trailer length distribution across niche-strategy/sim launches (training-recall, REPORTED):

- **<60s** — capsule-trailer / teaser slot. Almost always cut as a sizzle for Steam page autoplay capsule or short-form social. Counts as "short trailer" in GameDiscoverCo framing. Common for festival slots when budget-capped (Wholesome Direct often <60s segments, Triple-I Initiative often 30-60s slots). REPORTED.
- **60–90s** — modal Steam page top-slot for indie strategy/sim launches. Derek Lieu's repeated guidance: "If you can't make your point in 90 seconds, you don't know your point." Matches CK3 announce trailers, Manor Lords gameplay trailer (~75s primary), Old World launch (~80s), Wildermyth launch (~90s). ESTIMATED 50-60% of niche-strategy launches land here.
- **90–120s** — common for grand-strategy / 4X reveals where multiple systems need showcase. Stellaris expansion trailers, Civilization VII reveal, Hearts of Iron expansion launches frequently land 100-120s. ESTIMATED 25-30% of niche-strategy launches.
- **120s+** — risk band. GameDiscoverCo training-recall: viewer drop-off accelerates past ~90s for cold-traffic Steam page autoplay; ~120s is the publisher-A/B inflection where completion rate halves vs 75s cut. Long trailers work only for established-IP reveals (Civ VII deep-dives, Paradox dev diaries, KSP2 reveal — and KSP2's long trailer is widely cited as a contributing miscommunication factor). ESTIMATED 10-15% of niche launches, mostly IP-locked.

**Synthesis.** Modal length for a niche-strategy *Steam page top-slot launch trailer* is 75-90s. Teaser/capsule sizzles are <45s. Deep-dive dev diaries are 3-8 minutes but never serve as top-slot. Confidence: REPORTED for the 75-90s modal; ESTIMATED for the drop-off inflection.

## Hook in first 3 seconds

Derek Lieu's foundational rule: "The first three seconds decide whether someone watches the next twenty." Niche-strategy hook patterns that recur (REPORTED):

- **Cold-open on a hero shot of mechanical novelty.** Show the one thing this game does that no other game does — within frame 1. Manor Lords announce opens on a medieval village from a striking camera angle that signals "city-builder but historically-grounded" inside two seconds. RimWorld page trailer opens on the storyteller framing card directly. ESTIMATED 35-45% of successful niche trailers use this.
- **Cold-open on a piece of writing.** Text card with a register-defining line. Wildermyth opens on a storybook frame. Disco Elysium trailers open on prose. Works for narrative-strategy register; risks feeling like a literary-fiction book trailer if cinematic budget can't back it. ESTIMATED 15-20% of narrative-strategy niche.
- **Cold-open on a voice line / diegetic audio.** HD2 announce opens on the recruitment film register. Foxhole content trailers open on radio chatter. Diegetic-VO niche only. ESTIMATED 10-15%.
- **Cold-open on emotional beat / world-state moment.** A character looks up, a city burns, an empire's flag falls. Stellaris expansion trailers, CK3 announce. Works when cinematic budget supports it. ESTIMATED 20-25%.
- **Avoid: studio logo card → fade to black → game title.** GameDiscoverCo flags this as the single highest-correlated drop-off pattern. Burns the first 5 seconds on brand impressions cold audiences don't have. REPORTED.

**Counter-examples.** RimWorld's Steam page trailer is famously minimal — the trailer itself almost doesn't "hook" in the modern sense; the storyteller-framing card does the work. Worked because RimWorld pre-existed Steam's autoplay-capsule era. Songs of Syx page trailer similarly under-produced. These titles got away with weak hooks because their pitch ("emergent story sim with extreme depth") propagated via creator coverage and word-of-mouth rather than capsule-autoplay conversion. AMBIGUOUS whether a new launch can replicate that path in 2026.

**Synthesis.** Niche-strategy norm: first 3 seconds is a hero shot of the mechanical novelty + register-defining audio cue. Studio logo + black-frame ramp-up is contraindicated. REPORTED with ESTIMATED secondary.

## Gameplay vs cinematic ratio

Niche-strategy buyer tolerance for cinematic-heavy trailers is LOWER than the AAA average. Pattern across niche-launch trailers (REPORTED):

- **Gameplay-heavy (≥70% gameplay footage)** — modal pattern for indie/mid-budget strategy. Manor Lords, Old World, Wildermyth, Battle Brothers, RimWorld page trailers all sit ≥70% gameplay. Buyer mistrusts pure cinematic ("show me the game"). GameDiscoverCo training-recall: gameplay-ratio inversely correlates with bounce rate on Steam page for the strategy/sim cluster. ESTIMATED 60-70% of successful niche-strategy launches.
- **Mixed (40-70% gameplay)** — Paradox model. CK3 announce trailers lean cinematic with gameplay cutaways; CK3 gameplay-feature trailers invert. Works because Paradox already has the audience. ESTIMATED 20-30% of niche launches, mostly established-IP.
- **Cinematic-heavy (<40% gameplay)** — risky in niche. HD2 announce was cinematic-heavy and worked, but HD2 is shooter-niche not strategy-niche, and the cinematic supported a register-pitch ("you are a Helldiver") that the gameplay shipped on. Stormgate announce trailers were cinematic-heavy and contributed to the post-beta "where's the game" reaction. ESTIMATED <15% of niche-strategy launches succeed with cinematic-heavy framing.

**Niche tolerance for cinematic-heavy is conditional.** Cinematic-heavy works when (a) the cinematic IS the register-pitch (HD2 recruitment films), (b) the game already has audience trust (Paradox), or (c) the cinematic showcases a NPC/character system that IS the gameplay (Wildermyth's storybook intercuts). Cinematic-heavy fails when (a) it substitutes for gameplay because gameplay isn't ready, (b) it pitches a fantasy the gameplay doesn't deliver, or (c) it lacks UI/sim depth signals the niche needs.

**For Ouroboros cluster.** Lieutenant System + chronicle + era-spanning sim → cinematic-heavy is tempting (era cutscenes are dramatic). Niche data suggests danger: Cluster A (CK3/Old World/Wildermyth/BB) buyer wants to see the UI, the sim, the systems. Cluster B (RimWorld/Wildermyth/BB) buyer wants emergent gameplay shots. Cluster C (CK3/Old World/Stellaris) tolerates more cinematic at the top (Paradox-trained) but still wants map/UI cutaways. ESTIMATED 65-75% gameplay floor for niche fit.

## Captioning

Steam capsule autoplay defaults to MUTED. GameDiscoverCo training-recall: a non-trivial majority of capsule-autoplay views never unmute. Captioning is therefore mandatory for any audio-carrying narrative payload in the first 15-30 seconds (the capsule-autoplay window before viewer either clicks to expand or scrolls away).

Captioning norms (REPORTED):

- **Burned-in captions for any VO line, diegetic dialogue, or text-card prose.** Steam's CC-track support is inconsistent across the capsule autoplay player; burned-in captions guarantee delivery. Niche-strategy launches that ship burned-in captions: Wildermyth (storybook prose is literally the visual), Disco Elysium trailers (prose-on-screen), HD2 announce (propaganda-card captions). ESTIMATED 80%+ of successful niche launches burn in.
- **Placement.** Lower-third with safe-area margin. Avoid covering UI when gameplay is showing. Avoid the bottom-25-pixel band on Steam capsule (some thumbnail overlays clip there). REPORTED.
- **Readability.** Sans-serif, high-contrast (white-on-darkened-strip or yellow-edge), minimum legibility size that survives the Steam capsule downscale (capsule plays at ~616×353 default). Derek Lieu rule-of-thumb: caption text should be readable at 50% trailer resolution. REPORTED.
- **Frequency.** Don't caption every shot. Caption the load-bearing register lines (the one-sentence pitch, the era card if used, any VO claim). Over-captioning reads like a foreign-film export. ESTIMATED 4-8 caption beats in a 75-90s trailer.
- **Title and key beats.** Game title card should be readable at thumbnail scale. Steam page top-slot trailers conventionally show the title in the first 10 seconds and again at end-card.

**Niche-strategy specific.** Cluster A/B audience reads — these are players who read CK3 event text, Wildermyth prose pages, RimWorld colonist bios. Captions are not a friction; they're an in-register signal. Wildermyth's trailer leans into this aggressively. Foundation register specifically aligns: archival/encyclopedic captioning ("Year 2,347 of the Archive," "Cycle III, post-fall") is in-register.

## Music register

Music register by cluster (REPORTED, ESTIMATED on niche-mapping):

- **Cluster A — Dynastic / generational strategy (CK3, Old World, Wildermyth, BB).** Modal register: orchestral with period or pseudo-period instrumentation. CK3 trailers use medieval-orchestral. Old World uses orchestral-with-strings. Wildermyth uses sparse acoustic + storybook-narration scoring. BB uses dark-orchestral with low brass. ESTIMATED 70%+ orchestral. Electronic-heavy reads as wrong-genre for this cluster.
- **Cluster B — Emergent-story simulation (RimWorld, Wildermyth, BB partial).** Modal register: ambient + occasional motif. RimWorld trailer leans on the in-game soundtrack (acoustic-folk-ambient). Songs of Syx similar. Less score-driven, more environmental-audio-driven. ESTIMATED 50% ambient/folk, 30% orchestral, 20% other.
- **Cluster C — Grand strategy spatial (CK3, Old World, Stellaris).** Modal register: orchestral with cinematic-scale brass + choir. Stellaris cinematic trailers are the genre exemplar — large choral arrangements over space cinematography. Civilization series similar. ESTIMATED 80%+ orchestral-cinematic.
- **Cluster Foundation/hard-SF.** Less anchored by gameplay precedent; literary precedent. Foundation series (Apple TV) uses minimalist-electronic + orchestral hybrid. Isaac Arthur YouTube uses sparse-ambient-electronic. Hard-SF film precedent (Interstellar, Arrival, Annihilation) leans minimalist-electronic + orchestral hybrid. ESTIMATED hybrid is in-register.

**Cross-cluster pattern.** Choral/orchestral signals scale + time-weight. Minimalist-electronic signals authorship + restraint. Folk-ambient signals emergent-intimacy. Bombast-electronic signals action/RTS — contraindicated for Ouroboros per the RTS-framing kill in lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md.

**For Ouroboros.** Primary cluster (Cluster A overlap) implies orchestral with era-appropriate instrumentation that shifts across the 7 eras. Foundation/hard-SF differentiator implies minimalist-electronic + orchestral hybrid. AMBIGUOUS whether era-shifting score is feasible at trailer-budget scale; era-cut technique discussion below intersects this.

## Era-cut technique

Bible-locked: Ouroboros uses era-cut as transition device across 7 eras (Roman → far-future). Niche validation:

**Multi-era games in niche.** Civilization series uses era progression mechanically but trailers rarely *cut across eras* as a structural device — Civ trailers usually pick ONE era for cinematic focus and let the gameplay-cut sequence imply era progression. Stellaris expansion trailers occasionally cut across tech tiers but inside a single setting. CK3 spans centuries within a single setting, no era-cut in trailers. Manor Lords single-era. Old World single-era. Wildermyth has chapter transitions but trailers don't lean on them.

**Multi-era trailer precedent OUTSIDE niche.** Civilization VII reveal trailer cuts across historical eras as its central device — and the trailer was received with criticism (recall: "looks like a content compilation"). Mass Effect Andromeda + Legendary Edition cinematics cut across game eras within an IP. Total War warhammer cuts across factions, not eras. AAA cinematic precedent for era-cut exists but is mixed. REPORTED, ESTIMATED on reception.

**Risk profile (REPORTED).** Era-cut trailer technique carries two failure modes documented in trailer-postmortem literature:

1. **Content-compilation risk.** When era-cut shows brief glimpses of each era in sequence, viewer reads it as "this is a montage of separate games stapled together" rather than "this is one game across time." Civilization VII reveal hit this critique. Mitigation: thread a single character / artifact / institution across cuts so the era-cut reads as *continuity* not *compilation*.
2. **Identity-blur risk.** Trailer can't establish "what genre is this" if it shows 7 different visual registers in 75s. Cold-traffic Steam viewer doesn't know if it's a Roman game, a sci-fi game, a fantasy game. Mitigation: bias screentime to ONE anchor era for the hook + endcard, use other eras as transition flashes mid-trailer.

**Validation against niche.** Niche-strategy buyer tolerance for era-cut as the central structural device is UNTESTED at scale. CK3 sells continuity-of-dynasty within a single era. Wildermyth sells continuity-of-character within a single setting. The mechanical fantasy these audiences buy is *deep simulation of one thing*, not *montage across many things*. Era-cut technique aligns with Foundation/hard-SF register (Stapledon's Last and First Men, Asimov's Foundation millennia-spanning structure) — but that's the differentiator tribe, not the primary cluster.

**Synthesis.** Era-cut is in-register for the thematic-differentiator tribe (Foundation/hard-SF) and risk-bearing for the primary tribe (Cluster A). Mitigation patterns (threaded continuity object, anchor-era bias, hand-off shot grammar) exist but are author-skill-dependent. AMBIGUOUS whether era-cut as central trailer device serves the primary tribe; CLEAR that it serves the differentiator tribe.

## Pre-launch cadence

Niche-strategy multi-trailer launch cadence (REPORTED):

- **Announce trailer.** 12-24 months pre-launch. Cinematic-leaning, often pre-alpha. Purpose: wishlist seed + creator outreach + festival slot bait. Length 60-90s modal. Examples: Manor Lords announce (~2021), CK3 announce, Old World announce.
- **Gameplay-reveal trailer.** 6-12 months pre-launch. Gameplay-heavy. Purpose: convert wishlist into "this is real" conviction, drive coverage, second wishlist wave. Length 75-120s modal. Examples: Manor Lords gameplay reveal, Frostpunk 2 gameplay reveal.
- **Next Fest / Steam Fest trailer.** ~3-6 months pre-launch (or earlier; Next Fest cadence is quarterly). Demo-tied. Purpose: discovery via fest carousel, wishlist push, demo download conversion. Length ≤60s often (festival slot constraints), capsule-sizzle shape. Examples: Songs of Syx Next Fest trailers, Manor Lords Next Fest 2023.
- **Pre-launch / release-date trailer.** 1-2 months pre-launch. Highest gameplay-ratio, lowest cinematic. Purpose: drive wishlist closer to purchase + lock launch date in audience mind. Length 60-90s. Examples: Manor Lords release-date, Wildermyth launch.
- **Launch trailer.** Day-of-launch. Often the most polished cut; may reuse footage from prior trailers but with launch-stinger. Purpose: signal to algorithm + media that game is live. Length 60-90s.
- **Optional: festival-slot trailers (IGN First, Wholesome Direct, Day of the Devs, Future Games Show, Triple-I Initiative).** Cut-down or specific-festival-tailored versions. Often <60s. Slot cadence varies; festival-organizer relationships drive these.

**Cadence pattern.** 4-6 trailer releases across 12-24 months for a niche-strategy launch is modal. Fewer than 3 trailer releases = under-marketed. More than 8 = over-saturating cold traffic with content that won't differ much. REPORTED.

**Cross-cutting.** GameDiscoverCo training-recall: wishlist conversion peaks around announce + first gameplay-reveal, then plateaus until release-date trailer. Next Fest is the second-largest wishlist spike for most indies. ESTIMATED.

## VO vs VO-free

Voice-over pattern split in niche-strategy trailers (REPORTED):

- **Diegetic VO** (in-fiction voices, often character dialogue or radio chatter). Foxhole content trailers, HD2 propaganda films, Stellaris cinematic trailers (occasional alien-broadcast framing). Works when the game has voiced characters or in-fiction broadcast register. Foundation/hard-SF tribe precedent: archival-broadcast register ("Encyclopedia Galactica entry...") is in-register and used in Apple TV Foundation marketing. ESTIMATED 30-40% of niche-strategy launches use diegetic VO.
- **Narrator VO** (out-of-fiction omniscient voice). Less common in niche-strategy; more common in AAA reveals (Civilization VII has narrator VO). Niche reads narrator-VO as "marketing voice" and treats it with mild suspicion. RimWorld, Battle Brothers, Songs of Syx, Wildermyth all VO-free. ESTIMATED 10-15%.
- **VO-free, text-on-screen primary.** RimWorld, Wildermyth, Songs of Syx, Manor Lords (mostly), Old World launch (mostly). The trailer carries register via text cards + on-screen prose + music. Cluster A and Cluster B primary mode. ESTIMATED 50-60% of niche-strategy launches.
- **VO-free, gameplay-audio primary.** Manor Lords gameplay-reveal trailer uses minimal text and lets ambient gameplay audio + score carry register. Works when the game's atmosphere is the pitch. ESTIMATED 10-15%.

**Per-niche pattern.** Cluster A (dynastic/generational): VO-free text-primary modal. Cluster B (emergent-story sim): VO-free or sparse-narration. Cluster C (grand strategy spatial): split between narrator-VO (Civ/Stellaris) and VO-free (Old World/EU4 dev diaries). Foundation/hard-SF tribe: diegetic-archival-VO is in-register but rare in current niche game trailers — used in Apple TV Foundation marketing and adjacent literary-SF film trailers.

**For Ouroboros.** Primary tribe alignment → VO-free text-primary modal. Differentiator tribe alignment → diegetic-archival-VO is in-register. AMBIGUOUS whether one trailer can hold both; possible solution is VO-free text-primary for the main trailer + diegetic-VO for a Foundation-tribe-targeted festival cut.

## Cross-cutting findings

- **75-90s is the modal niche-strategy top-slot trailer length.** Going under (sizzle) or over (deep-dive) has specific use cases but is not the launch top-slot norm. REPORTED.
- **Captioning is mandatory.** Steam capsule autoplay defaults muted; any VO/text register must be burned-in. Niche-strategy audience reads captions as in-register, not friction. REPORTED.
- **Gameplay-ratio floor ~65-70% for niche fit.** Cinematic-heavy works only when the cinematic IS the pitch and an audience-trust foundation already exists. REPORTED.
- **Pre-launch cadence is 4-6 trailers across 12-24 months.** Announce → gameplay reveal → Next Fest → release-date → launch (+ optional festival cuts). REPORTED.
- **Era-cut as central structural device is bible-locked but niche-untested at primary-tribe scale.** Differentiator-tribe-aligned, primary-tribe-risk-bearing. Mitigation patterns (threaded continuity object, anchor-era bias) are documented. AMBIGUOUS on primary-tribe reception.
- **VO-free text-primary is the modal niche register.** Diegetic-archival-VO is in-register for the Foundation/hard-SF differentiator but rare in niche-game trailer precedent. REPORTED.

## Conflict surfaced

- **Era-cut technique vs primary-tribe trailer norms.** Bible-locks era-cut as transition device. Niche-strategy primary tribe (Cluster A) trailer norm is single-era visual anchor with simulation/UI emphasis. Tension: era-cut central → differentiator-tribe-aligned but risks primary-tribe "content compilation" read. Mitigation patterns exist but introduce trailer-author skill dependency. Design Lane fork: does trailer lead with era-cut (Foundation register) or anchor in one era + era-cut as flash transitions (Cluster A register)?
- **Cinematic-heavy trailer aesthetic vs niche-strategy gameplay-ratio norm.** Lieutenant System + chronicle prose-system is cinematic-tempting (character closeups, voiced lieutenants, prose intercuts). Niche norm requires ≥65-70% gameplay. Tension: if Lieutenant System trailer leans into character-cinematic, gameplay-ratio drops below niche threshold. Design Lane fork: does Lieutenant System get its own non-top-slot character trailer + gameplay-heavy top-slot, or fused?
- **Diegetic-archival-VO vs primary-tribe VO-free modal.** Foundation/hard-SF differentiator tribe is VO-receptive (archival register). Primary cluster is VO-free modal. Tension only if one trailer must serve both; festival-cut separation resolves.
