# TikTok / Shortform Viability

## Strategy titles w/ TikTok organic traction

Three documented cases anchor the strategy/sim TikTok success pattern. Each found organic traction through a different content shape; none of the three is a clean replicable formula.

**Manor Lords (Slavic Magic / Hooded Horse, 2024)** — pre-launch TikTok built a wishlist base reported variously in the 2 million to 3 million range across the 18 months before April 2024 launch. The dominant content shape was the medieval-architecture rotation: a single building rotating on its turntable with ambient sound, no narration, 7-12 seconds, captioned with the building name and a one-line lore tease. Greg Styczeń (solo developer) posted these consistently, often weekly. The format worked because it was hook-on-first-frame (a beautiful building, no preamble), low-effort to produce (the buildings already existed in-engine; the dev just spun them and screen-captured), and replicated the "wonder of a settlement" feeling the game promised. The Manor Lords case is the single most cited strategy/sim TikTok organic-traction example in indie-dev marketing writing. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Vampire Survivors (poncle, 2022-2023)** — not strategy per se but adjacent (auto-battler / horde survivor). Found viral TikTok traction in late 2022 and through 2023 via gameplay-clip TikToks where users showed escalating screen-fill chaos. The dev did not post most of these; players posted them, and TikTok's algorithm rewarded the visual escalation (small character to overwhelming particle storm in 30 seconds). The lesson here is that TikTok traction can come from the player base, not the dev, when the gameplay loop is visually unmistakable in 6-30 seconds. Vampire Survivors went from a $5 indie to a multi-platinum hit largely on word-of-mouth, much of which moved through shortform. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Lethal Company (Zeekerss, 2023-2024)** — co-op horror not strategy, but informative for niche-tribe shortform dynamics. Organic TikTok and YouTube Shorts traction came from streamer-and-friend group clips: jump scares, panic reactions, betrayal moments, character drama between voice-chat friends. Zero dev marketing input. The game went from solo-dev launch to one of the top-selling Steam titles of late 2023 on this base. Lesson: when emergent player drama is the gameplay loop, shortform clips itself organically without dev involvement. Confidence: REPORTED.

Per-title analysis. Manor Lords' format (architecture rotation) is the closest direct precedent for Ouroboros — it sold a "world being built" feeling through a still, narration-free, hook-on-first-frame format. Vampire Survivors' format depends on player-generated content, which requires either a viral gameplay moment or an audience already large enough to seed clips. Lethal Company's format depends on emergent character drama between players, which presupposes multiplayer with voice chat. Ouroboros is single-player at launch (per current bible), so the Manor Lords format is the only directly transferable case; Vampire Survivors and Lethal Company are instructive for ceiling but not for the launch playbook.

## Content shapes that work

Four shapes recur in strategy/sim shortform that finds traction. Each maps to a different dev-effort profile and a different audience hook.

**Emergent-story moment.** A 10-30 second clip showing one beat of an emergent narrative: a Crusader Kings 3 incest-spiral, a RimWorld colonist eating their cat, a Dwarf Fortress fortress collapse, a Wildermyth character permadeath. Works because the hook is character-driven and the payoff is in the caption ("My king just married his horse"). Content is high-payoff when it lands but requires either a deeply-mechanical story-generator simulation (Ouroboros has this) or pre-existing community to seed clips. Dev-effort to produce is moderate — the dev plays the game, screencaps the moment, captions it. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Era-cut.** A single clip cuts across multiple eras of a generational/legacy game: pre-industrial peasants in frame one, industrial smokestacks in frame two, atomic skyline in frame three, post-collapse ruins in frame four. Hook is the visual transformation on a beat. Works because it sells the generational/legacy fantasy in seconds. Ouroboros' 7-era arc maps directly to this format — the era-cut may be the single most TikTok-native asset the bible already implies. Dev-effort to produce is moderate — same camera angle across era saves, ~30 minutes per video once the pipeline exists. Confidence: ESTIMATED, no exact strategy precedent but the format is established in city-builder TikTok (Cities: Skylines era-evolution content, Manor Lords season-cut content).

**ASMR-style timelapse.** A 15-60 second timelapse of a settlement growing, a region developing, a population rising and falling, with ambient or minimalist music and no narration. Hook is the satisfying scale-shift. Works because it sells the "simulation is alive" feeling Ouroboros is built on. Dev-effort to produce is low once the timelapse capture exists (Ouroboros' chronicle / tick-replay infrastructure could feed this directly). Confidence: ESTIMATED, draws on Workers & Resources, Songs of Syx, and Anno community timelapse content patterns.

**Character drama.** A clip focused on one named character — a lieutenant, a faction leader, a chronicle figure — with a single hook line ("This man founded three civilizations and burned all three down"). Hook is parasocial attachment in 6 seconds. Works in generational/legacy strategy because the audience cares about names. Maps directly to Ouroboros' lieutenant system + chronicle output. Dev-effort to produce is moderate — requires a character that the dev finds compelling and a one-line hook. Confidence: ESTIMATED, draws on Crusader Kings 3 character TikTok content and Wildermyth character meme economy.

## Algorithm pattern 2026

TikTok algorithm patterns documented through late 2024 / early 2025 consistently report:

- **6-15 second sweet spot for cold-traffic discovery.** Longer clips (30-60s) perform better for warm audiences but worse for breakout reach. Strategy content that finds organic traction is overwhelmingly in the 6-15 second range. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Hook-first-frame is non-negotiable.** Algorithm weights retention from frame 1 to frame 3 heavily; if the first second isn't a hook, the clip is buried regardless of the rest of its quality. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Vertical 9:16 required.** Horizontal clips are deprioritized. Captures from a horizontal-native game (Ouroboros is horizontal) require cropping or letterboxing decisions. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Captions / on-screen text are heavily weighted.** TikTok's algorithm reads on-screen text and silent-viewing accessibility is a retention signal. Strategy content without captions underperforms strategy content with captions even when the audio is informative. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Trend-piggyback amplifies.** Using current trending audio or trending caption formats boosts initial distribution but ties content to a short half-life. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Algorithm changes frequently.** 2026 specifics are AMBIGUOUS — TikTok has historically rebalanced the For You page algorithm multiple times per year, and platform-side US regulatory pressure through 2024-2025 has accelerated changes. Anything in this section should be assumed to be 60-70% accurate by 2026, with the underlying principles (hook, vertical, captions) more stable than the specifics (optimal length window, trending audio half-life).

## YT Shorts viability

YouTube Shorts functions as a secondary surface for strategy/sim shortform with a different payoff profile than TikTok.

Pros for niche strategy. Higher dev-control over discovery — YT Shorts can attach to a creator's existing YouTube channel and feed long-form video subscribers, which is more durable than a TikTok follow. Audience demographic skews older than TikTok (median creator-reach age on YT Shorts trends 25-44 vs TikTok's 16-34 in most US data through 2024). Discovery surfaces overlap with the strategy/sim YouTube ecosystem (Splattercat, Wanderbots, Nookrium, Quill18) so Shorts can feed and be fed by long-form coverage. Confidence: REPORTED.

Cons. Lower viral ceiling than TikTok — YT Shorts payoffs cluster around 10,000-100,000 views for indie game content; TikTok payoffs occasionally break into the 1M-10M range. Algorithm rewards channel-internal retention more than TikTok does, so a brand-new channel starts cold; a channel with an existing audience starts warm. Lower native-shortform creator economy means fewer organic re-clippers. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

Net. YT Shorts is plausibly a better fit for Ouroboros than TikTok if the primary tribe (30-42 age skew per T-A3) and secondary tribe (35-48) does not heavily use TikTok. The secondary surface is also easier to maintain — clipping from an existing long-form video or stream into Shorts is lower-overhead than producing TikTok-native content from scratch.

## Shortform-to-wishlist conversion

Conversion estimates are noisy and platform-asymmetric. Drawn from publisher post-mortems and Chris Zukowski's ranges:

- **TikTok clip with 100,000+ views.** Conversion to wishlist is ESTIMATED at 0.1% to 2% of unique viewers, with the wide range driven by hook quality and whether the clip includes a Steam page link in bio or caption. Floor case: a 100k-view clip yields ~100 wishlists. Strong case: a 100k-view clip with a great hook and clear CTA yields ~2,000 wishlists. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **TikTok viral hit (1M+ views).** Conversion typically drops in percentage terms (more casual viewers, less intent) but absolute number rises. ESTIMATED 0.05% to 0.5%. A 1M-view clip yields 500-5,000 wishlists. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **YT Shorts.** Conversion ESTIMATED slightly higher per-view than TikTok (0.2% to 3%) because YT Shorts viewers can click directly to a creator's long-form video, store page link, or pinned comment without leaving the platform. A 50k-view YT Short with a Steam CTA may yield 100-1,500 wishlists. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **Volume implication.** To generate 10,000 wishlists from shortform alone at a 0.5% midpoint conversion requires ~2 million total platform views. That is a substantial volume target — the equivalent of one large viral hit or 50 mid-tier clips at 40k views each. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

Conversion floor caveat. These ranges assume the Steam page is competent (capsule, trailer, copy aligned with the shortform hook). A shortform clip that promises one fantasy (e.g. building rotation) and a Steam page that delivers a different fantasy (e.g. RTS combat) will convert near zero. Cross-reference `docs/research/topics/steam_page_conversion_norms.md`.

## Posting cadence

Dev-driven shortform cadence patterns observed in indie strategy/sim:

- **Daily.** Highest discovery ceiling, highest burnout risk. Manor Lords' pre-launch period and Cities: Skylines DLC pre-launch periods sometimes hit daily. Sustainable only when the format is low-effort (architecture rotation, screen-capture clip).
- **3-5x weekly.** Most common for dedicated shortform pushes; sustainable for ~3 months but not indefinitely without a content pipeline.
- **Weekly.** Sustainable long-term for a solo dev or small team. Lower ceiling, but compound effect over 6-12 months can build a meaningful follower base. Most strategy/sim devs who maintain shortform presence sit at this cadence.
- **Sporadic / dump-on-release.** Most common pattern. Most indie devs post 5-20 clips across the launch window and effectively zero outside it. Lowest ceiling but lowest cost.

Per-video effort. Estimated 30 minutes to 2 hours per shortform video for a dev-produced clip (game capture, edit, caption, upload, hashtag research). At weekly cadence that is 2-8 dev hours per month; at daily cadence that is 15-60 dev hours per month. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

Cumulative ceiling. The compound effect of consistent shortform over 6-12 months is the main mechanism behind cases like Manor Lords — no single clip carried the wishlist trajectory; the cumulative 100+ clips built the audience together. A single dump-on-release campaign rarely replicates this. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Effort-vs-payoff

The effort-vs-payoff calibration is the key calibration for the Ouroboros shortform decision.

**Per-clip math.** At 1-2 hours per clip and an ESTIMATED 100-2,000 wishlists per landed clip (with most clips landing in the 10-200 wishlist range and the upside being rare viral hits), the per-hour wishlist yield is highly variable. A weekly cadence (4 clips/month, 4-8 hours/month) yielding a median 200 wishlists/month is approximately 25-50 wishlists per dev-hour. A daily cadence yielding 2,000 wishlists/month is approximately 30-60 wishlists per dev-hour. At higher cadences the median per-clip yield drops as the dev runs out of best hooks first, so the per-hour return is not strictly linear. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

**Comparison.** Chris Zukowski's published position has been that for most indie devs, equivalent dev-hours spent on Steam page optimization, capsule A/B testing, festival applications, or one well-produced trailer yield more wishlists per hour than ongoing TikTok cadence. The exception is when the game has a clip-friendly visual hook that the dev can post cheaply (Manor Lords' architecture rotation is the canonical case). Confidence: REPORTED.

**Ouroboros-specific read.** Ouroboros has at least three potentially clip-friendly assets per the bible: era-cuts across the 7 eras (visual transformation in seconds), lieutenant character moments (parasocial 6-second hooks), and chronicle / timelapse output (ASMR-friendly settlement evolution). All three are dev-productive at moderate cost once the capture pipeline exists. The brief surfaces this as a fact, not as an endorsement.

## Niche-tribe consumption

The key calibration. Whether the primary tribe (30-42 age skew, per T-A3, generational/legacy strategy buyers) and secondary tribe (35-48 age skew, Foundation / literary hard-SF readers) actually consume shortform in volumes that justify investment.

**Primary tribe (30-42, generational/legacy strategy).** TikTok demographic data through 2024 reports US TikTok user base skews 16-34 with rapid 25-44 growth post-2022. The 30-42 band is in the rising-but-not-dominant cohort. The tribe overlaps significantly with the Crusader Kings 3 audience, which has visible TikTok presence (the /r/CrusaderKings TikTok-clip economy exists but is mid-sized, not dominant). The tribe is more reliably reached on YouTube long-form, Reddit, and Discord than on TikTok. Confidence: REPORTED with ESTIMATED edges.

**Secondary tribe (35-48, Foundation / literary hard-SF).** Older skew. TikTok consumption among 35-48-year-old book-readers / hard-SF fans is documented as low — this is the cohort that most consistently reports preferring text, longform podcast, longform YouTube essay, and Reddit. The Apple TV Foundation viewership halo lives more naturally on YouTube and traditional review surfaces. YT Shorts may be a better secondary surface for this tribe than TikTok. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Net.** The Ouroboros target tribes are not TikTok-native in the way the 16-28 cohort is. This reduces the upside ceiling for a TikTok-first push and increases the relative attractiveness of YT Shorts as the primary shortform surface (if any shortform is pursued at all). The Manor Lords precedent is real but Manor Lords' audience also skewed older — meaning TikTok can work for older audiences via algorithmic crossover, just at a lower base rate than for younger-audience titles. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

## Cross-cutting findings

- Strategy/sim TikTok organic traction is rare but reproducible when the dev has a clip-friendly visual hook and posts consistently — Manor Lords is the canonical case, not the only case.
- Per-clip wishlist conversion is high-variance — most clips return near zero, a few return well, rare viral hits return outsized. Cadence smooths the variance but does not eliminate it.
- Ouroboros' bible-implied assets (era-cuts, lieutenant character moments, chronicle timelapse) map well to documented strategy/sim shortform shapes; the format-fit is real even if the audience-fit is moderate.
- YT Shorts likely fits the Ouroboros tribes better than TikTok on demographic grounds, with a lower viral ceiling but easier dev-side production from existing long-form / stream capture.
- Effort-vs-payoff favors shortform when the format is low-effort and the cadence is sustained for 6+ months; favors Steam page optimization and trailer investment when the format would be high-effort or the cadence would be sporadic.
- Algorithm specifics for 2026 are AMBIGUOUS — anchoring strategy on platform mechanics is brittle; anchoring on hook quality and visual differentiation is durable.

## Ouroboros shortform options

The brief surfaces options without picking. Five approaches surface:

**Option 1: Dedicated TikTok push, daily-to-weekly cadence.** Maximize ceiling. Highest dev-hour cost; highest upside if a clip breaks out. Suits a dev who finds the shortform format energizing and can sustain output for 6+ months. Risk is dev-hours sunk on a tribe (30-48 age) that is not TikTok-native.

**Option 2: YT Shorts-only push, weekly cadence.** Lower ceiling, better fit for the target tribes. Attaches to a YouTube channel and feeds long-form coverage. Dev-side production can clip from existing playtest recordings or streams, lowering per-clip overhead.

**Option 3: Clip-from-stream / clip-from-influencer secondary surface.** Dev does not produce native shortform; instead, streams playtests live, plays in front of influencer ecosystems (Splattercat / Wanderbots / Quill18 / SsethTzeentach), and lets viewers/creators clip moments. Lowest direct dev-hour cost. Lowest direct control over what gets clipped. Highest dependence on a Steam page that converts when traffic arrives.

**Option 4: Hybrid — YT Shorts dev-produced + TikTok auto-mirrored.** Produce for YT Shorts (best demographic fit), cross-post to TikTok and Reels with minimal additional cost. Captures upside from either platform's algorithm catching a clip without dedicating to one.

**Option 5: Skip shortform entirely.** Allocate the equivalent dev-hours to Steam page optimization, capsule A/B testing, Next Fest preparation, trailer production, and direct community work on Reddit / Discord. Chris Zukowski's default recommendation for most indie devs at the primary-tribe age bracket Ouroboros targets.

The Research Lane surfaces these. The pick belongs in the Design Lane.

## Conflict surfaced

The brief surfaces one potential conflict against the bible. The bible's audience targeting (primary tribe 30-42, secondary tribe 35-48) and the documented TikTok demographic skew suggest TikTok-first is misaligned with audience-targeting; this is not a hard conflict — TikTok does reach 30-48-year-olds, just at a lower base rate — but it is a calibration tension that the Design Lane should weigh. No conflict against the bible's tone, IP-isolation, or generational/legacy fantasy commitments; era-cut and lieutenant-moment content shapes actively reinforce those commitments.

No conflict against the RTS-framing-kill decision (per `docs/bible/game_bible.md ## In Flux` 2026-05-16 entry) — shortform content can showcase strategy/lieutenant content cleanly without invoking RTS framing, and in fact does so better than long-form trailer formats because the 6-15 second window forces a fantasy-first hook rather than a feature-checklist read.
