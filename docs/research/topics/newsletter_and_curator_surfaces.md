# Newsletter and Curator Surfaces

## What this brief is

Inputs map for newsletter + Steam-curator surfaces relevant to Ouroboros Manifold's pre-launch and launch-window awareness strategy. Brief covers Chris Zukowski's Game Maker's Garage Strategy & Wargames newsletter, Hooded Horse publisher curator, Strategy Gamer outlet curator, SsethTzeentach's Steam curator, and a small tail of niche-strategy curators (Splattercat, MandaloreGaming) that recur in adjacent strategy/sim post-mortems. Brief lays out pitch acceptance criteria per surface, ESTIMATED conversion at each step, and the sustained-awareness vs launch-spike role split each surface plays.

Non-recommendation disclaimer: brief surfaces inputs, does not pick. Conversion figures are ESTIMATED with a training-data floor — WebFetch is disabled for this capture, so subscriber counts, follower counts, and click-through ratios cannot be re-verified against live pages. Numbers below are best-effort recall from publicly-discussed figures circa training-cutoff plus dev post-mortem mentions. Treat individual numbers as ±30% to ±50%. Treat the synthesis as REPORTED — one consistent reading of available evidence, not the only reading.

This brief feeds Ouroboros' sustained-awareness plan (the multi-month wishlist drip preceding launch), T-C3 (press pipeline targeting), and T-W2 (wishlist velocity — sustained slow accumulation vs concentrated launch-day spike).

## Game Maker's Garage Strategy & Wargames newsletter

Chris Zukowski operates the Game Maker's Garage newsletter ecosystem as a strategy-niche-specific arm of his How To Market A Game / Game Discoverability Day consulting practice. The Strategy & Wargames newsletter is one of several genre verticals (the others include the broader How To Market A Game newsletter, and adjacent verticals such as Cozy Games and Horror that he has spun up over time).

Subscriber count: ESTIMATED 3,000 to 8,000 for the Strategy & Wargames vertical specifically, REPORTED 20,000+ for the broader How To Market A Game newsletter as of training-cutoff. The vertical newsletter is smaller-N than the umbrella newsletter but is far more niche-fit for a strategy/sim title. Zukowski has stated in conference talks that his strategy vertical readership skews heavily dev-side (other strategy devs reading for marketing intel) but also includes a meaningful slice of strategy-enthusiast buyers — perhaps 30 to 50% buyer-side per Zukowski's own framing. ESTIMATED.

Niche-fit: very high for Ouroboros. The newsletter explicitly covers wargames, grand strategy, 4X, simulation, and the dynasty/legacy strategy axis that the primary tribe (per `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md`) sits inside. Past issues have featured Old World, Field of Glory: Empires, Workers & Resources, Songs of Syx, Nebulous, Sea Power, and related Hooded Horse catalog titles. Pattern: the newsletter selects games at meaningful pre-launch and launch-window inflection points (Next Fest entries, demo drops, full launches, major patches/DLC).

Editorial voice: Zukowski writes from the marketing-craft lens. Inclusion typically pairs with a brief tactical note on what the developer did well — capsule quality, demo timing, tag set, store page structure. This makes inclusion doubly valuable: the title gets exposure to the readership AND the title's marketing surfaces are implicitly validated for the dev-side readership who will mention it to their players.

Cadence: REPORTED biweekly to monthly depending on the vertical. Strategy & Wargames runs less frequently than the umbrella How To Market A Game newsletter — perhaps 12 to 20 issues per year. ESTIMATED.

Click-through behavior: niche newsletters of this shape and size run ESTIMATED 1 to 3% click-through to the featured game's Steam page per featured title. With 3,000 to 8,000 subscribers and a 1 to 3% CTR, expect 30 to 240 Steam page visits per featured title. Wishlist conversion of those visits depends on Steam page quality — see T-W2 calibration and `wishlist_conversion_benchmarks_2026.md` for the downstream conversion math.

## Hooded Horse curator base

Hooded Horse operates a publisher Steam curator that aggregates their catalog (Old World, Falling Frontier, Manor Lords, Workers & Resources, Terra Invicta, Against the Storm, etc.) plus occasional spotlight picks from outside their catalog where the title fits the publisher's strategy/sim niche.

Follower count: ESTIMATED 80,000 to 200,000 followers as of training-cutoff. Hooded Horse has been one of the fastest-growing strategy-focused publishers in the 2022-to-2025 window, and their curator base has tracked roughly with their catalog growth. REPORTED that the publisher curator is a meaningful discovery surface for any title they sign or spotlight.

Editorial voice: dynamic in the Steam sense — the curator publishes regular reviews and recommendations tied to catalog releases, sales events, and occasional outside picks. Tone is enthusiast-curator, not corporate. Hooded Horse's branding leans on the "for strategy fans, by strategy fans" identity, which makes their recommendations carry weight inside the niche.

Niche-fit: highest of any strategy-only curator. Hooded Horse's catalog overlaps directly with the audience Ouroboros' lieutenant pivot is targeting (per `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md` ranked niches 1, 3, and 4 — generational/legacy strategy, narrative-weight survival-strategy, and indie strategy curiosity buyers).

Pitch path: outside-catalog spotlight inclusion is editorial-discretion; the publisher is approached by other devs and selects based on niche fit. ESTIMATED that a small fraction of submitted titles get spotlighted — perhaps 5 to 15% of qualifying strategy titles that approach them through known channels (the Hooded Horse pitching email, conference meetings, mutual industry contacts). REPORTED that being signed by Hooded Horse as a publisher gives automatic curator inclusion plus the publisher's full marketing apparatus.

Per-feature conversion: ESTIMATED 5 to 15% of curator followers who see a featured title's recommendation will click through; of those, ESTIMATED 10 to 25% wishlist on the same visit. With 80,000 to 200,000 followers, a successful Hooded Horse feature could yield 400 to 7,500 wishlists per feature, depending on follower engagement rate and Steam page quality. Wide range reflects honest uncertainty.

## Strategy Gamer curator

Strategy Gamer is a UK-based strategy/wargame outlet that operates both a website and a Steam curator. The outlet covers grand strategy, wargames, 4X, real-time strategy, and the wider simulation-adjacent space. The curator surfaces titles the editorial team reviews favorably plus occasional broader recommendations.

Follower count: ESTIMATED 15,000 to 40,000 followers on the Steam curator as of training-cutoff. The outlet itself is smaller-N than mass-market sites (PC Gamer, IGN) but punches above its weight inside the strategy niche due to genre-specialist credibility.

Editorial voice: review-driven. Curator recommendations track the outlet's review coverage with some lag and editorial selection. Tone is informed-enthusiast — the reviewers are knowledgeable strategy-genre veterans and the curator inherits that voice.

Niche-fit: high for Ouroboros, especially the generational/legacy and grand-strategy-adjacent shape. Strategy Gamer covers Crusader Kings 3 expansions, Old World, Workers & Resources, Field of Glory, the Slitherine catalog, and similar — all neighbors of Ouroboros' target audience.

Pitch path: curator inclusion typically follows editorial review. The outlet accepts review code requests through standard press channels; reviewer pickup is editorial-discretion based on genre fit, press-page polish, and demo/code availability. ESTIMATED 30 to 60% of genre-fit press requests get assigned a reviewer at strategy-niche outlets of this size, with maybe 40 to 70% of those resulting in a published review. Curator inclusion is approximately automatic for favorable reviews (positive recommendation tag) and selective for mixed reviews.

Per-feature conversion: ESTIMATED 3 to 8% of curator followers click through on a recommended title; ESTIMATED 8 to 20% of clickers wishlist. With 15,000 to 40,000 followers, a Strategy Gamer recommendation could yield 35 to 650 wishlists per feature — meaningfully smaller than Hooded Horse but with a more editorially-credentialed reach.

## SsethTzeentach Steam curator

SsethTzeentach operates one of the largest indie-strategy-and-RPG-leaning Steam curators on the platform. The channel itself (YouTube) drives Steam curator follow rates higher than typical for a curator of this shape because the audience explicitly trusts Sseth's taste in idiosyncratic, deep, often Eastern-European-or-obscure-Western strategy titles.

Follower count: ESTIMATED 150,000 to 400,000 followers on the Steam curator as of training-cutoff. Sseth's YouTube channel has REPORTED 1M+ subscribers; the curator follower count is a fraction of that but a high-conversion fraction given that following the curator is a deliberate signal of "I want Sseth's takes on Steam."

Editorial voice: idiosyncratic, sardonic, deeply genre-literate. Sseth's recommendations skew toward titles with strong authorial voice, mechanical depth, and Eastern European or otherwise off-the-beaten-path provenance. Past coverage includes Battle Brothers, Caves of Qud, Workers & Resources, Songs of Syx, Mount & Blade, Underrail, HighFleet, and similar — heavily overlapping the passionate-niche cluster identified in `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md`.

Niche-fit: very high for Ouroboros' thematic profile (literary hard-SF, deep simulation, authorial voice) and somewhat misaligned for tone (Sseth's coverage tends to feature games with darker humor or grittier framings than Ouroboros' tone implies — see `CLAUDE.md ## Tone`).

Pitch path: famously closed-off. Sseth does not respond to standard press pitches; coverage emerges from the channel's organic discovery process, often months or years after a game ships. Curator inclusion can occur independently of YouTube coverage but is also editorial-discretion. ESTIMATED that less than 5% of pitched titles get curator inclusion and far less get YouTube coverage. REPORTED that a Sseth YouTube video on a niche strategy title can move 10,000 to 100,000+ wishlists in the days following the video — extreme outlier among strategy creators.

Per-feature conversion: highly variable. Curator-only inclusion (no YouTube video) ESTIMATED 5 to 12% click-through, 10 to 20% wishlist conversion of clickers — with 150,000 to 400,000 followers, that's 750 to 9,600 wishlists per curator feature. YouTube + curator combined feature is a different scale entirely — outlier event, REPORTED multiple cases of 10,000+ wishlists in a single video's wake.

Sustained-awareness vs launch-spike role for Sseth coverage specifically: idiosyncratic. Sseth videos often surface years after a game's launch and create a long-tail second wind rather than a launch spike. The curator follow is launch-spike-capable; the YouTube video is sustained-awareness-and-second-wind.

## Newsletter-pitch acceptance criteria

Newsletter inclusion at Game Maker's Garage Strategy & Wargames (and adjacent strategy-vertical newsletters such as GameDiscoverCo when they spotlight strategy titles) shares a recurring set of criteria. Synthesized from published Zukowski blog posts, conference talks, and dev post-mortem mentions:

1. **Steam page quality.** Capsule art clarity, screenshot composition, tag set, short and long description quality, trailer. Newsletters spotlight games whose marketing surfaces are already at standard — they will not cover a game with a placeholder capsule or a missing trailer because they cannot credibly recommend an unmade marketing surface. ESTIMATED hard floor.
2. **Demo availability.** Demo titles get featured at substantially higher rates than non-demo titles. Newsletter readers want to try, not just wishlist. REPORTED in multiple Zukowski blog posts.
3. **Near-launch timing.** Newsletters time features to inflection points: Steam Next Fest entries, demo drops, full launches, major patches/DLC, festival nominations. Features at random points outside an inflection window are rare. REPORTED.
4. **Niche-fit.** Strategy newsletters cover strategy titles; genre-fit is a hard gate. Adjacent-genre titles (e.g., simulation, management) get covered when the strategy angle is foregrounded. ESTIMATED.
5. **Story or hook.** Solo dev, unusual mechanic, IP angle, art direction, post-mortem-worthy backstory — newsletters lean into the narrative. A title with a clean mechanical hook ("psychohistory-flavored generational strategy with autonomous lieutenants") will outperform a title pitched as "an indie strategy game." REPORTED.
6. **Press kit availability.** A complete press kit (presskit() format or similar) lowers the cost of coverage for the writer. ESTIMATED.

Acceptance rate: ESTIMATED 20 to 40% of strategy-niche-fit pitches with all six criteria met get newsletter coverage at Game Maker's Garage. Lower-niche-fit or weaker-press-kit pitches drop below 10%.

## Curator-pitch acceptance criteria

Steam curator inclusion splits into two operating modes: auto-curator (volume) and manual review (selectivity).

**Auto-curator mode.** Many curators run something close to "if you send a key, I'll add the game to the curator with a recommendation if the team likes it." This is the dominant mode for high-volume curators (Splattercat, mid-tier strategy outlets, smaller-scale enthusiast curators). Pitch path: send a Steam key via Curator Connect with a brief description. Acceptance is editorially-light; review writeup is short or absent. Conversion is meaningful in aggregate (dozens of small features compounding) but small per-feature. ESTIMATED 40 to 70% of niche-fit submissions get included.

**Manual review mode.** Larger or more selective curators (Hooded Horse, Strategy Gamer, SsethTzeentach, MandaloreGaming) hand-pick titles based on editorial fit, often after the team plays the game. Pitch path: standard press email with code, sometimes a separate curator-specific channel. Acceptance is selective. ESTIMATED 5 to 20% of pitched titles get manual-review inclusion at the larger selective curators.

Cross-cutting criteria for both modes:

- **Genre fit is a hard gate.** A strategy curator will not feature a platformer regardless of polish.
- **Steam page quality** matters less for curator features than for newsletter features — curators are recommending the game, not the page. But a broken or unfinished page reads as a credibility signal.
- **Demo or release availability** matters — many curators only recommend games that players can currently access (demo or full release). Pre-launch curator inclusion is rarer.
- **Reviewer-relationship history** matters at the selective tier. Devs with prior coverage relationships get easier inclusion. First-time devs face a higher bar.

Acceptance rate aggregate: ESTIMATED 15 to 40% of strategy-niche-fit pitches across the curator surface as a whole result in at least one curator inclusion. Mass-pitching the auto-curator tier reliably produces some inclusions; manual-review tier inclusion is the harder unlock.

## Sustained-awareness vs launch-spike

Newsletter and curator surfaces play different roles in a strategy/sim title's pre-launch and launch arc.

**Newsletters drive sustained-awareness, low-velocity wishlist accumulation.** Each issue features a small handful of titles to a few-thousand-subscriber audience. Per-issue wishlist contribution is small (tens to low hundreds) but cumulative across pre-launch (six to twelve months of biweekly-to-monthly issues) can compound to a meaningful base. More importantly, newsletter inclusion validates the title in the eyes of other press, dev-side readers (some of whom amplify), and serious enthusiast buyers who use newsletters as a discovery filter. The role is "drip irrigation" — slow, steady, building the wishlist base that converts at launch.

Multi-newsletter strategy works. Pitching Game Maker's Garage at one inflection point (demo drop), GameDiscoverCo or adjacent newsletters at another (Next Fest entry), and other vertical newsletters where relevant — each touch reinforces the title's presence in the strategy-press ecosystem. Sustained-awareness math: ESTIMATED 6 to 12 newsletter features across the pre-launch year, each contributing 30 to 300 wishlists, totals 200 to 3,500 wishlists over the year. This is small as an absolute number but meaningful as a base for launch-day virality (Steam's launch-day algorithm boost compounds against existing wishlists per `steam_algorithm_visibility.md`).

**Curators drive launch-spike visibility.** A coordinated curator-feature wave at launch — Hooded Horse spotlights, Strategy Gamer recommends, MandaloreGaming covers, plus the auto-curator tail — concentrates discovery in the launch-week window when Steam's algorithm is most active. Per-feature wishlist + sale contribution at launch is higher per-impression than pre-launch because Steam's "new and trending" lists amplify titles that are already getting traction.

Curator launch-window math: ESTIMATED 3 to 8 curator features during launch week, each contributing 200 to 5,000 wishlist conversions and a meaningful slice of launch-week sales. Cumulative curator contribution at launch ESTIMATED 1,000 to 20,000 wishlists + sales depending on coverage quality and Steam page strength.

**Hybrid role: a Sseth or Mandalore YouTube video.** These outlier events combine launch-spike (immediate wishlists) with sustained-awareness (long tail of discovery over months). They cannot be scheduled — they emerge organically. The sustained-awareness component is the more valuable part for a long-tail strategy title.

**Implication for T-W2 (wishlist velocity).** If T-W2 targets a balanced wishlist curve (sustained drip + launch spike), newsletter pipeline owns the drip and curator pipeline owns the spike. If T-W2 targets a launch-spike-dominant curve, curator pipeline carries the load. If T-W2 targets a sustained-drip-dominant curve (less common for indie strategy), newsletter + content marketing + community-building carries the load.

## Cross-cutting findings

- Newsletter and curator surfaces are non-overlapping in their primary function. Newsletter is sustained drip; curator is launch spike. A title that pitches only one surface leaves the other half of the funnel underbuilt.
- Hooded Horse is the highest-leverage single curator surface for Ouroboros' target audience overlap. Publisher signing would unlock automatic inclusion plus the full marketing apparatus; outside-spotlight inclusion is reachable but selective.
- The Sseth + Mandalore tier is uncontrollable but high-payoff. Sustained-awareness strategy should not depend on coverage from either, but should be optimized to be discoverable to them (depth, authorial voice, unusual mechanic) on the chance organic coverage emerges.
- Game Maker's Garage Strategy & Wargames is a credibility-and-amplification surface, not a wishlist-volume surface. Coverage there is worth pursuing as a marketing-stack tile even though raw wishlist conversion is small per issue.
- Strategy Gamer and adjacent strategy-niche outlets (PCGamer-Strategy-vertical, RockPaperShotgun-when-on-strategy-beat, Eurogamer-when-on-strategy-beat) bridge curator and press — coverage often produces both a review and a curator feature. T-C3 press pipeline should treat strategy-outlet curators as a side-effect of press review coverage, not a separate workstream.
- Acceptance rates compound. A title that meets newsletter-pitch criteria (Steam page quality, demo, near-launch timing, niche fit, hook, press kit) also meets curator-pitch criteria for the manual-review tier. Investment in the marketing-surfaces stack lifts acceptance rates across all surfaces.

## Conflict surfaced

Sseth and Mandalore's audiences overlap heavily with the Foundation/literary tribe; Hooded Horse's audience overlaps heavily with the generational/legacy tribe. Both audiences are reachable through these surfaces with no conflict, but the marketing-copy register may need to flex slightly per surface (more authorial-voice-forward for Sseth/Mandalore pitch, more depth-and-systems-forward for Hooded Horse pitch).
