## Submarine and naval games: active vs passive sonar, what a ping reveals, counter-detection, towed arrays, when to ping

### Takeaway
In every naval sim covered, active sensing is modeled as an information trade: it buys fast range/bearing data and classification-poor detection, and it announces the emitter to anyone listening, at a longer range than the emitter itself can detect at. Players describe the decision as "go active only when stealth is already lost, or just before a shot."

### Cited Findings
- Real-world rule the games copy: "In combat situations, an active pulse can be detected by an enemy and will reveal a submarine's position at twice the maximum distance that the submarine can itself detect a contact." Active sonar is "normally considered a backup to passive sonar." Passive sonar can outrange active when the target is loud enough and also allows identification. — [Wikipedia, Active sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_sonar) — VERIFIED (as Wikipedia states it; the "twice" figure is a rule of thumb, see Inferences)
- Cold Waters: hearing a ping does not mean you are detected. Players say you hear pings "FAR before you can be detected by them" because active sound travels out and back (two-way). — [Steam thread "If I'm pinged, am I detected?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1290691937729379755/) — REPORTED
- Cold Waters: the contact/signature screen shows per-sensor signature numbers; a negative number against the enemy's active sonar means that sonar has zero chance of detecting you; the model includes aspect (orientation to the ping), speed, depth and thermal layers. Players list countermeasures: slow down, change depth, go silent, turn toward the pinger to shrink aspect. — [same thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1290691937729379755/) — REPORTED
- Cold Waters: when an enemy pings you, the player gets "a yellow line briefly pointing away from your submarine on the map in the bearing of the active source"; bearing only, no range. Soviet surface ships ping aggressively because "they know they're already louder and detected." — [Steam thread "Active Sonar of Opfor"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/2921102177257146984/) — REPORTED
- Cold Waters, player on own pinging: active sonar is useful at about 2.5 km or less (varies with thermocline); "The moment I go active they launch on me. Within about 30 seconds." Useful vs quiet diesel boats, to complete a partial firing solution, or when already detected. Characterized as trading bearing secrecy for fast range and bearing: "A nickel, and it is likely a wooden nickel." — [Steam thread "Is active sonar useless?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1368380934295009433/) — REPORTED
- Cold Waters: a forum poster says detection status is read from the Signature tab: all negative means undetected, any positive means you are or are about to be detected; "a +10 threshold between first contact detection and re-gaining contact"; sonobuoys about 4-5 km effective range, MAD about 400 yards. — [Subsim, Cold Waters active sonar evasion theory](https://www.subsim.com/radioroom/showthread.php?t=235846) — REPORTED (a Steam-search snippet phrased the threshold differently, "up to 10 dB they do not know you are there"; the two readings of the number are not clearly the same; treat the exact threshold as AMBIGUOUS)
- Cold Waters, a player complaint about realism: submarines get detected within roughly 1,000 yards regardless of stealth measures. — [Steam thread "Active Sonar of Opfor"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/2921102177257146984/) — REPORTED
- Dangerous Waters tips page: "AI subs fire on Active sonar bearing easily most of the time"; use active mainly to refine a position solution or find surface ships while keeping EMCON; frigate (FFG) guidance is to ping just before firing then break contact and evade; "If you begin a mission by pinging away on active sonar, you will have already given away your presence as an ASW threat." — [Subsim Dangerous Waters tips](https://subsim.com/tips/tip_dangerwaters.html) — VERIFIED (tips page text as fetched)
- Dangerous Waters towed array: narrowband lines are often the first detection because low frequencies propagate farther; a towed array lags behind turns and can show as a ghost contact until it re-aligns; mirror (left/right) ambiguity is resolved by turning about 45 degrees and watching which contact jumps; own-ship speed raises self-noise (quoted thresholds about 5 knots hull array, 15 knots towed array). — [Subsim Dangerous Waters tips](https://subsim.com/tips/tip_dangerwaters.html) — VERIFIED (tips page); mirror-contact detail also in the search snippet from [Steam Dangerous Waters thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1600/discussions/0/3203652426723237544/) — REPORTED
- Dangerous Waters mast rules: raising periscope/radar masts above about 8-10 knots risks damage; ESM/radio need about 10 knots max. — [Subsim Dangerous Waters tips](https://subsim.com/tips/tip_dangerwaters.html) — VERIFIED
- UBOAT / Silent Hunter style: a hydrophone hears a boat's real noise, ASDIC "hears" the echo of its own ping; a boat lying on the seabed can look like a rock to ASDIC but a hydrophone still hears noise it makes; silent running = nonessential systems off, crew quiet, speed cut. — [Steam search snippets, UBOAT discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/494840/discussions/0/3482997257338515514/) — REPORTED; real-world silent running definition (systems off, low RPM below cavitation band, aimed at passive sonar) — [Wikipedia, Silent running (submarine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_running_(submarine)) — VERIFIED
- Sea Power: passive submarine sensors detect active pings at extended ranges (game models this); radar paradox: "you turn on your radar to find the enemy" and become visible to ESM. — [search snippets from Sea Power Steam guides](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3492075776) — REPORTED (guide page itself returned 429)
- Sea Power ESM marking: player saw a contact marked "E" and "R" (emission and radar detection) while own radars were in EMCON; ESM picked up incoming missile seekers at about 15-20 nm; radar emission lets AI classify an ambiguous contact as hostile without visual ID. A player concluded "EMCON will get you killed" against IR-guided sea-skimmers (visual detection only at about 4 nm). — [Steam thread "Your radar will kill you"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1286220/discussions/0/597386590728244241/) — REPORTED
- Command: Modern Operations: EMCON is set per unit/side; sensors can be toggled per group ("UNIT EMCON") or per sensor (F9); radars, sonars and OECM are covered; ships keep their assigned silent/radiating posture unless attacked, then self-defense overrides EMCON ("Ignore EMCON while under attack"), default ON since Build 522 after players complained units "spontaneously light up their radars" when it was off. — [Matrix Games forum thread](https://forums.matrixgames.com/viewtopic.php?t=256722) — REPORTED; EMCON/sensor window basics from [search snippets](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1076160/discussions/0/4413047527586922193/) — REPORTED
- Command: MO added an "intermittent emissions" feature so a sensor can emit in intervals rather than only continuous or off; horizon propagation factors of 1.23 (radar), 1.5 (ESM), 1.06 (EO/IR) are modeled. — [Command: MO Steam news / developer posts (search snippet)](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?feed=steam_community_announcements&appids=1076160&appgroupname=Command:+Modern+Operations&enddate=1677159249) — REPORTED

### Inferences
- The games implement counter-detection asymmetrically in different ways: Cold Waters gives the pinged player a bearing-only warning line and a numeric signature readout; Sea Power gives an "E/R" emission flag on the contact; Dangerous Waters leaves the cost implicit (AI fires on the ping bearing).
- Active sensing in these games is not just "reveals position"; it also makes the pinger identifiable as hostile (Sea Power) and triggers a response within tens of seconds (Cold Waters player report).

### Gaps
- No primary source (manual or devlog) found giving Cold Waters' actual counter-detection formula or ping source level. Only player readings of the signature screen.
- Silent Hunter series: no source found on any submarine-side active ping; only UBOAT/SH3 destroyer ASDIC and hydrophone behavior via forum snippets.
- Numbers for Dangerous Waters counter-detection range of a ping were not found.
- Cold Waters towed arrays: a search snippet says contact info lists "active, passive and (if the target has it) towed array"; no rules for the player's own towed array found.

## Nebulous: Fleet Command: radar types, search vs track, EW, going dark, developer description

### Takeaway
Nebulous models radar as an emission that is itself the main target: ARAD and home-on-jam seekers fly down emissions, ELINT shows "a radar is in this direction" from farther than the radar can see, and "stealth" means shutting radar and comms off (blind but unseen) or jamming (seen as a noisy blob but not locked). The community states the core trade as "It's either Stealth or detection."

### Cited Findings
- Player summary: three radar types (Frontline, Parallax, Spyglass; Spyglass longest range), radiated power matters ("High is better"), and "It's either Stealth or detection. You can compromise between the two but you can't max both on the same ship." To be stealthy "deactivate radar and comms." — [Steam thread "Spotting/Radar/Jamming how do these things work?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/887570/discussions/0/3189115186362121110/) — REPORTED
- ELINT: "All it does is say 'There is an active RADAR transceiver in this direction'", needs an ESM mount, does nothing if the enemy radar is off, and "however far a given radar can see, ELINT can see that radar from farther." — [same thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/887570/discussions/0/3189115186362121110/) — REPORTED
- Jamming: two kinds, comm jamming and radar jamming; "multiple jammers" can make a ship "undetectable if you manage to jam all the radar in range hard enough." Another thread: "jammers only send you gibberish data... pay close attention and you will be able to see the real radar signature among the gibberish"; "winning is often a game of who can find who first, or who can attack the other while not being seen"; "Hangup" jammers cut inter-ship data sharing. — [same thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/887570/discussions/0/3189115186362121110/), [Steam thread "Advice on how to counter Jam Spam?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/887570/discussions/0/3275813923393513315/) — REPORTED
- Missile seeker table (community wiki, Wikidot): ACT = own forward radar emitter/receiver; SARH needs illumination from another ship; ARAD homes on ship search radar emissions; HOJ is an ARAD subset homing on jamming; CMD is datalink-guided from the launching ship; EO seeks visual/optical signature (ANS only); WAKE tracks engine wake particles "emitted every 5 seconds". Radar off: blinds ACT, SARH, ARAD, HOJ; CMD still works; EO unaffected; WAKE needs engines off, and "engines have to be off for 15 seconds before they become fully inactive." Jamming: ARAD can switch to HOJ; CMD defeated by comms jamming (Hangup jammer). — [NEBFLTCOM Wikidot, Missiles](http://nebfltcom.wikidot.com/mechanics:missiles) — VERIFIED (community wiki read directly; may lag current patch)
- EMCON as used in Nebulous: "EMCON... effective against HOJ by turning off all sources of radar jamming, against ARAD you will also have to turn off your radars (including dedicated fire control ones)." — [Steam guide on softkill (search snippet; guide returned 429)](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3137161888) — REPORTED
- Two stealth approaches described by a player: passive stealth (EMCON, "prowler drives", hull angling; radar-blind but gives no indicator of position; example signature 1,101 m2 with comms down) vs active stealth (jammers hide exact position but the general area is revealed by the jamming signal). A 25% signature reduction "usually is only going to mean a difference of a couple hundred meters"; dedicated stealth ships reach -66% to -70%; ELINT can detect nearby enemies within about 6 km even if stealthy; inside 3 km even heavily jammed ships are visible optically. — [Steam thread "Stealth: how does it work?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/887570/discussions/0/4409669834848556865/) — REPORTED
- Diminishing returns were added so each additional jammer on one target has less effect; all ships carry EO and WAKE signatures; "ships can run cool by turning systems inactive to hide from detection." Developer is described as "a Navy officer making a game about space navies." — [search-result summary of Steam/press pages, incl. Devlog #27](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/887570/view/3601221006617404855) — REPORTED (devlog page itself not read)
- Getting a radar contact vs a lock: guides distinguish search from fire-control radars and "bullseye"/"parallax" as recommended EW minimum; players recommend floodlights to raise enemy radar cross-section. — [Steam thread "Advice on how to counter Jam Spam?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/887570/discussions/0/3275813923393513315/) — REPORTED

### Inferences
- Nebulous gives the "dark" option two flavors with different leakage: radar off leaks nothing electronic but leaves optical/wake/engine signatures; jamming hides identity but leaks bearing (HOJ and ELINT exploit it).
- Because ARAD and HOJ seekers require emissions, the same switch (radar off) is both the stealth button and a missile defense in this game.

### Gaps
- Official hoodedhorse Nebulous wiki (Radar, Electronic Warfare pages) was not readable (HTTP 403); exact formulas (signature, radiated power, burn-through) not obtained. A Steam guide "A Model for Radar and Jammer Detection" returned 429.
- No direct quote from the developer (Eridanus Industries) on the design intent of the sensor game was found beyond the "Navy officer" descriptor; Devlog #27 on railguns and jamming was not read in full.
- Search-vs-track distinction in Nebulous is only partially sourced (community notes); no rules text read.

## HighFleet: radar on/off, ELINT, IRST, self-detection, guidance

### Takeaway
HighFleet makes radar the thing that betrays you: ELINT reads a radar at about double its own range, turning radar off makes you invisible to enemy radar, and IRST is a passive, undetectable heat sensor with short range that is the fallback when radar is off.

### Cited Findings
- Radar 500-750 km range; detection depends on the lesser of your radar range and the target's radar signature value; "Turning radar off makes you undetectable by enemy radar systems." ELINT is a passive radar warning receiver at 1,000-1,500 km. IRST is infrared, depends on target IR signature (210 km+ for ship engines). Radio/antenna interception about 3,000 km (exact unknown). Tracking "lets you see ALL types of contact in realtime" with heading and speed. Visual detection about 80-100 km. — [Steam thread "A radar question"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1434950/discussions/0/4345408777045049710/) — REPORTED
- ELINT "will usually pick up radar signatures at around double the radar's range"; "Jammers will absurdly increase your detection by Elint"; two ranges matter: tracking range (enemy appears on map, missiles can lock) vs visual range (enemy shows only in radar module display); IR scanner "only really useful as a substitute detection system when your radar is turned off", very short range; anti-radiation missiles can be evaded by turning radar off if outside the firing ship's tracking range; regular missiles show a red scanning cone; a "blinking blue contact light" tracks enemy awareness. — [HighFleet basic guide, gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/11966-highfleet.html) — REPORTED
- Numeric ELINT ratio: maximum ELINT pickup distance is 2x radar range for the MP-404 and 1.4x for the MP-21; there is only one IRST module (IRS-1 Mars, 300 km), which "doesn't require the enemy to be using radar... and cannot be detected." — [Fandom wiki Sensors page (search snippet; page returned 402) and Steam threads](https://highfleet.fandom.com/wiki/Sensors) — REPORTED
- Player tactic: "If your ELINT is lit up, it's pretty safe to assume that the enemy's ELINT can see your radar too if it's on. Turn that sucker off and only use a sector ping if absolutely necessary"; missiles can home on the detection circle so a plane can spot a fleet and a strike group fires at the red blip before radar range. — [Steam search snippet, HighFleet discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1434950/discussions/0/3046108212595911971/) — REPORTED
- Radar controls: single sweep, continuous sweep, sector search toggle, ground sweep. — [HighFleet basic guide, gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/11966-highfleet.html) — REPORTED

### Inferences
- HighFleet exposes emission discipline as graduated actions (continuous, single sweep, sector ping, off), not a binary.

### Gaps
- No HighFleet manual or developer statement read. The Fandom wiki was blocked, so ELINT ratios are from snippets plus guides.
- IRST's "cannot be detected" is a player/wiki claim; no rule text.

## Starsector: sensor profile, sensor strength, Go Dark, transponder, Active Sensor Burst

### Takeaway
Starsector separates "how far you can see" (sensor strength) from "how far you can be seen" (sensor profile), sums them to get detection range, and gives the player three levers: transponder, Go Dark, and Active Sensor Burst. The burst improves your own sensing and spikes your visibility at the same time, and it deliberately pulls hostile fleets toward you.

### Cited Findings
- Detection range = your sensor strength + their sensor profile. Fleet profile = 300 + top 5 ship profiles; fleet strength = 300 + top 5 ship strengths. Both fleets detect each other at the same moment unless modifiers apply. Fleets in combat get +1000 profile. 2000 units = one sector map grid square. — [Starsector Wiki, Sensors](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Sensors) — VERIFIED (community wiki)
- Transponder on: +1000 profile, and full fleet information on detection. Transponder off: progressive reveal as range closes; a contact at long range is a triangle sized to fleet strength. — [Starsector Wiki, Sensors](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Sensors) — VERIFIED
- Go Dark: -50% sensor profile (-75% with Sensors skill level 2), at cost of speed; when used at sufficient distance, pursuers "lose track and go in the direction of your last detected position". — [Starsector Wiki, Sensors](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Sensors); [Starsector Wiki, Avoiding combat](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Avoiding_combat) — VERIFIED
- Active Sensor Burst: +3000 sensor strength and +5000 sensor profile; activation 0.2 days, duration 0.1 days, cooldown 1 day; fleet is limited to go-slow speed (half the burn level of the slowest ship) while active; no resource cost listed; gives exact information about contacts (separating, e.g., dormant remnant fleets from salvage structures). — [Starsector Wiki, Active Sensor Burst](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Active_Sensor_Burst) — VERIFIED
- Burst consequence: "Hostile fleets within range will then start going towards the location/direction of the sensor reading to investigate"; players use it to bait patrols away, then slip through while dark. Burst can block Interdiction Pulse if timed; can be used at jump points or during Transverse Jump to dodge the speed penalty. — [Starsector Wiki, Avoiding combat](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Avoiding_combat); [Starsector Wiki, Active Sensor Burst](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Active_Sensor_Burst) — VERIFIED
- Player-facing advice: "Avoid having the Transponder on outside of colonized system space." Low profile with high strength is "generally useful." — [Starsector Wiki, Avoiding combat](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Avoiding_combat) — VERIFIED
- Developer's design post (2015 blog "Sensors") as summarized by search: three information tiers by range (max range = "sensor contact" blip with fleet size only; about half range = basic composition but not ship status or faction; very close = everything); transponder is a fleet-level option that changes how much is learned at range. — [Fractal Softworks, Sensors (search-result summary; page returned 403)](https://fractalsoftworks.com/2015/04/13/sensors/) — REPORTED
- The wiki states sensor strength-to-profile ratio varies by ship size (30/60/90/150) — [Starsector Wiki, Sensors](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Sensors) — VERIFIED (meaning of the four numbers not explained in the summary; AMBIGUOUS on which size maps to which)

### Inferences
- Detection range in Starsector is additive and symmetrical by default (both fleets detect each other at the same range) which makes a visible spike (burst) cost the same range for everyone nearby, not only the target.
- Starsector separates detection from identification (blip, then composition, then full detail), the same split SupCom uses between radar blip and vision.

### Gaps
- Developer blog and forum design thread ("Sensors and Signatures") were not readable; the only developer-intent statements here are second-hand.
- Whether the burst's +5000 profile is observable by the AI as an "alert" beyond the investigate behavior is not stated.

## EVE Online (and Elite Dangerous): directional scanner, combat probes, silent running

### Takeaway
EVE splits scanning into a free, repeatable, directional "d-scan" (no distance, no notification; but probes show up on it) and a slow, multi-step probe process that the target can only counter by noticing the probes on d-scan. Elite makes heat the visibility signal and silent running a limited-time dark state with heat as the cost.

### Cited Findings
- D-scan: range up to 14.3 AU (1, 5, 10, 14.3 AU presets or custom), cone from 5 to 360 degrees, 2-second reset after each scan; it lists ships, structures, drones, wrecks, celestials and scanning probes by name but "does not show distance or coordinates"; cloaked and certain scan-immune ships do not appear; "Probes appearing without visible ships suggest a cloaked ship is out there somewhere." — [EVE University, Directional scanning](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Directional_scanning) — VERIFIED
- Combat probes: base scan range 0.5 AU (core probes 0.25 AU); resolve ships, structures, drones; cannot scan wrecks, cargo containers or cloaked ships; probes last about 1 h 6 m 40 s (standard); accuracy depends on signal strength, where stronger signal means less deviation. — [EVE University, Probe scanning](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Probe_scanning) — VERIFIED
- How the target knows: combat probes are visible on d-scan; "if you see combat probes on D-Scan, assume you're being probed down NOW"; if you cannot see probes you are not about to be scanned down; difficulty to scan down depends on signature radius and sensor strength; in wormholes the d-scan window to see probes can be about 15 seconds. — [EVE University search results / Cepheus D-Scan & Intel guide / EVE Forums](https://www.cepheuscorp.com/guides/dscan-intel) — REPORTED (page content not retrievable; claims from search summary of EVE University, forums and a corp guide)
- Ship/cargo scanner: target gets no notification; the only cue is a "very subtle" faint blue beam and sound visible when zoomed in; cargo scan time 3 s (T2 module) or 3.5 s (meta), down to 2.25 s with max Survey skill. Passive targeting means no "yellow box" either. — [EVE University, Cargo scanning](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Cargo_scanning); [EVE Search thread](https://eve-search.com/thread/891465) — VERIFIED (wiki) / REPORTED (forum)
- Elite Dangerous silent running: shields offline, cooling vents shut, signature drops to appear as 0% heat, internal heat builds with risk of module/hull damage; heat below about 15% makes a ship nearly undetectable on most ship radars; "as soon as you get within 499m of anything you become instantly visible, no matter how cold"; no amount of heat suppression hides you from visual observation. Larger ships are easier to lock from farther away. — [Elite Dangerous Fandom wiki, Silent Running (search-result summary; page returned 402)](https://elite-dangerous.fandom.com/wiki/Silent_Running) — REPORTED
- Elite player report: being scanned while silent is possible, "especially in big ships, especially if the scan started before you went silent running"; speed (outrunning scan completion) is recommended over relying on silent; chaff and retracting hardpoints are used to buy time at stations. — [Steam thread "Scanned while silent running"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/359320/discussions/0/1519260397790943180/) — REPORTED

### Inferences
- EVE's d-scan is free and non-announcing, so the cost of probing is borne by the prober, whose probes become visible objects in space; the information about "I am being looked at" arrives via a different, visible artifact, not a scan alert.

### Gaps
- EVE scan-cycle time for probes was not stated on the wiki page read; the d-scan 2 s reset is the only cycle figure found.
- Whether Elite notifies the target when scanned (kill warrant scan) was not established.

## Supreme Commander / Forged Alliance and Beyond All Reason: intel layers, stealth vs cloak, jamming, blips vs identified

### Takeaway
Both games treat intel as separate channels (vision, radar, sonar, omni) each with its own range, upkeep, and counter. Radar yields blips, vision yields identification, and stealth and cloak are separate defenses that stack, with omni or vision needed to defeat both.

### Cited Findings
- SupCom: intel types are vision/LOS, radar, sonar, omni. "Vision is the only Intel that detects a unit with both Cloak and Stealth," but it is short-ranged and costly. Stealth hides from radar but not LOS or omni; cloak hides from LOS but not radar, revealable by omni; a unit with both needs omni sensors. Radar blips are unidentified; "Vision is the only Intel type that will identify units (their type, their HP etc), rather than just their location." Radar jamming "reduces the effectiveness of radar by producing confusing fake signals, but does not eliminate its effect." T3 radar: range 600, omni range 200, energy drain 2000. — [SupCom Fandom wiki, Intel / Radar / Stealth / Cloaking (search-result summaries; pages returned 402)](https://supcom.fandom.com/wiki/Intel) — REPORTED
- BAR radar: "Shooting at units that are radar dots is inaccurate. Attacking units will miss most of the shots"; targeting facilities offset this (1 gives +50% accuracy, 2 gives 75%, 3 removes most of the penalty; benefits whole team). Radar is blocked by high terrain. — [BAR guide, Advanced mechanics](https://www.beyondallreason.info/guide/important-knowledge-on-advanced-mechanics) — VERIFIED
- BAR stealth vs cloak: stealth hides from enemy radar but unit stays visually visible; cloak makes the unit visually invisible but it keeps a radar signature ("Cloaked units still have a radar signature! They can be shot at via radar"); cloaked units cannot shoot, build, repair or reclaim without decloaking and cost significant energy, more while moving. Intrusion Countermeasure System shows cloaked units as blinking red circles with an audio alert but only when they move. Perimeter cameras give vision while invisible to radar. — [BAR guide, Advanced mechanics](https://www.beyondallreason.info/guide/important-knowledge-on-advanced-mechanics) — VERIFIED
- BAR underwater: "Submarines, and any unit or structure that stands on or traverses the ocean floor can only be detected via sonar"; floating radars include short-range sonar; jamming disrupts sonar; submerged units create visible subwakes slightly beyond normal LOS. — [BAR guide, Advanced mechanics](https://www.beyondallreason.info/guide/important-knowledge-on-advanced-mechanics) — VERIFIED
- BAR radar tower: radar 2100 units, sight 680, cost 60 metal + 630 energy. BAR Veil jammer: jammer range 760, upkeep 125 energy/s, sight 155, cost 125 metal + 19,000 energy; description "prevent your units and structures from being targeted by long-range weapons." Mobile jammer (Obscurer) "constantly consume[s] energy to maintain the jamming field." Juno's missiles target "micro-electronics" (radars, jammers, mines). A cloaked plus stealth or jammed unit is the only truly invisible state; the only counter is the Intrusion Countermeasure System. — [BAR, Radar Tower](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/armrad); [BAR, Veil](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/armveil); [BAR unit pages (search snippets)](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/coreter) — VERIFIED (unit pages) / REPORTED (snippets)

### Inferences
- In both games these are passive-coverage structures: radar and jammers are placed and fed energy, not triggered per use; the cost shows up as upkeep and exposure of the structure, not as a one-shot ping. BAR's Juno targets exactly those structures.
- The layers differ: land/air by radar plus vision; underwater by sonar only (BAR) with a separate radar-invisible state for submerged units.

### Gaps
- All SupCom pages were behind 402 errors; numbers (energy, ranges) come from search snippets, not read pages; FAF unit database not read.
- No SupCom/BAR commentary found on whether radar emissions are detectable (they are not modeled as emitters you can hear; I found no evidence of a passive emission-detection channel). Absence not confirmed.
- Air vs surface differences in SupCom omni/radar not sourced.

## Games where a scan or ping warns the target, and design commentary

### Takeaway
Warnings are implemented in at least four ways: a directional blip or bearing line (Cold Waters), an emission flag on the contact (Sea Power), a visible object in space (EVE probes on d-scan), and an AI behavior response (Starsector burst draws patrols; Cold Waters/Dangerous Waters AI fires on ping bearing). Scarcely any developer commentary on the choice was found; most commentary is player-side.

### Real-world basis (brief)
- EMCON: management of electromagnetic and acoustic emissions "to prevent an enemy from detecting, identifying, and locating friendly forces"; objectives are to deny the enemy any way to locate you and let your actions go unnoticed; graduated conditions (ALPHA complete silence through DELTA minimal limits). "For each emission a ship releases, it broadcasts its position at twice the range it can 'see'" (a glossary source's phrasing). — [Windward EMCON glossary / USNI Proceedings (search summaries; USNI pages returned 403)](https://windward.ai/glossary/what-is-emcon/) — REPORTED
- Why emissions outrange detection: radar is two-way; echo energy "drops with the fourth power of the distance", while an interceptor receiving the outgoing pulse sees it "drop off only as the square of distance," so the receiver "is always at an advantage over the radar in terms of range" (assuming no antenna disparity). LPI techniques (frequency agility, pulse compression, long low-power pulses, low sidelobes) narrow this gap. — [Wikipedia, Low-probability-of-intercept radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-probability-of-intercept_radar) — VERIFIED
- ESM: passive listening that detects, identifies, locates and records radiated emissions with bearing; "ESM collection platforms can remain electronically silent and detect and analyze RADAR transmissions beyond the RADAR detection range because of the greater power of the transmitted electromagnetic pulse with respect to a reflected echo." ELINT is one product of this collection. — [Wikipedia, Electronic warfare support measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_warfare_support_measures) — VERIFIED
- Active sonar: two-way vs passive one-way; counter-detection at twice the detecting range is stated in the Active sonar article (see Naval section). — [Wikipedia, Active sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_sonar) — VERIFIED

### Inferences
- ESTIMATED derivation of "twice or more": for a monostatic radar with echo power falling as 1/R^4 and a one-way intercept falling as 1/R^2, if the two receivers needed equal signal power S at the limiting range, then radar range R_d satisfies S = K_d/R_d^4 and intercept range R_i satisfies S = K_i/R_i^2, so R_i = (K_i/S)^(1/2), R_d = (K_d/S)^(1/4), and R_i/R_d = (K_i^2 / (K_d * S))^(1/4). The ratio is not fixed (K_i and K_d are the transmitter, antenna and target terms of each equation, S is the shared sensitivity floor); it depends on target RCS, receiver sensitivity and power, so "2x" is a rule of thumb. Game values observed: HighFleet ELINT 2x (MP-404) and 1.4x (MP-21) of radar range; Nebulous "farther than the radar can see" with no number; Command: MO ESM horizon factor 1.5 vs radar 1.23.
- Across games, "being looked at" feedback is inconsistent: some give the target a bearing or flag, some none, some a visible object. Only EVE gives the target a way to tell a scan is coming before it resolves (probes on d-scan), and it is a prober-side artifact.
