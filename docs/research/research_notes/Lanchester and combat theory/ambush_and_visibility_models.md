## 1. Deitchman (1962): equations, state equation, stated conclusions

### Takeaway
Deitchman's model pairs aimed (square-law) fire from the hidden ambusher with area (linear-law) fire from the exposed side, giving a "mixed" law with a quadratic term for the exposed force and a linear term for the hidden force. In the normalized form used by Kress and by Atkinson et al., the hidden side's advantage is a factor of 2 on per-capita kill rate, equivalently sqrt(2) on numbers; larger numerical overcomes (e.g. 10:1) require a very large per-capita effectiveness gap, which is a separate parameter, not a result of hiding alone.

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic: S. J. Deitchman (Institute for Defense Analyses), "A Lanchester Model of Guerrilla Warfare," Operations Research 10(6), Nov-Dec 1962, pp. 818-827. Abstract states that the paper applies a variation of the Lanchester equations to explore the force ratios required to win in guerrilla/counter-guerrilla warfare, and that "an attacking guerrilla force can, by using tactics that compensate for its weaknesses, defeat a force of defending regulars that has over-all superiority in numbers and weapons." — [IDEAS/RePEc abstract page](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/oropre/v10y1962i6p818-827.html); same sentence surfaced in [INFORMS page](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.10.6.818) and [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Lanchester-Model-of-Guerrilla-Warfare-Deitchman/81c563d6e3d89155cfc8c701f276335df46fc322) search snippets. VERIFIED (abstract sentence).
- The abstract also says the high overall ratios of defending regulars to attacking guerrillas "that have characterized recent warfare of this kind in which the defense has been successful are extremely difficult to reduce" (wording from search snippets of the abstract; the RePEc page summary agrees). I did not see the surrounding abstract text in full. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/oropre/v10y1962i6p818-827.html). REPORTED.
- Setup: guerrillas "well hidden in an ambush, or mixed in the civilian population, use aimed fire at the regular forces, who are fully exposed"; regulars are "shooting in the brown" and "can only apply area fire"; regular effectiveness "depends on the density of the guerrillas' live combatants. As the number of guerrillas decreases with attrition, it is harder to acquire a live target." — [Kress, "Lanchester Models for Irregular Warfare," Mathematics 8(5):737, 2020, Sec. 3](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf). VERIFIED.
- Legacy laws for reference (Kress 2020, Sec. 2): aimed fire B' = -rho*R, R' = -beta*B; state equation beta*(B0^2 - B^2) = rho*(R0^2 - R^2); parity (mutual annihilation) beta*B0^2 = rho*R0^2. Area fire B' = -rho*B*R, R' = -beta*B*R; state equation beta*(B0 - B) = rho*(R0 - R). — [Kress 2020](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf). VERIFIED.
- Deitchman mixed law (Kress 2020, Eq. 5-7): B' = -rho*R (B = regulars, hit by aimed fire); R' = -beta*B*(R/R0) (R = hidden guerrillas, hit by area fire). Dividing: dB/dR = rho*R0/(beta*B). State equation: (beta/2)*(B0^2 - B^2) = rho*R0*(R0 - R). Parity: rho*R0^2/(beta*B0^2) = 1/2. Quote: "ceteris paribus, Blue (the regular force) will need to double its per-capita effectiveness (kill rate) or increase its initial force size by sqrt(2) to achieve parity with Red (the guerrillas)." — [Kress 2020, Sec. 3](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf). VERIFIED. The same equations, parity, and "double its kill rate, or increase its numbers by sqrt(2)" appear in Atkinson, Kress, MacKay, "Targeting, Deployment and Loss-Tolerance in Lanchester Engagements," Operations Research (2020 preprint), Eqs. 8-10, there derived as the "absent Battle Damage Assessment" case — [preprint](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Atkinson_Kress_MacKay_2020.pdf). VERIFIED.
- Raw (un-normalized) form, ambusher group 1 (size m, aimed-fire coefficient alpha_m) vs ambushed group 2 (size n, area-fire coefficient alpha_n): dm/dt = -alpha_n*n*m; dn/dt = -alpha_m*m; state equation 2*alpha_m*(m0 - m) = alpha_n*(n0^2 - n^2); ambusher wins an all-out fight iff alpha_m/alpha_n > n0^2/(2*m0). Mesterton-Gibbons' worked example: "if each individual in a group of 10 has a thousandfold advantage in combative effectiveness (ability to hit an opponent) over each individual in a group of 100 by virtue of being hidden from them, then the smaller group would defeat the larger group despite being ten times smaller—although only ... half its members would survive." Arithmetic in source: m0 - (1/2)*n0^2*alpha_n/alpha_m = 10 - 0.5*100^2*10^-3 = 5. — [Mesterton-Gibbons, Lecture 2, "Lanchestrian Models of Human Combat," FSU MAP5932 (2016)](https://www.math.fsu.edu/~mesterto/NewCourses/MAP5932/2016/Notes/lecture02.pdf). VERIFIED. He restates Deitchman's conditions: aimed-fire law applies "when each side is visible to the other"; area-fire law applies "when each side is invisible to the other, and each fires into the area believed to be occupied by the other."
- Epstein (1985, p. 13), quoted by Mesterton-Gibbons: the "so-called ambush variant . . . may well be the most plausible of all Lanchester variants." Epstein's general criticisms of Lanchester models (no withdrawal, no trading space for time, no diminishing returns to concentration) are listed in the same lecture. — [FSU Lecture 2](https://www.math.fsu.edu/~mesterto/NewCourses/MAP5932/2016/Notes/lecture02.pdf). VERIFIED (as quotation of Epstein; I did not read Epstein).
- Schramm (2012) restates Deitchman as "a mixed model of combat to describe an 'ambush' case where a surprised defender uses area fire against an attacker using aimed fire," with equations dR/dt = -beta_B*B*R, dB/dt = -beta_R*R. — [Schramm, "Lanchester Models with Discontinuities: An Application to Networked Forces," Military Operations Research 17(4), 2012](https://hdl.handle.net/10945/37797). VERIFIED. (Note the role labels differ: Schramm's "attacker" is the aimed-fire side. Same math.)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATED, parity in plain terms. From Eq. 7, the hidden side (R) wins iff rho*R0^2/(beta*B0^2) > 1/2. With equal per-capita rates (rho = beta) that is R0/B0 > 1/sqrt(2) = 0.707, i.e. the hidden side can be outnumbered about 1.41 to 1 and still win. In general the exposed side needs B0/R0 > sqrt(2*rho/beta). For the hidden side to beat a 10:1 numerical disadvantage, rho/beta must exceed 100/2 = 50. For 3:1, rho/beta > 4.5.
- ESTIMATED, check of the Mesterton-Gibbons example against this: n0/m0 = 10, so alpha_m/alpha_n > n0^2/(2*m0) = 10000/20 = 500; 1000 clears the threshold; survivors = 10 - 10000/(2*1000) = 5. Matches source.
- AMBIGUOUS (presentation, not math): Kress and Atkinson et al. normalize the area-fire coefficient by R0 (using R/R0), so beta is a per-shooter kill rate at initial target density and the hiding penalty is a clean factor 1/2. Mesterton-Gibbons' raw alpha_n multiplies target count directly, so the "thousandfold" is a ratio of coefficients with different units/density dependence, not a ratio of per-shot hit probabilities. Both are algebraically consistent; they differ in what the coefficient means. Atkinson et al. flag the same subtlety: in the area-fire model "b and r now mean something different: they are attacking units' kill rates per unit time and per enemy unit" ([preprint, Sec. 2](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Atkinson_Kress_MacKay_2020.pdf)). Anyone quoting a force-ratio number from Deitchman needs to say which normalization.

### Gaps
- Deitchman's own numerical examples, his stated force ratios for Malaya/Philippines-type campaigns, and his weapon-selection / group-size conclusions were not read (paywall). The only direct text is the abstract. A user wanting "the force ratio the conventional side needs" as Deitchman states it must read the paper body.
- No source found for Deitchman's own derivation of the 1/2 parity constant; it appears only in Kress (2020), Atkinson et al. (2020) and the FSU notes.

## 2. Schaffer (1968) and other extensions of the guerrilla model

### Takeaway
Schaffer (RAND, 1968) extended Deitchman to small-force guerrilla engagements with supporting weapons, troop discipline/morale, and time-dependent weapon-efficiency coefficients; later work generalized the hidden side's visibility from binary to a continuous parameter (Kress-MacKay 2014) and then to an arbitrary acquisition function (Kaplan-Kress-Szechtman 2010).

### Cited Findings
- Marvin B. Schaffer (RAND), "Lanchester Models of Guerrilla Engagements," Operations Research 16(3), June 1968, pp. 457-488. Abstract (as summarized): Lanchester-type equations for small-force guerrilla engagements typical of early insurgency, including the effects of supporting weapons and the discipline or morale of troops; the mathematical novelty is "time-dependent weapon-efficiency coefficients." The page summary also says the models are meant to evaluate the credibility of casualty claims and identify critical tactical parameters, that the theory suggests justification for particular hardware "particularly regarding ambush scenarios," and that the author concedes the models cannot predict insurgency outcomes. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/oropre/v16y1968i3p457-488.html); [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.16.3.457). REPORTED for the extra details (they come from a tool-generated summary of the abstract page; the time-dependent-coefficients and supporting-weapons/morale lines were also in an independent search snippet).
- Kress (2020): "Deitchman model was extended by Schaffer [18] who used the model for analyzing new combat hardware." — [Kress 2020, Sec. 3](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf). VERIFIED.
- Generalized Deitchman model (Kress and MacKay, "Bits or shots in combat? The generalized Deitchman model of guerrilla warfare," Operations Research Letters 42(1):102-108, 2014, DOI 10.1016/j.orl.2013.08.004): abstract says it "generalizes Deitchman's guerrilla warfare model to account for trade-off between intelligence ('bits') and firepower ('shots'. Intelligent targeting leads to aimed fire; absence of intelligence leads to unaimed fire, dependent on targets' density." They derive the conserved quantity and use it to analyze the intelligence-vs-firepower trade-off. — [York Research Database](https://pure.york.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/bits-or-shots-in-combat-the-generalized-deitchman-model-of-guerri/). VERIFIED (abstract).
- Two-sided target-information model (Kress and MacKay): mu in [0,1] = Blue's information on Red's targets, upsilon in [0,1] = Red's information on Blue. B' = -rho*R*(upsilon*B0 + (1-upsilon)*B)/B0; R' = -beta*B*(mu*R0 + (1-mu)*R)/R0. mu = upsilon = 0 gives Lanchester area fire; mu = upsilon = 1 gives aimed fire; mu = 0, upsilon = 1 gives Deitchman. Generalized Deitchman model is upsilon = 1, 0 <= mu <= 1. — [Kress 2020, Sec. 4, Eq. 11](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf). VERIFIED. (Per Kress, cited as reference [21]; I did not read the 2014 paper body.)
- Generalized Deitchman parity: rho*R0^2/(beta*B0^2) = ((1-mu)/2) * (1 + mu*ln(mu)/(1-mu))^(-1). Equals 1/2 at mu = 0 and 1 at mu = 1. Kress's quoted values: mu = 0.5 -> "slightly higher than 4/5," Blue needs to raise kill rate by "less than 25%"; mu = 0.1 -> "more than 65%"; mu = 0.8 -> "less than 8%." Quote: "In general, information is less 'valuable' than kill-rate; a small increase in kill-rate is more valuable than an equivalent proportional increase in target information." — [Kress 2020, Sec. 4, Eq. 13](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf); the same Eq. (13) with delta in place of mu appears in [Atkinson-Kress-MacKay 2020, Eq. 13](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Atkinson_Kress_MacKay_2020.pdf) together with a small-loss expansion rho*R0^2/(beta*B0^2) = 1 - (1-delta)/3 + ... near delta = 1. VERIFIED.
- Optimization over bits vs shots: maximize beta*B0^2 * ((1-mu)/2)*(1 + mu ln(mu)/(1-mu))^(-1) subject to c1*mu + c2*beta <= C. — [Kress 2020, Eq. 14](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf). VERIFIED.
- Kaplan, Kress, Szechtman ("Confronting Entrenched Insurgents," Operations Research, DOI 10.1287/opre.1090.0728) replace the linear acquisition factor mu + (1-mu)*R/R0 with a monotone non-decreasing p(R), "the per-shot probability of successfully acquiring and engaging a Red target." Parity: beta*B0^2 = 2*rho * integral from 0 to R0 of x/p(x) dx. Blue wins iff B0 > sqrt((2*rho/beta) * integral_0^R0 x/p(x) dx); survivors sqrt(B0^2 - B_hat^2). — [Kress 2020, Eqs. 15-16, citing ref. 22](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf); paper listed at [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1287/opre.1090.0728). VERIFIED (as stated by Kress); original paper body not read.
- Atkinson, Kress, MacKay (2020) add two more constraints to Lanchester engagements: (a) imperfect acquisition (decoys/cover, "firing into the brown"), modeled by replacing R0 in the denominator with R+ > R0 and sigma = R0/R+ so parity becomes rho*R0^2/(beta*B0^2) = sigma/2; (b) constrained deployment (only B_max can engage). Abstract conclusion: "more units and better weapons (higher attrition rate) are preferred over improved targeting capability and relaxed deployment restrictions unless these are poor." Key framing: poor targeting "would classically be thought of as leading to the Linear Law ... but, in fact, ... leads to a Square Law with a penalty factor." — [preprint, abstract and Sec. 1, 3.1](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Atkinson_Kress_MacKay_2020.pdf). VERIFIED. Their illustration of a deployment constraint: Battle of Ein-A-Tinna (1982), unconstrained ratio 6 Syrian vs 30 Israeli tanks became an effective 6 vs 1 because terrain forced a single column (citing Gabriel 1984, not read) — same source. REPORTED (secondary citation of Gabriel).
- Kress 2020 also lists the narrow-passage model (Red concentrates fire; Blue only fires from front weapon): B' = -rho*R, R' = -beta; parity rho*R0^2/(beta*B0) = 2, so the attacker "will need an initial force size in the order of the square of the Red initial force." — [Kress 2020, Eq. 8-9](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf). VERIFIED.
- Ahern, Zuparic, Hoek, Kalloniatis (2023), "Unifying warfighting functions in mathematical modelling: combat, manoeuvre, and C2," arXiv 2301.09949: couples a Lanchester model with Kuramoto-Sakaguchi oscillators for C2 coupling; claims that with appropriate coupling, manoeuvrability and "even weaker lethality" a force can overcome a stronger adversary. It cites Deitchman 1962 and Schaffer 1968 as the guerrilla lineage and notes that command/manoeuvre "are buried in the combat effectiveness constant" of standard models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.09949). VERIFIED (abstract/intro); a recent preprint, not peer-reviewed as far as I could tell.

## 3. Aimed fire vs area fire; how target acquisition time enters attrition coefficients (Bonder-Farrell, Taylor)

### Takeaway
Aimed fire (square law) = every shooter always has a target and kill rate is independent of target count; area fire (linear law) = kill rate scales with target density because acquisition is the bottleneck. Acquisition-time models of the attrition coefficient (Bonder-Farrell; Taylor's corrections) make the coefficient a ratio of expected kills per engagement cycle to expected cycle length, so the "aimed" and "area" regimes appear as the saturated and acquisition-limited ends of one formula.

### Cited Findings
- Lanchester's aimed-fire model describes situations where "all units on both sides are in action, aim their fire, know when they have incapacitated their targets, and can quickly acquire new ones." Unaimed fire: losses proportional to both attackers and defenders because of "density-dependence in the effect of indirect artillery fire, or because of the effects on direct fire of poor TC [targeting capability], causing fire to be wasted on decoy or inactive targets." — [Atkinson-Kress-MacKay 2020, Sec. 2](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Atkinson_Kress_MacKay_2020.pdf). VERIFIED.
- Kress 2020, Sec. 4: "Lanchester's aimed fire model assumes perfect visibility of targets on both sides, while the area fire model assumes none—both sides shoot 'in the brown', such that their effectiveness depends on the density of live targets in the area." Also: "Irregular warfare is also characterized by asymmetry in the mechanism that provides information, intelligence and situational awareness to the two sides." — [Kress 2020](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf). VERIFIED.
- Mesterton-Gibbons/Deitchman framing: aimed model applies when "each side is visible to the other, and each individual on each side is able to fire on any opposing individual"; area-fire when "each side is invisible to the other, and each fires into the area believed to be occupied by the other." — [FSU Lecture 2](https://www.math.fsu.edu/~mesterto/NewCourses/MAP5932/2016/Notes/lecture02.pdf). VERIFIED (as his restatement of Deitchman 1962).
- Bonder-Farrell approach: pioneered by Bonder and Farrell (1970; see also Bonder 1967, 1970; Barfoot 1969), it computes a Lanchester attrition-rate coefficient "from an analytical model, independent of any high-resolution model": consider a single typical firer, compute the rate at which it kills enemy targets from a micro-combat model. The alternative is the hierarchy-of-models approach (fit coefficients to a high-resolution Monte Carlo simulation). — [Taylor and Neta, "Explicit Analytical Expression for a Lanchester Attrition-Rate Coefficient for Bonder and Farrell's m-Period Target-Engagement Policy," NPS/DTRA Working Paper #5, 9 July 2001, Sec. 2](https://faculty.nps.edu/bneta/papers/LanchesterAttrition-RateCoefficientTaylor&Neta.pdf). VERIFIED.
- Taylor's principle: a Lanchester attrition-rate coefficient is "the ratio of the expected number of kills for a given firer type against a specific target type per target-engagement cycle divided by the expected length of this target-engagement cycle." The general serial-acquisition expression depends on two quantities: (1) the probability the next target type engaged is type i, and (2) the expected time to acquire a target that will be engaged. Serial acquisition = no new target can be acquired while one is being attacked. — Taylor and Neta 2001, Sec. 2-3 (same URL). VERIFIED.
- Acquisition assumptions in that framework: targets identical and independent; acquisition time per target exponential with rate lambda; with n_i targets of type i in view, the rate of acquiring the next one is n_i*lambda; line of sight (LOS) intermittent via a two-state Markov chain, steady-state P_LOS = eta/(eta + mu) (eta = rate LOS appears, mu = rate LOS lost), net acquisition rate P_LOS*lambda*x_i for x_i targets. Lanchester models use a real-valued target count as "an approximation to the average number of combatants." — Taylor and Neta 2001, Sec. 6 (same URL). VERIFIED.
- Bonder-Farrell m-period target-engagement policy: in period k (from t_{k-1} to t_k) any target among the top k priorities is engaged immediately upon acquisition; lower-priority targets are held under surveillance while LOS lasts; at the period's end, the next-priority target still visible is engaged. It is an open-loop policy. Taylor and Neta cite Taylor's [2000a] claim of "a number of serious flaws in Bonder-Farrell methodology," including inconsistent treatment of parallel acquisition. — Taylor and Neta 2001, Sec. 4 and footnote 5 (same URL). VERIFIED.
- Taylor and Neta state that essentially all aggregated-force models (ITEM, CEM, VIC, JWARS, AWARS) base attrition on some Lanchester-type model, so these coefficients carry the weight of acquisition modeling in campaign models. — Taylor and Neta 2001, Sec. 2 (same URL). VERIFIED.
- Lanchester attrition-rate coefficients "depend on variables such as firing doctrine, firing rate, rate of target acquisition, and force separation" (search-engine paraphrase of the same literature; not a direct quote I read). — [search result summary of NPS/DTIC literature](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA363016.pdf). REPORTED.
- RTS-domain statement: Stanescu, Barriga, Buro, "Using Lanchester Attrition Laws for Combat Prediction in StarCraft," AIIDE-15 (AAAI, 2015): the attrition-order exponent "represents the advantage of a higher rate of target acquisition," and the authors say what matters for the linear vs square law "has little to do with range – what is really important is the rate of acquiring new targets"; they fit an attrition order of about 1.56 for StarCraft battles. I read this from a local PDF copy; the URL was not recorded this session. VERIFIED (text read), URL missing.

### Inferences
- ESTIMATED, single-target-type reduction of the Taylor/Bonder-Farrell form (my algebra, not a quoted result). Let alpha = rate an engaging firer kills an acquired target, mu = rate LOS to it is lost, L = P_LOS*lambda*x = rate of acquiring the next target when x targets are present. Probability the engaged target is killed before LOS is lost = alpha/(alpha + mu); expected engagement length = 1/(alpha + mu); expected acquisition time = 1/L. Then kill rate a = [alpha/(alpha + mu)] / [1/L + 1/(alpha + mu)] = alpha*L/(alpha + mu + L). For large x (L >> alpha + mu), a -> alpha: constant per-shooter rate, aimed-fire square law. For small x, a ~ alpha*L/(alpha + mu), proportional to x: area-fire linear law. This is consistent with the Taylor-Neta description but I could not cleanly parse their printed Eq. 2 (the PDF text extraction was garbled), so treat the exact heterogeneous-target form as unread.
- ESTIMATED (my reading across the sources above): The Deitchman factor R/R0 (or mu + (1-mu)R/R0, or p(R)) can be read as a reduced-form version of this acquisition-limited regime: the hidden side's target density falls as it dies, so the exposed side's per-shooter rate falls with it.

## 4. Models that put information/sensing into combat: Kress-Szechtman, stochastic duels, first-shot value

### Takeaway
Information enters Lanchester models as a targeting-effectiveness multiplier (mu, p(R), delta, sigma), as an endogenous variable tied to insurgent size and collateral damage (Kress-Szechtman), or, in the stochastic-duel tradition, as search/detection time before firing begins. Duel results make the value of shooting or detecting first explicit but depend on the specific exponential-time assumptions.

### Cited Findings
- Kress and Szechtman, "Why Defeating Insurgencies Is Hard: The Effect of Intelligence in Counterinsurgency Operations: A Best-Case Scenario," Operations Research, 2009, DOI 10.1287/opre.1090.0700. INFORMS release (16 July 2009) calls it "the first unified model combining military intelligence, attrition, and civilian population behavior." Quotes: "Absent intelligence, insurgents escape unharmed and continue violent attacks; poor government targeting causes innocent civilian deaths, which increases popular support for the insurgents." and "If a government does keep its intelligence gathering capabilities high, it can keep a hold on the insurgency, and after a while, when the insurgents realize they can't win, a political compromise may be reached." — [INFORMS news release](https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/Mathematical-Model-Shows-Why-Defeating-Insurgent-Groups-Like-Taliban-is-So-Difficult); journal page [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.1090.0700) (403, abstract via search snippet: even in the best case "the government cannot totally eradicate the insurgency by force; the best it can do is contain it at a certain fixed level"). VERIFIED (press release), REPORTED (abstract snippet).
- Model (as presented by Kress 2020, Eq. 24-25): P = civilian population (large, constant); mu = target information; insurgent signature R/P; B' = -rho*R + alpha (alpha = government reinforcement rate); R' = -beta*B*(mu + (1-mu)*(R/P)) + theta(C), with collateral casualty rate C = beta*B*(1-mu)*(1-R/P) (fraction of fire hitting civilians is (1-mu)(1-R/P)) and recruitment theta(C) increasing in C. For linear recruitment theta(C) = theta*C: if mu <= theta/(1+theta), "the insurgency cannot be eradicated, regardless of the initial force sizes and the attrition rates." If alpha/rho < P*(1 - 1/((1-mu)*(1+theta))), the state loses; otherwise the state can only contain the insurgency at the constant level P*(1 - 1/((1-mu)*(1+theta))). If mu > theta/(1+theta), the insurgency wins when R0 exceeds a threshold depending on all parameters, otherwise the state wins. — [Kress 2020, Sec. 5](https://faculty.nps.edu/mkress/docs/Lanchester_Models.pdf). VERIFIED (equations as presented in the review). Caveat: the PDF text extraction of the review's R' equation was partly garbled (recruitment term placement); I read theta(C) as recruitment adding to R, consistent with the review's prose, but the exact printed form was not confirmed.

- Duels with searching: Li and Liu (2012), "The Many-on-One Stochastic Duel Model with Information-Sharing," Applied Mathematics 3:637-640, DOI 10.4236/am.2012.36097, review the literature (Ancker; Gafarian and Ancker two-on-one; Friedman, Kikuta, Kress many-on-one; Wand et al. and Liu one-on-one with searching) and model hidden defenders vs a hidden attacker: defenders share detection, "start their firing process immediately after the attacker being detected," assume the attacker "will be killed before he can return fire," so "the attacker is a passive target after being detected." Time to kill T = detection time + sum of firing times; with exponential distributions the density is derived. — [Li & Liu 2012](https://content.scirp.org/pdf/am20120600021_62499088.pdf). VERIFIED.

Li, "Investigating the 2-player stochastic duel," 17 Aug 2025, Eq. (5) with n -> infinity; this is an unreviewed student paper and is used only for the formula form. [PDF](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/HSpapers/duel-justin.pdf). REPORTED.

### Inferences
- ESTIMATED, closed forms and numbers (my algebra):
  - Alternating duel, A first, infinite ammo: P(A wins) = p/(1 - (1-p)(1-q)) = p/(p + q - p*q). For p = q: 1/(2 - p). p = 0.1 -> 0.526; p = 0.5 -> 0.667; p = 0.9 -> 0.909. So with equal marksmanship the first shot is worth a few percentage points when hit chance is low and up to a two-thirds-or-more win chance when hit chance is high.
  - Continuous-time duel with exponential kill times at rates alpha (A) and beta (B), memoryless: with no head start P(A wins) = alpha/(alpha + beta); the first shot has no inherent value. If A gets an undetected head start tau during which B cannot fire (B has not detected A), P(A wins) = 1 - exp(-alpha*tau)*beta/(alpha + beta). With alpha = beta: alpha*tau = 0.5 -> 0.697; alpha*tau = 1 -> 0.816; alpha*tau = 2 -> 0.932 (1 - 0.1353*0.5 = 0.932). So under these assumptions the value of sensing first is entirely the length of the one-sided window relative to the mean time-to-kill.
  - After the window closes (B detects A), the duel reverts to the symmetric race: the advantage is a one-time offset in survival probability, not a persistent multiplier. By contrast the Deitchman mixed-law advantage persists as long as the hiding persists, because it is encoded in the dynamics.
- ESTIMATED (my reading across the sources above): These duel models treat detection as a random time; Deitchman-type models treat it as a standing probability of acquiring a target per shot (p(R)). The two are the same idea in different forms; the duel version is a first-passage problem, the mixed-law version is a flow.

## 5. Empirical and doctrinal values for surprise and ambush; decay once revealed

### Takeaway
Dupuy's Quantified Judgement Model assigns surprise combat-power factors of about 1.10 (minor) to about 2.2-2.5 (complete), applied to vulnerability and mobility, lasting three days and declining by one-third each day. The Dupuy Institute itself says Dupuy set these values by judgement and never validated them himself; sources also disagree on the headline value (2.24 vs 2.20 vs 2.5), likely because they describe different models or different quantities.

### Cited Findings
- Dupuy categorized tactical surprise as complete, substantial, and minor, "defining the level achieved was a matter of analyst judgement." QJM (Numbers, Predictions and War, 1977): "factors for surprise were applied to calculations for vulnerability and mobility, which in turn were applied to the calculation of overall combat power," net value "from a factor of about 2.24 for complete surprise to 1.10 for minor surprise." Simplified version in Attrition: Forecasting Battle Casualties and Equipment Losses in Modern War (1990): combat-power multiplier "between 2.20 for complete surprise and 1.10 for minor surprise." Duration: "The combat effects of surprise in battle would last for three days, declining by one-third each day." Validation: "Dupuy established these values for surprise based on his judgement ... He never validated them back to his data himself"; Chris Lawrence tested them on expanded databases (reported for his book War by Numbers). — [Shawn Woodford, "The Combat Value of Surprise," Dupuy Institute, 29 June 2018 (orig. 1 Dec 2016)](https://dupuyinstitute.org/2018/06/29/the-combat-value-of-surprise/). VERIFIED (blog quoting Dupuy's work; Dupuy's books not read).
- TNDM/QJM attrition comparison: "The multiplier for complete surprise is 2.5 (although this degraded by historical period)," listed among TNDM casualty multipliers (force size up to 1.5, surprise, opposition force ratios, terrain, weather, season, rate of advance, crossings, day/night, fatigue). — [Christopher A. Lawrence, "How Attrition is Calculated in the QJM vs the TNDM," Dupuy Institute, 26 Dec 2019](https://dupuyinstitute.org/2019/12/26/how-attrition-is-calculated-in-the-qjm-vs-the-tndm/). VERIFIED (exact sentence).
- Dupuy's "Verities" on surprise: surprise "the greatest of all combat multipliers" (Understanding War, 1987); mechanism is that it increases the opponent's mobility value, reduces vulnerability for the surpriser and raises it for the surprised. A 2004 TDI study for OSD on situational awareness found informational advantage improved combat effectiveness but surprise conferred greater benefits, and the "primary value of knowledge advantages lay in preventing enemy surprise rather than achieving it" (blog paraphrase). Jim Storr (2009) is cited as saying achieving tactical surprise produced effects "greater than a 10:1 force ratio." — [Woodford, "Dupuy's Verities: Surprise," Dupuy Institute, 8 Apr 2019](https://dupuyinstitute.org/2019/04/08/dupuys-verities-surprise/). REPORTED (blog summary; Storr and the TDI 2004 study not read).
- Dupuy's QJM vulnerability/posture defaults used in a Marine Corps implementation: default offensive vulnerability 0.9, default defensive 2.8; posture offense 1.0, hasty defense 1.3, prepared defense 1.5, fortified 1.6 (from Understanding War, 1987); surprise is one of the operational variable factors multiplied into combat power. — [Desmond, "Implementation of the Quantified Judgment Model to Examine the Impact of Human Factors on Marine Corps Distributed Operations," NPS thesis, Sept 2007](https://faculty.nps.edu/nlmiller/docs/Matthew%20Desmond.pdf). VERIFIED (Table 3 and operational-factors list; surprise values were not given in the text I extracted).
- Guerrilla casualty claims: Schaffer's abstract says the models are meant to help assess the credibility of casualty claims in insurgent conflicts (see Sec. 2). REPORTED.

### Inferences
- AMBIGUOUS, headline surprise value: 2.24 (QJM 1977, net factor on combat power after vulnerability and mobility effects), 2.20 (1990 simplified, direct multiplier on combat power), 2.5 (TNDM, stated in a casualty-attrition context and "degraded by historical period"). These likely describe different models and different quantities (combat-power multiplier vs casualty multiplier) rather than a true disagreement on one number, but no source states that explicitly; a report writer should cite the specific model and quantity.
- ESTIMATED, reading of the decay statement. "Lasts three days, declining by one-third each day" is ambiguous. One reading: the surprise effect is at full strength on day 1, two-thirds on day 2, one-third on day 3 (zero after). Another: multiplies by 2/3 each day. I could not find Dupuy's own wording. Under the first reading a complete-surprise factor of 2.2 would be applied as an excess of 1.2 that decays to 1.2, 0.8, 0.4 above 1.0, giving combat-power factors of 2.2, 1.8, 1.4 on days 1-3 (my arithmetic, assumes linear decay of the excess).
- ESTIMATED (my reading across the sources above): Theory vs doctrine on decay once revealed: the continuous-visibility Lanchester models (Sec. 2) give a static function of mu (0.5 parity factor falling toward 1.0 as visibility rises); Dupuy gives a temporal profile (3 days); the duel model (Sec. 4) gives a one-time head-start offset that disappears once the window closes. These are three different mechanisms and I found no single source that integrates a time-varying revelation of the hidden side into a Lanchester system beyond Schaffer's time-dependent coefficients (abstract only).
