## 1. Academic work on automated balancing and simulation-based playtesting: which figures, what each shows

### Takeaway
The balance papers mostly present results as (a) a table of restricted-play win percentages with a plain-words interpretation per row, (b) win-rate heat maps over a grid of agent skill levels, (c) learning curves, Pareto-front scatters or archive heat maps that report on the optimizer rather than on the game, and (d) matchup or payoff matrices. Only Jaffe's work and Zook, Harrison and Riedl (2019) present charts aimed at a designer reading game behaviour. AlphaStar's figures are aimed at an ML audience (Elo bars, win-rate matrices, a Nash-weight timeline).

### Cited Findings

**Jaffe et al., "Evaluating Competitive Game Balance with Restricted Play" (AIIDE 2012)**
- Core measure: the win probability of a *restricted* agent (e.g. "never plays Red 1/1", "plays only greedily", "only looks k steps ahead") against an unrestricted agent that knows the restriction and exploits it; a tie counts as 50%. — [Jaffe et al. 2012 PDF](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/jaffe2012ecg.pdf) — VERIFIED
- Fig. 1 (tool output). Caption: "A basic analysis using our tool. The results show the performance of varieties of restricted play against a restriction-exploiting player. Players who never play Red 1/1, for example, win 11.54% of the time." Body text: the web tool takes game parameters on its left side and shows, on the right, the balance measures "ranked by their intensity, so that the restricted behaviors that are most effective or ineffective are highlighted." The chart type is not named; axis labels extracted from the thesis copy of the figure ("Omit R1/1", "Prefer B1/6", "Prefer G1/2", ...) suggest one labelled mark per restriction. — [Jaffe 2012](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/jaffe2012ecg.pdf); [Jaffe thesis, Fig. 5.1](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/bitstreams/9817c1be-4389-4a4d-8f6f-8a5077d78330/download) — caption/text VERIFIED; "one bar per restriction, sorted" ESTIMATED
- Table 1 is the designer-facing summary: nine design iterations (A to I) of the card game's power cards. Columns: the power cards tried; "Noteworthy Balance Measures" (for example "Omit Green: 13.54%, Omit Blue: 7.44%, Omit Red: 7.83%"); and "Interpretation" in words ("Green is too weak. (Green cannot beat Red, even by playing +1/2.)", "Random play is unreasonably effective", "All values appear reasonable... Ready for playtesting."). — [Jaffe 2012](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/jaffe2012ecg.pdf) — VERIFIED
- Reading rule the authors state: if removing an action barely lowers win rate, that action is irrelevant; if "omit" performs near-perfectly while "prefer" performs terribly, the action is a decoy or self-destructive. The authors cite Greedy and Random win rates against the exploiter as the measure of how much long-term strategy matters. — [Jaffe 2012](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/jaffe2012ecg.pdf) — VERIFIED
- Fig. 3 "Balance parameters across variations": a graph across the design variations A to I. Caption: "The gaps between Greedy and Random convey the relative roles of long-term strategy and short-term tactics. The designer can easily discover trends such as the power gap between G1/2 and R1/1 due to a 1/2 power card." The authors say tracking this graph "across iterations" gave "a novel form of intuition for the dependencies of the game on parameter choices." Axes are not stated; my reading is x = design variation, y = win rate of each restricted player, one line per restriction. — [Jaffe 2012](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/jaffe2012ecg.pdf) — text VERIFIED; axes ESTIMATED
- Stated reason for using optimal (not heuristic) agents: results "are not affected by the secondary issue of how well particular AI agents play." — [Jaffe 2012](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/jaffe2012ecg.pdf) — VERIFIED
- Authors' own use: roughly twenty iterations; they say the tool saved "up to 20 phases of manual evaluation." — same source — VERIFIED

### Inferences
- The designer-facing charts in this set are few and share traits: a small number of marks, a plain-words reading rule in the caption or table, and a labelled threshold (50% or 45 to 55%). The optimizer-facing charts (Pareto fronts, QD curves, accuracy curves) are not usable as models for a designer report. (ESTIMATED)
- Jaffe's Fig. 7.1 versus Fig. 7.2 is the clearest documented case of a simple chart misleading designers; the remedy was to add one more axis (skill) and draw lines, not bars. (ESTIMATED reading of a VERIFIED statement)
- The 3 x 3 skill-vs-skill diverging heat map is the form closest to "which side beats which at each skill level"; the Jaffe thesis band (Figs. 6.9 to 6.11) is the closest to "advantage as a region in a two-variable plane."

## 2. Matchup tables and counter charts

### Takeaway
Matchup grids come in three families: numeric win-chance cells (fighting games, "x out of 10"), diverging red/white/blue colour scales centred on the neutral value (Graells and Astroza 2014; Liu and Marschner 2017; Sorochan 2022; AlphaStar Ext. Data Fig. 8), and letter-grade cells (S to E) that encode how much health the winner has left (Mechabellum community matrix). Rows are usually sorted by strength so a transitive backbone shows as a colour gradient.

### Cited Findings
- Street Fighter tier-list matrix (Game Developer, Graells and Astroza, December 2014; data: Shoryuken community tier list for Super Street Fighter IV Arcade Edition 2012). Rows and columns = characters; cell = integer 0 to 10 = wins out of 10 matches between equally skilled players (6 means the row character wins 6); colour = divergent: white for 5, blue for negative matchups, red for positive; row and column order = top-tier characters first, which makes red dominate one corner ("you can see how red dominates the upper-right part of the matrix"). The same article adds violin plots of tier points (sum of a character's matchup values) per cluster, a clustering dendrogram on matchup profiles (four clusters), and a dominating-set graph of characters that beat everyone. The authors say the tier list was "built from the experience of skilled players who have gathered to discuss and come to an agreement." — [Game Developer article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/from-game-balance-and-meta-game-to-matchup-prediction-understanding-the-street-fighter-tier-list) — REPORTED (read through a page summarizer; quoted phrases came from it)
- Eventhubs SF4 matrix: cell = chance in 10 the row character beats the column character; characters ordered by total table score; 39 characters (78 counting ultra variants). — [Jaffe thesis, ch. 8](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/bitstreams/9817c1be-4389-4a4d-8f6f-8a5077d78330/download); [Önal and Bülbül, Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.3897/jucs.121185) — VERIFIED
- Super Street Fighter 2 Turbo chart before and after balancing (win rate for the row player; red favours row, blue favours column). — [Liu and Marschner Fig. 4](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12924/12772) — VERIFIED
- Rock-paper-scissors payoff matrices: Önal and Bülbül derive, from a table of "power of row unit against column unit", the payoff matrix by taking power differences; colours red/blue mark who is superior. — [Önal and Bülbül](https://doi.org/10.3897/jucs.121185) — VERIFIED
- AlphaStar payoff matrix: blue = row agent wins, red = loses, white = draws; sorted by agent type and creation time (see Question 1). — [AlphaStar PDF](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf) — VERIFIED
- Mechabellum community unit matrix (Steam guide "Mechabellum Unit Matrix"): grid of units; each cell holds a letter S to E for the row unit against the column unit in one-on-one combat. S = "the best and basically the hard counter"; A = "wins with most of their HP left"; B = "still wins, but is damaged (about 50% HP left)"; C = "barely survives the fight"; D = "will lose the fight but still damage the opponent"; E = essentially unusable. Units are "matched with similar costs" (a 400-cost unit faces several cheaper units of equal total cost). Colours: red = poor, green = favourable, purple = hard counter. Footnote asterisks mark matchups that depend on placement, tech, or synergy. — [Steam guide, matrix](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?l=german&id=2981751067) — REPORTED (summarizer reading)
- A companion Mechabellum "counter" tool maps the same letters to numbers (S=5 to E=0) and lets a user pick enemy units and see ranked counters; its author says units are measured "without any tech and without positioning," so results "may not be the best choice within a real game." — [Steam guide, counters](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3345544924) — REPORTED
- A Mechabellum wiki says "There is no reliable one-line counter chart" because "technology, unit levels, formation, and support change the result." — [Mechabellum wiki](https://mechabellumwiki.wiki/counters/mechabellum-counter-list) — REPORTED (search-result snippet only)

## 3. "How much is X worth" presentations

### Takeaway
The sources reached give text and formulas more than charts: cost curves and "vanilla curves" (Gutschera), the Lanchester square law (Adams; Win-Vector; Wikipedia), and resource-exchange ratios (the EA paper). I found no well-sourced chart of break-even army size or DPS-versus-effective-health scatter; search snippets mention time-to-kill formulas only.

### Cited Findings
- Lanchester square law, in words: "if blue has five units and red has three, the number of units left at the end will be four" (sqrt(25 - 9)). Adams says the laws "should be particularly useful in problems like setting the cost and production rate of different unit types"; doubling production at equal efficiency gives a fourfold strength advantage, so units should cost proportionally more, adjusted by playtesting. Article caveats: no terrain, morale, surprise. — [Adams, Game Developer, 2004](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-designer-s-notebook-kicking-butt-by-the-numbers-lanchester-s-laws) — REPORTED (summarizer)
- Win-Vector: "10 armies facing 8 is not just a net-2 advantage, it can be thought of as a net-6 advantage"; survivors follow sqrt(x0^2 - y0^2). The page uses equations and solution curves of army size against time rather than charts, per the summary. — [Win-Vector, 2010](https://win-vector.com/2010/09/17/lanchesters-law-why-small-advantages-swell-in-starcraft/) — REPORTED
- Wikipedia's Lanchester article carries one idealized simulation image of two forces damaging each other; the caption says it illustrates the square law, with no axis labels given in text. Real combat exponent of about 1.5 is mentioned. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanchester%27s_laws) — REPORTED
- Stanescu et al. use Lanchester-based attrition as a combat predictor (accuracy tables, no army-curve plots). — [Stanescu 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12780/12628) — VERIFIED
- Gutschera: costing means giving each object a price so a fixed budget buys equal power; "vanilla curve" = cost vs power of objects with only basic stats; "how much of one stat is worth how much of another"; once the vanilla curve is right, ability costs add on top. Written as prose, no chart. — [Gutschera](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc07/slides/S3709i2.pdf) — VERIFIED
- Ian Schreiber's "Level 3: Transitive Mechanics and Cost Curves" is prose with arithmetic ("Add up the costs... Add up the benefits... get those two numbers to be equal") and no charts. — [Game Balance Concepts](https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/07/21/level-3-transitive-mechanics-and-cost-curves/) — REPORTED
- Ryan (Game Developer, 2009): traditional method is stat tables in spreadsheets "calculated and graphed to show approximate lethality, lifespan, damage over time, damage at range"; he argues for graphing real session data (kills, deaths, accuracy, equipment use) against designer target values, because theory cannot cover customization combinations. — [Ryan 2009](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/using-game-session-data-to-balance) — REPORTED
- Casteel (Game Developer, 2018) argues "If pure DPS is king, games are always going to come to who can churn out the most DPS the fastest," and wants non-binary combat outcomes; screenshots only, no data charts. — [Casteel 2018](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/avenues-for-success-and-the-cost-of-combat-in-rts) — REPORTED
- Riot's champion balance framework tracks win rate adjusted by ban rate in four skill tiers (Average, Skilled, Elite, Professional): a champion flagged overpowered in any tier is nerfed; one underperforming in all four is buffed; thresholds are bands around a target win rate; patch 10.12 reported 93% viable champions in solo queue. The page's chart forms were not described by my reader. — [Riot dev post](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-balance-framework-update/) — REPORTED
- Warhammer example: Liu and Marschner interpret optimized "handicaps" as point-cost multipliers per strength or toughness value. — [Liu and Marschner](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12924/12772) — VERIFIED
- EA "Exploring Gameplay With AI Agents": exchange-ratio table (resources exchanged per action saved) so designers can compare object prices. — [arXiv 1811.06962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.06962) — VERIFIED

### Inferences
- Where numbers for "how much is X worth" appear, they are mostly a single ratio (survivors, cost-per-action, points-per-stat) shown as a table or a line, not a chart type with strong conventions. (ESTIMATED)

### Gaps
- No GDC deck from Blizzard, Relic, Riot or Uber about cost-efficiency curves, break-even plots, or resource-exchange charts was read. GDC Vault leads exist (Manabe, Square Enix, "Balancing Nightmares," 2019; "Real-Time Strategy Game Balance," play/1022530) but only the first abstract was read.
- DPS-vs-EHP scatter plots and time-to-kill tables: only forum and tutorial snippets (formula TTK = H / D); no sourced chart found. Brave searches for TTK charts hit rate limits.

## 4. Presentations of parameter sweeps to designers

### Takeaway
Direct, sourced examples are sparse. Found: Jaffe's per-iteration table and line graph (one row or point per tuning iteration), Jaffe's 45 to 55% win-rate band redrawn for each value of a scoring parameter, Önal and Bülbül's before/after payoff matrices and convergence plots, Liu and Marschner's before/after matchup chart, and MAP-Elites archive heat maps. No tool with sliders linked to charts was found in the sources reached.

### Cited Findings
- Jaffe (2012): the tool is an input form for parameters on one side and a ranked list of restricted-play win rates on the other; each design change redraws the list; Table 1 records the history of nine iterations with a reading per row; Fig. 3 plots the measures across iterations. — [Jaffe 2012](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/jaffe2012ecg.pdf) — VERIFIED
- Jaffe thesis Figs. 6.9 to 6.11: same chart (kill rate vs death rate with a 45 to 55% band) repeated for three values of the scoring constant κ. — [Jaffe thesis](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/bitstreams/9817c1be-4389-4a4d-8f6f-8a5077d78330/download) — VERIFIED
- Önal and Bülbül: unoptimized and optimized payoff matrices and strategy-mix-over-time plots shown in pairs (left/right) for 5, 50 and 39 units; Table 2 (designer's initial power parameters) vs Table 3 (optimized values). — [Önal and Bülbül](https://doi.org/10.3897/jucs.121185) — VERIFIED
- Sorochan: the same 3 x 3 win-rate heat map drawn twice, one condition per figure (new unit for Player 1 only; for both). — [Sorochan et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03387) — VERIFIED
- de Mesentier Silva (Ticket to Ride): small multiples, one per map variant, plus an explicit "changing even a single component" comparison (USA vs USA 1910 destination deck) drawn as two board colour maps side by side. — [FDG 2017](http://www.nealen.net/papers/3102071.3102105.pdf) — VERIFIED
- Zook et al. active learning: curves of model error against number of playtests, used to argue fewer human playtests are needed. — [Zook et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01417) — VERIFIED
- MAP-Elites archives: a grid whose two axes are design "measures" and whose colour is the objective (Zhang et al. above); Alvarez et al. (AIIDE 2021) analyze archives over design-edit steps with "Temporal Expressive Range Analysis" in a co-creative level-design tool, reporting coverage (percent of archive cells covered) per step. — [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.03534); [Alvarez et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/18899/18664) — VERIFIED (text), form of Alvarez figures not seen
- Mechabellum tool (mecha-cc.com): described as a combat calculator for choosing mods and attacker/defender and viewing combat statistics and upgrade breakpoints; output views not described by the page content I could read. — [mecha-cc.com](https://mecha-cc.com/) — AMBIGUOUS
- GDC 2019 (Manabe, Square Enix) abstract: a genetic algorithm searching >10^100 equipment combinations plus "a data visualization method to find significant data from a large amount of log data that GA outputs"; the method itself is not described in the abstract. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/browse/gdc-19/play/1026365) — REPORTED

## 5. Stated lessons on readability for non-statisticians

### Takeaway
Stated lessons are scattered: use a diverging scale centred on the neutral value; order rows by strength; show an extra axis (skill) when a bar chart might mislead; keep a plain-words interpretation beside the numbers; do not hide the caveats of the data source; keep per-chart marks few. The sources say these things; none runs a readability study.

### Cited Findings
- Diverging colour with neutral in white: "a 5/neutral matchup is displayed as white, a negative matchup is displayed with shades of blue, and a positive matchup is displayed with shades of red." — [Graells and Astroza, Game Developer 2014](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/from-game-balance-and-meta-game-to-matchup-prediction-understanding-the-street-fighter-tier-list) — REPORTED
- Sorting rows by strength so structure shows: AlphaStar payoffs "sorted by mean winrate"; the SF matrix "ordered in a way that places characters with positive matchups at the top"; Czarnecki: payoffs sorted "for easier visual inspection". — [Czarnecki et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09468); [Graells and Astroza](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/from-game-balance-and-meta-game-to-matchup-prediction-understanding-the-street-fighter-tier-list) — VERIFIED / REPORTED
- Same chart can mislead if skill or matchmaking is ignored: Jaffe's bar of average kills (Fig. 7.1) would have led designers to nerf the wrong character; plotting against Elo (Fig. 7.2) fixed it; filtering to games within 100 Elo removed a matchmaking artifact even though it cut about 3/4 of the data ("sacrificing statistical significance is the right choice, rather than be misled by unrepresentative data"). — [Jaffe thesis, ch. 7](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/bitstreams/9817c1be-4389-4a4d-8f6f-8a5077d78330/download) — VERIFIED
- Jaffe on measures chosen for interpretability: average kills per game is "easier to interpret than score, and more informative than win percentage, which does not capture the margin of the win." — same — VERIFIED
- A complex visualization is acceptable if it becomes learnable: Jaffe's flow-map for special attacks "is somewhat complicated to learn to read initially, but quickly becomes second nature, allowing for rapid scanning." — same — VERIFIED
- Tables with a reading column: Jaffe's Table 1 pairs numbers with an "Interpretation" column in words. — [Jaffe 2012](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/jaffe2012ecg.pdf) — VERIFIED
- Show the scope and caveats of a chart: AoE2 matrix author states fixed conditions (fully upgraded, one-on-one, no micro, resource-value scenario) and per-scenario matrices; Mechabellum matrix footnotes placement- and tech-dependent cells; Jaffe lists the bias, skill-level and revision caveats of the Eventhubs matrix. — [AoE forums](https://forums.ageofempires.com/t/age-of-empires-2-cost-efficiency-matrices/85494); [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?l=german&id=2981751067); [Jaffe thesis](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/bitstreams/9817c1be-4389-4a4d-8f6f-8a5077d78330/download) — REPORTED / REPORTED / VERIFIED
- Unrepresentative matchmaking and small beta populations make aggregates unreliable: see above. Volz et al. argue 2,000 simulated games per deck were needed and that human playtests cannot supply that. — [Volz et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.03795) — VERIFIED
- Fixed rating bands used as thresholds are common: 45 to 55% "margin of acceptability" (Jaffe); Riot's per-tier overpowered/underpowered bands around a target win rate. — [Jaffe thesis](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/bitstreams/9817c1be-4389-4a4d-8f6f-8a5077d78330/download); [Riot](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-balance-framework-update/) — VERIFIED / REPORTED
- AlphaStar's caption removes unit clutter on request: "Worker units are removed for clarity"; caption of ablations says "Only the best-performing acquisition functions... are shown for clarity" (Zook et al.). — [AlphaStar PDF](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf); [Zook et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01417) — VERIFIED
- Liu and Marschner caution that matchup-chart handicap values are mathematically sound but "it is not clear whether the logistic handicap function itself is an accurate model, nor how to interpret the resulting handicaps quantitatively in terms of what should be changed about the characters." — [Liu and Marschner](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12924/12772) — VERIFIED

### Inferences
- A pattern across sources: the number of marks is small (3 x 3 to about 40 x 40 cells; 3 to 20 characters; a handful of lines), a threshold or neutral value is marked, and a sentence says how to read it. Whether non-statistician designers read these better is not tested in any source. (ESTIMATED)
- The user's reported difficulty (a margin-vs-size line chart, a break-even scatter) maps to the Jaffe warning that an aggregate chart without the context axis misleads; no source studies this directly.

### Gaps
- No study, GDC talk or article found that tests designer comprehension of balance charts.
- No source on colour-blind-safe palettes for red/blue balance matrices; all examples use red/blue or red/green/purple.
