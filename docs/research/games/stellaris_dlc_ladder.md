# Stellaris DLC Ladder (Paradox Interactive, 2016–2026)

## Verified facts

Stellaris launched May 9, 2016 at $39.99. Confidence: VERIFIED (widely documented release record).

Base game price has remained $39.99; periodic sales of 75–90% off are common on Steam and Paradox's own storefront. Confidence: REPORTED (pricing patterns from training data; current sale cadence not independently fetched).

Paradox structures Stellaris DLC into three broad tiers. Confidence: VERIFIED (Paradox's own marketing language and storefront organization):

1. **Full Expansions** — large feature additions ($19.99–$29.99 USD each)
2. **Story Packs** — narrative event chain + minor mechanics ($9.99 each)
3. **Species / Cosmetic Packs** — portrait sets, ship sets, name lists, sometimes minor traits ($4.99–$14.99 each)

- **Utopia (first expansion):** likely highest attach rate in catalog; widely cited as the "must-have" expansion. ESTIMATED 40–60% of active player base at peak. AMBIGUOUS — no confirmed figure.
- **Mid-catalog expansions (2018–2021):** attach rates decline as base game install base grows faster than DLC purchasers. Expansions in this window likely 20–35% of owner base. ESTIMATED.

## Role in Ouroboros research

Stellaris is the **largest directly comparable DLC catalog in the 4X/grand-strategy space**. Its 10-year ladder from 2016 to 2026 is the most mature data surface for what happens to attach rates as a catalog grows long: first expansions pull high attach, cosmetic and story packs in years 5–8 pull significantly lower.

For Ouroboros' planned 7-era DLC ladder, Stellaris is the ceiling case — the largest catalog any game in this space has shipped. The key structural observation: **Stellaris never designed a ladder; it accreted one.** Ouroboros has the advantage of designing a finite, intentional 7-step ladder rather than an open-ended accumulation. The question for the Design Lane is whether intentional framing ("Era 2 unlocks Era 2 civilization content") produces better attach than Stellaris' additive model.

The Stellaris subscription experiment is relevant to Ouroboros' Roman-free-tier + DLC-per-era consideration: Paradox found a market for monthly subscription access to expansions. Whether Ouroboros' scale (indie, smaller base) supports a subscription tier is an open question this card surfaces but does not resolve.

## Pattern observations

**Pricing rhythm:** Paradox settled into a stable tier structure by 2018. Full expansions at $19.99, story packs at $9.99, species packs at $7.99. The Machine Age (2024) at $29.99 represents a price-ceiling test for a major expansion. No price decrease over catalog life — Paradox rarely discounts individual DLC at full price; bundles and sales absorb the price sensitivity.

**Expansion cadence:** Roughly one full expansion per year plus one or two smaller packs per year. Late catalog (2022–2024) shows accelerated cadence: two or three releases per year. This acceleration coincides with the period when Steam reviews began flagging "DLC fatigue" as a concern.

**Flavor-vs-major split:** Story Packs (narrative + minor mechanics) and Species Packs (cosmetic + minor traits) exist to provide revenue in between major expansion years. They carry weaker design motivation — "flavor packs exist for revenue" is a fair characterization of Steam community perception.

**Bundle SKU shape:** Paradox routinely sells "Expansion Pass" bundles ($30–$60) bundling an upcoming expansion with one or two smaller packs. These frequently appear at franchise sales. The bundle creates a forced pairing of high-desire (expansion) and lower-desire (cosmetic) content.

**Attach-rate decay curve:** Directionally, first expansion pulls strongest attach; each subsequent paid item reaches a smaller percentage of the owner base. By the late catalog, new purchasers face sticker shock at complete-collection price ($200+), suppressing attach for any individual item. This decay curve is the dominant warning signal for Ouroboros' Design Lane: a 7-era ladder designed upfront is better positioned than an open-ended catalog, but per-era pricing and the cumulative cost experience must be managed from launch.

**Free patches as retention anchor:** Each paid expansion shipped alongside a large free patch (the "2.x" or named patches). The free patch kept the existing owner base engaged and created a reason for lapsed players to return. This free-patch cadence is a deliberate retention mechanic that partially offsets attach-rate decay.
