# King Arthur: Knight's Tale (NeocoreGames, 2022)

## Verified facts

King Arthur: Knight's Tale is a turn-based tactical RPG developed and published by NeocoreGames. It entered Early Access in January 2021 and reached full release on March 29, 2022. A Nintendo Switch port released in 2024. Confidence: VERIFIED (Steam store listing, Wikipedia, multiple press sources).

As of mid-2025, the game holds approximately **74–76% positive reviews on Steam across roughly 8,000–9,000 user reviews**, placing it in the "Mostly Positive" band. Confidence: ESTIMATED (no direct capture available; figure synthesized from multiple 2024 press references and review-aggregate context; should be verified via live Steam page).

The game is priced at approximately **$39.99 USD** at full release. Confidence: REPORTED (multiple retail and press sources; subject to regional pricing and sale events).

NeocoreGames had approximately **7–12 full-time developers** on the project at various stages. Confidence: AMBIGUOUS (studio size inferred from credits and interviews; no public headcount commitment verified).

Lifetime sales figures are not publicly disclosed by NeocoreGames. One industry-adjacent estimate places the game in the **100,000–250,000 units sold** range across all platforms (PC + Switch), but this is unconfirmed. Confidence: ESTIMATED (no primary source; do not cite as authoritative).

**Roster and permadeath mechanics.** The player commands a roster of named Arthurian knights — Sir Mordred (fixed protagonist), plus a pool of ~20+ recruitable heroes across the campaign. Knights have individual backstories, moral alignment axes (Christianity vs. Old Faith), skills, and permanent injuries. Knight death is permanent across the campaign. Confidence: VERIFIED (extensive press and player documentation, 2022–2024).

**Court management layer.** Between tactical missions, the player manages Camelot as a hub: recruits knights, constructs buildings, makes decisions in a morality-driven council system. This layer generates campaign-scope decisions that persist; it is not a full civilization simulation. Confidence: VERIFIED (Steam store feature list + press coverage).

**Chapter structure.** The campaign is divided into discrete chapters, each with a map of missions. Completion of all missions in a chapter advances the narrative arc. There is no procedural-generation layer: mission maps are authored, not generated from a living simulation. Confidence: VERIFIED (Steam description + player documentation).

**Moral alignment system.** Two axes — Christianity vs. Old Faith, Tyranny vs. Righteousness — affect available quests, recruitable knights, and narrative branches. The system creates playthrough differentiation without procedural world simulation. Confidence: VERIFIED (NeocoreGames official game description + press coverage).

**Sequel.** King Arthur: Knight's Tale 2 was announced for 2025 with expanded scope; no full release confirmed as of August 2025. Confidence: REPORTED (NeocoreGames announcement, 2024).

## Role in Ouroboros research

King Arthur: Knight's Tale is the clearest commercial embodiment of **Fork 1 option B (discrete chapter-per-era run shape)** in the WS5 competitor matrix. Each chapter functions as a discrete unit with its own map, mission set, and narrative beat — structurally analogous to a "per-era" episode. The campaign does not simulate a living world between chapters; the macro layer is authored rather than emergent.

The game demonstrates that **a permadeath knight roster with named personality-adjacent traits (moral alignment, backstory, injury state) is commercially viable** at the ~$40 price point even without generational sweep. Its "Mostly Positive" reception (rather than "Overwhelmingly Positive") is worth noting: the chapter structure and authored-world approach appear to limit replayability, which is a recurring criticism in negative reviews. Confidence: ESTIMATED (theme synthesis from review aggregate; individual reviews not systematically analyzed).

The game is **shallower than Ouroboros' locked plan on multiple axes**: no simulation-driven world, no generational lineage (knight succession does not extend across centuries), no chronicle persistence, no collapse-rebuild cycle, and no emergent faction behavior. It validates the tactical roster atom but does not validate the civ-sim wrap or generational depth.

The **court management hub** is the closest structural analog to Ouroboros' macro simulation layer, but it is a static hub (build queue, fixed decision tree) rather than a living simulation with drift, tension, and emergent faction behavior. This distinction matters for WS5 Fork 1: if Ouroboros picks the discrete-chapter shape (option B), it still differs from King Arthur in that its "chapters" would be produced by a living sim rather than authored content.

## Competitive shape

If Ouroboros picks **Fork 1 option B (chapter-per-era episodic)** with authored or semi-authored era structure, King Arthur: Knight's Tale becomes a **direct shape competitor** — permadeath roster, chapter structure, moral alignment differentiation. Ouroboros would need to differentiate on living-sim world generation, generational sweep, and Foundation/hard-SF thematic register. Price-point overlap is likely.

If Ouroboros picks **Fork 1 option F (collapse-rebuild ouroboros cycle)** or any procedural-generation-forward option (C, D, E, H), King Arthur: Knight's Tale becomes a **substrate precedent only** — it validates the tactical roster atom but does not crowd the procedural-generational space.

The game's "Mostly Positive" rather than "Very/Overwhelmingly Positive" reception suggests the authored-chapter shape carries a replayability ceiling. This is relevant evidence for the WS5 Blue Ocean lens on option F: if the collapse-rebuild cycle generates novel runs procedurally, it may avoid the replayability ceiling that limits King Arthur's ceiling.

## Connections to other research

King Arthur: Knight's Tale shares the permadeath-roster pattern with Battle Brothers (Overhype Studios), which holds "Overwhelmingly Positive" at a lower price point. The gap in reception between King Arthur ("Mostly Positive") and Battle Brothers ("Overwhelmingly Positive") is partially explained by replayability: Battle Brothers generates novel campaign runs procedurally; King Arthur does not. This is a live signal for WS5 Fork 1 option differentiation.

The court-management hub in King Arthur is structurally related to the morality/decision systems in Wildermyth's between-mission layer, though Wildermyth ships richer character-transformation narrative beats at shorter campaign scope (~15 hours vs. King Arthur's 30–50). Both demonstrate that a roster-plus-hub structure is well-understood by the niche audience.

The chapter-per-era structure is the Tier-3 B crowding identified in WS5 research: it occupies the same shelf position as Massive Chalice's dynasty-chapter shape, though King Arthur executes tactical depth more successfully than Massive Chalice did. The comparison between King Arthur and Massive Chalice is a natural pairing for WS5 Fork 1 B-territory analysis.

Darkest Dungeon (also documented in this research set) occupies a different run-shape territory (time-window crusade vs. discrete authored chapters) and holds higher review sentiment. The contrast between King Arthur's authored-chapter structure and Darkest Dungeon's procedurally-stocked run shape is directly relevant to WS5 Fork 1 options B vs. G.
