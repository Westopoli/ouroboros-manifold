# Mechabellum (Game River, 2023–2024)

## Verified facts

Mechabellum is an auto-battler strategy game developed and published by Game River (a small Chinese studio). It entered Steam Early Access in May 2023 and reached full release in September 2024. Confidence: REPORTED (EA start and full-release window from training data; not independently verified at capture via live page).

**Monetization model:** Mechabellum launched in Early Access as a paid game and remained a paid title at full release. It did NOT transition to free-to-play. The base game is priced at approximately $19.99–$24.99 USD. Confidence: REPORTED (pricing and paid-model status from training data; specific price not confirmed to exact figure at capture. The claim that it remained paid rather than pivoting to F2P is REPORTED — training data does not contain a confirmed F2P transition announcement).

Steam review status: Mechabellum holds "Very Positive" Steam reviews. As of training data, review count is in the range of 8,000–15,000 reviews with approximately 85–90% positive sentiment. Confidence: ESTIMATED (figures from training data context; live Steam page not fetched at capture; review count may have grown since training data).

The game's core loop: players compose armies of unit types before each round, then watch automated combat resolve. Between rounds, players spend resources to upgrade, add, or reposition units. Skill expression is in pre-battle composition and adaptation, not real-time execution. Confidence: VERIFIED (mechanic is universally described in press coverage and reviews; well-established).

Mechabellum supports both PvP multiplayer and PvE modes. The multiplayer scene has been a significant driver of community retention. Confidence: REPORTED (community documentation from training data).

Cosmetic DLC has been released for Mechabellum post-launch — skins and visual variants for units. The cosmetic-DLC approach is a common strategy-game post-launch revenue model that avoids pay-to-win criticism. Confidence: REPORTED (DLC release documented in training data; specific DLC catalog and revenue contribution AMBIGUOUS).

Developer size: Game River is a small studio, primarily Chinese-based. Exact headcount AMBIGUOUS. The game received Chinese studio investment and has Chinese-language content as a first-class support tier. Confidence: REPORTED.

Lifetime sales: AMBIGUOUS. No public figure confirmed. Given "Very Positive" at high-four-digit to low-five-digit review count and a $20–$25 price point, rough estimates place lifetime units in the 300,000–800,000 range, but this is an ESTIMATED calibration only.

**Note on F2P claim in the brief:** The task brief notes "Originally launched paid; transitioned to F2P or remained paid — confirm what you know." Training data does not contain a confirmed F2P transition for Mechabellum. The available evidence is consistent with Mechabellum remaining a paid title. ## Role in Ouroboros research

Mechabellum is in the Research Lane for **T-M1 (free-tier + paid-DLC precedents — F2P-on-strategy precedent)**.

The core research question: does Mechabellum represent a strategy game that successfully transitioned to F2P (a precedent for strategy-genre F2P viability), or does it represent a paid strategy game that held its price point despite genre-level pressure toward F2P? The answer determines which lane of the T-M1 investigation this card occupies.

Based on available training data, **Mechabellum appears to have remained paid** — making it a case study in the "paid strategy survives" lane rather than the "strategy game pivots to F2P" lane. This is the more commercially conservative precedent: Game River built a healthy multiplayer-driven strategy game at a $20–$25 price point, used cosmetic DLC for post-launch revenue, and did not require a F2P pivot to sustain the community.

If this reading is correct, Mechabellum sits alongside Menace and Battle Brothers as evidence that paid strategy at $20–$30 is a viable commercial model for games with sufficient depth and community retention.

The auto-battler genre context is also relevant: auto-battlers as a category have seen significant F2P adoption (Teamfight Tactics, Legends of Runeterra's auto-battler mode) because they have roots in competitive mobile and MOBA ecosystems. Mechabellum operating as a paid PC game in this genre is notable precisely because it resists that genre-level drift toward F2P. It suggests the hardcore-PC-strategy slice of the auto-battler audience is price-insensitive in the same way the broader hardcore strategy audience is.

## Competitive shape

Mechabellum is not a direct genre competitor to Ouroboros — it has no narrative layer, no generational structure, and no character system. The competitive relevance is at the monetization-model and community-retention level.

If Ouroboros is deciding between paid, freemium, and F2P models (T-M1), Mechabellum is relevant as a paid-strategy data point with multiplayer community retention: the game built a lasting competitive scene at a paid price point, using cosmetic DLC to extend post-launch revenue, without requiring F2P to grow or retain its player base.

The auto-battler community in Mechabellum skews toward tactically-minded players who enjoy optimization and composition problems — an audience that overlaps meaningfully with the Ouroboros target demographic. The retention pattern (deep mechanical mastery + competitive iteration) is the type Ouroboros wants to cultivate.
