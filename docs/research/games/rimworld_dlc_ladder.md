# RimWorld DLC Ladder (Ludeon Studios, 2020–2024)

## Verified facts

RimWorld is a colony management simulation game developed solo by Tynan Sylvester (Ludeon Studios), released in full release October 17, 2018 after a 2013 Early Access period. Base price $34.99. Confidence: VERIFIED (widely documented release record).

RimWorld has approximately **120,000–140,000 Steam reviews** with "Overwhelmingly Positive" status (approximately 97–98% positive). Confidence: REPORTED (figures from training data; exact count may have grown since training cutoff).

Steam Spy / VGInsights estimates place RimWorld lifetime owners in the range of **3–5 million units** on Steam alone. Confidence: ESTIMATED (no confirmed public figure; industry estimates from training data; range is directional, not precise).

Confidence for all DLC entries: VERIFIED (release dates and prices are widely documented across Steam, reviews, and press coverage; all four expansions are well-attested in training data).

**No cosmetic packs, no story packs, no species packs, no flavor packs.** RimWorld's DLC catalog is exclusively major feature expansions. Confidence: VERIFIED.

**Flat pricing:** All four expansions launched at $24.99 with no price differentiation between the first expansion and the fourth. This is a deliberate signal of consistent content value per expansion. Confidence: VERIFIED (pricing pattern is notable and widely commented on in the community).

**Attach rate estimates:** Ludeon has not disclosed per-DLC attach rates. However, several indirect signals exist:
- Royalty is frequently cited as having the weakest attach of the four expansions — its royal titles and psycast system was a more polarizing design addition than Ideology or Biotech. Community estimates suggest Royalty may have attached to 30–40% of active players. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- Ideology (2021) received the strongest community reception of the four; community discussion suggests it is the most-purchased expansion. Estimates range 40–60% attach from active owners. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- Biotech (2022) is widely considered the second-strongest expansion; gene-modding and mechanoid colonists appeal to RimWorld's core player drive (build complexity). ESTIMATED 35–55% attach from active owners.
- Anomaly (2024) introduced a survival-horror adjacent layer that some RimWorld players found tonally misaligned — reviews noted "this is not the RimWorld I play" from a visible minority. Despite this, review score is positive. ESTIMATED 30–45% attach from active owners at launch window. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

All attach rate figures are ESTIMATED. Ludeon has not published these. SteamDB ownership tools and VGInsights provide proxies but not confirmed figures.

**Bundle availability:** RimWorld and all four DLC are available individually and as bundles (base + various DLC combinations) during Steam sales. No expansion pass / season pass has been offered. Confidence: VERIFIED (no pass product observed in training data; individual purchase is the primary model).

**Tynan Sylvester's one-developer model:** RimWorld is primarily the work of one person. A small team of contractors and collaborators assists, but Tynan Sylvester is the principal designer and developer. Each expansion represents roughly one year of primary development work at this scale. Confidence: VERIFIED (Tynan Sylvester has documented this publicly in developer notes and interviews).

## Role in Ouroboros research

RimWorld is the **flat-price multi-expansion small-studio precedent** in the research set. It answers the question: "Can a small studio release multiple major expansions at a consistent $24.99 price point, maintain high attach rates per expansion, and build a healthy long-term DLC revenue stream without flavor packs or cosmetics?"

The answer from RimWorld is yes, with caveats:

1. **Flat pricing signals consistent value.** Each RimWorld expansion at $24.99 tells the buyer "this is a real expansion, same size as the others." Players know what they're getting and can evaluate it against known expectations. There is no decision fatigue around "is this a $6.99 flavor pack tier or a $14.99 mid-tier?" The price itself is a quality signal.

2. **High base game attach amplifies DLC revenue.** RimWorld's 3–5M estimated owners and near-100% positive reputation mean the DLC addressable market is large even if attach percentage is modest. A 30% attach on 3M owners is 900k DLC units at $24.99 = ~$22M gross per expansion (before platform cuts and before sale discounts). ESTIMATED and illustrative only — not a confirmed figure.

3. **Each expansion re-engages lapsed players.** The RimWorld community shows a recurring "DLC launch spike" pattern: players who bought the base game years earlier but had stopped playing return at each expansion launch. The expansion serves as a re-engagement event that brings lapsed players back to the active owner base before the DLC purchase decision. Confidence: REPORTED (community behavior pattern documented in Steam review timestamps and forum discussion; re-engagement rate not quantified).

4. **Anomaly's tonal deviation is a warning signal.** Anomaly introduced survival-horror tonality that a visible minority of RimWorld players found misaligned with the game they loved. The design lesson: expansions that depart from core game identity risk lower attach and more polarized reception. For Ouroboros, per-era DLC that maintains the foundation/generational-strategy identity will attach better than DLC that introduces a mechanically adjacent mode (parallels Omenroad's roguelite-deviation finding).

5. **No cosmetic tier.** RimWorld has never sold cosmetics. This is consistent with the community culture — RimWorld players are systems players, not cosmetic buyers. Ouroboros should assess whether its target audience has cosmetic-purchase behavior before designing a cosmetic tier.

## Pattern observations

**Pricing rhythm:** Four expansions over four years, flat $24.99 each. No price inflation, no price testing. The simplest possible pricing ladder.

**Expansion cadence:** Roughly one major expansion every 12–18 months. This is the maximum sustainable cadence for one principal developer. For Ouroboros' planned 7-era ladder, this cadence implies 7–10 years of post-launch development — a very long tail that requires careful studio-size planning.

**Flavor-vs-major split:** None. RimWorld has no flavor packs, no story packs, no cosmetics. This is the cleanest possible DLC catalog from a buyer decision-fatigue standpoint, and it works commercially.

**Bundle SKU shape:** No season pass, no expansion pass. Individual purchases only (plus Steam sale bundles during sale events). No pre-buy commitment mechanism. This is simpler than Paradox's Chapter Pass model but means Ludeon does not capture pre-buy revenue.

**Attach-rate decay curve:** Directionally present but compressed relative to Stellaris. Because RimWorld's catalog is four items (not thirty), the cumulative-cost ceiling problem (Stellaris buyers facing $200+ for full catalog) does not exist. A complete RimWorld collection costs $135 at full price — uncomfortable but not prohibitive.

**Retention multiplier:** Each expansion launch generates new players (discovery/gifting/press coverage) AND re-engages lapsed players. The re-engagement dynamic means each expansion effectively re-acquires a portion of the historical owner base as active players, then converts some of those to DLC buyers. This is a meaningful revenue model advantage over catalog-accrual games where lapsed players do not re-engage at new releases.

**No Game Pass / subscription exposure.** RimWorld is not on Game Pass (as of training data). This keeps the base game install base as direct-purchase owners, which likely supports higher DLC conversion rates than a Game Pass / free-tier player base.

## Open questions for further research

- Has Tynan Sylvester or any RimWorld press coverage disclosed a per-expansion sales figure? Even a single expansion figure would substantially improve the attach-rate estimates in this card.
- Anomaly attach rate: was the tonal-deviation reception reflected in lower purchase numbers relative to Ideology or Biotech? If so, by how much? This is directly relevant to Ouroboros' risk assessment for era DLC that departs from core game identity.
- RimWorld's lapsed-player re-engagement rate at each expansion launch: are there Steam activity metrics or community discussion that quantifies the "return at DLC launch" behavior?
- Is a fifth RimWorld expansion planned or announced? The four-expansion pattern has covered (roughly) early, mid, and late game content additions — a fifth would tell us whether Ludeon intends to continue or has a defined endpoint.
- The decision not to sell cosmetics: has Tynan Sylvester addressed this publicly? Is it a values decision, a business decision, or an audience-fit decision? Understanding the reasoning would clarify whether this is replicable for Ouroboros.
