# Old World

## Verified facts

Old World (Mohawk Games / Hooded Horse) released on Steam August 2, 2022, after an Epic Games Store exclusive period beginning May 2021. Developer is Mohawk Games, founded by Soren Johnson (lead designer of Civilization IV). Confidence: VERIFIED (widely reported).

Steam review count: as of late 2024 / early 2025, Old World sits in the range of approximately 4,000–6,000 Steam reviews at "Very Positive." Confidence: ESTIMATED (training data; independent web verification unavailable for this session). Commercial scale is substantially smaller than CK3 — this is a specialist/enthusiast title rather than a mainstream crossover.

Base game price: approximately $39.99 USD on Steam. Confidence: REPORTED. The game shipped with "Wonders and Dynasties" DLC and the "Egypt and Mesopotamia" DLC adding additional factions; overall DLC footprint is modest relative to Paradox titles. Confidence: REPORTED.

Core mechanical signature: Old World is a **turn-based 4X strategy game set in antiquity** (Mediterranean / Near East). The defining distinction from Civilization-style 4X is the **character layer**: rulers, heirs, and court figures are named individuals with traits, opinions, ambitions, and relationships. The **Ambition system** drives player goals — each turn the player pursues one or more Ambitions (e.g., "Construct three wonders," "Train an heir in military skill"), and completing ambitions advances the family legacy score. Succession is an active game mechanic — the ruler ages and dies; the heir they trained inherits, carrying their traits and relationships. Confidence: VERIFIED (Soren Johnson design interviews, press coverage, widely documented).

The run is **single and continuous** within a scenario. Old World does not have a collapse-and-restart mechanic. The scenario ends at a fixed turn count with a legacy score. Confidence: VERIFIED.

The family (dynasty) is the persistent entity across ruler lifetimes — Old World frames progression explicitly as family legacy, not individual ruler achievement. This is the **family-as-progression-unit** model. Confidence: VERIFIED.

## Role in Ouroboros research

Old World provides the clearest commercial precedent for the **family-as-progression-unit** design in a 4X substrate. Where CK3's character model is embedded in a grand-strategy simulation with near-infinite time scale, Old World demonstrates the same family-legacy attachment model in a tighter, scenario-bound 4X structure — a more accessible entry point for players who find CK3's scope overwhelming.

The Ambition ladder is the most direct precedent in the generational niche for **player-facing goal structure within a dynasty run**. Each ambition is a mid-term goal that structures player attention without prescribing a single optimal path. This is directly relevant to Ouroboros' WS5 Fork on progression design — how does the player know what to pursue across a multi-generation run?

Old World's Soren Johnson pedigree (Civ IV lead) makes the game design-documentation-rich. Johnson publishes design notes and podcasts; the Ambition system's design rationale is publicly documented and can be used as reference for Ouroboros' own goal-structure design.

The commercial scale differential between Old World (~5k Steam reviews) and CK3 (~80k+ Steam reviews) is itself a data point: the 4X substrate with character layer appears to pull a narrower audience than CK3's pure dynasty-sim register. Ouroboros sits closer to the CK3 register thematically but with a tactical layer addition; this gap is worth tracking.

Old World does not have a tactical lieutenant layer. Armies are strategic-abstraction units as in most 4X games. The named-character layer applies to the dynasty/court level, not to the battlefield. This gap is the same as CK3 — both validate the dynasty/character model without validating the lieutenant-tactical combination.

## Competitive shape

Old World occupies **Tier-3 A adjacent** territory — single continuous run, family-as-progression-unit, character-driven 4X. It is smaller-scale commercially than CK3 and operates in a tighter scenario format.

If Ouroboros picks **Fork 1 option A (single continuous run)**, Old World is a partial shape competitor — the family/dynasty progression model overlaps, but Old World's 4X substrate is distinct from Ouroboros' civ-sim + tactical substrate. The audience overlap is real but not complete.

If Ouroboros picks **Fork 1 option F (collapse-rebuild cycle)**, Old World becomes a substrate precedent for the dynasty/character model only — not a competitive threat.

Old World's modest commercial scale relative to CK3 does not disqualify it as a precedent. It suggests the addressable audience for character-driven 4X is real but specialist; Ouroboros' Foundation/sci-fi register may draw a partially distinct audience from Old World's antiquity setting.

## Connections to other research

Old World is the tightest mechanical neighbor to CK3 in the generational niche research set. Both share the family-as-progression-unit model and the single-continuous-run shape. The distinction is substrate: CK3 is grand-strategy open-ended, Old World is 4X scenario-bound.

Star Dynasties ports a similar dynasty-succession model to sci-fi but without the 4X mechanical depth Old World provides. The comparison between Old World and Star Dynasties is instructive for Ouroboros: Old World has deeper mechanical systems and stronger reviews despite a non-sci-fi setting; Star Dynasties has the thematic register overlap with Ouroboros but less mechanical density.

Wildermyth applies character transformation at campaign scale with a much smaller party than Old World's court/family model. Old World's scope (dozens of named characters, multi-generation succession) is closer to Ouroboros' planned scale than Wildermyth's (3–6 heroes per campaign leg).

CK3 at 4M+ units vs. Old World at ~5k Steam reviews is a calibration point for what "dynasty-as-core-loop" can achieve at different price points, platform strategies, and scope levels. The delta is large enough that setting and scope appear to matter significantly — not just the dynasty mechanic itself.
