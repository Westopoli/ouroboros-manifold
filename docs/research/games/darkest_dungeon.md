# Darkest Dungeon (Red Hook Studios, 2016)

## Verified facts

Darkest Dungeon is a gothic roguelite RPG developed and published by Red Hook Studios (a two-person studio at founding; small indie throughout development). It entered Early Access in February 2015 and reached full release on January 19, 2016. A console port to PlayStation 4 released in September 2016; Nintendo Switch in late 2017. A sequel, Darkest Dungeon II, released into Early Access in 2022 and fully in May 2023. Confidence: VERIFIED (Steam store page, Wikipedia, multiple press sources).

As of mid-2025, Darkest Dungeon holds approximately **95% positive reviews on Steam across roughly 130,000–145,000 user reviews**, classified as "Overwhelmingly Positive." Confidence: ESTIMATED (figure synthesized from multiple 2024 press and review-aggregate sources; exact count not captured live — verify via Steam page).

The base game is priced at approximately **$24.99 USD**. Multiple DLC packs (The Crimson Court, The Shieldbreaker, The Color of Madness) each priced at $5.99–$9.99. Confidence: REPORTED (multiple retail sources; subject to sale events).

**Lifetime revenue.** Multiple industry sources estimate Darkest Dungeon's lifetime gross revenue (PC + console, base game + DLC, through approximately 2023) at approximately **$40–60 million USD**. The $50M figure cited in this card's brief is within that range. Confidence: ESTIMATED (no primary Red Hook revenue disclosure; figure derived from VGInsights-class estimates and press interviews referencing "millions of copies sold" — see note below).

Red Hook co-founder Tyler Sigman stated in a 2019 GDC talk that the game sold "well over 4 million copies" across all platforms as of that date. At a blended average selling price (accounting for sales, console pricing, DLC attach rate), 4 million units generates a plausible $40–60M gross revenue range. Confidence: REPORTED (Tyler Sigman, GDC 2019; revenue range is derived estimation, not a disclosed figure).

**Run shape.** The player manages a hamlet (hub) and dispatches expeditions into procedurally-generated dungeon corridors. Each expedition has a defined scope and time pressure. The campaign has a defined end-state: completing four Darkest Dungeon runs (the game's final tier dungeons). The overall campaign shape is a **goal-bounded crusade with a time-window pressure layer** — the player pushes toward a fixed narrative/mechanical endpoint while managing an attriting roster. Individual expeditions are short (20–90 minutes). Confidence: VERIFIED (extensive gameplay documentation, player wikis, press coverage).

**Hero roster and permadeath.** The player recruits named heroes from a procedurally-restocked stagecoach. Heroes gain permanent traits (positive and negative), stress, quirks, and diseases through play. Hero death is permanent and recorded in a graveyard. The graveyard is a persistent in-run record, not a cross-run legacy layer — it does not carry over to a new campaign. Confidence: VERIFIED (Steam store description + player wiki + press coverage).

**Stress and affliction systems.** Heroes accumulate stress during expeditions; at stress threshold, they may become afflicted (negative behavior state) or, in rare cases, virtuous (positive behavior state). Afflicted heroes impose costs on the party during combat and between missions. Managing stress is the game's primary resource puzzle on top of HP. Confidence: VERIFIED (extensively documented; core design pillar per Red Hook interviews).

**Procedural dungeon generation.** Dungeon corridors, room contents, and enemy encounters are procedurally generated within authored encounter pools. The hamlet, town events, and hero-pool composition are semi-procedural. There is no living world simulation underneath the hamlet: faction behavior, borders, drift, and macro civilization state do not exist. Confidence: VERIFIED (design documentation + gameplay structure).

**Difficulty and accessibility.** The game shipped with a "Stygian" (hard) mode and later added an "Radiant" mode (shorter, easier) in response to player feedback about campaign length. Campaign length on a first playthrough is approximately 50–100 hours. Confidence: REPORTED (press coverage of Radiant mode addition, 2017; playtime ranges from player reports).

## Role in Ouroboros research

Darkest Dungeon is the canonical commercial reference for **Fork 1 option G (time-window run)** in the WS5 competitor matrix. The goal-bounded crusade shape — push toward a defined endpoint against an attriting roster under time pressure — is the structural core of option G. The game's "Overwhelmingly Positive" reception at ~130k+ reviews and an estimated $50M revenue demonstrates this run shape has strong market acceptance in the niche target audience.

The **stress + affliction system** is directly relevant to the Ouroboros Lieutenant System personality model (WS3 Fork A1, currently paused at awaiting-pick). Darkest Dungeon demonstrates that hero behavioral states induced by campaign stress — not just stat penalties, but actual decision-making shifts — are legible and satisfying to players. The affliction/virtue branching maps loosely onto the personality-variance model being designed for Ouroboros lieutenants. This is not a fork-decision reference; it is an implementation-adjacent design vocabulary reference.

The **graveyard as a persistent record within a run** (but not across runs) is the Darkest Dungeon answer to Fork 1 option G's death/loss shape. It creates emotional weight for permadeath without committing to cross-run legacy persistence. This is a design constraint Ouroboros has already departed from: the locked Lieutenant System implies named lieutenants persist across a commander's lifetime, and the chronicle spoke implies some persistence survives collapse. Darkest Dungeon shows one end of the persistence spectrum; Ouroboros' planned depth is further toward the persistent end.

The **absence of a living world simulation** beneath the hamlet is the structural gap between Darkest Dungeon and Ouroboros. Darkest Dungeon's hamlet is a static hub; its dungeons are procedurally populated but do not simulate a civilization. Ouroboros' civ-sim wrap (drift, factions, regions, era progression) is a depth extension Darkest Dungeon does not attempt. This means Darkest Dungeon is a **substrate precedent** for the run shape but not a close competitor on civ-sim depth.

## Competitive shape

Darkest Dungeon is the **dominant occupant of the time-window crusade run shape** in the niche target audience's frame of reference. If Ouroboros picks **Fork 1 option G**, it enters a shelf where Darkest Dungeon is the defining title, with over a decade of community inertia and a sequel reinforcing the position. Differentiation on that shelf would require the civ-sim wrap and Lieutenant System personality depth to carry sufficient incremental value — the run shape alone would not distinguish Ouroboros from Darkest Dungeon 1 or 2.

If Ouroboros picks **Fork 1 option F (collapse-rebuild ouroboros cycle)** or any shape other than G, Darkest Dungeon becomes a **substrate precedent** — it validates the permadeath-roster + stress-behavioral-state pattern that Ouroboros' Lieutenant System will use, while occupying a different run-shape position. On option F, Ouroboros would be able to reference Darkest Dungeon as validation for the lieutenant behavioral layer without competing directly for the same run-shape shelf.

The **Darkest Dungeon II** sequel shifted to a run-by-run roguelite shape (per-run stagecoach journey, no persistent hamlet) — a significant departure from DD1's crusade shape. This suggests Red Hook itself moved away from the time-window crusade as the definitive form of the game. That is weak evidence that the crusade shape has a ceiling or that the audience preferred the roguelite variant. This is worth factoring into WS5 Fork 1 option G analysis.

## Connections to other research

Darkest Dungeon's permadeath-roster + behavioral-stress system shares the most structural DNA with Battle Brothers among the catalogued titles. Battle Brothers lacks the stress/affliction layer but shares the procedural-world encounter generation, the named hero investment, and the open-ended run without a fixed endpoint. The contrast in run shape (Battle Brothers: open-ended mercenary existence; Darkest Dungeon: goal-bounded crusade) maps directly onto the Fork 1 option D (commander-lifetime episodic, open-ended) vs. option G (time-window, goal-bounded) distinction.

King Arthur: Knight's Tale (also in this research set) is the authored-chapter counterpart. The contrast between King Arthur's authored chapter structure and Darkest Dungeon's procedurally-stocked run — and the gap in review sentiment between them (Mostly Positive vs. Overwhelmingly Positive) — is direct evidence that procedural encounter generation raises the replayability ceiling even within the same permadeath-roster genre.

Wildermyth shares the named-character narrative investment and transformation-through-play arc. Where Wildermyth's characters transform through story events (mythic transformation, relationship formation), Darkest Dungeon's characters transform through behavioral states induced by stress — a different mechanism for the same design goal of "characters who feel changed by their experiences."

Massive Chalice (Double Fine) attempted a generational-dynasty layer that Darkest Dungeon explicitly does not — Massive Chalice's heroes die and are replaced by offspring across campaign centuries. The absence of a generational layer in Darkest Dungeon is notable: the game achieves strong emotional investment in hero permanence without the dynasty mechanic. This is relevant to the question of whether Ouroboros' generational sweep is necessary for the lieutenant investment to feel meaningful, or whether the stress/behavioral-state layer alone is sufficient.

Menace (Overhype Studios spiritual successor) occupies a similar permadeath-roster space at squad-tactics scale. Where Darkest Dungeon's behavioral states emerge from stress accumulation, Menace's lieutenant variance is personality-systemic from character creation. These are two distinct implementation models for lieutenant-level behavioral differentiation.

## Open questions for further research

What fraction of Darkest Dungeon's $40–60M revenue estimate is attributable to PC vs. console? Console attach rate matters for Ouroboros platform planning.

Does Darkest Dungeon II's run-by-run roguelite shift (away from the crusade shape) reflect player preference data, or was it primarily a design pivot by Red Hook? If Red Hook has commented on this in GDC talks or interviews post-2022, that would sharpen the crusade-shape ceiling hypothesis.

What is the Steam tag overlap between Darkest Dungeon and the other niche-target titles (Battle Brothers, King Arthur: Knight's Tale, Wildermyth)? High tag overlap would confirm they are drawing from the same discovery pool, which informs how Ouroboros should position its own tags.

What does Darkest Dungeon II's commercial performance look like relative to DD1? If DD2 underperformed despite the brand, that would suggest the crusade → roguelite pivot did not grow the audience, which is data for WS5 Fork 1 option G risk assessment.

Is there a commercially successful title that combines the Darkest Dungeon crusade run shape with a living-world simulation layer? If not, that gap is open market space. If yes, it would be a critical card to add to the research set.
