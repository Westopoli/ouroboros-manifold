# CK3 DLC Ladder (Paradox Interactive, 2020–2026)

## Verified facts

Crusader Kings III released September 1, 2020 at $49.99. Base game received "Overwhelmingly Positive" Steam reviews. Confidence: VERIFIED (widely documented release record and review status).

CK3 sold approximately 1 million copies within its first month, making it the fastest-selling title in Paradox's history at that point. Confidence: REPORTED (press coverage from September–October 2020 training data).

Paradox structures CK3 DLC into four identifiable categories. Confidence: VERIFIED (Paradox storefront organization and community documentation):

1. **Major Expansions** — large mechanical overhaul ($29.99)
2. **Chapter Passes / Expansion Passes** — bundle SKUs covering future releases
3. **Flavor Packs** — culture/dynasty/region-specific content packs ($6.99–$12.99)
4. **Cosmetic Packs** — portrait and unit cosmetics ($4.99–$9.99)

## Role in Ouroboros research

CK3 is the **closest structural precedent in the research set for Ouroboros' planned 7-era DLC ladder**. The reasons:

1. **Niche-targeted flavor packs.** CK3's flavor packs (Northern Lords for Norse players, Fate of Iberia for Iberian peninsula players) are the closest existing model to Ouroboros' planned per-era DLC — each targets a specific player interest within the same game. The era-specific framing ("Era 2: Bronze Age Collapse DLC") is structurally analogous to CK3's culture-region framing.

2. **Tiered price structure.** CK3 separates large mechanical overhauls ($29.99) from mid-tier content packs ($14.99) from flavor/event packs ($6.99). This three-level ladder within a single game's DLC catalog is a tested model for segmenting willingness-to-pay.

3. **Character-driven simulation.** CK3's core design is a character-with-traits dynasty simulation — the closest genre neighbor to Ouroboros' lieutenant + chronicle layer inside a larger simulation. The DLC catalog extends this by adding new character-interaction systems (Royal Court's grandeur, Tours & Tournaments' travel). Ouroboros' per-era DLC concept of adding new era-specific character arcs is downstream of this model.

4. **Subscription experiment.** Paradox tested subscription access to CK3 base + older DLC. The tension between subscription access and individual DLC sell-through is directly relevant to Ouroboros' roman-free-tier + DLC-per-era model.

The key structural difference: CK3's flavor packs target **geography/culture within a fixed timeline** (the medieval period). Ouroboros' per-era DLC targets **temporal progression** — each era is a different historical register. This is a stronger differentiation axis, but it requires that each era feels sufficiently distinct for a player to want to pay for access to it.

## Pattern observations

**Pricing rhythm:** CK3 moved away from the pure $9.99/$19.99 Stellaris model. The $6.99 flavor pack (low-commitment purchase), $14.99 mid-tier, and $29.99 major expansion create a deliberate ladder where players who love CK3 have a natural upgrade path. The $6.99 entry point is low enough that casual fans buy flavor packs as "supporting the devs" gestures, not just for content. Confidence: ESTIMATED (interpretation of pricing pattern from community reception; not a Paradox-stated design goal).

**Cadence:** Roughly 2–4 releases per year since 2021. Paradox has maintained a consistent release rhythm without the "DLC fatigue" language appearing in CK3 reviews as prominently as in Stellaris — likely because CK3's catalog remains smaller and more coherently organized. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Flavor-vs-major split:** CK3 uses flavor packs to fill between major expansion releases and target specific sub-audiences (players who love playing Norse, players who love playing in Iberia). This niche-targeting within a single game's DLC calendar is the key pattern for Ouroboros.

**Bundle SKU shape:** Chapter Passes bundle upcoming content and create pre-buy incentive. The pass model reduces individual-item decision fatigue — buyers commit to the next year of content at a discount. For Ouroboros, an "Era Pass" bundling multiple era DLCs would replicate this pattern.

**Subscription model tension:** Paradox's subscription has created community friction — players who paid $29.99 for Royal Court see it included in subscription after a window, creating perceived devaluation. This is a known risk for Ouroboros' design lane if a free-tier + subscription model is considered.

**Attach rate decay:** Flavor packs after the first few likely see declining attach as casual players have already made their DLC-purchase decisions. The players who buy Northern Lords AND Fate of Iberia AND Friends & Foes are a dedicated core; the marginal buyer per pack decreases over time.

## Connections to other research

- **Stellaris DLC Ladder (G-16):** sister Paradox title with the larger, older catalog. CK3 is the "newer and more intentionally structured" version of the same developer's DLC strategy. CK3's flavor-pack model evolved from Stellaris' species-pack + story-pack model.
- **RimWorld DLC Ladder (G-19):** contrasting approach — flat $24.99 per major expansion, no flavor packs. RimWorld achieves high attach per expansion by keeping the catalog short and avoiding low-commitment micro-SKUs. CK3 and RimWorld bracket the design space between "many-SKU tiered ladder" and "few-SKU flat-price ladder."
- **Wildermyth Omenroad (G-18):** small-studio single-DLC precedent at the opposite scale from CK3's catalog depth. Useful contrast for Ouroboros' team-size considerations.
- **Wildermyth (existing card):** CK3 and Wildermyth both implement character-with-traits narrative at different simulation scales; CK3 is the large-studio version of the dynasty-character model that Wildermyth proves at indie scale.

## Open questions for further research

- Royal Court attach rate: has any Paradox investor presentation or GDC talk disclosed a figure? This is the most useful single data point for calibrating first-expansion attach expectations.
