# H-076: every-layout-measures-something

"Each layout should measure something, not just be randomly placed."

Flag harness; tool Workflow; no scene. ## Why

Westley, 2026-11-02, about L-0048, verbatim: "I don't really know what utility this layout provides. Each layout should measure something, not just be randomly placed. If there wasn't an "idea" behind the layout, than it shouldn't be used for the simulation lab. Certain layouts will need to test specific unit role performances, specific unit performances, specific arcs or chokes, etc. This one isn't obvious what it tests, I have a feeling it was just created randomly. Dispute that if it's not the case."

Finding (Architect, verified in `scripts/skilllab/catalogue/produce.py` on main, 2026-11-02): it was not a feeling. `_layout2` draws each side's role mix with `mix = rng.choice(MIXES)` from five mixes (DRAFT-role-mixes), seeded, so a round two layout's composition is random. L-0048 is open_field at 32:16: side A holds 11 Bastion, 11 Warden and 10 Bombard, side B 6, 6 and 4. The two sides share a mix by the draw, not by a plan. The pair 32:16 is the open_field entry of `B2_PAIRS` (DRAFT-batch2-headcounts, H-025 round two item 5), one fixed pair per situation kind, chosen to test nothing in particular; `ONE_PAIRS` is batch one's list and stays as a history flag. Its `predict_why` reads "Neither side holds a positional edge in open_field, so side A wins on numbers", which states a prediction and no test. Round two is therefore retired as a measuring instrument (H-025, ruling of 2026-11-02). Round three layouts are his drawn layouts and each `predict_why` quotes his number (L-0073: "Westley's number for this layout: blue wins 75% of the time."), so they have an idea and stay.

The rule this story enforces is recorded in the lab README section 2 ("Every layout measures something") and in DEC-layout-purpose-2026-11-02.

## Change (the Architect's draft; Westley's call on the exact validation strictness in part 3)

Six parts, all in the lab harness.

1. The layout file gains a required key `purpose` (string; what the layout tests and what each result would mean; one or two sentences), written to the TOML and the JSON beside `predict_why`. An authored situation carries it as the `Measures` line of its SIT file (`situations/TEMPLATE.md`); the situations converter (H-040 part 5) copies that line into `purpose`, and a SIT file without `Measures` waits unconverted. SIT-0101 to SIT-0108 get a `Measures` line drafted by the builder from their `What to expect` line, which the Architect reads.
2. The producer takes every layout from a test plan table in `produce.py`: one row per layout with kind, role mix or role pair NAMED, headcounts, the arc or choke where it applies, the prediction and the purpose. It never draws a role mix with `rng.choice`. `MIXES` is replaced by named mixes used only where a plan row names them. The numbers-only control row has the same mix on both sides at its stated ratio.
3. `check_layout` gains rule R7 in `rules.toml`: a layout whose `purpose` is not a string, is shorter than 40 characters (DRAFT-purpose-min-chars, the Architect's number; Westley rules) or contains the word "random" or "default" is refused. `check_layout` returns `R7` and the producer prints `REJECT <id> R7 <detail>` with the layout id. The check is crude on purpose: it stops an empty or evasive purpose and judges nothing about whether the idea is good; that stays his eye.
4. The review page prints the purpose under each picture's title, so he reads what a layout tests before judging it. The layout shot's legend, which the fight replay reuses (H-040), prints it too (see Open).
5. Backfill: round three layouts get a `purpose` from their `predict_why` and his drawn-layout notes (the builder drafts, the Architect reads). Round two layouts are marked retired and are not regenerated; how the mark is written is the builder's call, one line shown to the Architect, and R7 does not apply to a retired layout.
6. The layout TOMLs and JSONs are regenerated once for the new key. His verdicts carry over by id.

Open for Westley: how strict R7 is (the 40 characters and the two words are the Architect's draft); and whether the legend line stays in this story. The legend is drawn by `scenes/proto/layout_shot/layout_shot.gd`, a client file, so keeping it makes part 4 a client change with the play proof; splitting it to a small client follow-up keeps this story harness only.

## Tests

`tests/skilllab/test_layout_purpose.py` (new; a different Sonnet writes it, red on main).

1. A layout without `purpose` is refused by `check_layout` (`R7`) and the producer's REJECT line names its id; a layout with a valid purpose passes.
2. Positive control: the pre-H-076 L-0048 (its content held as a fixture in the test) is refused with R7; a purpose under 40 characters, or one containing "random" or "default", is refused too.
3. The producer never calls `rng.choice` on a mix: a source check on `produce.py`, and a run in which two seeds give the same composition for the same plan row.
4. A numbers-only plan row has the same mix on both sides, with the stated headcount ratio.
5. The review page prints each layout's purpose under its title.
