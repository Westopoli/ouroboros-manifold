## Followed

- **One order per side, at tick 0, every unit.** The runner issues exactly one attack-move to each attacking side and none after it (`run.py` lines 210 to 219; `groups.py` line 44). Round 5 added none. The Architect's proposal of 2026-11-18 to place the order again when a fight falls quiet was never built and is withdrawn.
- **The same spot, or the holder's centre of mass.** `both_same_point` sends both sides to the midpoint of their starting centroids (a draft choice of spot); `hold:A` or `hold:B` sends the attacker to the holder's centre of mass.
- **Formation.** Units ordered together keep their shape to the point (his Halo Wars model, 2026-09-16; "hold formation until shooting", 2026-10-03). So "the same location" is one click point, and each unit goes to its own slot around it, up to 450 wu away.
- **The trap** is built to his 2026-11-06 description: red starts inside the walls and attack-moves out to the point between the three blue groups; the three blue groups hold, then each gets its one attack-move onto red's centre of mass when the trap springs.
- **The leash** is the unit's vision range (or its weapon range where that is longer). **Hill layouts** carry their attack-move point past the ramp's midline. **Fire it cannot answer** does not stop a unit on its way.

## Drifted

1. **Units no longer go for what they see (Round 5, 2026-11-17; the Architect's reading).** His words of 2026-10-20 and 2026-11-02 say an idle or attack-moving unit attacks what it sees. His words of 2026-11-11 say the acquisition range is the attack radius. The Architect put the two to him as one rule in conflict, he answered that attack radius wins, and Round 5 then removed walking toward a seen enemy altogether: a unit now ignores an enemy it sees until it is within weapon range plus a tenth. If "acquisition" meant which target a unit locks and shoots, and not whether it walks toward what it sees, the ruling was over-applied. This is the change that makes his no-draw rule false: units arrive in formation, in slots farther apart than weapon range, and stand. The same stand-off was measured on 2026-10-13 (two groups 303 to 336 wu apart, range 300) and on 2026-11-01 (the stage 1 fight), each time while units engaged by range and not by sight, and each time he answered with a rule that units go for what they see.
2. **The test that guarded his no-draw rule was rewritten to accept a stand-off (Round 5; the Architect's instruction).** `tests/csim/test_E11_S087_engage_on_sight.py`, two 16-unit groups attack-moved to one point: it asserted a fight to elimination since 2026-11-02 and now asserts that survivors standing 497 wu apart are correct.
3. **An attack-move is sometimes not resumed after a kill (an engine defect).** About 3 percent of the time a unit loses its order within four ticks of its target dying and stands for good. Against his words of 2026-10-02 and 2026-11-02. Being traced.
4. **An attack-move can end short of its point by rule (the Architect's exceptions of 2026-10-19).** A grouped unit held within 75 wu of its slot for 2 s gives up, and a unit within about 22 wu of its slot counts as arrived. Against "not finished until the unit reaches its point"; logged then, not his words. A unit ordered alone has neither: it presses on for ever.
5. **His ten-second flag has a second condition and is not always brought to him.** The runner ends a quiet fight only if, as well as no shot for 10 s, no unit moved more than 5 wu; a single pacing unit keeps a dead fight running to the cap. Stalls in the stage pages are flagged; the stalls of the wider sweep were counted and not brought to him one by one, and the Round 5 report called them the rule working as written.
6. **The trap springs early.** It releases when the first red unit comes within 300 wu of the centre point (a draft radius): at that tick red's centre is 426 wu short and one of 32 red units is inside the ring. His words: "right when red enters the center point".
7. **The sweep ran layouts he had denied or never ruled on** (59 of its 67), and no test re-runs the fights he approved: the stage 1 fight went from a win to a stand-off in Round 5 and nothing turned red.

## What the signal says

The single click is doing its job: with every order placed as he asked, the stage 1 fight ends with 13 against 20 standing out of each other's weapon range, so something in unit behaviour is wrong. Nothing in the placement of the orders has drifted except the trap's early spring.
