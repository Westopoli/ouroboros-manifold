# Economy readiness: can E-22 be built as the beta staircase reaches it? (2026-11-08)

Requested by Westley on 2026-11-08: before the beta staircase reaches the economy stages ("B9 and after" in `docs/northstar/story_reviews/westley/beta-staircase.md`), check that every economy rule is defined from the player's view well enough to build, and that no architectural question is left that would make the build slow or fragile. His example of the failure to avoid: targeting once re-checked nearly every unit nearly every tick. Three read-only investigations ran against main `dc9829d6` (the docs and flags, the C simulation, the client and the cross-epic hooks). Nothing in the simulation or the client was edited; the documents this report names in section 6 were.

The rulings Westley must give are in `docs/northstar/story_reviews/westley/economy_rulings_2026-11-08.md`. The Architect's decisions are rows in `docs/northstar/story_reviews/westley/decisions.md` dated 2026-11-08 with `econ` in the id.

## 1. Verdict

The economy's shape is ruled and consistent across eleven epics. It is not buildable yet, for three reasons, in order of weight:

1. Nine rule questions have no answer that a test could pin (section 3). Six of them were never flagged; they are flagged now.
2. Nine numeric questions were closed on 2026-10-21 as "named draft, Westley tunes it by feel", and no draft value was ever written (section 3, item 9). Tests are the spec and Westley owns every threshold, so no economy test can be written until a table of draft values exists and he has seen it.
3. Six architectural traps in the simulation and the pipe would turn a literal reading of the rules into the targeting problem again (section 4). Each has a decision now; four need their own engine or contract story before the stage that depends on them.

The staircase row "B9 and after" is one sentence. Section 5 proposes eight stages with a "done when" he can judge from his seat, and two pre-stage landings.

## 2. What is solid

The rules decided on 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-21 cover the whole system: three resources per era (Food, Wood, Coin; Metal, Water, Uranium) with a 75/25 common-to-rare split; literal, killable workers that walk to a source and back to a drop-off and bring in less the farther they walk; one population cap raised by housing to thousands; production buildings focused on one family with a timeline per building; each role costing two of the three resources in a fixed pattern; base kinds per mission archetype; bases occupied, never destroyed, with fall-back to an expansion and a partial-failure ceiling after the original main base is lost; four doctrines (boom, fortify, press, balanced) picked in the lobby and switched live; rules with perfect hands and no foresight, except the counter shift from what the side has seen; one dial plus three pointing requests for the solo commander; shipments and caravans as blind events on the map; raid lanes from the generator, walls, gates and towers; the enemy on the same rules with foresight set by difficulty; nothing carries forward in the beta.

The cross-epic hooks exist: screens (E-16 S-230, S-231, S-233, S-235), settlements, places and lots (E-17 S-166, S-191, S-193), enemy macro (E-14 S-215, S-216), outcomes and base loss (E-10 S-210, S-212), seat commands over the wire (E-19 S-242), non-combat units (E-15 S-129), the record of enemy types seen (E-12 S-206), drawing (E-18 S-237, S-238).

The simulation has a settlement foundation: a density field grown from one core (`csrc/economy_density.c`, every 60 ticks), a lot table with five stages, rubble and rebuild after 200 ticks (`csrc/economy_lots.c`, `csrc/economy_waves.c`), player placement with a 5x5-cell footprint (`csrc/economy_place.c`), strongpoints, a doctrine enum and a wave log, all behind `om_economy_step`, which runs at the head of every tactical tick (`csrc/unit.c:2412`). The pipe already reserves `economy:{doctrine, resources:{}}` on every tactical frame and sends `lots_changed` on every delta (`docs/architecture.md`, tactical frame). The client draws lots (`scenes/settlement/settlement_layer.gd`) and its ticker has a `TickerBeats` hook (`scenes/tactical_c/hud/events_feed.gd`). Reusable engine pieces: `om_unit_spawn_non_combat`, the four-cohort targeting throttle (`csrc/unit.c:2242`), the BFS field pool with its terrain-epoch dirty flag (`csrc/tactical_move.c:1411-1420`), and the budgeted scale suite (`tests/csim/scale/`).

## 3. Rule gaps (Westley rules)

Each is a rule meaning the player would see, or a number he owns. The player-view sheet for him is `economy_rulings_2026-11-08.md`; this section carries the ids.

1. **The dial's form.** Q-180-01, open since 2026-10-02, no text in its status cell. Three positions per axis, a slider, or a point in a triangle. S-180 and E-16 S-231 cannot be built without it.
2. **Who builds.** Q-168-03 asked "construction time, who builds, cost" and was closed as a numeric named draft, but "who builds" is a mechanism: workers leave gathering to raise a building, or buildings rise alone. New flag Q-168-06 on R-168-04.
3. **Which source gives which resource.** R-167-01 says each resource is "gathered in its own way" and names no way. R-167-05 says natural sources are fast and finite and built ones slow and permanent, without saying which resources have which. R-175-02 says a main base's sources "never run dry but yield less over time" without saying whether that is per-source depletion or a base-wide decay. New flag Q-167-04.
4. **Technology in the beta economy.** Q-159-12 (what a level does), Q-173-01 and Q-173-02 are parked post-beta, yet R-173-05, R-177-08 and R-180-01 make technology one of the dial's three axes. Either a level does something in the beta, or technology is out of the beta economy and the dial has two axes. New flag Q-173-03.
5. **Scope of the seat in the beta.** The staircase row says "the economy seat's orders". The paired seat (placement, timelines, technology choice, disguise) is E-19 multiplayer; the solo commander's hand (dial, doctrine, pointing, switches) is the single-commander game. Confirm the beta's economy stages are the solo hand only. Asked on the sheet; no flag, since the beta's scope is his.
6. **"Uncontested" and "occupied".** R-175-01: a settlement held "uncontested for a length of time (for now)". R-178-02: a base is occupied when "enemy forces hold its central zone for a time and its main building is destroyed or captured, as the archetype sets". None of "uncontested", "central zone", "a time", or the per-archetype choice is defined. New flags Q-175-05 and Q-178-06, each with a proposed reading (no enemy combat unit within one radius of the main building for one duration; one archetype column for destroy or capture).
7. **Neutral villagers join the winner.** R-177-09: "the neutral villagers become part of the winning team". Whether they become the winner's workers (and count against its cap) or only the settlement's lots and yields is a different base to look at. New flag Q-177-08.
8. **Shipment visibility.** Two of his answers conflict. 2026-10-02 (Q-238-02): "your side can't see its own shipment on the way, no vision at all". 2026-10-06 (Q-176-04, option c): visible to both sides, no vision for its own side, no ticker line when killed. E-22 R-176-02 and R-176-05 follow the later answer; the E-22 Invariants line, E-18 R-238-02 and E-16 R-235-04 follow the earlier. New flag Q-176-05; the stale lines are rewritten the day he confirms which stands. E-12 has no shipment rule at all; the shipment's non-combat behaviour is E-15 R-129-06.

Each register row says "the lane asks with a shot when it reaches the item (ROADMAP section 3)"; section 3 was removed on 2026-11-03. Nothing in `docs/` or `csrc/` holds a value for any of them. The proposal, on the sheet: the Architect writes one `DRAFT-econ-*` table anchored on the pacing he has already set (missions run 30 to 60 minutes, Q-076-01; the starting force is about a fifth of what a full mission fields and production pays for the rest, hub "The commander"), he approves or amends the table once, and tunes by feel in the stage he plays. ## 4. Architectural traps and the decisions (Architect decides, Westley may veto)

Numbers below are from the code as read; nothing was compiled or timed.

1. **Workers count against the tick-rate drop.** E-13 R-099-03 drops the tick rate from 20 to 10 per second above 1,000 live units, and R-209-02 counts workers in that figure. Workers spawned as ordinary units enter the spatial grid (`csrc/unit.c:686`), the targeting pass, the movement pass (`om_move_tick_unit` for every id), the fog pair checks and the pipe delta. Two bases with 60 to 100 workers each spend a fifth of the budget before a fight begins. Decision DEC-econ-workers-cheap-2026-11-08: workers are non-combat units that the targeting pass skips; each worker's task logic runs on a timer wheel (a `due_tick` per worker; the step touches only the workers due this tick); a worker walks a route shared per (source, drop-off) pair, cached outside the 16-field movement pool (`OM_MOVE_FIELD_POOL`), so the pool is not thrashed by shuttle traffic.
2. **Event-driven economy.** Gathering, construction, production, shipments, caravans, promotion timers and the doctrine rules all advance by scheduled events in tick buckets, never by scanning every actor every tick. The doctrine rules evaluate about once per second (every 20 ticks), consistent with the existing 20-tick lot step and 60-tick density step. Decision DEC-econ-event-driven-2026-11-08. This is the direct answer to the targeting lesson.
3. **Walls invalidate every route.** Any bump of `terrain_epoch` (`csrc/tactical_move.c:643`, `:3015`) stales all kept fields and the walk-route cache, and every unit replans; one BFS fill is up to 280x280 = 78,400 cells per distinct destination. A rules AI placing buildings and walls freely would stall movement. Decision DEC-econ-route-invalidation-2026-11-08: a building footprint never bumps the epoch (buildings are density, R-193-08; only walls and gates block cells, R-174-05); blocking changes are batched and applied at most once per 20 ticks; invalidation becomes regional (a field is stale only when the changed cells' bounding box touches it) as its own engine story, landed before the stage with walls.
4. **Pipe cost of workers.** Every moved unit is serialised in each delta (`csrc/pipe/pipe_tactical.c:299`); the pipe budget is one round trip under 100 ms at 5,000 units. Decision DEC-econ-worker-routes-on-wire-2026-11-08: a worker's route and departure tick are sent once and the client interpolates; a worker's position appears in a delta only on task change, damage or death. A contract change.
5. **Contract first.** `om_economy_place(w, building, x, y)` has no faction argument (`csrc/ouroboros_sim.h:1524-1535`), so only the player can place; the enemy economy needs one. New frame keys: `resources` (reserved, additive), population and cap, construction progress, production focus and queue, shipments and caravans, worker routes, dial and doctrine echo, two new wave kinds (base occupied, fall-back). New saved fields bump `OM_SAVE_FORMAT_VERSION` (13 today) and extend `csrc/checksum_full.c`. Decision DEC-econ-contract-story-2026-11-08: one contract story with one ABI bump lands alone before B9; consumers build after it (CLAUDE.md, Architecture rules).
6. **One settlement core.** `economy_density.c` grows one settlement from `core_cell`, the objective's centre, read once at mission start. The game needs several cores with an owner and growth state each: both sides' bases, neutral settlements grown to a seeded age (E-17 R-191-06), expansions, forward works. Decision DEC-econ-multi-core-2026-11-08: the density step becomes multi-core in the places lane (ROADMAP "Land generator, part 2", S-191), which is a pre-stage dependency of B9.
7. **The lot cap is reachable.** A 28,000 wu canvas at 100 wu cells is 78,400 cells; density at or above 0.10 over five percent of the map is about 3,900 lots, above `OM_LOT_CAP` 2048 (`csrc/om_internal.h:80`), and `om_economy_place` refuses with `E1E_FULL` at the cap. R-168-02 calls the cap a memory limit no mission reaches; with several settlements that is unproven. `lot_hit_tick` is keyed by array index and `om_lots_remove_row` shifts indices, so per-lot state is fragile. Decision DEC-econ-lot-cap-2026-11-08: the contract story gives lots stable ids and raises the cap to 8192, and a scale test grows every settlement on the largest layout and asserts the count under the cap.
8. **The counter shift reads a record that does not exist in code.** E-12 S-206 already rules a per-side record of enemy types seen (R-206-03, R-206-04), read by S-179 to S-181. Fog in code is pair-wise (`csrc/fog.c:486`) with no memory. Decision DEC-econ-seen-record-2026-11-08: the record is a per-side tally updated from the existing visibility pass and decayed on a timer, built in the vision stage (B6) or the contract story, never recomputed by extra fog queries.
9. **Determinism.** Stockpiles, yields and costs are integers; timers are ticks; the economy draws no world RNG (the density field already does not); every economy value is in the trace and the checksum. Decision DEC-econ-integer-state-2026-11-08.
10. **Budgets as tests.** `tests/csim/scale/` gains: tick cost with 400 workers and 2,000 lots on two bases inside the existing 100 ms round trip at 5,000 units; worker route cache hits above a threshold; a wall storm (20 segments in 20 ticks) keeps movement under its 3 ms budget at 1,000 units. Decision DEC-econ-scale-tests-2026-11-08. Thresholds are Westley's and appear on the sheet as drafts.

## 7. Ways this report could be wrong

- The lot-count estimate (3,900) assumes five percent of the canvas at density 0.10 or more; the real density footprint of several settlements has not been measured. The scale test settles it.
- The worker cost estimate counts workers as full units; if the places lane or the budgets ruling for thousands (E-18 S-240, Q-240-01) changes what counts as a unit, item 1 of section 4 shrinks.
- Regional route invalidation is the costliest decision here; if walls are rare in play (a handful per mission), batching alone may be enough, and the engine story can be deferred to a finding in B15.
- The eight stages assume the solo hand is the beta's scope; if Westley wants the paired seat in the beta, B13 splits and a seat-screen stage follows B12.
