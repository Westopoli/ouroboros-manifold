# S-103 opening frame, ground era: what it shows, where decisions and frame diverge, the classic RTS camera, what could be done

Written 2026-10-21 for Westley by an Engineer (Sonnet 5.5). Analysis only: no code was changed and the game was not run.

Your ask (Batch A, point 5, 2026-10-18), on `A-S103-start-view-ground-era.png` (original `docs/northstar/story_reviews/shots/20261013-1625-c79d141/S-103_start_view_ground.png`, build c79d141, 1920 by 1080, ground era): no home base in sight; the map ends near the middle of the screen and the top half is empty background; the camera is too low by about 15 degrees, so find the classic RTS angle and swap to it; check the decisions against that feedback; find out why the home base was meant to sit near the starting army and why it did not here. I measured the picture and its state file (`S-103_start_view_ground.json`) and read the code at origin/main 600b1831. "wu" is world units; one terrain cell is 100 wu.

**In short.** When the mission opens you stand at the very top edge of a 24,000 wu map, looking up at that edge. Your army is in the top strip, in four clumps. The objective, about 4,000 wu behind the army, sits just under the bottom of the screen. At the place the army calls home there is nothing to draw: the game has no base object there, and the town lots belong to nobody and sit at the objective instead. The camera lens is very wide (75 degrees vertical), so the top row of the screen looks only 12.5 degrees below horizontal; the far edge of the map, the one you are standing beside, lands about mid-screen and the clear colour fills what is above it. The lens is not the one you approved either: the landscape mockup ran a 55 degree lens and the in-game rig, ported from it, never set one and so runs Godot's default 75. These are separate causes with separate fixes, and the centre pitch is the smallest of them. Two readings of this frame in circulation do not hold against the shot: the canvas is 24,000 wu, not 12,000 (it varies by archetype), and the screen never shows the horizon, it shows the map's edge, so the audit's "sky" is background above the map.

## 1. What the frame shows and why

**How I read it.** The state file gives the camera: pan target (13600, 2800) on a canvas of 24000 (240 cells of 100 wu), height 3000 above the ground under the target (3050 wu high there), pitch 50, Godot's default vertical field of view of 75. I rebuilt that camera from `camera_rig_3d.gd` and projected all 330 own units; the result matches the file's `units_px` to 0.3 px RMS, so the numbers below are measurements of this frame, not guesses. Two screen readings come from the picture itself: the bare background covers 34 percent of the pixels, the terrain's top edge averages row 372 of 1080 (34 percent of the height), and sits at row 527 (49 percent) along the left edge, where the map boundary line is drawn.

**No home base in sight.** Three causes stack.
- There is no base object to see. A mission's home is only a spawn anchor: the player's is requested at (canvas/2, 15), the middle of the north edge (`csrc/mission.c:2749`), snapped to the nearest walkable cell (`:2772`), and stored as a bare spawn-point map node (`:2783`, node kind 7). Nothing draws it and `qa_state` does not report map nodes. Your rule R-246-07 says the army stands "where its first economy area and structures stand"; no code puts an economy area or structure there. The four settlement lots in the state file are unowned (owner -1) at (14250 to 14450, 6850 to 6950) because the settlement field is seeded at the mission objective (`csrc/economy_density.c:153`, Q-193-07 (a), marked interim; on 2026-10-13 you ruled that the layout engine decides where towns and bases go, nothing hard coded, `ambiguity_register.md:499`), and a lot gets an owner only when a building is placed (`csrc/economy_place.c:47`). The bases the layout engine is meant to place are lane P, not built; the plan says "the home base itself waits on lane P" (`plans/2026-10-19-enforcement-and-redirect.md:45`).
- The camera never got A18's framing in 3D. A18 frames the player's units, spawn point and owned lots with a tenth of the viewport as margin (`visible-game-spec.md:390`), but the A18 builder's brief cut it to the 2D path (Architect gap ruling g5, 2026-10-12, recorded as ledger row FU-A18-3d, `push_ledger_2026-09-22.md:1070`; the spec paragraph does not say "2D only", decision-to-spec audit row 1). The ground path calls `_force_camera(false)` (`scenes/tactical_c/tactical_view.gd:371-372`), which centres on the box of own units only, no spawn term, no lot term, and the rig has no height setter, so nothing can zoom to fit. The pan target (13600, 2800) is the centre of the box of the four groups (x 10520 to 16680, y 920 to 4680).
- Nothing could have failed. The ground variant of the S-103 play asserts that every own unit is on screen and that the units' own box keeps the margin; its spawn clause uses the unit centroid as a stand-in because the map nodes are not in `qa_state` (`tests/qa/desktop/plays/A/test_S_103_start_view.py` docstring, tests 2 and 3). The audit's "base projects to y 1751" is the objective: `objects_px` is built from the mission objectives (`ground3d.gd` `objects_px`). Solving the projection backwards puts it near (14400, 6960), about 4,000 wu south of the army and 670 px under the bottom edge, in front of the camera but out of frame.

**The map ends mid-screen and the top is empty.** The horizon is not what ends the map; the map's north edge is. The top row of the screen looks 12.5 degrees below horizontal (pitch 50 minus half of 75), so no ray ever reaches the horizon. What happens is that the army stands at the north edge by R-246-07, the box centre is only 2,800 wu from it, and the camera looks north (`camera_rig_3d.gd:192-193` puts the camera south of the target looking toward -y; the rig has no yaw). The ground mesh stops at the canvas edge with no skirt, sky or haze, so the 3D viewport's clear colour shows (`ground3d.gd:16`, 0.55, 0.68, 0.82), darkened by the fog overlay to the slate the picture shows (63, 78, 94; the same colour is pale blue inside the sight discs). The red line across the picture at row 523 is the 2D map layer's boundary polyline (`scenes/tactical_c/map_layer.gd:8,72`); my camera model puts the north edge at height 0 at row 524, so it is the map's end. The two diagonal red lines are its east and west sides running to corners behind the camera, which `unproject_position` mirrors. The blue shape at (760, 235) I did not trace. Where terrain rises toward the edge (middle and right) the ground reaches rows 190 to 450, which is why the edge looks ragged. So your reading holds at the left (49 percent of the height) and is "top third" overall.
Three things make it worse. The lens: 75 degrees vertical is 107.5 degrees horizontal at 16:9 (Godot docs), so ground depth is spread hard: on flat ground the top row sees ground 13,500 wu from the camera and a unit at the top row is drawn 4.6 times smaller than one at the bottom row. On the mockup's 55 degree lens the same pitch would see ground 7,200 wu out and the ratio would be 2.6. The boundary rule: R-103-02 says the canvas edges bound the camera and it never leaves the map; the Architect's 2026-10-13 ruling reads it as the camera centre inside 0 to canvas, and the rig clamps the pan target to minus 15 percent to plus 115 percent of the canvas (`camera_rig_3d.gd:62,119,124`), so the code allows exactly this view. The orientation: for a player whose edge is the north one, "up" on screen is the way off the map and the objective and the enemy are under the frame.

**The camera is too low.** The 50 degrees is a ported constant, not a decision. The landscape mockup's rig (commit aa90a1ae, 2026-09-21) took it from the P2 brief, "about 50 degrees ... copy the feel from tactical.gd", whose fixed pitch was 65 degrees (`plans/briefs/2026-09-21-p2-landscape-mockup.md:26`); the P6 layers mockup runs 60 (`briefs/2026-09-22-p6-layers-mockup.md:62`); the ground rig copied 50 (`camera_rig_3d.gd:17`). Your words on 2026-09-22: "present is classic RTS, I'm not sure if that's 45 degrees", recorded as "classic RTS angle; the pitch number is not a decision" (`research/options/camera/brief.md`, section 8). The lens was lost in the port. The landscape mockup you approved (pass 6b) sets its camera to a 55 degree vertical lens in the scene file (`scenes/mockups/landscape/landscape_mockup.tscn:42`; the layers, objectives, hud and fog mockups also use 55), so the approved landscape look was pitch 50 on 55. The in-game rig builds its camera in code and copied `near` and `far` but not `fov` (`camera_rig_3d.gd:39-40,72`, commit 169a1a04, 2026-10-08), so it runs Godot's default 75. That moves the top row of the screen from 22.5 degrees below horizontal to 12.5.

**Four groups.** No rule makes four. R-246-08 says three (one large, two small, on the way to the objective) and is built by VG-A-startforce, whose three pieces are queued. At the shot's build, and on main today, the ground army is one grid: `om_mission_spawn_force` fills the walkable cells nearest the anchor first, nine slots per cell, skipping slope cells and other bands (`csrc/mission.c:2462-2464`, from commit 75b15ca8, 2026-10-06). On this mountainous seed the 330 units came out as four terrain fragments of that one grid: 181, 86, 36 and 27 units at about (16500, 1500), (12600, 1000), (10550, 4200) and (14900, 4600), up to about 5,400 wu from the requested anchor (12000, 15). They are not designed groups, and they are not on the line to the objective.

**Why the home base was meant to be near the army, and why that did not happen here.** On 2026-10-09 you answered the lane D spawn question: "Army should spawn right near the home base. Right near the player's first economy area." (architect log step 37, `retro_2026-10-10/architect_log_2026-10-07_to_10.md:113`). The tests written right after it measured the old space-era spawn at 7,490 and 17,537 wu from the two anchors on seed 1, with the armies 1,160 wu apart (step 39, `:109`): the armies were not at their bases at all. The answer became R-246-07 (`E-17:397`): each army in a cleared pocket at its home base, "so an approach march separates the armies as open space did". On 2026-10-12 you extended it to every era ("Armies start near the home base of that specific player", Q-A17-01, `ambiguity_register.md:523`) and the same evening asked for three separated groups within 30 seconds of the home base (R-246-08). The spawn half of that is built: the army stands at its anchor. The base half is not: nothing exists at the anchor to stand beside, so "near the home base" is true and invisible. Separately, on 2026-10-03 you ruled the player's home base "extensive and impressive" for the compact archetypes (R-188-06, `E-17:232`), which is the opposite of what the frame shows.

I looked for pitch from horizontal, projection and vertical field of view. **What "15 degrees more eagle-eyed" is against this.** 50 plus 15 is 65. That is 9 degrees steeper than StarCraft II and Warcraft III (56), 18 to 20 steeper than Age of Empires III (45 to 47), and the same as this project's own old camera (65). Of the games I could source, only Supreme Commander is steeper, and that is a player's report. So your two instructions, "swap to the classic angle" and "about 15 degrees more", point 9 to 20 degrees apart: the classic perspective RTS camera is 45 to 56, not 65. Which one you meant is yours to say.

The lens matters more than the centre pitch for the "eagle-eyed" look. What the top of the screen sees is the pitch minus half the field of view: 12.5 degrees now, 22.5 on the approved mockup's lens (the same 50 degrees at 55), 42.1 in StarCraft II (56 minus 13.9). That, not the centre pitch, is what makes ours read as a low, wide-angle camera. Restoring the approved lens alone buys 10 of the 15 degrees you asked for at the top of the frame. To get StarCraft II's 42 at the top on a 75 degree lens you would need a pitch near 80.

## 4. What could be done

Options only; every number is a candidate and yours.

**(a) Pitch to the researched value, and the 3D rig frames the home with A18's margin (the queued `VG-A18-3d`).** Sets: `PITCH_LOW_DEG` 50 to 56 (StarCraft II, Warcraft III) or 65 (your +15, and the old camera); the fit: the box of own units, the spawn point and owned lots, margin 0.1 of the viewport (`DRAFT-a18-margin`), a rig height of about 3,400 to 4,050 on the current lens (4,450 to 5,500 on the approved 55 lens), and the Home key restoring that height. Costs: the rig needs a height setter and a fit function it does not have; the call site `tactical_view.gd:371-375` must pass the anchors and the height, and the piece row owns only `camera_rig_3d.gd` and its test, while `scenes/tactical_c/**` belongs to VG-A and three queued VG-A pieces also edit that file. The spawn point is a map node `qa_state` does not expose, so the play cannot yet assert it. The pitch is shared: `scenes/space3d/space_view.gd:11` uses the same rig, `DRAFT-space-cam-height` 5,400 was derived from pitch 50 and lens 75 (`2026-10-13-d-view-spec.md:12`, `tests/test_vg_d_view.gd:1149`), and the queued sight-disc test pins "sin(50) = 0.766 within 0.05" (`visible-game-spec.md:405`; 0.829 at 56, 0.906 at 65). On its own this does not make a base appear: it frames the army and an empty anchor, and the edge stays at row 227 to 327 facing north.

**(b) Also restore or narrow the lens, and raise the default height, so the map fills the frame.** Sets: `Camera3D.fov` 75 to 55 (the lens you approved, so the first candidate: not a new look), or narrower (45, or StarCraft II's 27.8); and `_height` (`camera_rig_3d.gd:30`, 3000) to the fit height: 4,450 to 5,500 at 55, 5,950 to 6,700 at 45, 9,200 at 27.8. Costs: it moves every unit size and sight disc on screen and the D-VIEW numbers above (they pin 75), and every 3D shot taken so far was taken at 75. At 55 the fit height stays inside the tactical band (under `TACTICAL_MAX` 6000); at 45 and narrower it does not, which turns on the 2D terrain layer (`tactical_view.gd:406`) and starts easing the pitch toward 90, so the band thresholds and the pitch law would have to move with it. At 55 and pitch 65 the top of the frame looks 37.5 degrees below horizontal and units are 1.6 times, not 4.6 times, smaller at the top, which is within reach of how StarCraft II is built (42 degrees, 1.4 times).

**(c) The horizon rule, as a test the piece carries.** Rule: at the ground-era opening frame the top row of the screen and both top corners land on canvas ground, no background above the map's far edge, and bare background only on the near side within the A18 margin (10 percent, 108 px). The test is deterministic and needs no screenshot: `rig.pick_ground` at the top corners and top centre, each inside 0 to canvas (`pick_ground` marches rays over a height field clamped at the edge, so a miss returns a point outside the canvas, not an error). Costs: it is a gate on the other options, because it cannot pass with the base on the north edge and the camera looking north, for any pitch or lens in the table (edge row 198 to 327, that is 18 to 30 percent of the frame empty). It passes once the opening frame faces away from the home edge (edge row about 972 to 975, void only in the bottom 10 percent) or the anchor sits inboard by roughly half the frame's depth.

**Found on the way, not on your list.**
- Heading. The rig has no yaw and R-103-07's flip key (a 180 degree turn about the target) is not built in 3D. A camera that faces the objective from the opening frame would put the edge at the bottom (row 971 to 975, void only in the bottom 10 percent) and the objective inside the frame for the 75 and 55 degree lens candidates (rows 76 to 204; on the top edge at 65 on 55), while the narrower lenses push it above the top. This is the lever that decides where the void goes, and it is a design choice, not a number I should set.
- A drawn home. Before lane P builds a real base, "home base in sight" needs something at the anchor: a marker, a ring, a label. The plan row mentions "the spawn marker answers" (`enforcement-and-redirect.md:45`); what it should look like is yours.
- R-103-02. Either "never leaves the map" means the view (then the rig's plus or minus 15 percent overscroll and the 2026-10-13 ruling go) or it means the centre (then this frame is allowed). The rule text says the first.

## 5. Limits and one filename note

The flat-ground table ignores relief, so rows are indicative; I did not rebuild the terrain for this seed or run Godot. The shot was taken at c79d141; the spawn grid, `_force_camera(false)` and the rig constants it shows are all still on main, though main's spawn code now spawns `start_units` of the composed force (E7, commit d3bf1614, 2026-10-19), so today's opening frame has fewer units than the shot's 330. Halo Wars and Company of Heroes pitch and FOV were not findable with the search tools available; a measurement from a frame of each game would settle it. SC2 pitch direction and Warcraft III FOV are as stated in the table. The `VG-A18-3d` row in `pieces.toml` cites this report as `followups/s103_camera_report.md`; this page is `followups/03_s103_camera_report.md`.
