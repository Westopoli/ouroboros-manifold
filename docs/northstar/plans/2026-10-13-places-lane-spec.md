## Rev 5 direction: layout first, terrain obeys (Westley, 2026-10-15; Q-191-14 closed (b))

Westley's two rulings, read together: on 2026-10-03 (amended under Q-191-04, E-17 R-191-10) "the landscape engine should take the inputs from the layout engine and lean on them ... if the layout engine calls for something like a choke, the landscape engine is required to produce it"; on 2026-10-13 "the town is supposed to follow the biome engine in its stochasticity (remember it's a *sister* system)" and "let the economy system (layout engine) decide where those go, nothing hard coded"; on 2026-10-15 "layer first, terrain obeys, I've already ruled on that." So the order of a ground mission becomes:

1. **Requests** (the layout engine, before any terrain): from the seed, canvas, archetype and region, the engine fixes the spine: the two bases with their kinds (R-177-02), the objective, the two resource sites at equal distance (R-191-04), and the plays the layout must offer (R-192-04: a choke with its width, a slope top, tall cover) as positioned requests. Nothing in this step reads the terrain, because there is none yet.
2. **Terrain obeys**: terrain generation takes the request list as an input and is required to produce each request at its zone (a choke of the stated width, a vantage, flat walkable ground at a base, water near a resource site), and to leave at least two walking routes to every strategic point, one of small-army width and one of big-army width, with a seeded roll above the minimum (R-189-07; widths and count are playtesting drafts). The map check verifies every request on the finished terrain and ships with a positive control.
3. **Towns settle**: on the produced terrain the neutral settlements are found by P1's scoring exactly as specced (water, flatness, noise), with ages and satellites; spawns are the requested base positions, no longer the canvas edge.

Algorithm; the canvas is read as a grid of side/100 cells, `h[cell]` = `height_at` at the cell centre, `water[cell]` = `h < 0`, `slope[cell]` as `om_layout_slope` on that grid (an edge cell takes its inner neighbour, as `om_layout_slope` steps inward); the heights are read once (57,600 calls at most), then every score reads the cached grid.
1. **Objective**: kind 0, `side` -1, at (objective_x, objective_y).
2. **Bases**: player then enemy, at the given positions, `side` = base_side[i]. The kind is the E-22 R-177-02 table, verbatim, a rule not a draft (archetype ids `mission.c:161-170`; "home" rows are BASE_HOME, camps BASE_FORWARD): 0 Defensive Stand: player HOME, enemy FORWARD; 1 Siege Breaking: FORWARD (relief camp), FORWARD (siege camp); 2 Reconquest: FORWARD, HOME (the city being taken); 3 Assault: FORWARD, HOME; 4 Suppression: HOME (garrison in a city), FORWARD (hidden cells); 5 Multi-Front Defense: HOME, FORWARD; 6 Interception: FORWARD (field camp), FORWARD (staging camp); 7 Evacuation: HOME (the place being left), FORWARD (pursuing base); 8 Containment: FORWARD, FORWARD; 9 Counterinsurgency: HOME (garrison in a city), FORWARD (hidden cells). HOME bases get `age_steps` = `DRAFT-home-age` 40; FORWARD bases 0.
3. **The score** `s` of a candidate (cells whose indices are 1 mod 4, so a 400 wu grid, `DRAFT-places-grid`; a candidate is valid when its cell is not water and `walkable_at` is true at its centre): `s = 0.45·water_within + 0.30·flatness + 0.25·noise01` (`DRAFT-places-weights`). `water_within = min(1, n/12)`, n = water cells whose centre is within 600 wu (`DRAFT-places-water-radius`; 12 = `DRAFT-places-water-sat`). `flatness` = share of cells within 300 wu (`DRAFT-places-flat-radius`) whose slope is under 0.06 (`DRAFT-places-flat-slope`). `noise01 = (om_tn_fbm2(seed + 410, x_q16, y_q16, 3, 32768) + 65536) / 131072` with `x_q16 = (int32)((int64)x_wu · 65536 / 1600)` (one lattice unit = 1,600 wu, `DRAFT-places-noise-lattice`; octaves 3, gain 0.5: `DRAFT-places-noise-octaves`). R-193-01's rivers, coasts and flats drive it; there is no river mask on main, so water within 600 wu stands in for a river or a coast.
4. **Resource-hosting towns first** (R-191-04, Q-191-03, R-177-06; "opposite the objective", Westley 2026-09-23, means one each side of the objective along the line level between the bases). Let `B0`, `B1` be the two bases, `d` the unit vector perpendicular to `B1-B0`, `O` the objective, `t(p) = (p-O)·d`, `D(p) = |p-B0| - |p-B1|` (the equidistance error). For each side σ in (-1, +1): the candidate set is every valid candidate with `σ·t(p) >= 2400` (`DRAFT-resource-clearance`) and `|D(p)| <= 600` (`DRAFT-resource-fairness`), at least 4000 wu (`DRAFT-places-spacing`) from both bases, and whose satellite point `q = p + σ·300·d` (`DRAFT-satellite-offset`) is inside the canvas, not water, walkable, with `|D(q)| <= 600`. Take the candidate with the greatest `s` (no floor: the player always has the means, R-177-06). It becomes a NEUTRAL_SETTLEMENT at p, and a RESOURCE_SITE at q. If `|B1-B0| < 1` or the set is empty for a side, that side gets none (the P1 fixture asserts both found; `DRAFT-resource-sites-min` 2).
5. **Other neutral settlements by score** ("nothing hard coded"): repeatedly take the valid candidate with the greatest `s` that is at least 4000 wu (`DRAFT-places-spacing`) from every base, the objective and every town chosen, while `s > 0.35` (`DRAFT-places-floor`), the settlements and HOME bases together number fewer than `DRAFT-towns-max` 6 [A2 8] and the place list has room (`n + 2 <= 64`). The land and the floor decide the count up to the maximum; the maximum keeps the lot quota and the pre-growth cost bounded.
6. **Ages**: a NEUTRAL_SETTLEMENT's `age_steps = 10 + (30·u_q16 + 32768) / 65536` (integer division), `u_q16 = (om_tn_fbm2(seed + 450, x_q16, y_q16, 3, 32768) + 65536) / 2` at the town's position: 10 to 40 (`DRAFT-town-age-min` 10, `DRAFT-town-age-max` 40: the front radius at pre-growth is 1,000 to 4,000 wu, and the lot quota bounds a town long before the front dies, about hop 195), so towns run from hamlet to town [A2 1].
7. **Satellites**: every settlement that hosts no resource site gets an EXPANSION_SITE at 300 wu, on the first of the four axis steps (E, S, W, N, starting at index `(u_q16 >> 14) & 3` of its age noise) that lands inside the canvas on walkable, non-water ground; none if none does. Satellites are exempt from the spacing rule.
8. **Richness** of a RESOURCE_SITE: `round(100·clamp(0.2 + 0.8·region_economy·(0.5 + n), 0, 1))`, n = `noise01` with `seed + 460` at the site (`DRAFT-richness-formula`; sometimes limited, sometimes abundant, scaled by the region's economy, R-177-06). Defender slant: when one base is BASE_HOME, the resource site nearer that base gets +10 (`DRAFT-defender-slant`), capped at 100. 0 for every other kind.
9. **Order** of `out->places`: objective, player base, enemy base, towns in the order chosen (resource-hosting towns first), then satellites in the order of their towns (a resource site directly follows its town's turn). Cap `OM_PLACE_CAP` 64; no sort, no `qsort`.

- `om_lot_noise_q16`: `f = om_tn_fbm2(seed + 420, gx·8192, gy·8192, 3, 32768)` (octaves 3, gain 32768 = 0.5, `DRAFT-lot-noise-octaves`; lattice coordinate = cell index × 8192, so one lattice unit is 8 cells = 800 wu, `DRAFT-lot-noise-lattice`); `v_q16 = clamp(((int64)(f - lo) * 65536) / (hi - lo), 0, 65536)`; result `45875 + ((v_q16 * 45875) >> 16)` (`DRAFT-lot-noise-band` 0.7..1.4). The distance to a cell is scaled by up to 40 percent either way, so the rim of the town follows `g`. `lo`, `hi` (`DRAFT-lot-noise-range`) are the min and max of `om_tn_fbm2` over 10,000 cells for each of the seeds 1 to 64 that the P2 test measures; the source pins them, and the test asserts `lo <= measured_min`, `measured_max <= hi`, and that each pin lies within `DRAFT-noise-range-slack` 0 to 0.05 of the span of the measured extreme (the slack is the pin's tightness, not a tolerance on `g`).
- `om_lane_build` replaces `gx%4==3 || gy%4==3` (`economy_density.c:106`; R-193-09, streets along contour lines and rivers). Per cell, at lane level L (0..3): **contour** lane when `floor(h/(150·2^L))` (`DRAFT-lane-contour` 150 wu, from the cell-centre height) differs between the cell and its east neighbour (gx+1) or its neighbour at gy+1; **water** lane when any of its eight neighbours is water (`om_layout_is_water`; no river mask exists, so water adjacency stands in); **fallback** lane (`DRAFT-lane-fallback`) when no contour or water lane cell lies within `DRAFT-lane-fallback-reach` 4 cells (Chebyshev): `floor_mod(gx + wx, 4·2^L) == 4·2^L - 1` or `floor_mod(gy + wy, 4·2^L) == 4·2^L - 1`, with the warp `wx = floor_div(3·fx + 32768, 65536)`, `fx = om_tn_fbm2(seed + 430, gx·8192, gy·8192, 2, 32768)`, `wy` the same with `seed + 440` (a smooth warp of up to +-3 cells, so the grid wanders with the ground). Level per cell = the level of its nearest core (Chebyshev; ties to the lower index). **Cap**: a core's level is the lowest L in 0..3 for which lane cells are at most 30 percent (`DRAFT-lane-share`) of the non-water cells in the 41 x 41 window around its core cell (`DRAFT-lane-window` 20 cells); each doubling doubles the contour interval and the fallback period together. The result depends on terrain, seed and core cells only, never on growth, so a save and a load rebuild the same lanes. Heights come through the adapter at cell centres.
- `om_growth_probe_run` is a copy of the hop loop, the per-core maturity and economy, the pre-growth schedule and the lot rule of P3 (below), on a grid where every cell is dry flat ground: `factor = 0.99 · 0.99 (NONWATER_FAR, economy_density.c:34) · [0.9917 on a diagonal hop]`, the accept test `v > P[j] + 0.0005 and v > 0.02`, the Q16 truncation, and the lot rule: at each lot step (after every 4th growth step and after the last), for each core in index order, the eligible count `eligible_c` = cells with `core_of == c` and `density >= 0.10`; `quota_c = min(OM_LOT_CAP · age_c / Σ age, floor(DRAFT-lot-fill 0.6 · eligible_c), DRAFT-lots-per-age 15 · age_c)` (the third term keeps a town near the approved mockup's size, about 600 lots at age 40; rev 4a; the fallback core with age 0 ignores the third term); lots are created on eligible cells, in ascending `key = dist_cells · g/65536` (Euclidean distance from the core cell in cells; ties by cell index) within the square of `DRAFT-lot-scan-radius` 48 cells, until the core stands `quota_c` lots (lots already standing count and stay). The quota always binds, so a town is the inner 60 percent of its field by noisy distance; as the field grows the quota grows and lots are added outward in the same order. Lane cells are never lots (the probe has none). It is the prototype of the shape: the shape test runs on the real C noise before any held file is touched, and P3's sim must match it on flat ground for a single core with the quota on (a P3 test compares them).

**The hop** (`economy_density.c:180-224`, step body now `om_density_step_at`; `om_density_step` stays as `if (tick % 60 == 0) om_density_step_at(w, tick)`): `v = P[i] · factor(i, j)`, the value keeps today's metric; the one change is `DIAG_DECAY` (`economy_density.c:28`, applied :170) **replaced** by `DRAFT-diag-decay` 0.9917, not multiplied on top. Derivation: on dry ground an edge hop costs `BASE_DECAY 0.99 · NONWATER_FAR 0.99` (:27, :34, :176) = 0.9801, and a diagonal hop `0.99 · DIAG · 0.99`; an isotropic field decays as 0.9801 to the power of the Euclidean distance, so a diagonal (root 2) hop must cost 0.9801^1.41421 = 0.97197, i.e. `DIAG = 0.9801^0.41421 = 0.99171`. (The audit 3 ruling [A3 3] gave 0.9817; that makes a diagonal hop cost 0.9801^1.919, steeper than isotropic, and rev 3's 0.986 gave 1.70; 0.9917 is the isotropic value, exponent 1.4146 on the rounded constant. On an 8-neighbour grid the contour is then an octagon within 8 percent of a disc, not a disc; the drawn town is a disc by the lot order below. At young ages the field is the Chebyshev square whatever the diagonal decay, since the front advances one cell per step in every direction.) There is no noise on the value and no clamp (`v <= 0.99 · P[i]` always). On acceptance `core_of[j] = core_of[i]`.
