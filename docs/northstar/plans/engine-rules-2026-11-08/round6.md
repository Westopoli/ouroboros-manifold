## Engine track

### E1. An order is not lost in the pause after a shot (E-13 R-096-02; E-11 R-084-04)
"Given an attack-move ... when the lock ends ... it resumes toward the point ... an attack-move is not finished until the unit reaches its point". R-084-04: "Given a unit fires, When the attack lands, Then the unit pauses movement for 0.5 s": a pause, not the end of an order.
Cause (traced 2026-11-18, scratch `~/Projects/om-wt/engine-rules-baseline/r6-et/`): `om_walk_route` (`csrc/walk_route.c` about line 253) returns "no route" when the walker's speed is zero, which it is for the nine ticks after its own shot; `om_move_walker_step` (`csrc/tactical_move.c` about line 2402) then clears the destination. The unit's attack-move order survives with no destination, the unit reads as arrived, and nothing issues the move again. It strikes when a lock ends within about five ticks of the unit's own shot (the resume of `om_tac_end_pursuit`, `om_tac_release` and `om_tac_return` all end in the same move command), at every kind of lock end (the target dies, leaves the notice distance, is lost from sight, the blocked-target give-up). A player's move order given to a walker during the pause is lost the same way. Flying units are not affected.
Built: a walker that cannot step this tick because of the pause after its shot keeps the destination it was given and walks when the pause ends; it takes no step during the pause. This holds for the resume after every lock end and for a move or attack-move order given during the pause. The unit keeps its ordered point and slot. A route that truly does not exist still clears the destination as today. A braced Vanguard (R-086-03, immobile) is unchanged by this piece: a move order to it is dropped as today.
Proof: scenes in `tests/csim/`, asserted every tick. An attack-mover (walking; alone, and as a group member with a slot) fires, its target dies on the tick of the shot and, in further cases, one, two and three ticks after (killed by another unit): it does not move during the nine ticks, holds a destination from the tick the pause ends at the latest, walks on and arrives at its point. The same for a lock that ends by the notice distance and by loss of sight within three ticks of its shot. A walker given a move order during the pause after its shot walks to that point when the pause ends. Controls that pass today and must stay green: a unit whose target is alive does not resume; a walker ordered to a point with no route has no destination; a flying attack-mover resumes. The tracer's scratch scenes (`r6-et/scene_test_et.py`) show how the case is built.
State 2026-11-18: tests `tests/csim/test_E13_R096_02_order_kept_through_shot_pause.py` (26; 23 were red) and the build (`66fbc1ee9` on the engine branch: in `om_move_walker_step`, a walker whose pace is zero while its order speed is not waits with its destination kept, and plans when the pause ends; no new saved state) are on the branch. `tests/csim` shows no red outside the baseline and 26 baseline reds now green; `tests/transport` shows four reds that are red without the change too (to be traced by the verifier). Owed by a test writer: the strict xfail on `tests/skilllab/arena/test_invariants_round2b_wiring.py::test_R_087_04_finding_a_unit_holding_a_target_just_outside_attack_range_walks_toward_it` comes off.

### E2. A unit ordered alone gives up (E-13 R-099-05)
A unit ordered alone, walking or flying, that is held within 75 wu of its point for 40 consecutive ticks without reaching it ends its order where it stands, as a group member does at its slot. It has no arrival radius: with a clear path it walks to the point itself, as today (existing tests hold a lone walker to within 1 wu of its route's end and a guard to within 5 wu of its post; Round 5's try at an arrival radius for lone walkers, `fd342241c`, turned 14 csim tests red, and the first draft of this piece repeated it). The count is the one the group give-up already keeps, saves and hashes (`giveup_near` in the move state): no new saved state, and a game loaded in the middle of a count gives up on the same tick as the game that was never saved (determinism is a contract; the first draft's "not written to a save file" was wrong).
Proof: scenes, per tick: a walker ordered alone and held 60 wu from its point ends its order on the 40th consecutive tick inside the 75 wu disc and not on the 39th; one held 18 wu from its point likewise (it does not "arrive"); the count starts again when the unit leaves the disc; a flying unit ordered alone gives up the same way; a saved and loaded game gives up on the same tick; a group member's give-up is unchanged. Whole-run: L-0028 sight control on the Round 5 engine has units 6, 9 and 10 holding a destination 16 to 19 wu from their point for 10,000 ticks; after the fix no unit holds a destination inside the 75 wu disc for more than 40 ticks without a target in any regression fight.
The count runs only while the unit is trying to walk: it is zero on any tick the unit holds a target or is in the pause after its own shot. A unit that pursues or stands to shoot has left its group and is a lone walker whose destination is its target's place (measured 2026-11-18); without this clause the lone give-up would end the order of a unit shooting at an enemy that stands within 75 wu of it. Further proof scene: a lone attack-mover that stands to shoot for 100 ticks at an enemy 60 wu away keeps its order and, after the kill, walks on to its point.
