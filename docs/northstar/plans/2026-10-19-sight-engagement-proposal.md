# Engagement by sight: the fix for the game and for the lab (Architect proposal, 2026-10-19)

Westley, 2026-10-19: "a unit with no order that sees an enemy will walk towards and attack; a unit that was on a move command finishes the move command and then sees an enemy will attack (seeing an enemy does not interrupt a move command); and finally a unit still marching under attack move will move towards the closest target in its vision regardless of direction. We're just copying Starcraft 2 rules here, easy quick-reference." This page is the proposal: the rule as it would read in the bible, what the engine changes, what the lab changes, what it costs, and the three choices inside it that are his. Nothing here is in force until he says so. Facts are from the read-only investigation of 2026-10-19 (`scratchpad/vision_attack.md`; origin/main a13ad73c; file:line below are from it).

## 1. What the game does today, in one paragraph

Every attack trigger in the bible says "in range" or "enters attack range" (E-11 R-087-03, E-01 R-008-01/02, E-13 R-096-02), and one rule says the opposite of the new rule for the march: R-087-04, "does not walk toward an enemy it merely sees in sensor range; the formation holds until shooting, not until sight (Westley, 2026-10-03, A199; marked for review in playtesting)". The engine matches: acquisition searches `u->attack_range` (`csrc/unit.c:2164-2179`, `om_tac_nearest_actable` :2006), sight is a filter on candidates already inside weapon range, and the only live code that moves a unit toward an enemy is return fire (:2109-2114) and the attack-target order. An idle unit, an arrived attack-mover and a marching attack-mover all stand still with an enemy in plain sight just outside range. A function that does what Westley describes, `om_unit_acquire_target` (`csrc/fog.c:687-714`: nearest visible enemy, then attack-target), exists and is wired to nothing. The lab has been working around this twice: mop-up orders every 200 ticks on main and the re-engage order after 40 silent ticks on the L8 branch.

## 2. The rule, as the bible would read it (rewrite, never append)

Westley's three cases, in the bible's Given/When/Then form. Sight stays necessary (E-12 R-093-02: a unit acquires, pursues or fires only at what its side can see) and reach stays necessary (E-01 R-164-02: never at a layer offset outside the type's reach). "Sees" below means: inside the unit's own sensor radius and visible to its side.

- **R-087-03 (rewritten) Engagement by sight.** Given a unit with no order, When it sees an enemy its reach covers, Then it acquires the nearest such enemy, moves into attack range and fires; it keeps the target while the target is alive and visible; when the target dies or leaves its side's sight it acquires the next visible enemy, or holds where it stands if there is none. An engaged target still alive and visible is kept over a nearer one (as today).
- **R-096-01 (unchanged) The move command.** Given a plain move, When the unit travels, Then it acquires no target, returns no fire and does not stop when hit; on arrival it has no order and R-087-03 applies from that tick.
- **R-087-04 (rewritten) The attack-move.** Given an attack-moving unit, When it sees an enemy its reach covers, in any direction, Then it acquires the nearest such enemy, moves toward it and fires; when the target dies or leaves its side's sight it resumes toward its ordered point (R-096-02, Westley 2026-10-13: not finished until it reaches its point); on arrival R-087-03 applies. A199 ("formation holds until shooting, not until sight", Westley 2026-10-03, marked for playtest review) is superseded by Westley 2026-10-19: the formation holds until sight.
- **R-096-08 (unchanged) Guard.** The radius guard is the one stance that does not chase past its post; it already engages what its side can see within `GUARD_RADIUS` and returns.
- **R-087-06 (rewritten) Return fire.** Given a unit with no target that is hit, When the attacker is visible and in reach, Then it is acquired under R-087-03 or R-087-04 like any seen enemy; when the attacker cannot be seen or reached the unit flinches as today (R-087-01).

Lines that change with them: E-11 l.140 and E-13 l.45 (the "Direction changed 2026-10-02, rewrite pending" paragraphs become these rules and their "what the game does today" sentences go); E-01 R-008-01/02 ("in range" becomes "seen and in reach"); E-13 R-096-02 trigger, R-099-04 and R-099-05 ("the same state as an arrival" now means R-087-03 applies, not "stationary from that tick onward"); R-204-03 and R-202-01 (structures and workers by sight, unchanged in meaning); `docs/bible/algorithms.md` ALG-62 (stale today; rewritten to the rule above); the register gains one closed row for the A199 reversal with Westley's words; skill-lab decision 28 point 2 is corrected ("the engine is per rules here" was true of the old rules).

## 3. Three choices inside the rule that are Westley's

1. **How far an idle unit chases.** The rule above says "while the target is visible", no distance limit; an idle unit can be drawn off by a fleeing enemy as far as its side keeps sight. The alternative is Starcraft's leash: chase a short way, then return to where it stood. Candidates: (a) chase while visible, hold where the chase ends (as written); (b) chase at most `IDLE_LEASH` wu from where it stood, then walk back (number his; the attack-move leash today is 200 wu, R-087-05); (c) chase while visible, then walk back. Default if he says nothing: (a).
2. **The attack-move leash.** Today an attack-moving unit drops a target it has pursued more than 200 wu from its anchor (R-087-05, R-096-03). Under the new rule the chase ends when the target dies or leaves sight. Candidates: (a) retire the leash: sight ends the chase; (b) keep 200 wu as a cap on top of sight. Default: (a), Starcraft has none.
3. **Nearest by what.** "Closest target in its vision": straight-line distance to the unit (as the engine's nearest search does today), not route length. Default: straight line.

## 4. The engine piece: `VG-E-sightengage` (contract-free)

No header change, no pipe frame, no new saved field: the chase reuses the unit's destination and target fields and the anchor fields already saved and hashed. Determinism: no new input. Owner files: `csrc/unit.c` (the acquisition block of `om_tac_unit_ai` :2164-2179, the target-drop test :2143, the pursue and release branches :2205-2209 and :2224-2225, the `chase_idle` state :2044-2065, return fire :2096-2117), `csrc/tactical_move.c` (`om_move_giveup_pass` :2558-2579 must not undo a chase: a unit with a target is exempt from the give-up pass), `csrc/fog.c` (`om_unit_acquire_target` either wired as the idle acquisition or deleted as dead code, the builder's call, said in the report). Behaviour: on the cohort tick (R-087-08, every 4th tick) a unit with no target and order none or attack-move searches `u->sensor_radius` for the nearest enemy that is visible to its side and inside its reach; acquiring sets the target and a destination at the target's position, refreshed each cohort tick while the target moves; firing is unchanged (`om_tac_fire_pass`); losing sight of the target drops it, then an attack-mover resumes its route to the ordered point and an idle unit holds. Guard and attack-target paths untouched. Depends on: `LAB-REV2-E1` with E1b (they own the return-fire lines of `unit.c` on the lab branch) and `VG-CONTRACT-E7` (the spawn block), so it queues behind the lab landing and E7; wave 13.

**Tests first, a different writer than the builder, `tests/csim/test_VG_E_sightengage.py`:** (1) idle unit, enemy at sensor minus one, out of attack range: within 4 ticks the unit has a destination toward the enemy and fires once in range; (2) idle, enemy beyond sensor and unseen by the side: no destination, no target (control); (3) idle, enemy beyond its own sensor but seen by a friendly spotter: no acquisition (sight is the side's, acquisition radius is the unit's own sensor; the Bombard and Bastion, sensor 300, still fire beyond 300 at what a spotter sees once they have a target in range, R-093-02 unchanged) [this reading is choice 3's neighbour and is marked for Westley: should a spotter's sight let a unit chase?]; (4) move command past a visible enemy: no turn, arrives, then acquires within 4 ticks; (5) attack-move marching with a visible enemy 90 degrees off the route: turns to it, fires, then resumes to the ordered point after the kill; (6) attack-move: the enemy flees out of sight: target dropped, route resumed; (7) guard: unchanged, the E4 radius tests stay green (positive control); (8) cross-layer: an enemy one layer up is acquired by a Raider (reach 1) and not by a Vanguard (reach 0) at the same distance; (9) the give-up pass does not clear a chaser's destination; (10) the A99 fixture's hash moves exactly once (TD row) and two worlds differing only in a chaser's target hash differently (determinism control). Superseded tests, listed with the ruling that retires them: `test_R_087_04_A199_an_idle_unit_never_walks_toward_a_seen_enemy`, `test_PP_unit_pursuit.py::test_R_087_04_chase_persists_after_arrival`, the A199 marching pins in `test_PP_attack_move_no_sight_pursuit.py` and `test_PP_unit_pursuit.py`; the two `test_PP_unit_guard.py` tests that E4 already retires; hash goldens A99, F1/F2/F3 and `skilllab/golden_hashes.json` under one TD row, re-recorded once after the landing. Red reason on the built tree: test (1) finds no destination after 20 ticks (the acquisition radius is `attack_range`), assertion line pasted.

**Shot.** The S-087 story ("otherwise shoot the nearest enemy in range" becomes "in sight") gets a play: one idle player unit, one enemy walking past at sensor range; the shot shows the unit turning and closing; queued for Westley.
