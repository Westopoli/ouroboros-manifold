# Ambiguity Register

Every open design question that blocks a story from being Ready. Claude raises flags here instead of guessing. Westley answers; the answer becomes a rule in the epic file and the flag closes. Closed flags stay in the table with their answer so the reasoning survives.

Schema: `Q-<story>-<nn>` where `<story>` is the story number the flag blocks, or `E##` when it blocks a whole epic that has no story numbers yet.

| Q-E03-01 | Combat — Command Vocabulary | `bible_combat.md`'s Command Vocabulary section states, in the same breath, both "no group-move primitive" / "not a speed-matched group move" (line ~434) and then describes "Formation-preserving group move" (line 436): a multi-unit move command computes each unit's own offset from the group centroid and assigns it an individual destination that preserves the group's shape on arrival. Is the shape-preserving destination assignment meant to coexist with "no group-move" (i.e. "no group-move" only ever meant no *speed*-matching, and per-unit destination-slot assignment was always in scope), or is Formation-preserving group move itself the thing that should be cut/rewritten to match a stricter "every unit just goes to the raw click point, no computed offsets" reading? | (a) keep both as written — "no group-move" refers only to speed (units keep native speed, arrive strung out by speed tier), and destination-slot shape preservation is a separate, intentional feature; (b) cut Formation-preserving group move — every mixed-selection move order sends every unit to the same raw point, no computed per-unit offsets, full agreement with "no group-move primitive" taken literally; (c) something else (e.g. shape preservation only for single-type selections, or only below some spread threshold) | closed | (c) — Halo Wars model. Keep both mechanics, but reverse the speed-matching clause: a selection moves together at its slowest unit's pace by default (not strung-out arrival), and formation-preserving destination assignment is exactly the mechanism that makes uniform-pace cohesion work. Players can deliberately split off their own sub-groups to move independently at a different pace if they want a faster element to detach. Reverses `bible_combat.md` A-346's "no speed-matching" claim; A-347 needed no change. New mechanic recorded as `bible` A-557. Closed by Westley, 2026-09-10. |
