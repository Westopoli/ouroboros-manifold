1b. The L-0073 twin that doubled in length

Short answer

The twin is the city layout with its buildings removed. It went from tick 2156 (A wins 22:0) to tick 4395 (A wins 13:0). Most of that doubling is traced and was intended. The Round 5 change of 2026-11-17 (commit 3bc0a7095) made a unit take targets only inside its own notice distance, never inside the whole sight radius. The rule is DEC-targeting-sc2-2026-11-17, confirmed as "not drift" on 2026-11-18. Before it, all 24 blue holders saw red at 550 wu on tick 1, walked up, and every one had a target by tick 30. After it, blue stays put until red is inside its notice distance. Red arrives in one column, the front nine to eleven stop at about 300 to shoot, and the twelve behind them stand outside their own notice distance with an order and no target (five Raiders, six Interceptors, one Vanguard, standing from tick 85 to 128, shuffling forward in pulses). Red then loses about 2.6 HP per tick where it used to lose 5.8, and only about 14 blue units fire. That is the slow trickle.

Still unexplained: the last 744 ticks. The Round 6 engine-rule change E1 (66fbc1ee9) moved the end from 3651 to 4395. Its own text says it only touches a walker paused by its own shot. The first tick that differs is 159, where two blue Wardens keep a destination about 10 wu away. The structure stays the same (same twelve units standing, same slow duel), so the extra 744 is the tail of that duel. I did not revert E1 alone, so I cannot show it as the cause or as noise. The seed argument has no effect on this twin, so there is no noise estimate either.

The "seven orders ended short". They are not give-ups and not no-route clears. Six are blue units whose target died; the order cleared two to eight ticks after the kill, at their weapon range. One is a red Raider arriving 36 wu from its point. The lab's own "ended short" measure gives zero for all three versions on the clips below, so the number comes from a looser measure.
