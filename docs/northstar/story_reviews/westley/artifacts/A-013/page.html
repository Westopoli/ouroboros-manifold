Lab stage 5: facing, arcs and height bands

This is the simulation lab's stage 5, from lab-staircase.md: “Units have fronts; fire cones matter; a height band is a slower climb and a longer sight.” It is not the beta. Built overnight on its own branch. Update 2026-11-27: you approved lab stage 5 once L-0084 was redrawn; the redrawn slope clip is below, and the asks stay open without blocking.

The short version

  • Units now have a front that matters. A unit cannot fire at a target outside its firing arc. It keeps the target, turns, then fires. Before tonight facing was decoration: an attack from the front, the side and the rear gave the same fight.
  • You can see a flank hit a rear. In the pinned-flank clip, 233 of the 540 hits on the holders land on their rear, over 175 ticks, each marked in red.
  • But a flank costs the flanked side almost nothing yet. There is no rule that a hit from behind hurts more, and units turn so fast (a Vanguard turns half round in under a second) that the arc delays its answer by about 12 ticks, a little over half a second. The fights end the same way with the arc on or off.
  • The climb is real but too small to see at the engine's own number: 7 percent slower, 8 ticks late to the fight. With a stronger test number (2.0, labelled) the attackers arrive 113 ticks late and you can see it.
  • Height gives sight, not reach. High ground sees down and low ground cannot see up. Nothing makes a unit on a height see or shoot farther; that number has never been set.

So the stage is built and measurable, and its done-when is half met: you can see the flank land and the climb slow the attacker in the labelled comparison, but the rules as they stand make neither one decide a fight. That is the finding, and the first two asks below are how it gets settled.

What I need from you

  1. What should a flank cost? Today: only the turn delay. Options: (a) leave it; (b) slower turning, so a unit caught facing the wrong way stays exposed longer (uses numbers that already exist, one per category); (c) hits on the side or rear do more damage (a new rule); (d) a unit under fire from behind turns on the nearer threat instead of staying on its front target. I recommend trying (b) in the lab first, as a sweep, then deciding on (c).
  2. How strong is the climb? The engine's 1.1 is invisible. The 2.0 clip shows a strong one. Pick a number or a range to sweep.
  3. Should height reach farther? The staircase says “a longer sight”. Today it is only one-way sight. Say whether a higher band should see or shoot farther, and I will sweep the number.
  4. Arc widths. Tonight every unit type uses one width (90 degrees in these clips). Your ruling is one per category: Strike wide, Line medium, Siege narrow. That needs three new settings, a contract change of its own. Yes or no to writing it.
  5. The flank layouts. In your layout L-0067 the attackers never walk a flank route: all 774 hits on the holder land on its front. The probe scenes here are mine, made to isolate one thing each. The layouts are yours to redraw.
  6. One open check. The speed check for the 2.0 climb fails by a hair for 3 of 8 units (0.553 to 0.561 against a limit of 0.55), because units jostling in the rank blur the measure. The tolerance is yours.
  7. Whether to merge. Branch s/lab-stage5. It holds one engine change, the firing arc, which is off unless switched on.

How to read a clip

  • Blue holds, red attacks. The white triangle on a unit is its front.
  • The thin outline fanning out from each unit is its firing arc: it can only shoot inside it.
  • A ring flashes on a unit when it is hit: white for a hit on its front, orange for its side, red for its rear. The running count is at the top left: front N side N rear N.
  • A yellow bar above a unit fills as it climbs a slope. The small dot under a unit shows which height band it stands on.
  • The clips run at six times game speed: one second of clip is 120 ticks.

Known look problems: with many units the arc outlines overlap into a tangle; the hit rings are thin; in the pinned and climb clips the units are small in the frame.

What works

  • The firing arc in the engine: outside the arc a unit holds fire, keeps its target, turns at its normal rate, then fires. Switched off, every earlier lab fight is byte for byte what it was.
  • The lab switch --facing on: sets each unit's starting facing, sets the arc, and records for every shot whether it hit a front, a side or a rear, and how far up a slope each unit is.
  • Measures: hits by direction, how long a flanked unit takes to answer, how much a climb slows a unit. A check that no unit ever fired outside its arc passes on every fight here.
  • The replay draws arcs, hit rings, the climb bar and band marks.
  • Seven probe scenes and thirteen clips, each recorded twice (every tick for the numbers, every sixth for the clip) with matching results.

What does not work, or is not done

  • A flank does not decide a fight. The marks show it landing; the outcome does not move. See ask 1.
  • The climb at the engine's number cannot be seen. See ask 2.
  • “A longer sight” is not built beyond the one-way sight that already existed. See ask 3.
  • One arc width for all unit types, not your three. See ask 4.
  • The cone-shaped area attack (the staircase names its setting, set_cone) is a different rule and was left alone.
  • One check is red, the 2.0 climb speed tolerance. See ask 6.
  • Lab stage 4 is still open. The lock change, “same exact spot”, the trap exceptions and the three open questions were left exactly as they are. When they settle, the clips are re-recorded with one command each; no stage 5 code depends on them.
  • Nothing here is on the approved-fights list.

Decisions I made alone

  1. “Fire cones matter” was built as the firing arc, your own ruling (“add a firing-arc rule to the game”), not as the cone-shaped area attack the staircase line names.
  2. One engine change, the arc, off by default. It touches nothing a stage 4 decision governs: who targets whom, the lock, return fire and orders are unchanged. Keep-walking stands.
  3. Flank and arc scenes run with sight off, so the unsigned stage 4 sight rules cannot muddy them. Only the L-0084 clip uses sight.
  4. Added the pinned flank scene after the first measurements showed a lone side or rear attack barely registers.
  5. Kept the engine's climb number and showed 2.0 beside it as a labelled test, not a ruling.
  6. Probe scenes are mine, marked as drafts for you to redraw.
  7. I looked at frames from the clips, as you asked this time.
