# Prior art: economy as skilled play, not busy-work

Digest for the economy options pass. Facts only; no recommendations. Captured 2026-10-01. It extends `prior_art_resources.md` (resource models) and does not repeat it.

Tags: [V] VERIFIED, a page fetched this session said it. [R] REPORTED, a search-result summary or secondary page not fetched. [E] ESTIMATED, model knowledge, not sourced this session. Many sites blocked fetching (Liquipedia, Fandom, Medium, PC Gamer article bodies, AusGamers, Giant Bomb), and Wikipedia gave thin mechanics for several games, so a lot of the board-game detail is [R] or [E]. Each entry gives: loop, skill, time pressure or lookahead, what people say.

## A. Real-time economy as the whole game

### Offworld Trading Company
- Loop: claim plots, build harvesters and processors for resources, sell into a shared market whose price moves with every buy and sell; the win is buying a majority of rival shares; no armies. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offworld_Trading_Company. Black market: a per-match set of purchasable sabotage actions (goon squad and EMP always present, others random); each purchase raises that item's price; a bad credit rating bans aggressive items. [R] search summary of the Offworld wiki/Steam threads, https://offworldtradingcompany.fandom.com/wiki/Basic_strategies (fetch blocked).
- Pace: Johnson wanted matches "no longer than 30 minutes ... if you go longer than that, you're no longer a mainstream synchronous multiplayer game." [V] https://goombastomp.com/interview-soren-johnson/. Game length 30 minutes, 2 to 8 players, kept from RTS convention. [V] https://www.matchstickeyes.com/2014/10/01/offworld-trading-company-interview-with-soren-johnson/.
- Skill: reading the price graph as a weapon. Johnson: collapsing AoE2's separate buy and sell prices into one price "made the market mechanic so much more powerful" [V matchstickeyes]; "every time you buy a resource the price goes up, every time you sell ... goes down — the game just sort of balances itself" [V goombastomp]. Strong play corners or floods a resource and sabotages the rival's supply [R, Offworld wiki search]. Johnson: "money, not firepower, is the player's weapon." [V matchstickeyes].
- Lookahead and pressure: a price move is visible to all, so a corner is a bet on what rivals will need next; the black-market price rise after each purchase gives a rival time to buy a defence [R]. Auctions came from M.U.L.E., "but our auctions work differently." [V goombastomp]. The simultaneous (not turn-based) play is what let the game run short. [V matchstickeyes].
- Said: GDC talk framed it as de-emphasising micro dexterity for macro strategy [V] https://www.designer-notes.com/offworld-trading-company-gdc-postmortem/. Johnson's design-notes episode is billed as covering "the hardest part was ending the game"; the stock-buyout was built as the victory condition because killing the opponent was not available. [R] search summary of https://www.designer-notes.com/designer-notes-18-offworld-trading-company/ (no transcript fetched). Tycoon fans may dislike tense multiplayer, so the black market can be turned off in custom modes. [V matchstickeyes].

### Mini Metro / Mini Motorways
- Loop: draw lines between stations, assign scarce trains, carriages and tunnels given weekly; stations appear at random; passengers pile up; the run ends when a station stays overcrowded too long. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Metro_(video_game). Mini Motorways is the road equivalent. [E]
- Skill: restructuring early. Jarrett Walker: "winning lies in letting go of a network pattern as soon as it no longer fits the growing city" (hub-and-spoke early, grid later). [V] https://humantransit.org/2014/12/learning-how-transit-works-from-mini-metro.html.
- Pressure: you are given slightly less capacity than demand; the game is "triage" on a network a few bad decisions from collapse. [R] search summary of https://medium.com/gaming-is-good/mini-metro-and-mini-motorways-the-art-of-elegant-constraint-optimization-2571a32fdfe2 (fetch 403).
- Design intent: you can read the state at a glance, no submenus or hidden statistics. [R] search summary of a Peter Curry interview, https://www.giantbomb.com/profile/gamer_152/blog/interview-peter-curry-co-creator-of-mini-metro/131885/ (fetch 403).

### Factorio, Satisfactory, Dyson Sphere Program
- Loop: extract, belt, craft, expand; the player's layout is the economy; Factorio's enemies react to pollution. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factorio.
- Skill: find the current bottleneck, fix it, find the next. [R] search summary of a blog on Factorio decision-making, https://workingwithstrangers.substack.com/p/the-current-bottleneck-of-the-world.
- No fetched statement on what makes throughput puzzles engaging in the three games; blueprints reducing repeat labour is [E]. The throughput puzzle has no opponent in the base game, so time pressure comes from the player's own ambition and from biters. [E]

### Against the Storm
- Loop: short roguelite settlements; fulfil queen orders to raise reputation before an "Impatience" meter fills; each run about one hour, then the storm resets the map; unlocks persist. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Against_the_Storm_(video_game).
- Pressure: the impatience clock punishes slow play; forest hostility rises with glade clearing; wood is both fuel and material. [R] https://techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/against-storm-ambient-roguelite-city-builder (search summary); the glade-timing and wood tension [V] https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2023/06/design-review-of-against-storm-by.html.
- Designer statement: runs end before "the sweet taste of steamrolling through the challenges can turn into bitter boredom"; city builders otherwise reach a stable balance and the designer must break it. [V] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-against-the-storm-managed-to-mix-city-building-and-roguelite-play. The studio knew players tire of city builders after 2 to 3 hours. [R] search summary.
- Criticism (one blog): variation plateaus after 5 to 10 hours; many tier-2 chains look alike ("deckbuilding oatmeal"); random modifiers feel arbitrary. [V] radiator blog above.

### Frostpunk
- Loop: coal generator heat radius, resources (coal, wood, steel, food), hope and discontent meters, laws, daylight work and emergency shifts; generator can fail or explode. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostpunk.
- Pressure: scripted temperature drops and crises; each law is incrementally more extreme, a "slippery slope." [R] search summary of https://www.pcgamer.com/frostpunk-developers-on-hope-misery-and-the-ultimately-terrifying-book-of-laws/ (body not fetched).
- Said: critics praised the ethical choices; one found it "unexpectedly easy as a management game"; story beats repeat on a second playthrough. [V] Wikipedia page above.

### Dorfromantik and Islanders
- Dorfromantik: place hex tiles from a stack; matched edges score; quests on tiles (for example, border six grain fields) reward more tiles. [R] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorfromantik (search summary). Skill is balancing the long-term plan against the current tile. [R] same search.
- Islanders: place buildings from packs; each building scores from neighbours inside a visible sphere (circus likes houses, dislikes mansions); preview the score change before placing; point thresholds unlock new packs. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islanders_(video_game). Critics compared it to Tetris; "more a puzzle than a city builder," no income to manage. [V] same page for the Tetris comparison; the "puzzle not city builder" line [R] https://www.xboxtavern.com/islanders-review/ (search summary).
- Time pressure: none; both are untimed. [E]

## B. Spatial and adjacency placement puzzles

- Civilization VI districts: buildings were "unstacked" onto their own tiles; district yield depends on neighbouring terrain; encampments cannot touch the city centre. Ed Beach wanted the map "just as important as anything else"; GameSpot: it "creates greater variety in the types of thinking Civ demands." Districts can be attacked separately. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_VI. Turn-based, so lookahead is the whole skill. [E]
- Anno and Cities-style layout: coverage radius and road distance decide production; complaints about auditing chains in Anno are already in the earlier file. [R, see prior_art_resources.md]
- Supreme Commander adjacency (cheaper power use, factory discount) with chain-reaction vulnerability is in the earlier file. [V there]
- Backpack Hero: item effects depend on position and adjacency in a packed grid; the player must "reinvent" the pack between encounters as loot arrives; "inventory Tetris" made the core game. [R] search summary of https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-backpack-hero/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backpack_Hero.
- Suburbia: hex tiles raise income, reputation or population; tiles can affect neighbours including other players'; the core puzzle is income against reputation. [R] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbia_(board_game) (search summary).
- Cascadia: habitat tile plus wildlife token drafted as a pair; scoring cards change each game; Spiel des Jahres jury praised the "two-part puzzle." [R] search summary (https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/cascadia/ and others).
- Terra Mystica: building next to rivals gives them power and gives you cheaper upgrades, so each player trades adjacency against free space; round scoring tiles change what is worth building in each of six rounds. [R] https://www.yucata.de/en/Rules/TerraMystica (search summary). Terraforming is costly and faction-specific. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Mystica.
- Carcassonne, Townscaper, Dome Keeper: not researched; no source. Carcassonne is edge-matching with shared scoring features [E].

## C. Board games with an economy engine that feels like chess

For each: the one mechanism that forces thinking ahead.

- Brass: Birmingham. Two actions per turn, each spending a card (build, network, develop, sell, loan, scout); coal and iron must be connected to the building or bought from a market; turn order follows spending. [R] https://lehi-innovation.github.io/boardgame-rules/rules/brass-birmingham/ (search summary). Mechanism: card-limited actions plus network connection, so a build today decides what you can buy tomorrow; the era end wipes the weaker tiles and scores links. [E for the era wipe]. Rated top on BoardGameGeek February 2023. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass_(board_game).
- Power Grid. Auction for power plants; the leader goes last in turn order for buying fuel and building, a catch-up rule; connection costs depend on terrain; fuel prices rise as bought, "rewarding those who buy later." [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_Grid_(board_game). Mechanism: reverse turn order plus a shared market.
- Agricola. Worker placement, one worker per action space per round, 14 rounds, harvest feeding at fixed times; "penalized for focusing on any one aspect." [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricola_(board_game). Mechanism: blocked spaces plus a known future feeding deadline.
- Puerto Rico. Players pick a role; everyone does it but the picker acts first and gets a small privilege; unchosen roles accrue doubloons. "Anticipate opponents' needs"; picking denies others the role's privilege. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_(board_game). Mechanism: your choice hands every rival a free action.
- Terra Mystica. Mechanism: round scoring tiles revealed at the start, so you plan builds toward the rounds that reward them. [R] see B.
- Through the Ages. Cards in a shared row; civil and military actions are separate limited pools (Despotism: four civil, two military). [R] https://www.ultraboardgames.com/through-the-ages/subsequent-rounds.php (search summary). Mechanism: scarce action pools force a choice between growing and defending. Population, food and culture trade-offs [E].
- Splendor. On a turn: take gems, buy a card (permanent discount), or reserve one; reserving costs a turn but blocks a rival. [R] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splendor_(game) (search summary). Mechanism: engine building under a race, with denial through reserving.
- Dominion. Each player starts with the same ten cards and buys one card per turn; the deck is the economy and victory cards clog it. "They're income," copper costs zero so you always have a foothold. [V] https://cardboardedison.com/blog/meaningful-decisions-donald-x-vaccarino-dominion and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_(card_game). Mechanism: every purchase also dilutes or improves your future draws.
- Tigris and Euphrates. Score is your lowest of four colours; influence is hidden; tile hands are hidden. [R] https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/tigris-and-euphrates/ and others (search summary). Mechanism: the weakest category sets the score, so every move is checked against the weak colour.
- Go. Influence early, territory later; efficiency means securing area with fewest stones; sente (initiative) against gote (response). [R] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_strategy_and_tactics (search summary). Mechanism: initiative; a forcing move gains tempo.
- Hive. No board; pieces stay in one connected group; moving a piece that would split the hive is illegal, so beetles pin pieces; win by surrounding the queen. [R] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hive_(game) (search summary). Mechanism: pinning and a shared structure; not an economy game [E].

## D. Real-time and simultaneous board or card economies

- Galaxy Trucker. All players build ships at once from face-down tiles under an hourglass; flight then punishes weak builds with meteors, pirates and repairs. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_Trucker. Tension source: a build has to survive a later, partly random test, so speed is traded against robustness. [E]
- Space Alert. Ten-minute real-time phase with an audio track announcing threats; crew play action cards to move and act; resolution happens after, so plans are committed ahead of the result. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Alert.
- Captain Sonar. Teams of up to four with captain, first mate, engineer and radio operator; real-time mode has no waiting for the other team; the engineer and first mate manage breakdowns and charge. [V for roles and real-time mode] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Sonar. A reviewer noted fast turns and the player count "may not appeal to all." [V same page].
- Pendulum. Real-time worker placement with three sand timers (45 seconds, 2 minutes, 3 minutes); a worker cannot be placed on a section that has a timer running, and a section only acts when its timer is not there. Reviewers say the focus is time management, not speed. [R] https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/pendulum/ and others (search summary).
- Pit: not researched. Tension appears to come from a fixed time and a shared noisy market, [E].
- What makes real-time tense rather than frantic, across these sources: a fixed time window with a known end (Galaxy Trucker, Pendulum, Space Alert); a committed plan whose result arrives later (Space Alert, Galaxy Trucker); simple per-action decisions. No source fetched states this as a design rule; it is a pattern across the entries.

## E. RTS macro called skillful rather than a chore

- SC2 and AoE2 build orders and idle time. AoE2: the Town Center should never idle; a 15-second idle delays the Feudal Age and gives the opponent first access to pressure; a standard goal is Feudal at about 20 villagers with little idle time, villagers one per 25 seconds. [R] https://chosengambit.com/2025/02/ict/aoe-2-de-fast-castle/ and https://sitaux.com/practice-your-first-build-order/ (search summaries). Scouting informs build-order adaptation. [R] same search.
- Day[9]'s mental checklist: keep money low, constantly build workers, avoid supply block, watch the minimap, scout. Timings and strategic decisions are learned by experience, not covered by the checklist. [R] https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Mental_Checklist (search summary, fetch 403).
- Tom Chick: "the dirty little secret about RTSs is that they're based on economy"; novices differ from veterans by keyboard use, production capacity and "ABV" (Always Be Villagering). [V] https://www.quartertothree.com/fp/2016/06/02/suck-real-time-strategy-games-10-ways-improve/.
- Zerg. Larva constrains how many drones or army units are made; inject adds larvae; creep spread gives speed and vision and defines defensible expansions; pro Zerg stay light early and build army only after scouting an attack. [R] https://www.starcraft2.ai/en/guides/zerg and Liquipedia search summaries.
- SC2 designer statements (macro mechanics as chore or skill). Blizzard's David Kim: macro mechanics "difficult to do, and not many people can really tell how well someone is doing it"; goals "more action, micro ... less downtime." [V] via Kotaku, quoted in the earlier file. Browder: "Some games favor a macro gameplay, and some games favor a micro gameplay. Our goals are to have both be possible." [V] https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2010/04/08/an-extensive-interview-with-starcraft-ii-design-director.aspx. Tim Ismay, during the LotV beta: "The data is very split ... internally, I think that's an area we're very split on." [R] search summary of https://www.pcgamesn.com/starcraft-ii/david-kim-and-the-impossible-balancing-act-of-starcraft-2-legacy-of-the-void. With multiple building selection and automine, players feared too little macro challenge, so race-specific mechanics were added. [R] search summary of Liquipedia.
- Warcraft III upkeep. Rob Pardo: without upkeep "all you really had to control the players was a population cap ... they would just stockpile the gold ... both of us could basically instantly rebuild our army. There was just no economic tension." Loss framing of upkeep versus bonus framing was a known cost. Effects listed by a blog: less snowballing, soft supply cap, sharper attack timings. A critic calls it a "bandaid" for the late game. [V] https://www.maguro.one/2020/10/closer-look-at-upkeep.html. Sigaty: the community "never really loved it" but it was "a really good dilemma." [R] search summary.
- AoE2 market. Food, wood and stone trade for gold at global shared rates that move by 2 per 100 units traded, with a 30% fee reducible by techs. [R] https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Market_(Age_of_Empires_II) (search summary). Johnson cites collapsing this dual price into one for Offworld. [V matchstickeyes].
- AoE4. Age-up is a landmark building with two choices per age, the other locked out; sacred sites and relics add economy bonuses and an alternate victory. [R] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Empires_IV and https://gamerant.com/age-of-empires-4-landmarks-guide/ (search summaries).
- Northgard: winter and tile limits are in the earlier file. They Are Billions: zombies path to the command centre by the shortest route and attack walls; players build double walls and towers; walls only slow without guards. [R] https://www.pcgamesn.com/they-are-billions/they-are-billions-guide-tips-units-buildings-resources (search summary). Expansion is a placement bet against horde waves. [E]
- Not found: a Greg Street statement on RTS macro; a Dustin Browder quote calling macro "busy work" (searches did not surface one).

## F. Puzzle, action and timing fused into economies

- Puzzle Quest. Match gems on a grid; gem colours become mana, damage, gold or experience; spells alter the board; limiting an enemy's mana stops its abilities. [R] https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/PuzzleQuest and https://kotaku.com/puzzle-quest-3-does-the-match-three-rpg-a-little-differ-1846538180 (search summary). One move's matches feed both economy and attack, so the board is a resource converter. [E]
- Backpack Hero: see B.
- Timing mechanics: SC2 inject, MULE and chrono as repeatable timed bonuses are in the earlier file. Active reload in Gears is [E], not researched.
- Dominion: see C. Slay the Spire's per-turn energy as a hard budget on a hand is [E].
- Auto-battler economy (TFT). Interest: 1 gold per 10 held at round start, up to 5; streak bonus +1, +2 or +3 gold for 2 to 4, 5 to 7 or 8+ consecutive wins or losses; three sinks (champions, rerolls, XP for levels); "to econ" means banking gold instead of spending; about 50 gold held is a common target; a maxed economy earns about 9 more gold per turn. [R] https://tft.ninja/guides/game-mechanics/economy and https://www.thegamer.com/teamfight-tactics-tft-economy-gold-management/ (search summaries). Skill: when to stop saving and spend, against what the rest of the lobby is doing. Reroll-too-early is called wasteful. [R]
- Poker-like bidding: not researched; Power Grid and Offworld auctions above are the examples in this file.

## G. Asymmetric co-op, a support or logistics seat that stays fun

- Overcooked. Developers made sure "there were generally always more actions to perform than players available," forced role swaps with level disruptions (earthquakes, sliding counters, rats), and built waiting windows from cooking times so players choose between waiting and preparing; testers valued complex dynamic levels more than recipes. [V] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-building-truly-cooperative-play-in-i-overcooked-i-.
- Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes. Defuser sees the bomb, experts hold the manual, neither sees the other's material. Ars Technica: appeal for onlookers; one reviewer: players can "hit a chore-like rhythm" once modules are familiar. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_Talking_and_Nobody_Explodes.
- FTL. Pausable real-time; distribute power, assign crew to stations, fire weapons; pausing allows thought without constant pressure; critics called it punishing. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light.
- Barotrauma engineer. Runs reactor output (too high melts down, too low leaves no power), repairs, wiring; multiplayer chaos makes the role mission-critical. [R] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barotrauma_(video_game) and guide pages (search summary).
- Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime. Two players operate seven ship controls (steering, turrets, shield, special weapon), hopping roles; leaving a station without telling the partner can end the run. [R] https://gameinformer.com/games/lovers_in_a_dangerous_spacetime/b/xboxone/archive/2015/09/15/lovers-in-a-dangerous-spacetime-game-informer-review.aspx (search summary).
- Foxhole logistics and Savage/Natural Selection commander seats are in the earlier file. Guns of Icarus engineer: no source found.
- What keeps the seat engaging, as sources state it: more jobs than hands (Overcooked), a partner who depends on your output (KTANE, Lovers, Barotrauma), a visible state that fails quickly if neglected (FTL reactor, Barotrauma reactor), and pause or short windows that keep decisions readable (FTL). The chore risk is stated for KTANE once solved. [V/R as tagged above]

## H. Published design writing on interesting decisions versus busy-work

- Sid Meier, GDC 2012 "Interesting Decisions": "easier to look at ... what is not an interesting decision" (always picking the first of three, or random picks); good decisions have trade-offs, are situational, have persistent consequences, express a play style, carry risk against reward, and span short and long term (building structures with different timeframes). [V] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-sid-meier-on-how-to-see-games-as-sets-of-interesting-decisions. The line "a game is a series of interesting decisions" dates to 1989. [R] search summary.
- Game Developer, "Designing Interesting Decisions in Games (And When Not To)": three conditions (consequences matter, outcomes predictable enough to choose on, options non-obvious); dominant options remove choice; too many options cause paralysis; choices players make 95% of the time are "unnecessary clicks." [V] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-interesting-decisions-in-games-and-when-not-to-.
- Soren Johnson on Offworld: stripped defaults from the RTS, kept only economy, price feedback, short matches (see A). On Civ, Sid Meier's original real-time prototype overwhelmed players with too many systems. [R] search summary of https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soren_Johnson and Johnson's Designer Notes. No Johnson essay specifically on "busy-work" in economy design was found.
- Pardo on upkeep (see E): negative incentives need an offsetting bonus framing. [R] search summary, quoted from Designer Notes.
- Against the Storm GDC 2024 postmortem by Michal Ogloziński exists (session listed); content not fetched. [R] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/here-s-how-the-small-team-behind-against-the-storm-kept-project-scope-in-check (search summary).
- Game Maker's Toolkit: no episode on city builders or automation could be confirmed; Factorio appears on Mark Brown's list of 100 games that taught him design. [R] https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-100-games-that-taught-me-game (search summary).
- Mark Rosewater, Day[9] design talks on economy: not found in this pass.

## Recurring mechanisms (no ranking, no recommendation)

- Scarce action slots per turn or per window: Brass (two cards), Through the Ages (civil and military pools), Agricola (one worker per space), Overcooked (more tasks than hands), Pendulum (timers lock sections).
- Timed or deadline-driven pressure: Offworld (30-minute match), Against the Storm (impatience meter), Agricola (fixed harvests), Galaxy Trucker and Pendulum (hourglasses), Mini Metro (overcrowding timer), Frostpunk (scripted cold).
- Adjacency or radius scoring: Islanders, Civ VI districts, Terra Mystica, Suburbia, Backpack Hero, Dorfromantik, SupCom.
- Shared market with price impact: Offworld, Power Grid, AoE2 market, TFT (shared pool is [E]).
- Network or capacity limits: Mini Metro stations, Brass connections, Power Grid connections, Factorio throughput, Settlers roads (earlier file), RoN commerce cap (earlier file).
- Tempo and initiative: Go (sente and gote), Puerto Rico (picker acts first), Brass and Power Grid (turn order by spending or standing), SC2 build-order timings.
- Hidden information and bluffing: Tigris and Euphrates (hidden influence and tiles), Captain Sonar (hidden positions), Offworld black market (opponent unsure what was bought) [R].
- Commitment with delay: Space Alert (programmed ahead, resolved after), Galaxy Trucker (build then survive), AoE4 landmarks (one of two, other locked), Dominion (purchase pays off on later draws), Terra Mystica (round tiles revealed in advance).
- Denial of rival options: Splendor (reserve), Puerto Rico (role taken), Agricola (blocked spaces), Offworld (corner a resource, sabotage), Hive (pinning).
- Weakest-link scoring that forces breadth: Tigris and Euphrates (lowest colour), Agricola (penalty for neglecting a category).
- Negative feedback or upkeep to stop stockpiling: Warcraft III upkeep, Power Grid catch-up order, RoN commerce cap (earlier file), Against the Storm run-end before steamrolling.
- Catch-up or reset by ending the run early: Against the Storm (roguelite reset), Mini Metro (restructure the network).
- Legibility at a glance as a stated design aim: Mini Metro [R]; SC2 Kim on macro being hard to judge in play [V].
- Mandatory support output the partner depends on: KTANE, Lovers, Barotrauma, Foxhole, Savage commander.
