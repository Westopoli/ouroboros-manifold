# Making the economy seat more like chess: options

Written 2026-10-01 for Westley. Surface level. These sit on top of `current_system.md`. He asked for ways to make the economy player think ahead, watch the map, and read what the enemy is doing in his base: a few standard, a few wild. Sources are in the two prior-art files.

What "like chess" means here: a move that costs time to take back, an opponent whose plan can be read from the board, and a reward for seeing two moves further than he does.

---

## Standard

**S1. Counters and retooling.** Unit families counter each other, and a production building is set up for one family at a time. Switching it takes a minute or more and produces nothing meanwhile. The enemy's production buildings show what they are set up for to anyone who gets a look. So the economy player is always choosing what to be making three minutes from now, against what he believes the enemy is making.
- *Think ahead:* the switch delay. *Watch:* the enemy's production buildings. *Source:* StarCraft 2 tech switches; this is also where the Veil and the worker scout fit.

**S2. Prizes on a clock.** Things of value appear on the map at times both sides know in advance: a neutral merchant caravan crossing, a herd arriving, a wreck to salvage, a rich vein exposed by the season. Workers and an escort have to be there when it happens.
- *Think ahead:* sending workers before the prize appears. *Watch:* the map and the clock. *Source:* Age of Empires 4 sacred sites and relics; timed objectives in team games.

**S3. Walls, towers and raid lanes.** The economy player builds the defenses. Enemy raids on workers and shipments come along predictable lanes set by the terrain, and so do his own side's. He places walls, gates and towers to cover his lanes, and reads the enemy's to tell the commander where a raid would hurt.
- *Think ahead:* defenses go up before the raid. *Watch:* both sides' lanes. *Source:* Age of Empires walls; They Are Billions.

**S4. A shared market.** The three resources trade on one market both sides use. Buying raises the price for everyone. A price that climbs for no reason of yours is the enemy buying, which says what he is short of and what he is about to build.
- *Think ahead:* buying before the enemy needs it. *Watch:* the prices. *Source:* Offworld Trading Company; the Age of Empires 2 market.

**S5. Demands from the civilization.** During the mission the civilization asks for things with a deadline: so much of a resource delivered by minute twelve, a building raised, a route kept open. Meeting one pays in shipments or cap; missing one costs standing. Demands are announced ahead.
- *Think ahead:* stockpiling for a known deadline while still feeding the army. *Watch:* the list of what is coming. *Source:* Against the Storm's orders; Agricola's harvests.

## Wild

**W1. The Keystone.** Placement bonuses chain: a building that boosts its neighbours is itself boosted by others. A dense, efficient base therefore has a few buildings whose loss collapses much of the rest. Each economy player builds his own base to hide or spread its keystones, at a cost in efficiency, and studies the enemy's layout to find theirs and tell the commander where to strike.
- *Think ahead:* every placement is also a weakness or a defense. *Watch:* the enemy's base, closely. *Source:* Supreme Commander's adjacency chain reactions, turned into the main contest. Nothing found that does this.

**W2. The Contested Tree.** Technologies are drawn from a pool both sides share. Each technology exists in limited copies; when one side researches it, it becomes scarcer or dearer for the other, and some exist only once. Choosing a technology is also taking it away. Seeing what the enemy took says what is left for him and what he is building toward.
- *Think ahead:* taking what the enemy will want before he can. *Watch:* the enemy's research. *Source:* reserving in Splendor, role denial in Puerto Rico. Not found in a real-time strategy game.

**W3. The Schedule.** Production is laid out on a timeline several minutes ahead, building by building. Slots far out are cheap to change; slots about to start are costly to change. A well-planned schedule runs with nothing idle. The enemy who gets a look at a production building sees its next few slots.
- *Think ahead:* this is literally planning moves in advance. *Watch:* the enemy's schedule, when it can be seen. *Source:* Space Alert's programmed actions. A concrete form of the earlier Two Clocks idea.

**W4. Weather on the move.** The region's environmental trouble is a thing on the map that travels: a storm front, a dust wall, a flood rising up a valley, a solar flare sweeping across. Both sides can see where it is and roughly where it is going. It shuts down what it passes over. The economy player moves workers out of its way, times production to finish before it arrives, and tells the commander when it will be over the enemy's base.
- *Think ahead:* its path is known minutes ahead. *Watch:* the map. *Source:* Northgard's winter, made local and mobile. Fits the decision that regions have their own troubles.

**W5. Agents.** Each economy player has a few agents he can send into the enemy's base or onto his roads: one reads what a building is doing, one slows a works, one diverts a shipment. Each agent is away for minutes and can be caught by watchmen the other side has posted. Posting watchmen takes people from work.
- *Think ahead:* an agent sent now acts minutes later. *Watch:* your own base, for theirs. *Source:* the Offworld Trading Company black market. Blind shipments already make diversion possible.

**W6. Forward works.** Units leave the building that made them and have to walk to the fight. The economy player can build production forward, near the front or at an expansion, where it reinforces fast and is exposed, or keep it home where it is safe and slow. Where the works stand tells the enemy where the next push comes from.
- *Think ahead:* forward works take minutes to build and commit the pair to a direction. *Watch:* where the enemy builds his. *Source:* proxy buildings in StarCraft 2, made the normal way to play.

One from each column would give the seat three different things to look at besides its own base.
