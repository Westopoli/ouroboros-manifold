# The economy seat: a sketch of what the player sees and does

Written 2026-10-01. A sketch for Westley to react to, not a decision. It combines four directions from `economy_game_directions.md` (1 The Stewards, 5 Hands, 3 The Board, 8 The Order Board), adds technology choice to the seat as Westley asked, and expands 11 The Veil into information gathering. Names and numbers are placeholders.

## The four parts

- **Hands.** The population count is a number of crews, say eight at the start. Every action holds one crew for a set time. More housing gives more crews.
- **The Stewards.** Four named officials, each a piece on the map with one power that works only where it stands. They travel the roads at walking speed.
- **The Board.** The base and each settlement have a limited number of lots. A building's yield depends on what is beside it.
- **The Order Board.** A panel of tickets. Each ticket is something the commander needs, with a timer. The commander writes them by pointing at the map; some are written by the game (a wall breached, a garrison captured and waiting for a production type). There are always more tickets than crews. The economy player decides which to fill, and can write tickets back to the commander ("hold the ford two minutes", "scout the east works").

## What is on the screen

The same tactical map the commander sees, with the economy player's own overlay:

- **Top:** the three resources, each with a stock and a rate per minute, and the crew counter (free and total).
- **Left:** four steward portraits. Each shows where the steward is, what it is doing, and how long until it arrives if travelling.
- **Right:** the Order Board, tickets sorted by time left.
- **On the map:** lots drawn as outlines at the base and at settlements; roads with moving carts coloured by resource; a timer ring on anything a crew is working on.
- **When placing a building:** the yield it would have on each open lot, shown before the click.
- **Bottom:** the technology panel and the ledger of what is known about the enemy (see below).

## One minute in the seat

1. A ticket appears: "Armored company, north gate, 2:30". Armor needs Stock, and Stock is short.
2. The Surveyor is forty seconds from the unworked Stock site across the river. Send him. Nothing can be built there until he arrives.
3. Three crews are free. One goes to the smithy to start the armor. One goes to the road toward the Stock site so the haul is shorter when the site opens. The third is held back.
4. A second ticket: "Wall breached, east". This is why the third crew was held. Send it.
5. Housing would add a crew. The best lot for it is beside the market, but that lot is the only one where a foundry would touch both the smithy and the depot. Put the housing on a worse lot and keep the good one.
6. The Foreman is standing at the mill doubling Staple. Staple is fine and the smithy is the bottleneck. Walk him to the smithy: thirty seconds of travel in which he doubles nothing.
7. Expedite the smithy at the moment its cycle turns over, for the full bonus.
8. Tell the commander the armor will be twenty seconds late and the wall will hold.

Nothing in that minute is fast clicking. The skill is in step 3 (holding a crew back), step 5 (keeping the lot) and step 6 (paying travel time now for output later).

## Over a whole mission

- **Opening:** which site first, where the first road goes, which steward goes where. Like an opening in chess, there are known good ones and the enemy can be read from his.
- **Middle:** the commander's fight and the Order Board set the tempo. The significant resources sit opposite the objective, so the pair decides together whether to pay for a road and a guard out there.
- **Late:** settlements that have run steadily for a while manage themselves, and crews are freed for technology and the far sites.

## Technology on the economy seat

Westley, verbatim: "I think that also tech choice should be on the economic player, and that's where the veil can come in".

- The economy player chooses what the pair researches. Technology decides which unit families and upgrades the commander can field.
- Research happens in buildings on lots, holds crews, and takes minutes. It is a commitment with a delay.
- The technology tree branches, and a pair cannot afford every branch in one mission. Choosing a branch is choosing what the commander's army will be good against several minutes from now.
- So the question the economy player is always answering is: what will the enemy field in five minutes, and what do we need by then to beat it. That question cannot be answered without information, which is where the Veil comes in.

## The Veil

**What is hidden.** An enemy who sees one of your buildings from a distance sees only that something is built there, as a plain covered shape. What it is, and what it is researching, stays hidden until someone gets close, or until its first product walks onto the field. Cheap decoys look the same from a distance.

**What cannot be hidden.** Carts on roads, coloured by what they carry. Which sites are being worked. How many crews are moving. A side that hauls a great deal of Prize is researching something.

## Ways to gather information, as options

1. **The commander's eyes.** Any unit that gets close to a veiled building reveals it. The economy player writes a ticket asking for it. This makes the Order Board run both ways.
2. **A fifth steward, the Factor.** A piece that reveals what passes on any road it can see, and identifies a veiled building if it stands near one. It is slow and can be caught, and while it is out, it is not doing anything at home.
3. **Counting carts.** No special action. A player who watches the enemy's roads and knows the recipes can work out what is being built. Pure reading skill.
4. **Watch posts.** A building on a lot with a crew held in it. It sees far, and farther from high ground or an upper layer. Information costs a crew and a lot.
5. **The neutral populace talks.** Neutral settlements trade with both sides. A side that trades with a settlement learns what the other side has been buying there. This gives the contested populace a use for the economy seat without making the seat responsible for winning it.
6. **Captured ledgers.** When the commander takes an enemy settlement or depot, the economy player sees what it was supplying and to where.
7. **Price signals.** If there is any shared market, the price of a resource rising means the enemy is buying it.

## Ways to deny it

- Decoys on spare lots.
- A false convoy: carts sent out carrying nothing of value, coloured as Prize.
- The Warden standing at a building keeps the Factor from reading it.
- Building the real works deep inside the walls costs the best adjacency lots; hiding has a price.

## Why this is the chess

Technology is a slow commitment made on incomplete information against an opponent making the same commitment. Reading him takes crews, a steward, or a favour from the commander, all of which are scarce. Reading him right means the commander meets the enemy's army with the counter already built. The layer lab's finding on match-ups (the reactive follow-up: putting rocks on the enemy's scissors) is the military half of the same contest.
