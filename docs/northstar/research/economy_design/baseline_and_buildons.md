# The economy seat: a classic baseline and fourteen ways to build on it

Written 2026-10-01 for Westley. Surface level. The baseline is his: classic Age of Empires Online and Age of Empires 3, plus the pieces he has named so far. The build-ons draw on `prior_art_resources.md` and `prior_art_economy_gameplay.md`.

The skill he is after, in his words: "In starcraft if you didn't keep an eye on your pop count, you got supply blocked and it took ~30 seconds to fix it, if you were too busy micro'ing and forgot to keep building workers or go to the next tech or keep building production, you ended up behind. That's the kind of dynamic I'm looking for in terms of skill, needing to juggle a couple different things in your mind at once." Nothing on screen reminds the player; the list is in his head.

## The baseline

- **Three resources**, each gathered in its own way (Age of Empires 3's food, wood and coin are the model; the era pack names them).
- **Literal workers.** Trained one at a time at the base's main building. They walk to a source, gather, and are visible and killable.
- **Economic buildings.** Drop-off points near sources, and built sources (the mill and plantation kind) that are slower than natural ones and never run out.
- **Housing** raises the population cap.
- **Placement matters** (from The Board): lots are limited, and a building's yield depends on what is beside it.
- **Economic locations across the map**, with the significant resources opposite the objective.
- **A neutral populace** in settlements on the map. It is contested, and winning it is not the economy player's job.
- **Technology choice** belongs to the economy player.

**What the player already has to hold in his head with only this:**

| Forget this | And this happens |
|---|---|
| to keep training workers | income falls behind for the rest of the mission |
| to build housing before the cap | nothing can be trained until a house finishes |
| to spend | resources pile up doing nothing |
| to rebalance workers across the three resources | one resource floods while another starves |
| to advance technology | the commander fights with last age's units |
| to move on before a natural source runs out | workers stand idle at an empty site |

Each build-on below adds either one more thing to juggle or one more decision with a delay. Each names what goes wrong when it is forgotten.

## Build-ons

**1. Expansions.** A settlement worked hard enough grows into a second base with its own main building, worker production and lots. When to expand, and toward which resource, is the biggest decision of the mission; the rich sites opposite the objective are the tempting, exposed place to do it. *Forget it:* the home sites run dry and income collapses. *(StarCraft 2 bases; Westley's "a whole city can be built around a critical resource".)*

**2. One cap for workers and soldiers.** Workers and army units share the population cap. Every worker is a soldier the commander does not have. *Forget it:* too many workers late and the army is small; too few early and nothing can be afforded. *(StarCraft 2; Battle Realms.)*

**3. Upkeep bands.** Above set army sizes, income drops by a step. The pair chooses between a lean army with a strong economy and a full army that must fight soon. *Forget it:* a large idle army quietly drains the economy. *(Warcraft 3.)*

**4. From foraging to farming.** Natural sources are fast and finite; built sources are slow, permanent and take lots. The timing of the switch is a skill: too early wastes speed, too late leaves a gap. *Forget it:* an income hole in the middle of the mission. *(Age of Empires 3's hunts to mills, mines to plantations.)*

**5. Drop-off and haul distance.** Workers carry what they gather to a drop-off building. As a forest recedes or a site empties from the near end, the walk gets longer and income falls without any alarm. *Forget it:* the same workers bring in less every minute. *(Age of Empires 2 lumber camps; The Settlers.)*

**6. Districts.** A deeper version of the placement rule: economic buildings boost gatherers within a radius, production buildings beside a smithy train faster, housing beside a market yields a trickle of coin. Lots are scarce, so every placement gives something up. *Forget it:* a base that works at two thirds of what the same buildings could give. *(Islanders; Civilization VI districts; Age of Empires 4.)*

**7. Trading posts at neutral settlements.** A post built at a neutral settlement gives what that settlement has: a unique unit for the commander, a technology, or a trickle of one resource. The settlement deals with whichever side holds its ground, which is the commander's fight. *Forget it:* the enemy collects the allies and their units. *(Age of Empires 3 native trading posts.)*

**8. A trade route with visible caravans.** Caravans walk between the base and settlements, or between two settlements. Longer routes pay more and are easier to raid. *Forget it:* a route cut for two minutes before anyone notices. *(Age of Empires 2 trade carts; Rise of Nations caravans; Westley's "trade visibly moving between cities".)*

**9. Shipments from the civilization.** Before the mission the pair picks a small deck of shipments from what the civilization can offer, which depends on its state in the simulation. During the mission shipments are earned and called in: workers, crates, a unit company, a technology. The deck is something the pair owns and improves between missions, which makes it a candidate answer to what they carry forward (Q-159-07). *Forget it:* earned shipments sit uncalled. *(Age of Empires 3 home city.)*

**10. Ages by landmark.** Advancing an age means building one of two landmark buildings; the other is lost for the mission. Each gives a different bonus and opens a different part of the roster. The enemy can see which landmark went up if he scouts it. *Forget it:* stuck an age behind. *(Age of Empires 4 landmarks; Age of Empires 3 politicians.)*

**11. The Veil and the worker scout.** Technology and production buildings are unidentified to the enemy from a distance. The cheapest way to look is to send a worker, who is then not gathering and may not come back. *Forget it:* the commander meets an army nobody saw coming. *(StarCraft 2 worker scouting.)*

**12. One return-to-base ability per era.** A cooldown ability the era pack defines (call extra hands, bless a field, overclock a works) that must be recast by hand. Perfect use gives the 1.1x to 1.2x edge Westley asked for; never using it costs nothing more than that. *Forget it:* a small, steady loss. *(StarCraft 2 inject, chrono boost and MULE, with the size of the reward capped.)*

**13. A season.** A known, recurring hard period (a winter, a dry season, a blockade) in which one resource stops and consumption rises. The date is known from the start. *Forget it:* starvation at a moment the enemy can also predict. *(Northgard winter; Agricola harvests.)*

**14. Workers under raid.** Workers at exposed sites can be killed or driven off. The economy player can ring them into nearby buildings, which keeps them alive and stops their work, or arm them as a weak militia. Working the rich sites opposite the objective means asking the commander for cover. *Forget it:* a raid kills a quarter of the workforce. *(Age of Empires town bell; Battle Realms.)*

## Fusions

- **The expansion game:** 1 + 4 + 5 + 14. The economy moves outward across the map as near sources empty, and the commander has to cover it.
- **The city game:** 6 + 10 + 2. A dense base where placement and landmark choice decide what the pair is good at.
- **The trade game:** 7 + 8 + 9. Income from relationships and routes more than from gathering; the neutral populace matters most here.
- **The reading game:** 10 + 11. Technology as a hidden commitment, scouting as its counter.
- **The pressure game:** 2 + 3 + 13. Caps, upkeep and a deadline, all pulling against each other.

## Not included, and why

- **The Order Board.** Dropped by Westley: what the commander needs is something the economy player keeps in his head.
- **Crews as the population count (Hands) and The Stewards.** Replaced by literal workers in this baseline. An explorer-style single unit could return later as a build-on if wanted.
