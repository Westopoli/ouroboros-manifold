# What the economy player does: fifteen directions

Written 2026-10-01 for Westley. Surface level on purpose: many directions, none in depth. Sources and confidence tags are in `prior_art_economy_gameplay.md`.

Fixed for all of them (Westley, 2026-10-01): three resources; the economy player builds buildings and manages a population count or a variation of it; the work is skill-based like combat and needs thinking ahead; it is not busy-work. Economic locations are spread over the map and the significant resources sit opposite the objective. Speed of hand is worth about 1.1x to 1.2x.

## What the research says separates skill from busy-work

- **Fewer actions than things worth doing.** Brass gives two actions a turn, Agricola one worker per space, and Overcooked's designers made sure there were always more jobs than hands. The choice of what not to do is the game.
- **A commitment that pays off later.** Dominion purchases show up in later draws, Space Alert plans resolve after they are locked, Galaxy Trucker ships are built and then tested.
- **An opponent who can be denied.** Reserving in Splendor, taking a role in Puerto Rico, cornering a resource in Offworld Trading Company.
- **A state that can be read at a glance.** Mini Metro's stated aim; StarCraft 2's designers said of their own macro abilities that nobody can tell how well they are being done.
- **Something that stops stockpiling.** Warcraft 3's lead designer said that without upkeep there was "no economic tension".
- **No choice that is made the same way 95% of the time.** Those are clicks, not decisions.

Chess has all of these except the timer: one move per turn, every move changes the future, and the opponent is the puzzle.

## A. Games of position

**1. The Stewards (an invented piece game).** The population count is a handful of named officials, each a piece with its own rule: the Surveyor opens a site, the Foreman doubles a building's output where he stands, the Broker links two settlements for trade, the Warden keeps people from leaving under attack. A piece acts only where it stands and takes time to travel the roads. The enemy has the same pieces. Skill: placing few pieces where they will matter in three minutes. *Closest to "a game like chess inside the economy". Prior art: none found as a whole.*

**2. Stones (territory and initiative).** Outposts are cheap and project influence over the ground around them; a resource site works for whichever side has more influence there, and the commander's units count as heavy influence while they stand on it. Skill: taking ground with the fewest outposts, and making moves the enemy has to answer so the next move is yours. *Go's influence and initiative.*

**3. The Board (adjacency).** The base and each settlement have limited lots. Every building's yield depends on what is beside it and within its radius, shown before placing. Skill: leaving room now for the building that arrives two tiers later, and not building a chain the enemy can break by destroying one piece. *Islanders, Civilization VI districts, Supreme Commander adjacency.*

**4. The Yard (packing).** Buildings are shapes and the walled footprint is small. Fitting more inside the walls is the skill; enlarging the walls is costly and gives the commander more to defend. *Backpack Hero; Halo Wars' limited base slots.*

## B. Games of scarce moves

**5. Hands (real-time worker placement).** The population count is a number of crews. Every action on the map (work a site, raise a building, run a convoy, muster a company) holds one crew for a set time. Some stations on the map are neutral and take only one side's crew at a time. Skill: the order crews are sent in, and occupying the station the enemy needs next. *Agricola, Pendulum.*

**6. The Deck.** The economy is a deck of order cards. Every cycle the player draws a hand and plays it: build, haul, muster, trade. Each building adds its card to the deck, and the deck's makeup is the economy. Units mustered put a weak card in the deck (soldiers do not work), so a large army slows the economy by itself. Skill: building a deck that draws well in four cycles. *Dominion.*

**7. Edicts (role selection).** Each cycle the player picks one edict from a short shared list (harvest, build, conscript, trade, fortify). It applies in full to his side and, weakly, to the enemy, and the enemy cannot pick the same one that cycle. Skill: choosing what helps you more than it helps them. *Puerto Rico.*

**8. The Order Board (the kitchen).** The commander's needs appear as tickets with timers: forty line troops at the north gate in two minutes, a wall on the ridge, a bridge repaired. There are always more tickets than capacity. Skill: triage, preparing ahead for the ticket that has not been written yet, and telling the commander what will not arrive. *Overcooked.*

## C. Games against the other economy

**9. The Exchange (one market, two sides).** The three resources trade on one market shared by both sides. Buying raises the price for everyone and selling lowers it. Skill: reading what the enemy army will need from what has been scouted, and buying it first. *Offworld Trading Company, Power Grid.*

**10. The Muster Shop (draft and bank).** The roster's 20 to 40 unit types are offered a few at a time from a pool both sides draw from. The economy player buys offers for the commander, pays to refresh them, or banks resources that earn interest. Skill: when to stop saving and spend, and taking the unit the enemy is collecting. *Auto-battler economies.*

**11. The Veil (hidden builds).** What the economy player builds is hidden from the enemy until scouted, and he can build decoys. He reads the enemy's economy from what is visible: convoys on roads, smoke, which sites are worked. Skill: bluffing a composition and reading the other side's. *Captain Sonar, the Offworld black market.*

## D. Games against the clock

**12. The Line (network triage).** Demand on the roads grows through the mission and the player is always given slightly less capacity than he needs. Skill: tearing up a network that used to work as soon as it stops fitting. *Mini Metro.*

**13. Calm and Storm (build, then survive).** The mission alternates between quiet windows and announced shocks: a raid, a hard season, a demand from the civilization. What was built in the calm is tested in the storm. Skill: building for the test that is coming, not the one that just passed. *Galaxy Trucker, They Are Billions, Against the Storm.*

**14. Stages (locked choices).** The base advances through stages. At each stage the player picks one of two landmark buildings and loses the other for the mission. Upcoming rewards are shown ahead, so builds are planned toward them. Skill: committing early to a plan and reading which plan the enemy committed to. *Age of Empires 4 landmarks, Terra Mystica round tiles.*

**15. The Converter (a puzzle that is the economy).** The three resources are produced by solving a small continuous puzzle (matching, routing, sorting) whose board is fed by the sites the side holds. A better solve converts more. Skill: the puzzle itself. *Puzzle Quest. The furthest from a city on a battlefield, listed for range.*

## What "population count" could be

| Form | What it limits | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| Crews | how many things can be done at once | direction 5 |
| Named officials | where special actions can happen | direction 1 |
| Supply cap | army size, raised by housing | StarCraft 2 |
| One pool split between work and war | every soldier is a missing worker | Battle Realms, Trades and Arms |
| Upkeep bands | income falls as the army grows | Warcraft 3 |
| Deck size | how often a given order comes up | direction 6 |

## Small rules that fit under any direction

- **Weakest of three.** Output is set by the lowest of the three resources, so neglecting one is punished. *Tigris and Euphrates.*
- **Upkeep.** A larger standing army lowers income, so banking an army is a choice. *Warcraft 3.*
- **Expedite with a timing window.** A manual boost that is worth more if triggered at the right moment; the home of the 1.1x to 1.2x edge.

Most of these combine. A position game (1 to 4) can supply the map and buildings, a scarce-moves game (5 to 8) the minute-to-minute, and one of the opponent games (9 to 11) the contest with the other side.
