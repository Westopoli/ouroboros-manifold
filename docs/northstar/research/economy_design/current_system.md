# The current economic system

As decided by Westley through 2026-10-01. This file states what the economy is now; it is rewritten when a decision changes it. His words are verbatim in `README.md`. The bible holds it in E-22 Economy and Bases (S-167 to S-182), with the paired mode in E-19 S-159, strongpoints and weather in E-15, and terrain and settlements in E-17; the epics win where this file differs. Nothing here is being built.

The target is 2x to 3x the system complexity of a StarCraft 2 economy. Westley measured the first half of this (the seat, what the player runs, the map) at about 1.2x to 1.4x; the section "Thinking ahead" was added to close the gap. He calls the whole a strong foundation.

## The seat

- An optional second player runs the economy for a commander, in the paired queue. The two share one tactical map.
- The skill is holding several things in mind at once. Forgetting one puts the pair behind. Nothing on screen lists what the commander needs.
- Speed of hand is worth a small bounded edge, about 1.1x to 1.2x.

## What the economy player runs

- **Three resources**, each gathered in its own way. Classic Age of Empires Online and Age of Empires 3 are the model.
- **Literal workers**, trained at the base, who walk to a source and gather.
- **Economic buildings**, including drop-off points. The farther workers walk to a drop-off, the less they bring in.
- **Building placement and choice.** Lots are limited and what stands beside a building changes what it yields.
- **One population cap** shared by workers and soldiers. It can be upgraded a long way, up to thousands.
- **Production.** The economy player runs the production buildings and makes the units the commander receives. What to make is agreed by talking.
- **Technology choice.**
- **Expansions.** Settlements out on the map can be grown into further bases. The main base never runs dry, but a side that stays home is out-produced and overrun.
- **Shipments from the civilization.** They travel to the base and the side cannot see them on the way. A shipment that does not arrive gives no explanation.

## The map

- Every side has a base of operations it must protect. It is not always a city. It receives resources from elsewhere, generates them on site, or both, depending on the simulation and the mission.
- Economic locations are spread across the map.
- The significant resources sit opposite the objective, so a side picks one or fights for both.
- A neutral populace lives in settlements on the map and is contested. Winning it is not the economy player's job.
- Seasons and weather. Specific regions have specific environmental troubles.

## Bases and objectives

- **The balance.** Spending is balanced between economy, technology and army, as in StarCraft.
- **What kind of base.** The mission archetype and the supply distance in the simulation together decide it.
- **When a base falls.** A base is occupied, not destroyed; the taker gets its production and people, and it can be retaken. A side that still holds an expansion or forward base falls back to it. Once the original main base has been lost, the best result is partial failure.
- **Resources.** Base, objective and the significant resources are usually three separate places. How much there is follows the simulation's situation and is slanted by it; the player always has the means to do the mission. Ignoring the resources gets a side overrun unless it rushes.
- **Strongpoints.** The reinforcement structures sit at the slopes between height bands. Holding one holds the way up or down and gives production on that band.

## Thinking ahead

- **Counters and retooling.** Unit families counter each other. A production building is set up for one family at a time, and changing what it makes takes a while. Specific unit roles need specific resources, so heavy gathering of one resource shows which role a side is making more of, and the other side needs the counter. To be explored deeper.
- **The schedule.** Production is planned ahead on a timeline. Changing what production is focused on takes time, and an enemy who sees a building can see what it is doing.
- **Disguise.** Because production is slow to change and visible, players have a way to disguise or hide their buildings.
- **Walls, towers and raid lanes.** The economy player builds the defenses. Raids on workers and shipments come along lanes set by the terrain.
- **Weather on the move.** The region's environmental trouble travels across the map where both sides can see it, and shuts down what it passes over.
- **Forward bases.** Production can be built forward, near the front or at an expansion, where it reinforces fast and is exposed. Where it stands shows where the next push comes from.

## With nobody in the seat

- **Doctrines.** The rules run the economy by a named doctrine. The player can change the doctrine on the fly to adapt to what the enemy is doing.
- **How good.** Perfect hands, no foresight: the rules never idle a worker, hit the cap or float resources, and they do not scout, read the enemy, counter-pick technology or disguise.
- **The commander's hand.** One dial for the balance between economy, technology and army, and asking by pointing at the map (expand here, fortify this, I need siege there).
- **Units.** The rules pick the mix and the commander can override; the rules also shift production to counter what his forces have seen. The player can turn either off.
- **The enemy.** The same rules run the enemy's economy. The player chooses whether the enemy's economy also scouts, counters and disguises.
- If a human economy player drops out of a mission, the same rules take his seat.

## Long-term scale

Thousands of units and workers on one map, a busy screen, made possible by efficient simulation and visuals. Recorded in the bible hub under "Reference Hardware".
