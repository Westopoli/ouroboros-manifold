# Prior art: resource systems in strategy games

Digest for the economy options pass. Facts only; no recommendations. Captured 2026-10-01.

Tags: [V] VERIFIED, a fetched page said it. [R] REPORTED, a search-result summary or secondary page. [E] ESTIMATED, from model knowledge, not sourced this session. Many wikis (Fandom, Liquipedia, AusGamers) blocked fetching, so numbers on those are [R] or [E]. Wikipedia fetches returned thin gameplay detail for several games.

## Age of Empires III
- Three resources: food, wood, coin. Settlers gather them; shipments from the "Home City" deliver units, upgrades or resources, unlocked at XP thresholds earned by building, training and killing. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Empires_III
- Trading posts on native camps give alliances and unique units. [V] same page. Posts on a trade route generate XP by default (more XP gives more shipments); after the Stagecoach upgrade they can give resources instead. [R] https://aoe3.heavengames.com/cgi-bin/forums/display.cgi?action=st&fn=1&tn=37257. Route XP rate varies by map (Yucatan 1.36 XP/s, Silk Road 0.60) [R, same URL].
- Industrial Age factories "automatically produce resources or artillery". [V] Wikipedia page above. Mills/plantations as infinite sources: [E], not fetched.
- Asian Dynasties add "export", a fourth resource only Asian civs get, spent at the Consulate on foreign troops and techs. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Empires_III:_The_Asian_Dynasties. Export accrues automatically while villagers gather, slowly; its rate can be raised at the Consulate at the cost of other gather rates. [R] search summary (GameSpot hands-on, gamefaqs) via https://www.gamespot.com/articles/age-of-empires-iii-the-asian-dynasties-hands-on-wonders-export-and-deeper-strategic-decisions/1100-6178080/
- Tension: gathering rate versus XP/shipment rate; trade-post timing (too early leaves you weak, too late falls behind) [R, heavengames URL]. Advanced Trading Post called "the most unbalanced card" by a forum poster [R, heavengames search result]. Trade route and posts are physical map objects that can be contested [R, same thread: "map control", walls over routes discussed].
- Complaints: Eurogamer felt shipments excessive ("Stop with the gifts!... flick to a home city screen every few minutes"); Yahoo!/Eurogamer called the base economics lacking depth next to other RTS. [V] Wikipedia AoE3 page.

## Halo Wars 1 / 2
- HW1: a single resource, supplies, from supply pads whose count sets output; tech levels via reactor (UNSC) or a single temple (Covenant, lose it and lose all tech). Base space is deliberately limited, so resource buildings trade against production buildings. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_Wars
- HW2: supplies (pads or battlefield pickups) plus power from generators, which gates advanced structures and units; extra bases need resources and area control. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_Wars_2
- Simplification was intentional for console control; reviewers noted accessibility but limited depth for veterans. [V] same two pages.

## Company of Heroes 1 / 2
- Three resources. CoH1: manpower (constant stream, units and most buildings), fuel (tech and heavy units), munitions (abilities, doctrine powers; only from captured points). Territory income: strategic point +3 manpower/min; low/medium/high resource points +5/+10/+16 manpower plus fuel or munitions. [V] https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails?id=121561752
- Supply connection: points only pay if joined to the HQ by an unbroken zone of influence; enemy capture of a "cut-off" territory (on roads, valleys) breaks income. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_of_Heroes_2 and the Steam guide above.
- CoH2 changed this: manpower is fixed-start with upkeep drain per unit, fuel and munitions flow from territory and caches. [V] https://steamcommunity.com/app/231430/discussions/0/666827316124057734/ (forum poster, so treat as [R] for design intent).
- No physical goods move; the "line" is a territory-graph rule, raided by taking tiles. Tension: forward points raise income but are exposed; capturing is done by combat infantry, so economy and front are the same activity.
- Complaints: guide warns that floating manpower is "the most dangerous mistake" [V]; CoH2 "very poorly documented" [V forum]; PC Gamer on CoH2 "huge, powerful and just a little bit broken" [V, Wikipedia CoH2].

## Supreme Commander / Planetary Annihilation
- Mass and energy. Mass from extractors on limited deposits, or mass fabricators that convert energy; energy from generators anywhere solid. Reclaim of wrecks, rocks and trees gives instant resources. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Commander_(video_game)
- Flow model: storage is capped, overflow is wasted, shortage slows every consumer proportionally; energy shortfall disables shields. [V] same page. Tiered extractors give non-linear returns but are cost-inefficient early. [R] https://supcom.fandom.com/wiki/Economy (via search summary).
- Adjacency: structures beside generators use less energy, factories beside fabricators/generators build cheaper; the cost is chain-reaction vulnerability when one dies. [V] Wikipedia SupCom page.
- Resources do not travel; they are abstract flow. Raiding means killing extractors.
- SupCom 2 simplified the economy; critics said it "solved the accessibility issues of the first game, bought primarily by people who didn't want them solved"; an October 2010 update reworking how construction was paid for was "widely praised". [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Commander_2
- PA: metal and energy extractors, planets with differing resource distribution, so expansion spans worlds; a reviewer said multi-world control "breaks apart". [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_Annihilation

## StarCraft 2
- Minerals, gas, supply cap [E for supply role]. Workers mine patches; saturation limits per patch [E]. Legacy of the Void (patch 4.0): starting workers 6 to 12, half the patches in a base cut from 1500 to 900, MULE return 270 to 225 minerals, geysers 2000 to 2250. [R] search summary of https://starcraft.fandom.com/wiki/StarCraft_II_version_history/Patch_4.0.0 and Liquipedia 4.0.0. Wikipedia confirms resource quantities fell and worker spawning rose. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II:_Legacy_of_the_Void
- Macro mechanics: Terran MULE (temporary fast miner), Zerg queen larva inject, Protoss chrono boost. [R] https://kotaku.com/blizzard-is-quietly-making-huge-changes-to-starcraft-ii-1722892531 (search summary). Exact rates not fetched [E: MULE roughly 225 minerals over its life; inject 3 larvae; chrono +50% speed].
- Tension: army money versus expansion/worker count; macro mechanics versus attention on the fight.
- Complaints: Blizzard's own beta statement (David Kim, battle.net forum) that macro mechanics are "difficult to do, and not many people can really tell how well someone is doing it" and the stated goals of "more action, micro... less downtime". [V] Kotaku. Two options floated: nerfed autocast, or remove chrono and MULE with autocast, weaker larva. [V] same. Pros split: qxc "least exciting", Morrow thought Protoss/Zerg changes "go too far". [V] https://www.pcgamesn.com/starcraft-ii/starcraft-2-legacy-of-the-void-patch-removes-macro-mechanics-pros-respond
- Recent: patch 5.0.16 (May 2026) cut starting workers 12 to 8; reverted in 5.0.17 after backlash ("too many drastic changes in rapid succession"). [V] https://pcgamesn.com/starcraft-2/update-starting-worker-change-reverted

## The Settlers (II / III / 7)
- Physical goods chains: wood, stone, food, mining, smelting; goods carried flag to flag along roads in I/II; adjustable transport priority at flags; soldiers need swords, shields, beer; no direct control of settlers. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Settlers_II
- Settlers III dropped mandatory roads (serfs path freely); marketplaces and ships for trade; mixed reception (71%), "each mission is almost exactly the same". [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Settlers_III and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Settlers_(video_game_series)
- Settlers 7: storehouses as hubs, Victory Points for varied strategies (most coins, largest army, trading outposts). [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Settlers_7:_Paths_to_a_Kingdom
- Goods physically travel and can be cut or blocked at a road; defense is automatic.
- Complaints: 7 had "a lack of feedback" tracing failures (Game Informer) and a water shortage in an isolated area could "bring your entire economy crunching to a halt" (Eurogamer). [V] same page. Rise of an Empire, so simplified "even the worst settlement somehow runs fine". [V] series page.

## Anno 1800
- Production chains, population tiers with needs, regions, trade routes by ship between Old and New World. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_1800
- Goods move on ships along routes; routes are physical but combat integration is separate. [V]/[E]
- Complaints: lack of auto-balancing between islands, cargo delivery delays by late game, hard to audit chains, UI needs "a dozen extra clicks". [R] search summaries of Steam threads, e.g. https://steamcommunity.com/app/916440/discussions/0/3380536561710906714/ and https://primagames.com/featured/anno-1800-review

## Factorio (transferable only)
- Belts, trains, circuit networks and blueprints automate raw to product flow; biters attack in response to pollution; peaceful mode exists. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factorio
- Transferable ideas, labelled [E]: the player's build is the economy; blueprints cut repeat labour; pollution ties economy output to enemy aggression. Not sourced further.

## Offworld Trading Company
- 13 resources (water, aluminum, iron, silicon, carbon and more); prices fluctuate by supply and demand; black-market sabotage; victory is buying majority stock in rivals; no armies. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offworld_Trading_Company
- Randomly generated maps and HQ choice shift supply/demand; boom/crash random events. [R] search summary, https://www.gamewatcher.com/interviews/offworld-trading-company-interview/12129 (not fetched). Designer Notes episode page fetched but had no transcript. [V on absence]

## Northgard
- Food, wood, krowns (money). Map is territory tiles that need scouting; each tile has building limits and special resources (iron, stone, fish, grain). [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northgard
- Winter: farms stop producing food and wood consumption rises sharply. [V] same page. Winter described as the main seasonal stress test; low stocks cause starvation and cascade effects. [R] Steam guides via search.
- Villagers are re-assigned between jobs; krowns buy upgrades, soldiers and market trades. [R]

## Rise of Nations
- Six resources: food, wood, metal, wealth, knowledge, oil; all infinite. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rise_of_Nations, https://ron.heavengames.com/faq/official/
- Commerce limit caps total gather rate per 30 s (starts at 70, raised through Library research); overflow is wasted; knowledge is exempt. [R] search summary of the RoN Fandom/Heaven Games pages. The FAQ states collection is limited by the commerce track "regardless of how many citizens... [V]".
- Caravans carry wealth along routes; count limited by cities (usually one per nation) [V FAQ]. 32 rare resources raise basic collection or give bonuses if a Merchant is placed there [V FAQ].
- Borders define where you build and exploit; units in enemy/neutral territory take attrition. [V FAQ]
- Citizens left idle seek work themselves. [V FAQ]

## Dawn of War 1
- Requisition and power, not gathered by units. Requisition flows from HQ plus captured strategic points; power from generators (six per HQ) and slag deposits. [R] search summary of Fandom pages https://dow.fandom.com/wiki/Listening_Post_(Space_Marines); strategic-point capture also [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_of_War
- Listening post fortification doubles requisition rate (+6 to +12); sources decay over time, so holding more points is required. [R]
- Tension: territory spread versus defending it; infantry captures, so units double as workers.

## Total Annihilation: Kingdoms / Battle Realms
- TA:K: single resource, mana, from lodestones at special sites; reclaim of debris, trees, rocks gives instant mana. [R] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Annihilation:_Kingdoms (search summary).
- Battle Realms: rice, water, horses (gathered by peasants); yin/yang from combat. Peasants are produced free and automatically, at a rate inversely tied to army size, and are the only buildable unit; military buildings convert peasants into warriors for rice and water. [R] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_Realms (search summary). Distinctive: worker and soldier are the same pool, so army size trades against gatherer count.

## Foxhole (from existing note)
- docs/legacy/games/foxhole.md is a market/community card and has almost no economy mechanics. What it holds: players self-organise into logistics ("logi") roles, voluntarily taking the least glamorous job, and the 2022 strike is a case of players treating logistics as owned labour. [V in-repo, itself citing public record].
- Mechanics from outside the note: raw resources (scrap, components, coal, sulfur) are refined to Bmats/Rmats, then manufactured; every spawn consumes a supply item; goods move physically by truck and container. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxhole_(video_game); salvage mines run automatically when fuelled; resource container holds 5000 [R] https://foxhole.wiki.gg/wiki/Salvage_Mine (search summary).
- The L.O.G.I. group (1,800 members) struck for 49 days over logistics-system reform; ended with Update 48. [V] Wikipedia Foxhole page.

## Sins of a Solar Empire 2 (from existing note)
- docs/legacy/games/sins_of_a_solar_empire_2.md covers market positioning only. It has no economy mechanics. Mechanics here are from outside it.
- Credits, metal, crystal. Credits mostly from population tax (0.025 credits/s per population); metal and crystal from planet type and extractors; trade ports earn credits by linking planets/stars; logistics slots cap orbital structures per planet. [R] https://wiki.sinsofasolarempire2.com/space/SSEFW/1740898307 and the Sins 1 wiki pages (search summaries; some values may be Sins 1 data).

## Cross-cutting 1: asymmetric co-op, economy seat
- Savage: one commander per team plays RTS; up to ten NPC workers; gold also earned by players from kills; poor commander can sink the team "no matter how good the players are". [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savage:_The_Battle_for_Newerth; [R] Giant Bomb wiki quote via search. Matches could not start until commanders were chosen on some servers [V].
- Natural Selection 2: commander has top-down view, builds, researches and supports with team resources; players spend separate personal resources on gear; both streams come from collection structures. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Selection_2; the team/personal split [R] Unknown Worlds forum via search. Reception/commander shortage: not found.
- Nuclear Dawn: one random commander per team, builds infrastructure and designates spawns. PC Gamer: "no way to practice the commander role outside a live game." [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Dawn
- Eximius: commander does base, research, unit production, support abilities; team shares one base, resources and tech. [R] https://bitsummit.org/en/game/eximius-seize-the-frontline/ (search summary). Wikipedia fetch 404.
- SC2 Archon Mode: two players share one base, units and resources; either can do anything; a partner can "focus purely on the buildings and expansions" while the other leads the army. [V] https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/16658015/legacy-of-the-void-archon-mode for sharing; split example [R] GamesRadar search summary. Blizzard text says the split is left to the pair ("how well will you divvy it all up").
- AoE "Regicide" and team-shared-control: not researched; no source fetched. Unverified.
- Recurring pattern across these: the economy seat decides outcomes yet is hard to learn or practice, and depends on a human being there; none of these sources discuss a rules-run fallback.

## Cross-cutting 2: bounded APM edge by design
- SC2 macro abilities each give a bounded, repeatable bonus (temporary miner, extra larvae, faster production) rather than unbounded scaling [E: values]. Zerg inject must be kept up whether the game lasts five minutes or two hours. [R] search summary of TL.net.
- Designer-statement candidates: Browder, 2010, "we want the players to go back to their base... instead of relying on an automatic process" [R: surfaced in a search summary, not traced to a verified page]; Browder, "Some games favor a macro gameplay, and some games favor a micro gameplay. Our goals are to have both be possible." [V] https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2010/04/08/an-extensive-interview-with-starcraft-ii-design-director.aspx
- No fetched designer statement caps the size of the reward for speed. The nearest is Kim's: macro mechanics are hard to judge in play and "not many people can really tell how well someone is doing it" [V Kotaku], which is about legibility, not capping. The Blizzard "Mental Checklist" and Liquipedia Macro pages could not be fetched.
- Other examples: Rise of Nations' commerce limit caps raw economy regardless of worker count [V FAQ]; Battle Realms ties peasant spawn rate to army size [R]; Dawn of War makes requisition decay and bounds generators at six per HQ [R].
