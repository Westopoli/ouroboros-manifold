# Report: covering the two blind spots of the lab's firing count

Everything below was read from `origin/main` (c7961d75) with `git show`; the probe fights ran on a scratch archive of that commit in `scratchpad/agent-three/repo`, library built by the lab's own `engine.build_library` (13.8 s). Nothing in the repository was edited.

## What the engine actually checks before a shot, against what the lab counts

The engine's eligibility to fire is four tests, in `csrc/unit.c`: the target's layer offset is inside the attacker type's reach (`om_tac_in_reach`, unit.c:572-577); the distance is within attack range times the signed-offset range multiplier (`om_tac_in_range`, unit.c:1714-1719, reading `OM_TP_RANGE_UP_n/DOWN_n` through `om_tac_offset_param`, unit.c:579-583); the target is in the side's visible set (`om_tac_may_act_on` → `om_tac_faction_sees`, unit.c:1163-1178, which is any living friendly's `om_unit_can_see` or `om_fog_faction_sees`, that is the union of the side's sight plus live ping reveals, fog.c:486-500); and the line of fire is clear (`om_combat_can_shoot`, tactical_move.c:2582-2612, which is line of fire only: no range, no reach, no sight, per its own comment at 2583-2588). Damage is then scaled by `OM_TP_DAMAGE_UP_n/DOWN_n` (unit.c:1016-1020). Cross-layer sight is sensor radius times the `OM_TP_VISION_UP_n/DOWN_n` factor times the emission factor (`om_fog_can_see_at`, fog.c:753-781; factors at 716-719; emission 742-749).

The lab's count (`scripts/skilllab/firing.py:13-24`) tests two of the four: distance within the plain attack range (line 21) and `can_shoot` (line 21), and skips any enemy on another layer (line 18). No sight. The weighted count (firing.py:62-74) is a third definition: it counts only the unit's current engine target (line 66), does apply reach (lines 70-72), still no sight, and weights by the unit's own damage rate (lines 55-59). Conditions C1 and C2 read `firing_unit_seconds` (evidence.py:44-46, conditions.py:46-48 and 83), which comes from the unweighted count (fight.py:349), so the weighted series reaches no condition today.

Probe (scratch build, 16 Vanguard-clones with reach 0 up 1 down on layer 1 against 16 Vanguards on layer 0, both attack-moving into each other, 400 ticks, one seed):

## A. ABLE TO FIRE

**What the toy counted** (`toys/layerlab/SPEC.md:164`): a unit can fire on a tick when it is alive, not winding, and acquisition gave it a target "in range, seen when `fire_requires_vision` is on, eligible under the halfway rule, inside the arc when the gate is on, and the unit not under an unarrived MOVE order"; counted after acquisition, every tick. So the toy's definition already had sight, layers (halfway rule) and an arc; the harness dropped all three (harness spec clause 10, skilllab-harness-spec.md:25: "its own layer until the engine cascade lands reach").

**Candidate A1: re-derive eligibility in Python from engine queries (side sight, reach, offset range, line of fire).** Per sample, per living unit u on side s: let `seen` be `engine.visible_enemies(w, s, living enemies)` (engine.py:208-209, which is `om_player_can_see` → `om_fog_faction_sees`, fog.c:610-617 and 486-500: the side's union of sight plus ping reveals, R-093-02). u is able to fire when some e in `seen` has layer offset within `profile_reach(type of u)` (engine.py:177-181), lies within `attack_range × OM_TP_RANGE_{UP,DOWN}_|offset|` (read once per fight with `get_param`), and `can_shoot(u, e)` is true. This is the probe's count.
Pros: every knob the sweep turns (vision factors, emitting, ping, reach, range multiplier) is in the test; it needs no engine change; it is cheap (32 `om_player_can_see` calls per sample plus the existing pair loop); it is one definition for both predictions. Cons: it re-states the engine's acquisition rule in Python, so any later engine change (the arc, the cone) must be mirrored or the count drifts; it ignores the engine's cohort stagger (R-087-08) and backswing, so it says "could fire", not "did fire", which is the toy's definition too. Risks: `om_player_can_see_unit` returns 1 when player fog is explicitly off (fog.c:615), so the lab must never call `om_visibility_set_player_fog(w, 0)` (it does not today). Evidence: probe table above; fog.c:610-617; unit.c:572-583, 1714-1719.

**Candidate A2: count units whose engine target is a living enemy it can hit now (the engine's own decision).** Per sample, u counts when `unit_target(u)` is alive and passes reach, offset range and `can_shoot`; this is today's weighted count's eligibility (firing.py:66-72) with sight implied, because the engine never assigns a target the side cannot see (unit.c:1176-1178, 1998, 1748-1749).
Pros: nothing re-derived; the arc and cone enter automatically when the engine gates targeting on them; cannot drift. Cons: a unit with a dead or just-dropped target reads 0 for up to 4 ticks (cohort re-acquire, R-087-08), so the count is systematically low and noisy in a way that depends on unit ids; a unit holding a target that is in range but behind a blocker reads 0 even though another enemy is open (R-087-09 makes it seek a spot for 1 s first); return-fire targets outside the side's sight (R-087-06, still ungated per audit 2026-10-04 item 7) would count as "able". Risks: it partly restates the outcome: who has a target is who is shooting, and the toy found the whole-fight form "partly restates the outcome" (history_2026-09-30_to_10-01.md:459). Evidence: firing.py:62-74; test_firing_weighted_target.py:1-2.

**Candidate A3: ask the engine for eligibility through one new query** (`om_combat_can_engage(w, attacker, target)` = reach, offset range, side sight, line of fire, later arc), a contract piece.
Pros: one truth, no drift, the arc lands in one place. Cons: a header change is its own contract piece first (CLAUDE.md, Architecture rules), so the sweep waits on it; the engine's internal functions are static (unit.c:572, 1714) and would need exposing.

**Recommendation: A1 now, with A3 queued as the contract piece that lands with the firing arc.** A1 is the toy's definition on the real engine, it costs no engine change, and the probe shows it sees every knob. When the arc and cone land (one engine piece, Q-100-01 and Q-082-06), the arc test is added to A1 as `facing` from `unit_facing` (engine.py:275-276, `om_unit_get_facing`) against the bearing to e within half the arc width, and the same day A3 should replace the Python re-derivation so the lab cannot drift from the engine. Until A3 lands, a parity test must assert, on every sampled tick of the three controls, that no unit the count calls unable actually fired that tick (the trace already records `unit_target` per tick, fight.py:245), which catches drift the cheap way.

Specifics the count must state:
- *Reach by type and the offset multiplier* enter as above; same layer is always reachable at multiplier 1.0 (unit.c:573, 586).
- *Sight is the side's*, `om_player_can_see`, not the unit's `om_unit_can_see` (engine.py:211-213 `seen_by_sight` is per own unit and ignores ping reveals; it is the right query for "found by true vision", not for "may fire", R-093-02, E-12:91; R-008-04, E-01:158).
- *Emitting and the ping* enter through sight only; nothing else in the count changes.
- *A unit in transition* has one effective layer: the engine swaps `layer` at half the transfer (tactical_move.c:188, 2414) and `om_unit_get_layer` returns that effective layer (unit.c:3057-3060), so the count reads `unit_layer` and needs no special case; that is R-004-02 (E-01:84) as built. The count should also record `transition_progress` (engine binding missing; `om_unit_transition_progress` exists, tactical_move.c:3159) so a page can show "fired while crossing" when a layer play is measured.
- *The arc* (once built): a unit is able only if the target bearing is inside the arc about `unit_facing`; a flanked unit's behaviour (turn or not) is Westley's (Q-100-01, register:516) and the count must not assume it: it counts "inside the arc now".

**Both predictions change, on one eligibility.** Count-only is the A1 count. Type-weighted is the same count with each able unit weighted by `OM_TP_CATMULT_<attacker category>_<target category>` (read from the world, unit.c's matrix defaults in pp_contract.md:106-114, R-081-01 E-11:34) against the category of the enemy it would fire at: the nearest eligible enemy under the count's own rule (the game's target rule, R-087-03), or the engine target when one is set. That is the approved design's "category multiplier against its target's category" (history:344; transcript line 704 step 3) and it stays ignorant of hit points, damage per shot and interval. Today's `unit_weight` (firing.py:55-59, base damage over interval) must go: it weights by the unit's own stats, which the trap forbids ("deliberately ignorant of stats", history:340), and it is not type against type at all. With Vanguard-only sides the two predictions are equal by construction, which is the right null. One more thing from the toy to keep: a whole-fight sum partly restates the outcome; the opening window (first 40 ticks after the first shot) separated "who got shots off" from "a number decided" better (history:459-463). Record both: the whole-fight firing-unit-seconds and an opening window of DRAFT-opening-window-ticks (the toy used 40, the harness samples every 5 ticks), and let the page show both.
