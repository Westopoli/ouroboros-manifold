# Layer dynamics: start here

The work to make the layer system and tactical combat carry the game's central promise. This file is the current state: the goal, the principle, the measure, what is decided, what the engine can do, and the plan. It is rewritten when things change. How it got here, with every run of the toy simulator, is `history_2026-09-30_to_10-01.md`. Runs from now on are one file each in `runs/`. Where the work stopped and what is next: `LEAVE_OFF.md`.

## 1. The goal

Westley, 2026-10-01: "players with better understanding of the landscape, unit formation, layer awareness/intentionality, and the other players current position can defeat a much larger force". "The goal is to create a game with significant strategic depth. We get to this goal by tuning all the variables we laid out such that that sentence becomes a reality."

His original request, 2026-09-30, was for a simulator that varies everything an RTS varies (position, orientation, damage, health, vision, landscape), documents how the variables relate, and finds where the "lines cross"; in small steps, read as they come: "I think it'd be much faster to iteratively try small things, check the output, and tune repetatively."

On 2026-10-01 he added that there is more than one answer: "The *lines* can cross in multiple locations, there may be configurations where health/range/vision/ping distance is much higher/lower but the expected behavior is exhibited."

## 2. The principle

- **What decides a fight is how many units on each side can get shots off, and for how long.** "This is leading us to the core of the game: the differentiator is **can your unit get shots off**." It is the advantage over the whole fight that counts, not only the opening: "the number that we really care about is number of units firing, if A and B both have the same number of units firing than the engagement ends up even". (A finding from the toy, not his words: when the firing counts are level, the side with more in reserve outlasts the other.)
- **With everything identical, the larger force wins.** "Larger force wins, but this is only if both players have exactly the same units, unit formation, vision, layer organization. Hypothetically this situation would *never* happen in the real game, given there are 5 different layers, different vision, the landscape will be autogenerated and not symmetrical." So it is a control, not a scenario.
- **A skill has no fixed worth.** A play is worth what it does to the firing count in that situation: "it depends on the number of units that can get shots, not a basic ratio ... That will indicate the quality of the choke, not just that they should do *1.3x* better because it's not as simple as that." For all five skills together his first answer was "About 2x"; he then said "I like C" (singles of about 30 percent, full mastery worth 3x or more) and confirmed these as rough sizes only, not pass marks.
- **Unit type also weights each shot** ("not just unit values but their type as well"). When the side with more units firing does not win, "there's a number that's off".
- **More than one configuration can work.** The search describes every region of settings where the conditions hold, not only the first found. Each region is a candidate Climate of War for an era (E-20 S-151, R-151-02; Westley, 2026-10-15: "the whole point of the simulation lab is to find *multiple* points where the *lines cross*, not just one configuration"), so a report that stops at the first crossing is incomplete.
- **Parts and whole (planned, after the lab).** A configuration is judged by its parts and by its whole: a part (two roles alone, one factor alone) may fail where the whole crosses (Westley, 2026-11-02). No part carries one headcount, because the lines cross in several places and strategic depth is not a number. What the lab runs and reports is in `decomposition.md`; it is built after the lab is finished and changes no step of the plan below.

## 3. Five skills

| Skill | The skilled player | Source |
|---|---|---|
| S1 Landscape | fights where terrain limits how many enemies can fire | "understanding of the landscape" |
| S2 Formation | arranges units so more of them fire at once | "unit formation" |
| S3 Layers | uses the three layer actions on purpose: the ping, going dark, reach by unit type | "layer awareness/intentionality" |
| S4 Enemy position | finds the enemy first and fires first; draws the enemy into a trap with a lesser force | "the other players current position"; "induces traps" |
| S5 Unit-type matchup | puts its own rocks on the enemy's scissors | "it's bigger than just a subset of S2" |

Skill is represented by scripted plays (fourteen, registered in `scripts/skilllab/plays_space.py`): a skilled play and a naive play. A skilled play differs from the naive play only in arrangement, orders and which roster types stand where; both sides field the same units with the same values. The naive play is a blob or a line abreast (Westley, 2026-10-01); the baselines a play is read against are in section 4. Plays and their counters, accepted in outline ("your list looks good", "Everything else looks good"). The counter to S2's spread line is the one he said he could not picture; he was shown a diagram and has not confirmed it:

| Skill | Skilled play | Counter | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|

| S1 | Hold the top of a slope, far enough past the midline that climbers cannot reach | Take another way up, or fly; or shell the top from below with a type that fires upward | ground |
| S1 | Fight from behind tall cover | Get sight first: ping behind the cover, or move to a layer above its height | ground |

Refusing a fight is a counter: "a skilled player only takes a battle that they can win, skilled players make it so that the opponent doesn't have a choice." A play needs a counter on most maps, not all; "some situations are unbeatable (like a full surround or a trap where the door gets closed)". "Later" marks plays that need orders to change during a fight. After the prototype, if it works, a reactive version: one side or both re-form mid-fight to keep their rocks on the enemy's scissors.

## 4. The measure

- **The result is who was left standing.** A fight ends by elimination only: `side0`, `side1`, or `draw` (both sides die on one tick). A tick cap of 12000 stays as a bug detector; a `cap` is a lab defect to trace, never a result (register Q-LAB-03).
- **A cell is won by elimination.** A cell is one enemy headcount fought in both spawn orders over the replicate seeds; it is won when every fight is `side0`. A `draw` or a `cap` in a cell marks the cell `defect` and the scan goes on.
- **Break-even, in whole units.** For example: 16 units beat 17 enemies and lose to 18. By headcount, both sides fielding the same mix of unit types, scanned over 8 to 48 against 16 (bisection by default, every headcount with `--full-scan`). A play that wins every scanned cell is a ceiling hit: it beats 48, and that is never read as no gain.
- **Margin.** Reported beside every fight: the share of one side's strength left, minus the other's. It is not the result: win rate misleads in a system without dice, so who was left standing decides.
- **Units firing, per side, through the fight.** A living unit is firing when the engine's `can_engage` against some living enemy reads 0: it could fire this tick if ready (reach at the layer offset, seen by its side, in range at the offset, inside the firing arc, line not blocked). The target of record, per unit per sample, is the engine's target if `can_engage` against it is 0, else the nearest living hostile enemy with code 0, else the nearest living hostile enemy, so the recorded code carries the first failing reason (1 out of reach, 2 not seen, 3 out of range, 4 outside the arc, 5 line blocked); -1 only when no living hostile exists. The firing advantage is reported beside every result.
- **Two predictions per fight, both from the opening window after first contact.** The count-only prediction is the side with more unit-seconds firing. The type-weighted prediction weights each firing unit by the category multiplier of its category against its target of record's category and nothing else (never damage, interval, health or a cross-layer damage multiplier; a unit that cannot fire contributes 0). The `agreement` column says which prediction matched the outcome: `both_right`, `count_wrong_type_right`, `count_right_type_wrong`, `both_wrong`, or `n/a` (a draw, a cap, or a tie in either prediction). A mismatch is a finding, never smoothed over. Whole-fight totals are kept beside the window as the chart.
- **Maps.** Space maps (every unit flies; "it does have very free layers that can be taken advantage of") must pass. Land maps (ramps, walking units) are reported and do not block; the ground scene `s1_slope_top` (Two labs, above) is the first of them.

Conditions on a rule set, with draft pass marks that Westley has not yet confirmed:

| # | Condition | Draft mark |
|---|---|---|
| C0 | Control: a mirrored fight | the larger force wins and the break-even sits at equal numbers (tolerance to be set by Westley) |
| C1 | Skill wins the firing count | the skilled side's firing advantage is above the noise of the baseline, on at least 80 percent of the maps that offer the play; read against the mirror control, the naive attacker and the naive defender, one verdict per baseline and no combined verdict; how large is not a fixed ratio and is Westley's to set |
| C2 | The result follows the firing count | results rise with firing advantage (rank agreement at least 0.8); no more than one fight in five more than 25 percent off the trend; judged on the opening window first and on the whole fight beside it, and a verdict that changes between the two is listed |
| C3 | Skills stack | a combination beats its best part by at least 5 percent |
| C4 | No dominant skill | no single skill gives more than 60 percent of the full set's advantage |
| C5 | No dead skill | removing any one skill from the full set costs at least 10 percent |
| C6 | Counters exist | the counter takes back at least half the advantage, on at least 60 percent of maps |
| C7 | The ceiling is high | reported: the best situations, against the rough sizes of 2x and 3x |
| C8 | Robust | C1 to C6 hold on 80 percent of space maps |
| C9 | Fights stay readable | reported: fight length against E-11 S-085 |

"Lines crossing", made concrete: a line is the edge, in the space of settings, of where one condition holds; the crossing is a region where all hold at once; there may be several such regions. In a sweep, a crossing is a knob value pair between which the play's gain over a baseline (its break-even minus the baseline's) changes sign.

Rulings of 2026-10-13 (Westley), binding on every batch from here:

- Naive baselines for a defending play, reported both ways: `naive_attacker` (a naive blob per unit type attacking the same post, fielding the play's own mix on both sides, side 1 scaled with the headcount; rev 3 piece A, D4) and `naive_defender` (side 0 replaced by one blob per unit type at that type's mean anchor, same units and counts, the play's timeline dropped) ("#1: C").
- For a layer play the naive defender runs twice (DRAFT, Architect): `naive_defender_attacker_layer` puts every type on the attacker's layer (the same-layer fight) and `naive_defender_play_layer` keeps each type on the layer the play gave it. A baseline refused by the pre-flight (`no_fit`, `unroutable`, `no_reach`, `blind_post`) drops only itself and is listed with its role; the play still runs. A naive-defender post that no clear sightline (R-098-02, range ignored) joins to the nearest point of the attacker's start region is blind: the builder shifts it to the nearest free route-grid cell in the same region that sees the start, within the guard radius (200 wu, DRAFT), and reports the shift in the row; where no such cell exists the baseline is refused as `blind_post`. A meeting (side 0 has no guard-stance group) has no naive defender at all.
- A naive blob is the arrangement with no skill in it: where its next cell is blocked, placement takes the next free cell of its fill order inside the anchor's connected region. When the region cannot hold the count at some headcount, the scan stops at the last headcount where both baselines placed and the cell is marked `baseline_no_fit`; it is neither a ceiling hit nor a finding, and the artifact says "won through K, scan cut by baseline_no_fit" so it is never read as "no crossing exists".
- A baseline that wins every scanned headcount is listed as `baseline_ceiling`: no crossing is possible there, and it is not a notable.
- Scan range: enemy headcount 8 to 48 against a force of 16, so a play can show up to three times its size ("#2: A"); a play that beats 48 is reported as a ceiling hit (`ceiling_hit` 1), never as no gain. Ruling 4's 8 to 48 is the scan of the plays, not of the golden-hash controls: the controls and the `reach` command keep 8 to 24 enemies at 10 replicates.
- The fight ends by elimination only (register Q-LAB-03). A meeting is both sides attack-moved to one point (`Setup.point`, the midpoint of the two anchors). An assault is the attacker attack-moved onto the holder's centre of mass (the mean of the holder's placed positions, on the holder's layer) while the holder is on guard; each side keeps its own layer. The play `s2_mass_one_end` aims its attackers at one end of the holder's line instead. The pre-flight refuses a cell it cannot run (section 7).
- Same units, same values, on both sides, always (Westley, 2026-10-13: "Each 'era' will have both sides fighting the same units, same values"). A skilled play differs from the naive play only in arrangement, orders and which roster types stand where; the stat-scaled Vanguard variants are retired and replaced by roster types. The only asymmetry allowed is a labelled positive control (the planted 20 percent damage scan, `damage_play`), never a play.
- Emitting (S-163) is a lab knob, two multipliers, sight and seen-from, with seen-from always above sight (R-163-01); the sweep finds the crossing. Starting pair: sight x2.0, seen x3.0 (Westley, 2026-10-13: "let's make that a knob, let the sweep find the crossing"). The play for it, `s3_dark_striker`, silences side 0 and switches the enemy to emit at the family's values.
- No absurd outliers (Westley, 2026-10-13): every sweep starts at the bible's value and steps by a named factor inside a bounded band of 0.25x to 4x of the base (a ruling, not a draft number; three to five points per knob, clipped where the engine refuses), coarse grid first, then refined only around a crossing the coarse grid showed, halving the step between the two coarse points that bracket a sign change, at most two rounds; a point outside the band needs a reason written in the batch spec.
- Firing up is a penalty (Westley, 2026-10-13: "let's go with penalty for multipliers firing up"): the coarse grid for the cross-layer range and damage multipliers runs from the bible's 1.0 down to 0.25; a single point above 1.0 appears only as a labelled sign-flip control (family `sign_flip`), never as a sweep point.
- Cross-layer vision: asymmetric in ground eras (high ground sees down, low never sees up, R-005-09) and symmetric in space eras (R-005-05) (Westley, 2026-10-13: "it should only be asymmetrical in Ground eras ... whereas it needs to be symmetrical on space since there's more freedom of movement and ping system"). A space play never sets the up and down factors apart.
- Cross-layer fire works in ground eras too (Westley, 2026-10-13: "Ground fire should go cross-layer, that's a bug"): the reach table of S-164 applies in every era; a walker that cannot fire up a slope under reach (0, 0) is a finding, not a rule.
- Attack-move resumes (Westley, 2026-10-13, option (a)): an attack-move, group or lone, is not finished until the unit reaches its point and resumes after every engagement (R-096-02 rewritten). The engine resumes a group's attack-move after every engagement (piece E1).
- A return-fire redirect is one engagement (R-087-06 rewritten in E-11): the unit pursues the attacker that hit it from out of range and, when the pursuit ends (the attacker dies, the pursuer reaches the attacker's place, passes the 200 wu leash of R-096-03, or its side no longer sees the attacker), resumes at once toward its original point, which the redirect never overwrites (piece E1b).
- Re-engage (lab rule, decision 28): when a side has had no unit fire or move for `REENGAGE_SILENCE_TICKS` while an enemy is visible to it, every idle unit of that side holding an attack-move role is attack-moved onto the centroid of the enemies that side sees (each unit keeps its own layer); a guard-stance holder never gets it. It applies to both sides, naive included, fires once per silence period (a side re-arms only after one of its own units fires or moves, or after a no-op engage: a retry after another `REENGAGE_SILENCE_TICKS`), and the count per side is the CSV column `reengage_0` and `reengage_1`. Re-engage is a lab-issued order, exempt from the play's orders mode. In short, re-engage is a lab stand-in for the engine's pending sight-engagement rule (Westley's question whether a unit that sees an enemy should attack it), and the artifact header says so whenever a row re-engaged. The engine's R-099-05 give-up ends a group's attack-move where the unit stands and an idle unit does not chase, so a fight whose survivors stop out of range would otherwise run to the cap. A cap after a re-engage stays a defect. Decided is structural (reach table); stalled is positional and the re-engage stand-in's job until VG-E-sightengage lands.
- The type-weighted prediction's target (Westley, 2026-10-13, option (a); ruling 5 of the rev 2 spec): the target of record defined above; the weight is the category multiplier of the attacker's category against that target's category and nothing else.
- Orientation returns to the variable list once the firing arc is in the engine (Q-100-01): the lab side is built (`Setup.firing_arc_deg`, family `arc`); area damage as a knob with its own range and shape, on any role or unit, is a lab variable (Q-082-06): the lab side is built (`Setup.cone`, family `cone`). Section 7 says when they run.

## 5. How results are read

Small batches. One page of charts per batch, in the form of the sample Westley called "much better" on 2026-10-01 ("looks much better, especially the top graphic that makes it very clear") after the first draft read badly ("I had to stare at it to figure out what it meant"). The bullets below describe that sample; other forms are allowed "if the type of data we're looking at changes". The sample is at https://claude.ai/artifact/WtQDNnNZTR8hqx6LpxBaJ2 (private to Westley).

- The heading states the finding in soldiers, not ratios.
- A line of fights by enemy size, a dot per fight, won or lost, with the break-even marked ("beats 17, loses to 18").
- Units firing through the fight, each line labelled on the chart, the gap shaded.
- A table with a plain-words column in place of any scatter plot.
- For many rule sets: a map of two settings at a time, shaded where every condition holds; a short decision-tree summary in words.

Output rule (Westley, rev 2 spec ruling 12, 2026-10-13): the lab's output is data (CSV, JSON, run metadata). Claude analyses the data; thresholds are Westley's. The artifact Westley sees shows the notable layouts, matches and configurations as top-down diagrams with arrows marking the evidence for each assertion, explains what the data means, and above all where the lines cross. A number without its diagram is not evidence.

The artifact page (`python3 -m scripts.skilllab artifact <dir>`) reads `sweep_summary.json`, the family CSVs and `smoke.csv` of a sweep or smoke directory and writes `artifact.html` and one SVG per notable under `diagrams/`. Its sections, in order: drafts, findings, baseline ceiling, agreement, both wrong, refinement, cone grid, conditions.

- A diagram exists for every crossing (the play's gain over a baseline changes sign between two knob values), every ceiling hit (the play beat every enemy size to 48; the notable comes from the play's own rows only) and every `both_wrong` fight. Each is a top-down map of the placed units by side and layer, the walls, the covers and the choke gap, and the shared point, drawn from the same setup the fight ran. A fake sweep with no crossing produces no diagram.
- A `both_wrong` fight (both predictions wrong) is a finding of the measure, never discarded. Control and baseline rows are included with the role in the label; a `both_wrong` on a control is the strongest finding against the measure.
- A crossing diagram carries the bracketing arrows: for each of the two bracketing knob values, for the play and for its baseline, an arrow to the first row of the break-even cell and one to the first lost cell, each labelled (enemy_heads, outcome); a ceiling scan has only its top cell and a lost-everywhere scan only its first. The sentence under the diagram gives the counts, and every arrow label equals a CSV value.
- The legend of a diagram marks the DRAFT numbers its rows touch (the opening window, the arc family, the cone family). The drafts section lists every DRAFT number in force once.
- `artifact` takes an optional `--thresholds <json>` file of Westley's pass marks (every field of `evidence.Thresholds`; there are no defaults). Given it, the conditions section shows each verdict the CSV rows alone support (C2 on the window and the whole fight, C1 against each baseline) with its threshold name beside it. Without it the section says "no condition verdicts: thresholds not supplied" and still lists the raw agreement counts and the C1 advantages as numbers with no pass or fail. The mirror C1 is not computed here; it lives in the engine-run `evaluate_all`.
- A scan cut by `baseline_no_fit` is listed with its last headcount, and an always-won cut scan says "won through K, scan cut by baseline_no_fit".
- Every refused baseline is listed as one line "no naive <role>: <reason>", the reason one of `no_fit`, `unroutable`, `no_reach`, `blind_post` (for example "no naive defender: blind post"), in the last list of the findings section and again as a block right before the play's first finding (or after the cut-scan list when the play has none). The drafts section ends with the sentence "C1 for plays without a naive defender baseline rests on naive_attacker only" and the plays it holds for, whenever a play has a refused naive defender.
- A naive defender whose post is blind to the attackers' start is moved first, not refused: the nearest free cell of the route grid in the same region from which the start is seen, within the guard radius. Every such shift is listed as a line "naive defender: post shifted 110.4 wu" (one decimal; the role as written, one line per play, layout and role; the distance is the largest over the headcounts built), last in the findings section after the refused list and again as a block per play right after that play's refused block. The shifts are also the `shifted_posts` field of the smoke and sweep summaries; no CSV column holds them.
- The drafts section carries two notes when they hold. When a vision play is refused `no_fit` by its own pre-flight on a layout the vision family never ran on, it says which layouts ran: "vision cells: layout 192 only; 81 and 78 refuse the play: no_fit". When any row re-engaged it says that re-engage is a lab stand-in for the engine's pending sight-engagement rule.

## 6. Layer rulings in force

Canonical text is E-01 (`docs/bible/epics/E-01-layer-system.md`) and the register. In brief:

- Five layers, always. Vision is symmetric by layer distance. Passive cross-layer vision stays, at short range.
- Three layer actions, all costing no resource: the **targeted ping** (a chosen layer at a chosen place, a very short burst, long reach; the pinged side gets an alert and a sector of about 60 degrees toward the source), **going dark** (a force emits or stays silent), **reach by unit type** (each type fires only at certain layer offsets). E-01 S-162, S-163, S-164.
- The ping replaces the seer, "or make the seer really expensive".
- A unit never fires at what it cannot see.
- A unit takes the shortest route to its goal across layers.
- Flying and swimming units change layer anywhere nothing blocks; walking units only by slopes.
- On a slope, progress is position; the cross-over is the midline; a unit is on the layer on its side of it. A transitioning unit attacks from the layer it is leaving until the cross-over.
- Abilities are out of this work for now. No faster descent in space eras.
- Ideas recorded, not decided: travel lanes (E-17 S-165); the reactive formation.

## 7. The instrument

The toy simulator (`toys/layerlab/`) is frozen: its unit behaviour is not the game's, and results kept turning on that. Measuring is done by the **skill lab**, `scripts/skilllab/`, a harness that only calls the real C sim (`csrc/`): it runs fights, counts the units able to fire on each side through the fight, finds break-evens in whole units, sweeps the cross-layer knobs, and writes the chart page and the artifact. Its tests are `tests/skilllab/` and three controls: the mirror, numbers, and a planted 20 percent damage advantage. The harness contract is `docs/northstar/plans/skilllab-harness-spec.md`; the rev 2 spec and its decisions are `docs/northstar/plans/2026-10-14-skill-lab-rev2-spec.md` and `2026-10-14-skill-lab-rev2-decisions.md`.

- Commands (`python3 -m scripts.skilllab <command>`): `controls` and `reach` (the three controls and the range study, 16 units against 8 to 24 enemies at every headcount, one chart page), `batch` (the older named batches and the layout search), `sweep` (one knob family, or all, writes `sweep_<family>.csv` and `sweep_summary.json`), `smoke` (the landing check: vision 0.5 alone on the layouts 81, 78 and 192 of the run-1 draw, both vision plays with their baselines, and the three controls, at 12 workers; writes `smoke.csv` and `smoke_summary.json` with the sha256 of the rows, which must be the same at 1 and 12 workers; once E0 is in the landing it also carries a `parity` key, `firing.parity` on the open-ground mirror blob, 16 v 16 at tick 0, arc 360, and until then the key reads `skipped: E0 absent`) and `artifact` (section 5). `--workers` sets the process pool and `--full-scan` fights every headcount instead of bisecting. The families, in order: `vision`, `range_up`, `damage_up`, `emitting`, `arc`, `cone`, `sign_flip`. `sweep` runs with the inert-knob probe on (`run_family` defaults to off; `sweep.run` refuses `probe=False`): before a family is scanned on a play, the family's smallest grid value is fought against the same point with only the family's own keys at their base, at the sweep's coarse headcounts (8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48) and seeds 0 and 1, and a play where no row differs at any of those points is refused `inert_knob` in the summary's `refused` list (with the headcounts and seeds checked). Every probed play, refused or not, gets a `probed` record in the summary (the first differing point and a line such as `bites from 24 heads seed 0; silent at [(8, 0), (8, 1), (16, 0), (16, 1)]`), printed as a paragraph at the end of the artifact's findings; the base fights are kept across families that reset the same keys. Arc and cone are not probed. The `category` family (`OM_TP_CATMULT_*`) is listed under `unavailable` with FINDING-lab-d6 (the library never reads those settings) and is not swept.
- Pre-flight: before a cell runs, `scenes.preflight` runs no tick, moves nothing, and returns `ok` or the first refusal in this order: `no_fit` (a placed unit stands inside a blocked cell on its layer, or on ground a generated field says is not walkable), `unroutable` (a moving group has no path of open 50 wu cells from its placed positions to its point on its own layer; a flyer's open cells are those no block reaching its layer covers, a walker's also need walkable ground), `no_reach` (no type facing a holder reaches the holder's layer from its own) and `blind_post` (asked for with a naive defender only: the holder's post has no clear sightline (R-098-02, range ignored) to the nearest unit of the opposing start, and no free cell of its region within the guard radius (200 wu, DRAFT) has one; a post that can be moved is shifted instead, section 5). A refused cell is listed with its word in the `prefight` column, never run to a cap.
- The count: a unit is firing when the engine's `can_engage` reads 0 against some living enemy. Until the E0 contract piece is on main the library has no `om_combat_can_engage` and the lab composes the same five gates from public queries; after E0 that composition becomes the parity check (`firing.parity` lists the units where the composed and the engine's answers differ, and a difference is a defect). `fight` does not call it; the integration tests do. Limit: the composed count assumes target-by-sight on.
- Rows: one CSV row per fight. Beside the fight's own values (outcome, margin, strength left, survivors, ticks, first contact, the whole-fight and window firing and weighted unit-seconds, the two predictions and `agreement`) a sweep row carries `knob`, `knob_value`, `baseline`, `ceiling_hit`, `prefight`, `baseline_no_fit`, `blind_post` (1 when the fight capped and at the cap tick neither side saw any living enemy: a finding of a blind post, not a lab defect, counted apart from the defect cells), `reengage_0` and `reengage_1`, the labels `family`, `play`, `layout`, `kind` and `enemy_heads`, and the column `point`: the family-local series a row belongs to along its knob. A cone row has `knob_value` = range and `point` = `half=<degrees>`; an emitting row has `knob_value` = sight and `point` = `vision=<value>`; every other family writes `-`. A crossing is found within one (family, play, layout, point) series.
- Orders: `Setup.orders_per_unit` is True by default, so the opening attack-move is one `order_attack_move` call per unit; timeline steps stay group-level. The re-engage is a lab-issued order exempt from this mode: one `order_attack_move` per idle unit, onto the enemy centroid; a no-op engage (no ordered unit has a destination one tick later) re-arms and retries after another `REENGAGE_SILENCE_TICKS`. The flip of the default to group attack-move is conditional (decision 43 item 1): the 3-layout smoke is rerun with `orders_per_unit` False, and the flip rides the lab landing only if every cap left outside the `control_*` rows is a blind-post finding; any other cap keeps the default at True and the flip becomes its own later piece (`FINDING-group-orders-cap`, section 12).
- Arc and cone: the lab side is built (`Setup.firing_arc_deg` set on the world through `OM_TP_FIRING_ARC_DEG`; `Setup.cone` set through `om_roster_set_cone`; the sweep families `arc` and `cone`, run on `s2_concave` and `s2_mass_one_end`). The engine side lands with E2 (the E0 contract carries the parameter and the symbols, E2 the behaviour), so while the library lacks `OM_TP_FIRING_ARC_DEG` or a usable `om_roster_set_cone` the family is skipped with a recorded reason; the arc and cone families are batch two, and L7B updates their behaviour docs after the E2 and E4 landings.
- Ground scene: `s1_slope_top` (section 3, Two labs) runs from `scripts/skilllab/ground/scenes_ground.py` on the generated field of seed 11 at canvas 12000; its knobs `OM_TP_SIGHT_EYE_HEIGHT` and `OM_TP_GROUND_FLYER_SEEN_MULT` are inert in the natural scene, and batch two reports them. - Cone state: the engine keeps the per-type cone as lab-only state (`FINDING-cone-state`), so the lab applies `Setup.cone` on the fresh world of every fight.

## 9. Two machines

The Mac and the Linux machine work on this repo at the same time.

- No machine paths in code; the harness finds the repo root from its own file.
- The library builds by architecture; a committed file of result hashes for the control fights must match on both machines. A mismatch is a determinism finding.
- Scripts guard their entry point and pass plain data to worker processes; the two systems start workers differently.
- Work lands on `main` in small units. Merge, never rebase. Pull before editing a shared file. Cascade branches and worktrees stay local.
- One file per run in `runs/`; register edits one row at a time.
- Nothing generated is committed: libraries, result tables and pictures, Godot import files. The one exception is the small file of control hashes.

## 10. What the toy showed

Kept as leads, to be re-measured on the real engine (transfer class T2 or T3):

- A layer alone gives nothing under symmetric vision. Reach and damage penalties for firing up are steep: decisive within 20 percent.
- Width matters, curvature barely: against a blob, a line or a concave is worth three units in sixteen; a concave is worth one unit against a line.
- Holding a choke gave nothing, for three reasons: walls did not block sight; no stance between never moving and chasing anywhere; reach of only four unit-widths, so few units can focus one spot.
- For the ideal choke (attackers held to c firing, D defenders all firing) the arithmetic is D(D+1)/(2c).

## 11. Lessons about method

- Check what is written against Westley's own words (`crosscheck_2026-09-30.md`, `crosscheck_2026-10-01.md`).
- Look at what a scripted play actually does before measuring it.
- One skill, one map, one size; then widen.
- A surprising result is first a question about the instrument.
- Win rate misleads in a system without dice.
- Position matters when measuring numbers. Two armies walked straight at each other collapse into two flat rows, and the number measured is the walk's. "A real battle isn't going to be a line feeding into each other, it's going to be a blob if they're mediocre players, or a spread out line if they're skilled players" (Westley, 2026-10-02). Setups place units as a player of that skill would have them at contact (`runs/2026-10-02_harness_check.md`).
