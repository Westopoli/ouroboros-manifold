What it says against the goal

  • Shape does change who gets shots off. At equal numbers a line holds 57 percent of the firing time and a clump holds 50. The result follows that count: the line wins, the clump is a coin toss. This is the principle working as written.
  • The size of the effect is small. A line of 16 still wins at least half its fights against 18. A clump of 16 manages that against 15. So shape is worth about three enemy soldiers in sixteen on open ground, a fifth more force, not "a much larger force".
  • A concave buys nothing over a line here. It holds to 16 where the line holds to 18. The toy found the same: width matters, curvature barely.
  • The column is worse than doing nothing. It was meant as the counter to a line and it loses firing time (48 percent) and fights. As drafted, that counter does not exist.
  • Formation alone cannot carry the goal. If one skill is meant to be worth roughly 30 percent, this one delivers about 20 on the kindest reading, and only against an enemy that does not form up at all.

What this run cannot tell you

  • The lab's own break-even is wrong for this run. It prints "16 beat 11" for the line, because a size counts as beaten only when every fight there finishes with a win. Unfinished fights drag it down. The columns above show the line winning nearly everything up to 16.
  • Some fights never finish. A fifth of the column's fights, and 3 to 7 percent of the others, stall or hit the tick cap. Traced causes: a group ordered while one member had just fired froze for good (an engine bug, fix ready), and the lab's re-order aimed the wings of a line back where they stood.
  • Identical forces are not quite even. Raider clump against Raider clump at 16 goes 36 to 23 to our side. Something no player controls is tilting it. Not yet traced.
  • There is no terrain and one layer. Every unit in range can fire whatever the shape, which is the kindest case for the clump. A choke is where the research says shape should matter most, and no map here has one.
  • No chart of units firing through the fight. The lab records it per fight, but this batch kept it for one example fight per cell only.

What I would run next, and why

  • The same four shapes at a choke. Theory says a narrow front turns the fight from "numbers squared" to "numbers", which is where a small force should hold a large one. That is the test of whether landscape and formation together reach the goal.
  • Short range against wide front. Vary attack range from 150 to 400 with the line 600 wu wide. When range is shorter than the front, only part of a clump can fire. That is the first two-knob map: range against front width.
  • A real counter to the line. The column fails. A flank or a split is what the research calls defeat in detail.

Before any of those: fix the frozen-group bug and the re-order rule, so a fight that does not finish means something.
