Three decisions for the layout lab

Run 1 played about 245,000 fights on 200 map layouts. Three questions need your eye, not a calculation. Each section shows real fights from that run, redrawn here from a replay, and ends with one question you can answer with a letter. Every picture is drawn from the replay, not a screenshot. Map units are the game's own distance unit; a unit body is a few dozen of them.

The gaps between the two counts grow from pair b to pair f. Also shown: the most attackers each layout can beat with this play (its break-even).

How loud is the noise? Pair a is a quiet example. Layouts 0 and 17 are the same layout with different map seeds. Against 11 attackers (about the most these layouts hold against: 10 and 11), the same fight left 3 holders in one and 12 in the other: a gap of 9. So at the edge of what a layout can hold, chance alone can move the count by this much.

2. Who won a held fight?

This is layout 3, play screen (a thin front line stands ahead of the main group), 17 attackers against our 16 holders, open ground with a few patches of cover and no walls. Both sides lost one unit (holders 15 of 16 left, attackers 16 of 17 left). The attackers came forward and traded shots from just outside the circle, about 359 to 366 map units from its middle, and never walked into it. At tick 1785, 1200 ticks after the first damage, all 15 holders were still inside the circle and no attacker was, so the lab stopped the fight and recorded held, a win for the holders.

The dashed circle is 300 map units wide around the spot the holders were told to guard. The rule: after a minute of fighting (1,200 ticks since the first damage), if at least half the holders are inside the circle and no attacker is, the fight is "held".

  • (b) Nobody: the attacker never committed.
  • (c) This is a sim defect: attackers should always close.
  • (d) Count it for the defender only if the attacker took losses.

3. The corner that never ends

Some layouts never finish. Of 244,800 fights in the run, 16,989 hit the 12,000-tick limit with no result (called "capped"). Where the gap in the wall is 2 bodies wide and the walls close all five layers (blocked_layer_top = 4), 5,714 of 15,912 fights capped; everywhere else together, 11,275 of 228,888. For gap 4 with all layers closed it is 10,729 of 20,808.

What "blocked_layer_top" means: the game has five stacked layers (0 to 4), like floors, and a wall closes the layers up to this number. Where a wall is closed, a unit on that layer cannot cross it; a unit on an open layer goes over it. Here is what it means for this layout and, for comparison, a layout whose layers 3 and 4 are open:

The fight below: layout 124 (choke 2 bodies wide, 1 lane, walls closing layers 0 to 4, 2800 map units between the sides), play hold the choke, 8 attackers. All of its six fights capped with every unit alive (16 holders and 8 attackers left, 12,000 ticks). The trace shows every unit stayed on layer 0 for the whole fight, so no unit could ever go over a wall. The two sides ended up on either side of the wall and did not move again after tick 1200.

Question 3. What should the lab do with layouts like this one?
  • (a) Exclude these layouts from the search.
  • (b) It is a sim defect: units should be able to reach each other. Fix that first.
  • (c) They are allowed layouts, and the lab should score them as a stalemate.
