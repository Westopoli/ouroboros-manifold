# Era feel differentiation: options

This document presents four options for E-20 S-151, how eras could feel different on one shared skeleton, against the brief in `docs/northstar/research/options/era-feel/brief.md`. Two are grounded extensions of ideas already named in the bible and its history; two are wild.

## Option A — The Conflict Roster

The war itself is the difference. Every era keeps the same three families of unit and the same counters between them, but which twenty to forty units fill those families, and how hard the era leans toward one named rivalry, tells you where and when you are. Fog, pacing, and reinforcement never change; who you fight, and with what, does.

**Vignette.** The briefing names a front you have never fought. Your force spawns: a shield-wall silhouette you have never ordered, backed by skirmishers you read as Strike by their speed. The enemy's Line unit is a wall of spears where yours is shields. You already know spears beat your flank; you just did not know it would be spears that told you so.

Notice first: unfamiliar portraits sorted into category icons you already read. Relearn: which unit plays anchor or flanker here, and what its one ability does. Stays the same: counters, tier scaling, fight length, fog, reinforcement rate.

Reaches you as: every era already hands the game a list of twenty to forty units with stats and one ability each. This only asks that list to lean hard toward one rivalry instead of spreading evenly; the game reads whatever list it gets the same way every time.

Honors: the roster swing toward a named conflict (R-082-07); fixed counters and tier scaling (R-146-04); identical fog, tempo, reinforcement (A-321, S-085). Strains: reading a new era "at a glance" (section 6 Q4) costs real relearning; leans on the per-type silhouette rule now under review (Q-132-02).

Prior art: Rise of Nations ages a whole roster mid-match, units changing identity while keeping role, VERIFIED — [Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Rise_of_Nations/Units). Empire Earth swaps buildable units across fourteen epochs while some roles persist, VERIFIED — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Earth_(video_game)). StarCraft's three races feel unalike while staying balanced, VERIFIED — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Races_of_StarCraft).

Tradeoffs: a strong-identity era is memorable and gives real matchup homework, but the homework is real, and a roster built around one rivalry can feel thin if its unfeatured factions never show up.

## Option B — The Climate of War

Instead of who fights, this changes the conditions the fight happens under. Each era sets its own fog thickness, its own engagement pace, its own reinforcement rate. Units, categories, and counters never change; only the weather the war is fought in does.

**Vignette.** You cross into a new front and your Line unit's awareness barely reaches the treeline. You push a scout forward out of habit and it earns its keep for the first time in months. You lose a unit; the structure you hold takes what feels like forever to replace it. You start playing like reinforcements do not exist, because for now they might as well not.

Notice first: how far you can see, how long everything takes. Relearn: when it is safe to push blind, how much a captured structure will actually bail you out. Stays the same: the units and counters, and the rule that fog changes what you know, never what the fight does.

Reaches you as: every era already sets numbers for sensor reach through interference and how fast a held structure produces a unit. This only asks each era to set those numbers differently, once, the way two missions today already hand you different fog or garrison strength.

Honors: R-151-01's own list (fog, tempo, HP scale, reinforcement, set uniformly); sacred principle 3; the recon phase's complete-terrain promise (R-092-04), untouched. Strains: S-085's tempo targets are fixed identical at launch and explicitly deferred (A-339, A-344) — this is that deferred idea returning; a fog-heavy era risks reading as worse rather than different.

Prior art: the retired eight-axis matrix proposed exactly this dial cluster for historical settings, AMBIGUOUS as this project's own design work, not a shipped game — [`era_mechanical_axes.md`](../../../../design_questions/era_mechanical_axes.md). Company of Heroes 2's cold-weather front imposes a survival pressure the other front never has, REPORTED — [Neoseeker](https://www.neoseeker.com/news/19991-company-of-heroes-2s-unbelievably-realistic-coldtech-system-turns-winter-into-your-enemy/).

Tradeoffs: an information-starved era rewards patient scouting and punishes aggressive habits; a reinforcement-starved one makes every loss sting longer — a genuinely different rhythm, at the cost of other-era muscle memory being briefly wrong.

## Option C — Rise and Roster (wild)

This ties an era's feel not to the era but to where your civilization stands in its own life. The five-stage arc every civilization already lives — scrappy beginnings, real strength, overextension, decline, rebirth — decides which slice of that era's roster you field on a given mission. Play the same era across a career and the roster seems to rise and fall with you, with no mission changing its rules.

**Vignette.** Your commander is third-generation since a near-collapse; the mission opens on scavenged light units. Two campaigns later, playing a descendant after a run of good outcomes, the same garrison spawns something you have never fielded — the briefing once mentioned it in passing as something "the old guard used to field." You are not unlocking it. Your civilization is simply strong enough now to have it.

Notice first: a thin opening force, then, campaigns later in the same era, an unrecognizably rich one, without unlocking anything. Relearn: nothing about a new era — this never asks that; you relearn your own era as fortune hands you a different slice of it. Stays the same: the era's full roster, categories, counters, tempo; only which slice you draw changes.

Reaches you as: the game already checks civilization health to size your starting force before every mission. This only asks each era to sort its roster once, scrappiest to grandest, so that same check also decides which part of the list to draw from.

Honors: sacred principle 2, making granular civilizational state a visible mission outcome; the hub's "thriving gives well-equipped, declining gives scraps," extended into which units, not only how many; the five feelings, each with its own slice. Strains: section 6 Q4 does not apply — this answers a different question than fog/tempo/roster variance.

Prior art: none found for the compound mechanic. Nearest partial precedent: tech-tree games gating units behind player-directed research, such as Civilization, ESTIMATED — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_tree) — but that gate is a one-way choice, not a fluctuating, only-partly-controlled health reading.

Tradeoffs: nursing a civilization back to health is rewarded with a visibly richer roster, but a player wanting an era's grandest units on demand cannot simply choose to see them, and a civilization that will not stop declining may never show them at all.

## Option D — The Long Look (wild)

This leaves combat, fog, and roster untouched and changes the ritual of the study minute before a mission begins. Every era still shows the whole map, complete, with nothing about the enemy. This option lets an era decide how that picture is handed to you: some answer your questions instantly; others make you build the picture yourself, nothing highlighted, real felt attention.

**Vignette.** The mission has not started. You are looking at a plain rendering of the ground: no callouts, no search box. You pan west to east, tracing a ridge yourself, and only on the second pass notice a vantage point tucked behind it — nobody told you it was there. Last week, a different era, the same minute ended with every vantage point already circled. Here, you found it, and mission start is still a minute away.

Notice first: whether the study screen answers your questions or waits for you to ask them. Relearn: how much of your own attention this era's study phase expects, before you get the picture another era hands you free. Stays the same: every scrap of terrain and cover shown complete, no enemy anywhere on it, the clock not yet running, no unit moving.

Reaches you as: the study screen already has to be built once, the same complete way, for every era. This only asks each era to choose, once, how that picture is presented — the way a menu can look different in two places while doing the identical job.

Honors: the recon phase's complete-terrain promise (R-092-01, R-092-04), never withheld, only delivered differently; the mission clock and no-movement rules, untouched; opt-in complexity (sacred principle 4). Strains: a thin lever, since nothing about combat, roster, or fog moves; "is losing interesting" has no grip here, since the difference lives entirely before the clock starts.

Prior art: none found for a war game that varies the study ritual itself by faction or setting. Adjacent precedent: Total War's deployment phase expects manual terrain survey, REPORTED — [Total War Academy](https://academy.totalwar.com/battle-deployment-and-battle-terrain/) — though it does not vary that interaction by faction or era.

Tradeoffs: building your own picture rewards patient study with information nobody handed you, but the same minute in a rushed session can feel like a chore before the mission you wanted.

## Findings

- Section 6 Q1 (unconditional later differentiation, or only "if eras feel too similar") is a timing question, not a shape question. All four assume it eventually ships; none settles when.
- Section 6 Q3's middle possibility, restricting which layers a unit type can use per era, was left out of every option. It was the strongest lever in the retired eight-axis matrix, but sits closest to conflict with five-layers-always and with any skin working in any era (R-150) — a skin in a layer-restricted era could carry units with nowhere to stand. It needs its own pass, not a guess folded into one of these four.
- Section 6 Q4 (read a learned era at a glance, or is boundary confusion the point) gets a different implicit answer from each option — A and B ask for real relearning, C asks for none across an era boundary, D asks for a few seconds — rather than a settled position.
- The fixed per-type silhouette rule is itself open for revision (Q-132-02). Option A leans on silhouettes staying legible across an unfamiliar roster; if that rule changes, its "read a stranger's roster by shape" claim needs a second look, and so does every option's quieter assumption that a Strike unit always reads as Strike no matter whose roster it comes from.
