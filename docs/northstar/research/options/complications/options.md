# Mission complications: options

A mission complication is the one-in-a-hundred moment a mission stops behaving the way the briefing promised. Both options below describe only what a player experiences when one lands, never how the game decides to bring one.

## Option A — Crossed Signals

A complication is the chain of command misfiring: an order, a report, or a friendly unit's status turns out wrong. It never makes the enemy hit harder; it costs you something on your own side.

**Vignette.** A third of the wall's left to hold when the command channel goes quiet. A green icon on the north trench blinks red: the flanking squad you expected now reads unidentified, your gate gunners tracking it. Ten seconds before someone fires first. Pull the gunners off, or trust the squad and watch the real breach?

No warning precedes it. One thing changes: a report, a status, or an expected asset turns out false, never a stat, never an enemy. It forces a real-time choice between trusting the situation and spending force on a problem your own side made.

The debrief names it apart from the objective and force tallies, like a citation. If it changed the region's fate, the chronicle gives it one entry, told once, like a veteran's story. Harder settings nudge the odds up a hair, easier down; what it does never changes.

| Archetype | Example |
|---|---|
| Defensive Stand | Your relief column reports to the wrong sector and sits idle the rest of the fight. |
| Rear Guard / Evacuation | An uncounted group of evacuees turns up at extraction, doubling who you can save. |
| Siege Breaking | A stale scout report means your target already moved; its empty position is yours instead. |

Honors: decided before the mission, never live; a true surprise; one per mission; no numbers in the telling; named in debrief and chronicle. Strains: close in spirit to the already-shipped reinforcement wave, must read as clearly not that.

Prior art: XCOM 2's Dark Events, decided ahead and revealed on schedule (REPORTED, xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Events); theirs telegraphs a month out, ours never does.

Tradeoffs: trusting the confusion risks the real breach; pulling gunners off trades force used well for a maybe.

## Option B — The Echo

A complication is a flicker: for seconds the fight becomes two fights layered together, yours and one that hasn't happened, and one detail from the second becomes true. The field shows you something before deciding it's so.

**Vignette.** The fight is loud and ordinary, exactly as briefed, then for four seconds it isn't. The ground goes translucent and doubled: your line, laid over the same ground fought thinner, later, wrecked. The chokepoint span you've been holding is whole in the overlay, and it still could be, if you reach it before the image fades. Then it's gone, and the real span starts groaning, cracks spreading toward your line.

It announces itself as a visible, unmistakable break in the field, never a warning, gone as fast as it came. One thing changes, drawn from the overlay: a structure, a path, or a position becomes real. It forces a choice between chasing the vision before it's gone and staying with the fight you understand.

The debrief names what appeared and what came true, side by side, read as a described anomaly. In the chronicle it becomes its own entry: an old story about a commander said to have seen the fight before it happened, the sort of line later commanders ask after. Harder settings nudge the odds up a hair, easier down; the vision itself never changes.

| Archetype | Example |
|---|---|
| Containment | The border briefly shows itself years further gone, banners past it — and that's where real pressure lands. |
| Assault | Your commander's icon flickers with a rank they don't hold yet; the promotion is real in the debrief regardless. |
| Multi-Front Defense | One front briefly shows itself already empty and lost; the troops posted there report the same quiet for real. |

Honors: decided before the mission, never live; a true surprise; one per mission; nothing told as a number. Strains: a glimpsed future risks reading as the field reacting live, which it never does — set when the mission is, not summoned by the player.

Prior art: none for this shape. FTL's one-off unique events are the nearest analog for a rare thing seen once (REPORTED, ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Unique_Events), but none glimpse an outcome before part of it becomes real.

Tradeoffs: chasing the vision costs seconds owed to what's actually failing; ignoring it means meeting the real version unprepared.

## Findings

- Section 4 frames frequency as part of a difficulty-gated ramp; section 8 fixes it as a flat, celebrated rarity. Section 8 wins, so both options treat difficulty as a small nudge, not the strong ramp section 4 implies.
- The brief leaves open whether this feature and the shipped foreshadowed reinforcement wave (E-14 S-124) are one kind of thing or two separate rarities in the same mission. Both options treat them as separate, per the brief's own allowance, but neither resolves the relationship.
