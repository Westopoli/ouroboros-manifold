# Paired economy multiplayer mode: options

Three shapes for the economy player's chair, where one partner runs the city while the other fights on the same tactical map in real time. Each gives the role a full StarCraft 2-register economy and a way to keep its weight from growing without end across a campaign.

## Option A — The Works

The Works puts the economy player in direct, hands-on control of a production economy on the ground the commander fights over: several resources, a supply cap tied to housing, workers assigned by hand to gathering points and sites, a tech ladder unlocking building tiers and things the commander can call for, and building placement lot by lot.

**Vignette.** Four ore carts sit idle. You drag two to a fresh vein, queue a granary before the supply cap chokes production, and switch the eastern foundry to armor plating when the ticker shows your partner calling for Line units. A convoy passes a smoking crater from the last skirmish; you reroute it without breaking stride, then start a wall segment along the ridge your partner asked to hold.

**One minute:** reassign workers, adjust a queue, watch the supply cap, answer a ticker request, place or queue a structure. **One mission:** clear housing tiers, unlock a requested tech tier, keep several worker groups fed, finish one visible expansion of the city's footprint.

**Becomes the commander's units, walls, positions:** a foundry's queue is the pool a garrison draws replacements from; a placed wall is a real chokepoint; a tech unlock adds a unit or building type the commander can call for.

**Talk and sightlines:** the commander's ticker shows losses and open requests ("need Siege," "hold the north gate"); the economy player's queues and structures show as icons on the shared map, so either side sees what's coming unasked.

**Reset / load-capping:** research unlocks Autoharvesters and Foundries that take over worker assignment and hauling once built, so hand-routed carts give way to standing orders to automated crews, freeing attention for tech and defense.

**Shared mode:** either player can pick up the other's queues and worker groups; the interface doesn't change, only who drives it.

**Honors:** full register; same map, real time, no pause; the economy is a real pool the commander draws from; reset is automation upgrades, as proposed.

**Strains:** none identified against section 8.

**Prior art:** Savage: The Battle for Newerth split one player into a base-building commander, others into soldiers, on one continuous map — REPORTED (RTS/FPS hybrid retrospectives). StarCraft 2 and Age of Empires 2 for the register — VERIFIED, the named reference games.

**Tradeoffs:** a player who likes SC2's worker-drag tempo gets it straight, at the cost of a role reading as "SC2 next to someone else's RTS."

## Option B — The Two Clocks (wild)

The Two Clocks runs the economy player on a second, nested clock inside the commander's uninterrupted real time. Rather than acting continuously, the player plans a block of production, construction, and research — a shift — in a short, dense window; once committed, it plays out at the commander's pace while the player can only intervene through a few standing overrides (reroute a convoy, halt a build, call an emergency levy) until the next window opens. No shipped game pairs a teammate's uninterrupted battle with a partner on a separate, periodic planning tempo in the same match; that is the wild shape.

**Vignette.** The window opens: sixty seconds to lay out the next shift. You commit workers to the alloy vein, queue the barracks upgrade your partner's asked for, and set a wall extension along an exposed flank — then it closes and the city moves on its own. You watch the wall rise, hear a call for reinforcements, and spend your one override pulling workers from a collapsing quarry.

**One minute:** inside a window, several commitments against a countdown; outside one, zero to two overrides spent reactively. **One mission:** several shifts planned end to end, an override budget spent across the mission's crises, one shift that visibly changes the fight.

**Becomes the commander's units, walls, positions:** what a shift commits is what appears — a queued unit rolls out mid-shift, a wall rises across it, a levy call turns idle population into a wave the commander routes on arrival.

**Talk and sightlines:** the commander sees a locked-in shift's plan at once as "coming this shift" ticker items; the economy player sees the commander's requests and losses continuously, watching filling the gaps.

**Reset / load-capping:** Foremen, unlocked through research, absorb a shift's routine items so they run themselves unplanned; a late-campaign window goes to tech and expansion, not the same route again.

**Shared mode:** the commander can spend idle moments inside a still-open window adding a commitment before it locks — brief, opt-in overlap, not a full swap.

**Honors:** full register inside each window; same map, uninterrupted real time for the commander, no pause; reset is upgrades (Foremen) automating earlier tasks.

**Strains:** the economy player's sense of "real time" is syncopated, not continuous, which strains "consistent across every player" if read as everyone acting continuously; the shared clock itself stays unpaused.

**Prior art:** none found for a teammate on a compressed planning tempo inside a second teammate's continuous combat on one unpaused map. A chess clock's alternating budget is the closest cousin, tagged AMBIGUOUS.

**Tradeoffs:** a player drawn to sharp planning bursts gets a role built for it, at the cost of the moment-to-moment "drag a worker now" feel, since only overrides are moment-to-moment.

## Option C — The Commons (wild)

The Commons drops faceless "workers" entirely. The economy player manages the city through households — small, individually tracked bands of population, each with needs (shelter, food, safety) and a job the player assigns. Buildings rise only once a household commits to raising them; a household panicked by nearby combat drops its tools and flees, and the player must shelter, relocate, or replace it. No shipped game pairs individually tracked, needs-driven households with a live teammate's real-time combat on shared ground; that is the wild shape.

**Vignette.** The Ashford household is two minutes from finishing the granary when your partner's retreat draws raiders past their lot. Their safety need spikes; you have seconds to pull them to shelter or trust the wall. You trust it — they finish shaking, and the ticker logs "Ashford household: granary raised under fire."

**One minute:** check several need bars, reassign a job, resolve one panic, answer one request. **One mission:** grow from a handful of households to a dozen or more, convert two or three into guilds, keep panic from cascading, finish with more sheltered than started.

**Becomes the commander's units, walls, positions:** a household assigned to a garrison keeps its production timer running; a household building a wall is why it finishes on schedule, or stalls when frightened off; a sheltered household near a gate is a reason to hold it.

**Talk and sightlines:** the commander sees household icons and need states like friendly units, so a wavering bar near the front is a warning they can act on directly; the economy player sees the commander's engagements the same way, since a fight is what triggers panic.

**Reset / load-capping:** a household that survives long enough matures into a guild — an automation upgrade in household form — reassigning its own members within its specialty without individual orders; attention shifts to new households and guild decisions, so clicks per minute hold steady as population grows.

**Shared mode:** the commander can take direct command of one household near their position for as long as they hold it there, handing it back the moment they move on.

**Honors:** full register (households specialize like workers, housing caps population like supply, guild formation is a tech timing); same map, real time, no pause; reset is an automation upgrade, in the spirit proposed.

**Strains:** tying a household's safety need to the fight ties the role's fortunes to the battle more tightly than a straightforward split implies; under a hard push the player spends attention on crisis, not steady production.

**Prior art:** RimWorld and Dwarf Fortress for individually tracked colonists with needs — REPORTED (each game's defining layer). Pairing that with a teammate's live battle on identical ground is AMBIGUOUS; no precedent found.

**Tradeoffs:** a player who wants the city to feel inhabited, not administered, gets that directly, at the cost of a rhythm someone else's bad fight can knock off.

- Whether the pair shares one civilization or faces an opposing one, and how many peers share the outcome, is Q-141-01 and Q-141-02, still open. Each option describes only the fixed pair's chair and reads under either shape, but none resolves it.
