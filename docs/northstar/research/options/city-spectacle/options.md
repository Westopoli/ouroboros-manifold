# Self-running city spectacle: options

Three shapes for the city that sits on the battlefield and grows while the commander fights, with no economy for the solo player to run. Each keeps growth scripted and unmanaged while giving the eye, and in one or two small places the hand, something to hold on to.

## Option A — Ward and Wall

Ward and Wall ties the city's growth to the reinforcement structures already fought over on the map. While a side holds a garrison or militia station, the ward nearest it — matching that structure's production type — visibly thickens: roofs multiply, stalls and yards appear. Lose the structure and the ward stalls or recedes at the pace it grew. The city reads the capture-and-hold fight already underway; it asks no question.

**Vignette.** You break the enemy's grip on the riverside garrison at minute six. By minute nine the dockside ward behind your wall has three new warehouses and a crane swinging cargo. Across the valley the enemy's forward base still shows the burnt husk of their own dockside ward, lost with the garrison — it has not grown back. You held a flank, and the city noticed.

**One mission / one campaign:** two to four wards grow or stall with held structures, readable at tactical zoom over minutes; across a campaign, which wards took root shapes the city next time.

**Eye:** a captured structure's ward starting to build — scaffolding, cranes, stacked stone — where the fight just happened.

**Touch and the line:** the one lever is the production-type choice already made at a held structure, which also picks which ward blooms. Prioritizing between wards, or assigning materials to one, would be the line into economy management — never opened here.

**Forward base / death:** the attacker's base is a harder-edged cluster around structures it holds at start, a staging ward, not a home. When a city's core falls, every ward it fed freezes mid-construction; the ticker reads the loss and no reinforcement or replenishment follows. A burned ward's rubble persists to the next mission there, clearing at the civilization's pace.

**Honors:** no agent; grows during the mission; every side has a readable city and forward base; death ends reinforcement and replenishment; damage persists at the civilization's pace; the lever is one the player already uses.

**Strains:** growth rides on only 2 to 8 structures per map, so a lightly-contested city grows in one or two wards, smaller than the reference art's whole-canvas sprawl.

**Prior art:** Total War's siege damage carrying into the next battle at a settlement — REPORTED (totalwar.fandom.com/wiki/Siege_escalation). None found for growth-by-capture as a visible ward system.

**Tradeoffs:** a player who fights for structures gets a city that visibly rewards them; one who never contests them gets a quiet, small city.

## Option B — The Long Bloom

The Long Bloom treats the city as weather: a slow ambient construction curve off the region's economic field, ticking forward through the mission regardless of what the player does at any one structure. It reads closer to a diorama than a system — buildings rise the way clouds cross a sky — but the player gets one gesture: spending an earned mission reward to move a named district (harbor, terraces, market road) up the queue.

**Vignette.** Ten minutes in, the ticker offers a choice: spend this mission's supply cache on the harbor quarter or the hill terraces. You pick the harbor. Twenty minutes later, sails and a breakwater have appeared where there was open coast; nothing else visibly changed. You go back to the fight.

**One mission / one campaign:** the city advances one or two stages on its own schedule; the nudge only picks order. Across a campaign the curve compounds: a long-prosperous civilization's city arrives further along than one recovering from collapse.

**Eye:** a district crossing a growth stage mid-mission — a wall going from wood to stone — on the world's schedule, not the player's.

**Touch and the line:** the reward-spend nudge, plus one civic-identity pick (garrison or trade town) made once at mission start. A running build queue or a resource counter would be the line; only single either/or moments exist here.

**Forward base / death:** the attacker's base runs the same curve but starts mid-growth, harbor or terrace tracks suppressed — raised in a hurry. When a city dies its curve halts, the last stage stays frozen and damaged; the ticker announces reinforcement and replenishment have stopped. A district resumes at its last stage after the time jump, clearing fast for a rich civilization, slow or visibly incomplete for a poor one.

**Honors:** scripted curve, no agent; grows during the mission; forward bases leaner than homes; death halts reinforcement and replenishment; damage persists at economy pace; both levers stop short of managing resources.

**Strains:** growth runs on a fixed schedule independent of the fight, so a hard-fought defense and an easy one can produce the same city.

**Prior art:** Age of Empires Online's always-visible, customizable capital city — VERIFIED (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Empires_Online). Frostpunk's automatons, cosmetic-only when active — REPORTED (frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Automatons).

**Tradeoffs:** guaranteed spectacle for almost no attention cost, at the price of feeling disconnected from the fight beside it.

## Option C — The Tideline (wild)

The Tideline makes the city's footprint itself mobile: population is drawn, cluster by cluster, toward whichever stretch of riverbank, coast, or wall the player is actively holding, and new structures rise where it settles. There is no fixed growth site — the city's edge creeps along the valley or shoreline toward the safest ground in real time. No shipped strategy game moves a city's footprint live during a real-time battle in response to where the player stands; that is the wild shape.

**Vignette.** You dig in on the low bluff above the ford, holding it three straight minutes against a raiding push. On the fourth minute, roofs appear on the slope behind you — settlers fled from the contested riverside. By minute eight there is a street there, following the bluff's curve, not a grid. You just did not leave.

**One mission / one campaign:** the city's edge migrates along terrain — never leaping, never ringing a single unit — toward whichever ground stayed safest longest; two or three clusters typically root per mission. Across a campaign, the city's shape records where past commanders held the line.

**Eye:** small figures crossing open ground toward the player's held position and breaking ground behind it.

**Touch and the line:** the only lever is standing on undeveloped, terrain-suitable ground for a sustained stretch — already what holding means. Naming what gets built or choosing a site directly would be the line; the pull is one scripted rule (nearest safe suitable ground), never a joint decision.

**Forward base / death:** the attacker's base is deliberately thin and unresettled, new this mission, no time to draw population. When a city's core dies, every migrating cluster stops or scatters; the ticker reports reinforcement and replenishment ending there. Resettled clusters are as fragile as the rest of the city — a short time jump leaves them as rubble, clearing at the civilization's pace.

**Honors:** no agent (one distance-and-safety rule, not a decision-maker); grows during the mission as a direct consequence of play; follows terrain, never rings; death ends reinforcement and replenishment; damage persists at economy pace; the only lever, holding ground, cannot be mistaken for administering an economy.

**Strains:** growth needs sustained, safe holding, so a highly mobile, aggressive playstyle may see little resettlement in a mission — risking a read of "nothing is happening."

**Prior art:** none found for a live, combat-reactive, terrain-following city footprint. Manor Lords' organic, terrain-following, gridless town growth is REPORTED prior art for the shape itself, between sessions rather than live (thegamer.com/manor-lords-building-basics-complete-guide). The live-migration mechanism as a whole is tagged AMBIGUOUS.

**Tradeoffs:** a defensive player watches the city grow toward them, a direct reward for positional play the game already asks for; an aggressive player trades that spectacle for tempo.

## Findings

- "A deliberate spectacle... cheap enough to keep the screen busy at scale" (R-159-02) against the epic's own multiplayer scale (up to 4 peers, real-time, no pause) is not answerable from the player's chair: whether several simultaneously growing, damaged, rebuilding cities read clearly at once is a rendering-budget question, not a player-facing one, so no option resolves it.
- Q-159-03 (which pieces of the city the player builds) stays open by the confirmed brief itself. Each option proposes its own smallest nudge, as asked, but none fixes the buildable-piece catalog — that answer was never in scope here.
