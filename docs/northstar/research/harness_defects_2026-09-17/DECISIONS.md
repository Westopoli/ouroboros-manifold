**C3 ruled (Westley, 2026-09-19, verbatim):** "A = attacker, B = defender. Attack is an \"->\". A -> B, B defends, next tick A can -> B again if they were unsuccessful and still have the resources. But if I remember correctly, attacking is more of a committment than defending, especially if it's a \"surprise\" attack by doing it in fewer ticks. Eventually if B holds out A will have made a mistake by weakening itself in resources. So short answer: yes possible not not recommended." Resolution: a faction may attack the same target again on the **next** tick if the previous attack failed and it still has the resources; within one tick, at most one resolved attack per attacker-target pair, so step 3d does not re-declare against a target whose attack this faction already resolved this tick (the follow-up waits a tick). No penalty rule beyond the existing cost asymmetry (attack commits more than defence, surprise most of all). Consequences: one line in ALG-14/15 and the E-05 epic under the change protocol; a small `om_step_decisions` change with its own test and control; the R7i/R7k/204b3 fixtures become consistent with one attack per tick and the same-tick workaround fixtures can be undone.
