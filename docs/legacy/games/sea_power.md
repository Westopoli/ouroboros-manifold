# Sea Power: Naval Combat in the Missile Age (Triassic Games / Microprose, 2024)

## Verified facts

Sea Power: Naval Combat in the Missile Age is a Cold War naval simulation game developed by Triassic Games (a studio that emerged from the Killerfish Games team, creators of Cold Waters) and published by Microprose. It entered Steam Early Access in late 2024. Confidence: REPORTED (developer lineage and EA launch window from training data; exact EA date not independently verified at capture).

The developer Killerfish Games (predecessor team) released Cold Waters in 2017 to strong reception among the niche naval-sim audience. Sea Power represents the successor project from the same creative core, operating under the Triassic Games name and with Microprose as publisher. Confidence: REPORTED (public developer record from training data).

Microprose is a revived publisher label (relaunched circa 2020) that specifically targets simulation and wargame niches — flight sims, naval sims, wargames — that major publishers abandoned in the 2000s and 2010s. Its catalog includes titles like Task Force Admiral, Second Front, and Carrier Command 2. The Microprose publishing model is: low-budget, niche-targeted, community-marketed, long-tail support. Confidence: REPORTED (Microprose public catalog and publisher positioning from training data).

Sea Power's scope covers Cold War naval surface and submarine warfare in the missile era (approximately 1960s–1990s). Mechanics center on fleet command, anti-ship missile engagement geometry, and asymmetric Cold War scenario design. Confidence: REPORTED (game description and press coverage from training data).

## Role in Ouroboros research

Sea Power is in the Research Lane for two specific structural questions, not as a genre peer.

**T-CL1 (post-launch content cadence — Microprose pattern).** Microprose as a publisher demonstrates a specific post-launch support model for niche-hardcore games: extended Early Access periods with incremental content drops, community-driven scenario creation tooling, and long-tail engagement through modding or official scenario packs. Sea Power is the live test case. The question for Design Lane is whether a similar cadence (slow, deep content drops to a loyal small audience) is viable for Ouroboros post-launch, versus a broader content marketing cycle.

**T-F3 (EA vs full launch — niche-hardcore EA case).** Sea Power entered EA with a hardcore-simulator audience that had pre-existing loyalty from Cold Waters. This is a different EA risk profile than a game entering EA with no prior audience. The Cold Waters → Sea Power transition is a case study in how a niche developer leverages a prior title's audience to de-risk an EA launch: the audience trusts the team, tolerates incomplete features, and advocates within the niche community. Confidence: REPORTED (general pattern from Microprose/Killerfish history; Sea Power-specific EA performance AMBIGUOUS).

The key data point this card cannot confirm: whether Sea Power's EA launch actually performed to expectations or undershot. Training data does not have sufficient post-launch reporting at the resolution needed. This is the primary open question.

## Competitive shape

Sea Power is not a direct genre competitor to Ouroboros — it is a simulation game in a narrower realism register with no narrative/character layer. The competitive shape relevance is at the publishing-model and audience-activation level, not the design level.

The Microprose model demonstrates that there is a viable commercial channel for games that deliberately serve hardcore niches rather than expanding audience. The publisher explicitly does not pursue mainstream press coverage or mainstream pricing elasticity. This is a structural counter-model to the "broaden the tent" publishing approach.

For Ouroboros, the Microprose reference is most useful as a calibration point: if Ouroboros targets the niche-hardcore strategy audience (stellaris-to-old-world spectrum), what does a committed long-tail publisher relationship look like, and does it fit the Ouroboros content model?
