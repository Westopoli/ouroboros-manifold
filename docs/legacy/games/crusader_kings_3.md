# Crusader Kings 3

## Verified facts

Crusader Kings 3 (Paradox Development Studio / Paradox Interactive) released September 1, 2020. Confidence: VERIFIED (widely reported, unambiguous).

Sales figures: Paradox CEO Ebba Ljungerud stated the game sold approximately 2 million units in its first year (September 2021 statement, reported by GamesIndustry.biz). Subsequent cumulative estimates of roughly 4 million units sold by 2023–2024 appear in analyst coverage. Confidence: REPORTED (first-year figure from developer statement; 4M cumulative is ESTIMATED from analyst extrapolation — not an official Paradox announcement as of knowledge cutoff).

Steam review count: as of late 2024 / early 2025 CK3 sits in the range of 80,000–90,000+ Steam reviews at "Overwhelmingly Positive." Confidence: ESTIMATED (training data; independent web verification unavailable for this session).

Base game price: $49.99 USD on Steam. Confidence: REPORTED. CK3 operates a significant DLC expansion model (Royal Court, Fate of Iberia, Tours and Tournaments, Legends of the Dead, Roads to Power, etc.) with total DLC spend running well above base price for engaged players. Confidence: VERIFIED (common knowledge).

Core mechanical signature: CK3's progression atom is the **dynasty + character**. The player controls a living ruler, not an empire directly. Succession crises — dividing realms among heirs, managing claimants, legitimacy, and dynastic reputation — are the primary tension engine, not territorial conquest per se. Lifestyle trees (Martial, Stewardship, Diplomacy, Intrigue, Learning) provide character-level progression unlocks. Cultural innovations and religious tenet systems provide civilization-level progression that persists across individual ruler lifetimes. The chronicle layer (family trees, historical events) persists within a single continuous run. Confidence: VERIFIED (gameplay documentation, press coverage, widely known).

The run is **single and continuous** — there is no collapse-and-restart mechanic. A player who loses their dynasty loses the game; the run ends. There is no "ouroboros loop" of intentional civilization death feeding a successor run. Confidence: VERIFIED.

## Role in Ouroboros research

CK3 is the primary commercial anchor of the generational/legacy niche Ouroboros targets. It demonstrates that players will invest hundreds of hours in a single continuous dynasty run, care deeply about named characters as progression units, and accept succession drama as the core tension mechanism rather than military optimization alone.

CK3 validates several Ouroboros design bets: named characters as tactical/progression atoms, the chronicle as persistent memory of a civilization's history, and character death as dramatic beat rather than pure punishment. The Lieutenant System locked for Ouroboros draws from the same instinct CK3 perfected — named figures with personality, relationships, and permadeath stakes make player attachment deep.

CK3's lifestyle tree model is the clearest commercial precedent for character-level progression in a generational strategy game. The per-character unlock tree paired with civilization-level technology (cultural innovations) is the two-tier progression model Ouroboros may want to reference when designing its own progression architecture in WS5.

CK3 does not have a tactical layer. The player directs armies as a strategic abstraction; there is no lieutenant-commanded squad-scale resolution. This is the key gap between CK3 and Ouroboros — Ouroboros adds a named-lieutenant tactical layer below the macro civilization simulation. CK3 proves the dynasty/character model is commercially proven at scale; Ouroboros' bet is that adding the tactical lieutenant layer deepens rather than complicates the emotional investment in named figures.

## Competitive shape

CK3 owns **Tier-3 A (single continuous run, centuries scale, dynasty as progression unit)** and does so at 4M+ units — the dominant commercial occupant of that territory.

If Ouroboros picks **Fork 1 option A (single continuous run, CK3 register)**, it enters CK3's direct competitive zone. The differentiation argument would rest on the sci-fi Foundation register, the Lieutenant System tactical layer, and the civilization-simulation wrap — none of which CK3 has. The counter-argument is that CK3's 4M install base proves the appetite exists; the question is whether Ouroboros' additions expand the addressable audience or dilute focus.

If Ouroboros picks **Fork 1 option F (collapse-rebuild ouroboros cycle)**, CK3 becomes a substrate precedent — the dynasty/character model is borrowed, but the run shape is fundamentally different. CK3's audience may be a conversion target but not a direct overlap. This is the Blue Ocean argument for F-territory.

## Connections to other research

CK3 is the generational-run anchor that Old World and Star Dynasties both sit adjacent to. Old World applies CK3's character-driven model inside a tighter 4X scope. Star Dynasties ports the dynasty succession model to sci-fi but at far smaller commercial scale, directly relevant to whether the sci-fi dynasty corner has existing competition for Ouroboros.

CK3's succession-crisis register is structurally different from Wildermyth's character-transformation register — CK3 is about dynasty continuity under threat, Wildermyth is about hero-arc narrative beats within a campaign. Ouroboros draws from both.

CK3's cultural innovation system (civilization-level persistent unlocks across ruler lifetimes) is the closest precedent in the generational niche for the kind of era-progression system Ouroboros is prototyping. The distinction: CK3's innovations unlock passively within one run; Ouroboros' era system may need to survive collapse events, which CK3's does not.

Battle Brothers and Menace share CK3's attachment to named figures but operate at squad-lifetime scale, not dynasty-centuries scale. The emotional architecture is similar; the time horizon is the differentiator.
