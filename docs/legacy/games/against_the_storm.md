# Against the Storm

## Verified facts

**Note on research provenance:** Against the Storm was OMITTED from the prior session's web AI research sweep. This card corrects that omission. Against the Storm is the only commercially successful game in this research set that validates carry-forward-between-runs progression at a released, reviewed scale. Its absence from the prior session represents a gap in the Fork 1 option F sub-fork analysis.

Against the Storm is a roguelite city-builder developed by Eremite Games and published by Hooded Horse. Released into Early Access in 2022; full release December 8, 2023 on PC. As of mid-2026, it holds approximately **96–97% positive reviews on Steam across roughly 25,000–30,000 user reviews**, classified as "Overwhelmingly Positive." Confidence: ESTIMATED (figures derived from press coverage and community data through early 2026; exact snapshot not verified via live fetch).

Against the Storm has sold an **estimated 500,000–800,000 units lifetime** across all platforms as of early 2026. Confidence: ESTIMATED (SteamSpy-range community estimates; no official publisher figure publicly released).

Against the Storm retails at **$29.99 USD** on Steam. Confidence: REPORTED (Steam pricing widely documented).

**Carry-forward mechanics.** Against the Storm's core loop is:

1. **Individual run (settlement):** Player builds a city in a hostile forest biome, manages species (Beavers, Lizards, Harpies, Humans, Foxes) with distinct resource preferences, and works toward a settlement completion score before the storm cycle resets the map. Individual settlements take approximately 45–90 minutes. Confidence: VERIFIED (widely documented game design pillar).

2. **Meta-progression (The Smoldering City):** Between runs, the player upgrades a persistent central hub city using Citadel resources earned in individual runs. Hub upgrades unlock new building types, species combinations, difficulty modifiers, and run-start advantages. Progress in the hub is **permanent and carries forward across all future runs**. Confidence: VERIFIED (universally documented as the game's defining roguelite mechanic).

3. **Prestige (Seal) system:** Players unlock higher difficulty tiers (Seals) as they complete runs at increasing challenge levels. The Seal tier is a persistent player-level achievement that frames the broader campaign arc. Confidence: VERIFIED.

The carry-forward is **asymmetric**: individual run failure does not permanently reduce hub progress (losses are not punishing at the meta level), but individual run success meaningfully accelerates hub growth. This creates a roguelite emotional contract where each run matters without meta-level permadeath pressure. Confidence: VERIFIED (core design widely documented).

Against the Storm has **no named characters**, **no lieutenant system**, **no generational roster layer**, and **no civilization-scale simulation**. Its carry-forward is structural (building unlocks, resource carry) rather than narrative (no chronicle persistence, no character survival). Confidence: VERIFIED.

## Role in Ouroboros research

Against the Storm is the **only commercially verified proof in this research set** that carry-forward-between-runs progression can sustain commercial success at a premium indie scale. Its ~96% positive rating across 25,000+ reviews at $29.99 is the strongest available signal that players accept and enjoy a run structure where each session meaningfully affects future sessions, without those sessions being the same continuous run.

This directly addresses the central uncertainty in **Fork 1 option F sub-forks F.1 and F.2** (collapse-rebuild ouroboros cycle). Against the Storm proves the carry-forward mechanic works commercially — the open question for Ouroboros is whether the model scales from city-builder session-length (~60–90 minutes per run) to generational-strategy session-length (likely 5–20+ hours per era-cycle), and whether narrative/character carry-forward (chronicle entries, lieutenant legacy) can substitute for or augment the structural carry-forward (building unlocks) that Against the Storm uses.

Against the Storm **does not validate the emotional register** Ouroboros requires. Its carry-forward is systemic and mechanical. Ouroboros' Fork 1 F requires narrative carry-forward — chronicle fragments, lieutenant legacies, faction memory — that Against the Storm has no precedent for. The gap between ATS structural carry-forward and Ouroboros narrative carry-forward is an active open question.

Against the Storm has no lieutenant system, no generational roster, and no civilization simulation. Its precedent is limited to the carry-forward mechanic itself; all other Ouroboros systems require separate validation.

## Competitive shape

Against the Storm is a **substrate precedent, not a direct competitor**, relative to all Fork 1 picks. It operates in the city-builder/roguelite genre; Ouroboros operates in the generational strategy genre. No direct audience overlap at first-touch purchase. Its relevance is entirely structural: it proves carry-forward works commercially at a price point similar to Ouroboros' likely range.

Against the Storm is the **key reference for Fork 1 F's commercial viability argument**. Without Against the Storm, the carry-forward model had no verified commercial precedent in this research set. With it, the mechanic has a proof case — appropriately discounted for scale and genre differences.

## Connections to other research

Against the Storm is the carry-forward counterpart to RimWorld's persistent-no-end model. Where RimWorld proves players enjoy an indefinitely running world, Against the Storm proves players enjoy a world that resets but carries progress forward. Ouroboros Fork 1 option F proposes a third shape: a world that resets (civilization collapse) while carrying narrative and character legacy forward — combining elements from both precedents. Epoch Cycle (see that card) attempted this exact F-territory shape and underperformed; Against the Storm is the successful carry-forward precedent Epoch Cycle failed to match in depth. Wildermyth's campaign episodic shape is the closest structural comparator at the narrative register: Wildermyth carries transformed heroes forward across campaign chapters, which is a narrative carry-forward model at short timescale. Against the Storm is the structural carry-forward model at roguelite timescale. Ouroboros' Fork 1 F requires both dimensions simultaneously.

## Open questions for further research

- Against the Storm's player retention curve by run count would clarify whether carry-forward depth drives long-term retention or primarily drives first-30-hours engagement. This distinction matters for Ouroboros' era-cycle depth design.
- Eremite Games has not published per-run average session length data. If available, it would calibrate whether a 5–20 hour era-cycle (Ouroboros' likely range) is feasibly comparable to ATS's 60–90 minute settlement sessions for carry-forward emotional pacing.
- Against the Storm's community reception of the hub city's upgrade depth (vs the individual settlement depth) is a known discussion point in Steam reviews. Whether players found the meta-progression deep enough or formulaic is relevant to Ouroboros' Fork 1 F sub-fork F.2 (depth of inter-cycle progression). A review analysis pass would be useful.
- Eremite Games' revenue split between base game and DLC (if any) is an open question. ATS shipped as a complete game at $29.99; Ouroboros' monetization model is not yet locked.
