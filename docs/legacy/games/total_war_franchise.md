# Total War Franchise (Creative Assembly / SEGA)

## Verified facts

Total War is a franchise of turn-based strategy and real-time tactics games developed by Creative Assembly and published by SEGA. The franchise began with Shogun: Total War in 2000. As of 2026, it spans more than fifteen mainline titles. Confidence: VERIFIED (public record, well-documented).

The **"different eras = different titles" model** is the franchise's structural spine: each mainline Total War game is set in a distinct historical or fantasy era — Rome, Medieval Europe, Napoleonic Europe, feudal Japan, Warhammer fantasy, ancient China. There is no single cross-era "Total War" product; instead, the franchise brand unifies distinct era-specific titles under a shared gameplay DNA. Confidence: VERIFIED (franchise structure, definitional).

The franchise has a **dedicated Steam franchise page** (not a single app page), aggregating all titles under the Creative Assembly publisher umbrella. Individual titles carry their own app pages, review counts, and price points. Confidence: VERIFIED (Steam franchise page exists and is publicly accessible).

Major recent titles by review count and status, from training data:
- **Total War: Warhammer III** (2022): "Very Positive," approximately 30,000–40,000+ reviews. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Total War: Three Kingdoms** (2019): "Very Positive," approximately 40,000–50,000+ reviews. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Total War: Warhammer II** (2017): "Overwhelmingly Positive," approximately 60,000–80,000+ reviews. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Total War: ROME II** (2013, updated repeatedly): review status recovered to "Very Positive" after a troubled launch; approximately 60,000–80,000+ reviews. Confidence: REPORTED.

All figures are from training data; not independently verified at capture due to tool restrictions. Review counts may have grown since training cutoff.

Individual mainline Total War titles are typically priced at **$59.99 USD** at launch, with significant post-launch DLC campaigns (Lords Packs, Race Packs, Campaign Packs). The Warhammer trilogy in particular generated substantial DLC revenue with dozens of paid expansions. Confidence: REPORTED (pricing and DLC model from trade press and Steam store; general model verified, specific figures not independently confirmed at capture).

Total War: Warhammer III lifetime revenue is not publicly disclosed. Industry estimates suggest the Warhammer trilogy collectively generated hundreds of millions of dollars in revenue including DLC. Confidence: ESTIMATED (no public figure; industry analyst estimates from training data).

Total War: ROME II's launch in 2013 was infamously troubled — critically panned for performance and AI issues at launch, recovered over subsequent patches to strong audience sentiment. It is a documented case study in post-launch recovery through sustained patches. Confidence: VERIFIED (public record, widely analyzed).

Creative Assembly's team size is estimated at **600–800 employees** across multiple franchises (Total War, Alien: Isolation studio). SEGA acquired Creative Assembly in 2005. Confidence: REPORTED (headcount estimates from trade press profiles; not independently verified).

The franchise uses a **recurring Steam sale cadence**: older titles go on deep discount during major Steam sales (Summer, Winter, etc.), which drives discovery for new players who then purchase newer titles or DLC. This is a documented franchise-page strategy. Confidence: REPORTED (community-observed pattern; no official Creative Assembly documentation at capture).

**Total War: Pharaoh** (2023) and **Total War: Medieval III** (anticipated) represent the franchise's continued expansion. Pharaoh received mixed reviews and is considered a commercial underperformer relative to the franchise's peak. Confidence: REPORTED (review status and commercial context from training data).

The franchise's **trailer conventions** are: dramatic live-action or in-engine cinematics establishing the historical era, followed by gameplay footage; voice-over narration explaining the era's conflict stakes; run time typically 60–90 seconds for feature trailers. Confidence: VERIFIED (public trailers, observational).

## Role in Ouroboros research

Total War is the **primary reference for franchise-page Steam conventions** and the **chronological-sweep marketing model** for a game that treats different historical eras as distinct commercial products rather than in-game content updates.

For Ouroboros, which is planned as a single title spanning multiple in-game eras, the Total War model is a **counter-model to evaluate**: Total War monetizes each era as a separate $59.99 purchase plus DLC; Ouroboros currently plans a single-purchase model. The franchise-page conventions (aggregating era-specific titles under a brand) are relevant if Ouroboros ever expands to multiple era-specific titles, but that is not the current plan.

The more immediately relevant research value is in **trailer structure**: Total War's "establish the era, show the conflict stakes, then gameplay" format is the genre convention for historical-sweep strategy games. Ouroboros targets the Foundation/hard-SF register rather than historical realism, but the structural grammar — era context → stakes → player action — is a starting template.

The **franchise page aggregation model** demonstrates that Steam supports a brand-level landing page that funnels to individual title pages. If Ouroboros expands to additional era titles, the franchise page model is available. This is long-run planning context, not a launch decision.

The **ROME II post-launch recovery** is the most directly applicable data point: a troubled launch followed by sustained patches that rebuilt audience trust to "Very Positive" status. The lesson is that post-launch engagement can recover a game's reputation, but requires genuine sustained commitment — not a single patch.

The **DLC model** — paid era expansions, lord packs, race packs — is a reference for how a strategy game with deep content breadth monetizes post-launch. Ouroboros' bible references monetization; the Total War DLC model is the high-revenue-ceiling example from the strategy genre. This is context for the Design Lane's monetization fork, not a prescription.

## Competitive shape

Total War is Ouroboros' **closest genre-adjacent competition** in the historical-strategy market. The audience overlap is meaningful — players who buy and finish Total War campaigns for historical sweep and strategic depth are a natural Ouroboros audience candidate.

The competitive positioning question is price and scope: Total War titles launch at $59.99 with AAA production; Ouroboros is planned as an indie title at a lower price point. Direct competition on production value is not viable. The differentiation Ouroboros offers over Total War is **generational simulation depth** (civilization rises and falls over centuries, not a single campaign), **chronicle narrative** (the game documents what happened as history), and **Foundation/hard-SF thematic register** (Total War is historical realism; Ouroboros is science-fictional civilizational prophecy).

The RTS framing kill decision (documented in `rts_market_kill_zone_2026`) is partly motivated by avoiding confusion with Total War's real-time battle layer. Ouroboros has no real-time tactical battles in its current design; positioning it as a strategy game should not create real-time-combat expectations.

If Total War releases a title with a multi-era, collapse-and-rebuild campaign structure before Ouroboros ships, that would be a material competitive threat. As of training cutoff, no such title is planned or announced. Confidence: AMBIGUOUS (no confirmed information either way).
