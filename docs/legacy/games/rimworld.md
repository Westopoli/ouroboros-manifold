# RimWorld

## Verified facts

RimWorld is a colony management simulation developed and published by Ludeon Studios (Tynan Sylvester, solo development studio). Released into Early Access September 2016; full release October 17, 2018 on PC. As of mid-2026, it holds approximately **97–98% positive reviews on Steam across roughly 230,000–250,000 user reviews**, classified as "Overwhelmingly Positive." Confidence: ESTIMATED (figures derived from widely-reported press and community data through early 2026; exact snapshot not verified via live fetch).

RimWorld lifetime revenue is widely reported in industry press as **approximately $300M+ USD** as of late 2024, making it one of the highest-grossing premium indie games on Steam. Confidence: REPORTED (figure cited in multiple industry analyses and developer interviews through 2024; Ludeon has not published an official verified number).

RimWorld retails at **$40 USD** on Steam. The Biotech and Anomaly DLC expansions each retail at $22–25 USD; total catalog expenditure for a fully-DLC'd install is approximately $100–110 USD. Confidence: REPORTED (Steam store pricing widely documented).

**Progression mechanics relevant to Ouroboros research.** RimWorld is a **persistent sandbox with no mandatory run end**. The player manages a colony of named pawns — survivors, colonists, slaves, prisoners — each with individual traits, skills, backstories, and relationships. There is no explicit "win condition" enforced by the game unless the player actively pursues a specific end-game scenario (the "ship" launch or other escape options, added in expansions). Most players run their colony until catastrophic failure, indefinite stability, or personal satisfaction. Confidence: VERIFIED (core game design, universally documented).

RimWorld has **no cross-run carry-forward**. Each new game begins with a fresh colony; no progress, characters, or Chronicle entries persist from a prior run. The roguelite model does not apply. Confidence: VERIFIED.

RimWorld's **named pawn system** is central to player emotional investment. Each colonist has a generated name, appearance, traits (e.g., "Pyromaniac," "Kind," "Misogynist"), skills, and a relational graph with other pawns. Deaths of named pawns trigger mood debuffs in surviving pawns and are a core emotional event. Confidence: VERIFIED (widely documented game design pillar).

RimWorld includes a **storyteller AI system** (Cassandra Classic, Phoebe Chillax, Randy Random) that modulates the rate and severity of incoming events to create a narrative arc shape without scripted events. This is relevant to Ouroboros as a pattern for how a simulation can produce narrative texture without authored set-pieces. Confidence: VERIFIED.

## Role in Ouroboros research

RimWorld is the benchmark commercial reference for the **Tier-3 E run shape** (persistent open-ended sandbox, no forced run end). Its $300M+ lifetime figure is the high-water mark for the premium indie sandbox tier and defines the ceiling of the addressable market for games that let players determine their own run length and victory condition.

RimWorld validates that players invest deeply in **named characters at the individual level** even within a colony-scale simulation — not just faction-level abstraction. This is directly relevant to Ouroboros' Lieutenant System: if players in a colony sim mourn the death of a named pyromaniac farmer, they will invest in named lieutenants with personality in a civilization-scale simulation.

RimWorld's **no cross-run carry-forward** design is the contrast case against Fork 1 option F. RimWorld proves open-ended persistence is commercially viable; it does not validate the collapse-rebuild cycle that F proposes. Against the Storm (see that card) is the carry-forward reference RimWorld cannot provide.

RimWorld has **no civilization-scale simulation**, **no era progression**, and **no chronicle persistence across runs**. Its temporal scale is a single colony lifetime (years in-game, hours in real-time). Ouroboros operates at a fundamentally different temporal scale (generations, centuries, cycles) and adds layers RimWorld explicitly does not include.

## Competitive shape

RimWorld is an **orthogonal title** relative to all Fork 1 picks. It operates in the colony-sim genre rather than the grand strategy / generational strategy genre; it lacks era progression, lieutenant-as-tactical-atom structure, and civilization-scale dynamics. Ouroboros does not compete with RimWorld for the same player segment at first-touch. The overlap is emotional-investment-in-named-characters — a shared emotional layer, not a shared genre position.

The commercial significance of RimWorld to Ouroboros is as a **ceiling reference**: the $300M+ figure represents what a premium sandbox can achieve with a deeply invested named-character emotional core and no forced run end. Ouroboros is aiming at a smaller niche (generational strategy, not colony sim), but RimWorld confirms the premium market will sustain a high price for deep character investment in a simulation frame.

## Connections to other research

RimWorld's named pawn emotional investment model is the colony-sim analog to what Wildermyth achieves at campaign scale (named heroes with transformation arcs) and what Battle Brothers achieves at mercenary-company scale. All three validate that named-character emotional investment drives retention and word-of-mouth across different genres. Menace validates the same pattern at squad/tactical scale. Ouroboros combines all of these into a generational-roster layer (lieutenants) inside a civilization simulation. Stellaris is the relevant comparator for the civilization-scale simulation shell; RimWorld is the relevant comparator for the named-character emotional layer within that shell. Against the Storm is the one game in this set that adds carry-forward between runs, which neither RimWorld nor Stellaris include. Epoch Cycle attempted the collapse-rebuild cycle RimWorld explicitly avoids.
