# Stellaris

## Verified facts

Stellaris is a real-time grand strategy game developed by Paradox Development Studio and published by Paradox Interactive. Released May 9, 2016 on PC. As of mid-2026, it holds approximately **94–95% positive reviews on Steam across roughly 130,000–140,000 user reviews**, classified as "Overwhelmingly Positive." Confidence: ESTIMATED (Steam review counts shift continuously; figures are derived from widely-reported press coverage and SteamSpy-adjacent community data as of late 2025 — exact snapshot not verified via live fetch).

Stellaris has sold an **estimated 3–5 million units lifetime across all platforms**, with total franchise revenue (base game + DLC) estimated in the hundreds of millions of dollars. Confidence: ESTIMATED (Paradox has not published official lifetime figures; estimates sourced from analyst commentary and SteamSpy-range data reported in industry press through 2025).

The base game retails at **$39.99 USD** on Steam; total DLC catalog (as of 2025) runs to 20+ expansions and species packs with individual prices ranging from $2.99 to $19.99. Confidence: REPORTED (Paradox store and Steam store pricing, widely documented).

**Progression mechanics relevant to Ouroboros research.** Stellaris ships two interlocking macro-progression systems that function as era-equivalents:

1. **Traditions.** A branching tree of passive bonuses organized into thematic unity trees (expansion, supremacy, harmony, etc.). Traditions are unlocked by accumulating Unity resource over time. They represent the soft "era" gating of a civilization's strategic posture — a fresh empire has no traditions; a mature empire has fully unlocked several trees. Confidence: VERIFIED (widely documented in official Paradox wiki and press coverage).

2. **Ascension Perks.** Milestone-tier bonuses unlocked one per completed tradition tree. These are high-impact civilization-defining choices (Synthetic Evolution, Engineered Evolution, Transcendence, etc.) that alter the faction's fundamental character for the rest of the run. Perks are permanent and irreversible within a run. Confidence: VERIFIED (official Paradox wiki, press, and community documentation).

Stellaris runs are **open-ended sandbox with an optional victory condition** (score-based at a configurable end date). The game does not force a run-end; players can continue indefinitely. Stellaris ships with a **campaign editor and scenario mode** (the "Scenario" system added in later DLC cycles) alongside the standard sandbox start. This gives it a footprint in both Tier-3 C (campaign + sandbox split) and Tier-3 E (persistent world). Confidence: VERIFIED for the open-ended nature; REPORTED for the campaign-editor claim (added via DLC, widely covered).

Stellaris has **no generational layer** and **no cross-run carry-forward**. Each sandbox run starts fresh. The progression arc lives entirely within a single run. Confidence: VERIFIED.

## Role in Ouroboros research

Stellaris is the dominant commercial proof that players will engage with deep macro-progression systems at civilization scale over very long single-run sessions (runs routinely reach 20–60+ hours). The Traditions + Ascension Perks system is the most commercially successful real-money implementation of era-equivalent macro-progression in the genre.

Stellaris validates that players accept and enjoy a model where early-game and late-game civilizations feel qualitatively different — not just quantitatively larger — via unlocked progression tiers. This is directly relevant to Ouroboros' era-progression system, where earlier and later eras should create different strategic vocabularies, not just bigger numbers.

Stellaris has **no Lieutenant System** and **no generational roster layer**. It operates at the faction/empire level, not the named-character level. Its relevance to Ouroboros is structural (era-progression validation) rather than character-system validation.

Stellaris has **no collapse-rebuild ouroboros cycle**. A defeated empire in Stellaris is a run-end state, not a designed re-entry point. This is the gap that Fork 1 option F proposes to occupy.

## Competitive shape

If Ouroboros picks **F-territory on Fork 1 (collapse-rebuild ouroboros cycle)**, Stellaris is a **substrate precedent** — it validates deep civ-sim progression mechanics that Ouroboros would also use, but Stellaris does not occupy the collapse-rebuild run shape. Ouroboros would own territory Stellaris explicitly does not enter.

If Ouroboros stays in **D-territory or C-territory** (single-run episodic or campaign shape), Stellaris is an **indirect competitor on mindshare** — players who want civilization-scale grand strategy already have Stellaris; Ouroboros would need to differentiate sharply on the Lieutenant System, the Foundation/hard-SF register, and the generational/chronicle depth that Stellaris lacks.

Stellaris is not a direct competitor at any Fork 1 pick because it is real-time (vs Ouroboros' turn-based simulation layer) and because it lacks all three of Ouroboros' differentiating layers (lieutenants, generational roster, chronicle persistence).

## Connections to other research

Stellaris' Traditions + Ascension Perks model is the closest macro-progression precedent to what Ouroboros implements at era level. The Old World (Mohawk Games) is a closer structural comparator for the named-character layer within a grand strategy frame, but Old World operates at a much smaller geographic and temporal scale than Stellaris. CK3 is the strongest cross-reference here: both Stellaris and CK3 validate that players invest deeply in faction-scale progression over long sessions, but CK3 additionally validates the named-character / dynasty emotional layer that Stellaris lacks. Menace validates the Lieutenant System at squad scale; Stellaris validates the civ-sim progression shell that wraps the lieutenants. Against the Storm is orthogonal to Stellaris — it operates at city-builder scale with carry-forward, where Stellaris operates at galaxy scale within a single run. RimWorld and Stellaris share the open-ended sandbox shape (Tier-3 E adjacency) but differ sharply on scale and progression philosophy.
