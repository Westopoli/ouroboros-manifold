# Manor Lords (Slavic Magic / Hooded Horse, 2024)

## Verified facts

Manor Lords is a medieval city-builder with real-time tactical battles developed solo by Greg Styczeń under the Slavic Magic label, published by Hooded Horse. Released into Early Access on April 26, 2024. Confidence: VERIFIED (public release record, widely documented).

The game accumulated approximately **3 million wishlists** prior to EA launch, making it one of the most-wishlisted indie titles on Steam heading into 2024. Confidence: REPORTED (figure widely cited in press coverage from training data; exact count at launch date may vary by source).

**Week-1 sales exceeded 2 million copies**, with some sources reporting figures closer to 2.5 million within the first week of Early Access. Confidence: REPORTED (figures from press coverage in training data; exact figure varies across sources — 2M is the conservatively cited floor).

Steam reviews are in the "Overwhelmingly Positive" or "Very Positive" band. As of mid-2025, reviews numbered in the range of 80,000–100,000+ with approximately 85–90% positive sentiment. Confidence: REPORTED (training-data figures; exact current count not independently verified at capture due to tool restrictions).

The base price at launch was approximately **$29.99–$34.99 USD**. Confidence: AMBIGUOUS (some training-data sources cite $29.99, others $34.99; regional pricing variation may account for discrepancy).

Greg Styczeń (handle "Slavic Magic") is a single developer who built the game over approximately six years prior to EA launch. Confidence: REPORTED (widely covered in developer profiles and press).

Hooded Horse is the publisher, best known for handling niche strategy titles including Against the Storm and Terra Invicta. The Hooded Horse publishing deal is credited with part of the marketing infrastructure; the organic creator-driven wishlist growth predates the publishing relationship. Confidence: REPORTED (Hooded Horse publisher record and game association from training data).

The game generated substantial **YouTube creator coverage** including prominent channels in the city-builder and strategy space. A significant portion of the wishlist is attributed to creator-driven discovery rather than paid advertising. Confidence: REPORTED (press analysis and developer statements from training data).

**TikTok organic traction** was noted in industry analysis as a contributing vector for the game's pre-launch momentum, particularly among an audience segment not previously engaged with city-builder or strategy genres. Confidence: REPORTED (cited in industry coverage from training data; specific clip/creator attribution not available at capture).

The game is described as a blend of **Banished-style settlement building** with **Total War-adjacent tactical battle resolution**. The tactical layer is turn-free real-time, not a separate tactical campaign mode. Confidence: VERIFIED (mechanic widely documented in reviews and developer notes).

As of mid-2025, the game remained in Early Access with active development. Confidence: REPORTED (training-data status; current EA status not verified at capture).

Lifetime sales figures beyond the week-1 window are not cleanly broken out in available sources. The week-1 figure of ~2M copies is the most-cited datapoint. Confidence: AMBIGUOUS (post-launch trend data not available at capture).

## Role in Ouroboros research

Manor Lords is the **primary reference case for solo-dev EA launch scale via wishlist conversion**. It demonstrates that a single developer with a long-dev-cycle game, significant creator coverage, and organic social momentum can convert a large pre-launch wishlist into a week-1 sales event that rivals mid-sized studio launches.

The game is not a genre peer to Ouroboros — it is a city-builder / tactics blend, not a generational strategy simulation. Its relevance is entirely on the **go-to-market and launch-mechanics side**: how wishlists accumulate, when to launch EA, what creator-driven organic coverage looks like at scale, and what the conversion rate from wishlist to day-1 purchase can reach under favorable conditions.

The ~3M wishlist → ~2M+ week-1 copy figure implies a **conversion rate of roughly 65–70%**, which is dramatically above the typical Steam wishlist-to-purchase conversion rate (commonly estimated at 10–30% for most titles). The implication for Research Lane is that this conversion rate is not a baseline expectation but an outlier driven by specific conditions: years of creator-documented dev progress, genre-fit with the city-builder audience's high wishlist engagement, and near-perfect launch timing.

The **TikTok organic traction** angle is notable for Ouroboros because the generational strategy genre is not a TikTok-native genre. Manor Lords crossed genre audiences via visually compelling settlement footage. The question for Design Lane (not resolved here) is whether Ouroboros has analogous visual hooks for short-form organic spread.

## Competitive shape

Manor Lords is not a direct genre competitor to Ouroboros. The game occupies a different niche (medieval city-building + tactical battles vs. civilizational simulation + generational strategy). Audience overlap is partial — players who enjoy the management and long-session depth of Manor Lords are plausible Ouroboros candidates, but the core gameplay loop and register are distinct.

Manor Lords is a **go-to-market reference**, not a gameplay competitor. Its primary value to this research set is establishing what wishlist-driven solo-dev EA success looks like at the upper bound, which calibrates T-W1 and T-W2 research.

The Hooded Horse relationship is also a data point: Hooded Horse has now published multiple indie strategy titles (Against the Storm, Manor Lords, Terra Invicta) and has an established relationship with the niche strategy Steam audience. It is a potential publisher reference for Ouroboros' own publishing decision.
