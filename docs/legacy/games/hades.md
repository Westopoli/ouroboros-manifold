# Hades (Supergiant Games, 2020)

## Verified facts

Hades is a roguelite action game developed and published by Supergiant Games. It entered Steam Early Access on December 6, 2018, and released version 1.0 on September 17, 2020. Confidence: VERIFIED (public release record, well-documented).

The game received an **"Overwhelmingly Positive"** Steam review rating. As of mid-2025, review counts were in the range of **80,000–100,000+** user reviews, with approximately 97% positive sentiment. Confidence: REPORTED (figures from training data; Steam page not independently fetched at capture due to tool restrictions; count may have grown since training cutoff).

The base price is **$24.99 USD**. Confidence: REPORTED (pricing from training data; no DLC or battle pass model — single purchase, complete game).

Supergiant Games is an independent studio based in San Francisco with approximately 20–25 employees. Prior titles include Bastion (2011), Transistor (2014), and Pyre (2017). All titles self-published. Confidence: VERIFIED (public studio record).

Hades won the **Hugo Award for Best Video Game** in 2021 — the first video game to win a Hugo Award. It won numerous GOTY awards from outlets including PC Gamer, IGN, and Polygon for 2020. Confidence: VERIFIED (public awards record).

Reported lifetime sales as of late 2021 were approximately **1 million units** on PC, with additional sales on Nintendo Switch and other platforms. Some industry estimates place total cross-platform sales in the **3 million+ range** by end of 2022. Confidence: REPORTED (figures from developer interviews and trade press; Supergiant has not published official sales figures consistently; estimates from training-data context).

The **Steam page** for Hades is frequently cited in game-developer postmortems as a benchmark for confident, specific, low-hype game description copy. The page describes what the game is, uses crisp bullet points for features, and uses capsule art that is distinctive and character-focused. Confidence: VERIFIED (developer community discussions, GDC talks, and Steam-page-optimization postmortems repeatedly cite it).

The **Early Access strategy** was a documented success case: Supergiant used Early Access to iterate on the game's design and build community momentum over two years. The studio founder Greg Kasavin described the Early Access period as transformative for the game's narrative system design — player feedback on character relationships directly influenced how the narrative was deepened. Confidence: REPORTED (Kasavin interviews and GDC talks; specific quote details from training data).

The game's **narrative architecture** threads story progression through run repetition rather than against it: each run through the underworld produces dialogue, relationship advancement, and story beats that carry over into subsequent attempts. Death is diegetically integrated as a return to the starting hub, not a game-over screen. Confidence: VERIFIED (core design, universally cited in critical analysis).

The game ships with **no post-launch paid DLC**. A sequel, Hades II, entered Early Access in 2024. Confidence: VERIFIED (public release record; no paid DLC for original Hades).

The **trailer** for Hades uses tight action-clip editing with character voice lines as hooks, communicates the tone (mythological, irreverent, high-production audio) in under 60 seconds, and does not use a narrated overview format. Confidence: VERIFIED (trailer is publicly available; description from training data).

## Role in Ouroboros research

Hades is the **primary benchmark for a confident-indie Steam page** at the $24.99 price point with "Overwhelmingly Positive" review status. For Ouroboros' T-S4 research (Steam page conversion norms), Hades establishes the upper bound of what a well-executed indie page looks like in review sentiment, capsule art quality, and copy discipline.

The Hades Steam page's copy discipline — specific, declarative, zero hype inflation — is directly applicable to Ouroboros' planned page. Ouroboros is a complex simulation game with multiple systems; the Hades precedent argues against listing every feature and for leading with the emotional/thematic hook. The specific formulation Hades uses ("Battle through ever-changing environments as you slash your way out of the Underworld of Greek myth") is a genre-specific template for how to describe a game with procedural variance without saying "procedurally generated."

The **narrative-roguelite synthesis** Hades achieved — making run repetition feel like narrative progression rather than narrative interruption — is a design precedent for Ouroboros' planned chronicle persistence across commander deaths and era collapses. Hades solved "how do you tell a story in a game where you fail and restart" at the roguelite scale; Ouroboros faces the same problem at civilization scale.

The **Early Access iteration model** Supergiant used is a precedent for how community feedback can deepen a narrative system during development. Ouroboros is not currently in Early Access, but if an EA period is considered, the Hades model (use EA to deepen narrative responsiveness, not just fix bugs) is the reference case.

The **trailer structure** — character voice lines as hooks, no narrator, immediate tone establishment — is the T-V1 reference for how an action-adjacent indie game communicates identity in under 60 seconds. Ouroboros has less moment-to-moment action than Hades, which creates a trailer challenge: what is the Ouroboros equivalent of the combat clip that hooks in 3 seconds?

## Competitive shape

Hades and Ouroboros do not compete directly. Hades is a real-time action roguelite; Ouroboros is a turn-based generational strategy game. The audience overlap is partial — players who value narrative depth in a roguelite structure may be interested in both, but the play-context is entirely different (20-minute action runs vs. multi-hour strategy sessions).

The competitive relevance is in **market positioning**: Hades occupies "overwhelmingly positive, confident indie, $24.99, narrative depth" territory with near-universal critical recognition. Ouroboros targets an adjacent quadrant of the indie market (strategy vs. action, century-scale vs. run-scale). The presence of Hades in the market raises the bar for what "confident indie narrative depth" looks like; Ouroboros needs to meet that bar on its own terms.

If Ouroboros is priced at $24.99 (matching Wildermyth, Hades, and several other research-set titles), it enters a competitive comparison set that includes Hades. Players will unconsciously benchmark perceived value against it.
