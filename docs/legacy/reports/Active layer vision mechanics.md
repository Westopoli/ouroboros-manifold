# Paid Vision Is Common, Proven Depth Is Not

Shipped games price "looking" in many currencies, and the pricing is well documented: StarCraft II's Scanner Sweep costs 50 energy, the same as a MULE worth about 225 minerals, and shows a radius-13 circle for 12.3 s; Dota 2's team Scan costs one of two 210 s charges for an 8 s, 900-radius presence check; Homeworld Remastered turned a passive Sensors Array into a select-and-press ping that shows enemies for a few seconds. In naval, space and Supreme Commander-style games, sensing that is active tends to announce the sensor, and the real-world basis (radar echo falls with the fourth power of range, an intercept receiver sees the outgoing pulse fall with the square) is why emission is modeled as outranging detection. The layered-battlefield evidence is thinner and more negative than the reveal evidence: reviewers and players of Homeworld 3, Falling Frontier, BSG Deadlock and Ancient Space describe the extra axis as optional, automated, or without strategic depth, while layers that did change play (submarine thermal layers, Supreme Commander and Beyond All Reason's per-layer weapons and sensors, Wargame helicopter altitude, StarCraft II high ground) each came with an explicit rule, cost or counter. The notes contain no telemetry, win-rate data, developer postmortem or controlled comparison on whether forcing an action to use a layer or reveal adds depth. Every depth judgment below is a reviewer, forum-poster or wiki-author opinion, and the notes found no documented case of a shipped game removing an active reveal for attention-tax reasons, nor any developer statement about "busywork." The notes also found no shipped game with five stacked vertical layers where the player must trigger each layer's vision by a select-ping-layer-click sequence.

## Active reveals are priced in energy, charges, munitions and exposure

The best-documented active reveal is StarCraft II's Scanner Sweep. It costs **50 energy** from an Orbital Command, has whole-map range, reveals a **radius-13** circle for **12.3 s**, and shows cloaked, burrowed and hallucinated units ([Liquipedia Scanner Sweep](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Scanner_Sweep); VERIFIED on two reads of the same community wiki, no Blizzard primary found for the numbers). The cost is a trade against income: a MULE also costs 50 energy, lasts 64 s and returns **225 minerals** (nine trips of 25), and the MULE page itself says there are "few instances" where using that energy to scout would be worth the lost economy "especially with Scanner Sweep costing the same amount of energy" ([Liquipedia MULE](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/api.php?action=parse&page=MULE_(Legacy_of_the_Void)&prop=wikitext&format=json); VERIFIED that the wiki says this, community-authored). The notes' own arithmetic (ESTIMATED, not a source claim) is that 50 energy regenerates in about 63.5 s, so a scan roughly equals one minute of Orbital energy and about 225 minerals forgone. Pro usage is thinly sourced: the Scanner Sweep page attributes to the player Trump the advice to "nearly always scan your opponent's base at 6:30" to read the build, and lists matchup uses such as Dark Templar tech, cloaked Banshees and Lair-timing reads (REPORTED, quote unsourced on the page). Brood War's Comsat Station uses the same 50-energy cost but stores up to **four scans** (200 energy cap), so its limit is a stockpile rather than a trade against income, with a reveal of "6.8-11" s in one read and "about seven to ten seconds" in another ([Liquipedia Comsat Station](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft/api.php?action=parse&page=Comsat_Station&prop=wikitext&format=json); VERIFIED for 50/200, AMBIGUOUS for duration, no patch given). The wiki adds that being scanned "may indicate an imminent attack at that location" (REPORTED, an ordinary player inference) and that Dark Templars frequently target Comsats to avoid detection (REPORTED). Whether the enemy is notified of a Scanner Sweep was not established in the notes.

The two other StarCraft II reveal tools show repeated retuning rather than removal. Oracle Revelation went from 43 to 30 s duration in patch 4.0, then in 4.12.0 from 50 to 25 energy, 2 to 10 s cooldown and 30 to 15 s duration ([Blizzard 4.12.0 notes](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/starcraft2/23442717/starcraft-ii-4-12-0-patch-notes); VERIFIED for 4.0 and 4.12.0), then 15 to 20 s in 5.0.2 (REPORTED). The Sensor Tower, a persistent passive radar that "senses - but does not detect" approaching units and shows them as red exclamation marks, fell from 125/100 to 100/50 and from range 30 to 22 across 5.0.11 and 5.0.14 ([Liquipedia Sensor Tower](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/api.php?action=parse&page=Sensor_Tower_(Legacy_of_the_Void)&prop=wikitext&format=json); REPORTED). MULE itself was removed in patch 2.5.5, returned as an energy-free autocast, and reverted to the energy-based version within about a month, which changes the very trade that makes scanning costly; no rationale was found (REPORTED). Neither official notes page carries a designer rationale for any of these changes.

Dota 2 and League of Legends treat vision as a priced, timed, contestable resource. Dota's Scan, added in patch 6.87, "scans a targeted 900 AoE for 8 seconds," "indicates whether there are enemy heroes in that area," "does not show how many heroes there are, just if there are any enemies," counts smoked heroes, and "enemies do not know when your team casts it"; it began on a team-wide 4.5 minute cooldown ([Dota 2 6.87](https://www.dota2.com/687); VERIFIED). Later versions cut the cooldown to 210 s in 7.03 and added a second charge in 7.34 ([Liquipedia Scan](https://liquipedia.net/dota2/Scan); REPORTED). The patch notes give no developer rationale, and the notes' reading that Scan is a deliberate resolution downgrade from a ward (binary presence, no count, no identity, against a ward's six minutes of full vision) is an inference (ESTIMATED). The Observer Ward lasts **360 s** with **1600** vision, stock of 4 and a 135 s restock, and a dewarding bounty that scales at 4 gold per minute ([Liquipedia Observer Ward](https://liquipedia.net/dota2/Observer_Ward); VERIFIED on two reads). The notes could not find the patch that made observer wards free. In League, the trinket ward lasts 90-120 s with 2 charges, the Control Ward costs 75 gold and "reveals and disables enemy wards," the Oracle Lens sweeps for 8 s, and the long-range Farsight has a 4000 cast range and 500 sight radius ([LoL Wiki Stealth Ward](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Stealth_Ward), [Control Ward](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Control_Ward), [Oracle Lens](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Oracle_Lens), [Farsight Alteration](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Farsight_Alteration); all VERIFIED). Vision score credits both ward lifetime provided and denied, with modifiers for "staleness, redundancy, and safety"; Riot's vision designer called it "a useful metric to evaluate performance, but nowhere near a 'final score'" ([Surrender at 20, April 2017](https://www.surrenderat20.net/2017/04/red-post-collection-gameplay-thoughts_20.html); VERIFIED). Across both games the notes see vision paid in several currencies at once (gold, charges, restock time, exposure when placing) with denial separately rewarded; only a low-quality commercial guide states that framing, so it is ESTIMATED. No quantified data on dewarding frequency or vision value was readable (a Medium analysis returned 403, a Springer chapter was paywalled).

The closest documented analogue to "select, press a button, see for a few seconds" is Homeworld Remastered. A player wrote in 2015 that the Sensors Array "now requires that you select it and press a button to 'ping' and then you can see enemy units on the map but only for a few seconds, in the original games you could see them all the time" ([Steam: HW1 Remastered differences](https://steamcommunity.com/app/244160/discussions/0/617328967244650904/); VERIFIED that the post says this, REPORTED as fact about the game). Another poster put the ping at "1000" and "5-10 seconds" for all ships ([Steam Fog of War thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/244160/discussions/0/530646080858298060/); REPORTED), and one search summary adds a 10 s cooldown and a 500 RU scout ping (AMBIGUOUS/low confidence, a single unverified summary). The poster presents the change as a downgrade, but no developer rationale and no broader reception data exist in the notes. Company of Heroes 2 prices reveals in munitions: the Recon Sweep costs 4 command points plus 60 munitions, gives vision that "lasts only seconds with a single pass," and a fan guide calls it "lackluster" with extremely limited uptime and prefers a Major commander's two scout planes ([COH2.ORG](https://www.coh2.org/guides/21804/knowing-is-half-the-battle-recon-company-strategy); VERIFIED, fan guide). Flares cost 40-70 munitions with ranges and durations in the tens of seconds, and sweeps and overflights reveal camouflaged units while flares do not (REPORTED, patch unspecified). World in Conflict's Aerial Recon tactical aid is described by a fan summary as "mostly regarded as a waste of points as special forces behind enemy lines and helicopters offer more view and last much longer" ([World in Conflict wiki](https://worldinconflict.fandom.com/wiki/Tactical_aid); REPORTED, no official cost, radius or duration). The notes themselves observe that both complaints have the same shape (a short reveal priced in a scarce resource loses to a persistent unit that does the job), but flag them as community opinions on specific options, not generalizable facts (ESTIMATED).

Free or cheap scouts are documented but are never costless in something. The AoE IV Scout costs 65 food and is a light cavalry unit also used for hunting and herding ([Fandom Scout](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Scout_(Age_of_Empires_IV)); REPORTED). Northgard's Scout Camp costs 50 wood plus 15-20 Krowns of yearly upkeep, scouts have 50 health and no attack, and revealing a tile takes 20, 60 or 80 s depending on distance from the Town Hall ([Northgard Scout Camp](https://northgard.fandom.com/wiki/Scout_Camp); REPORTED). The StarCraft II Changeling is the nearest to free: **0 minerals, 0 gas, 0 supply**, lasting 107 s, but it needs an Overseer (50/50, 12 s morph from an Overlord) and Overseer energy ([Liquipedia Changeling](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/api.php?action=parse&page=Changeling_(Legacy_of_the_Void)&prop=wikitext&format=json); REPORTED). Its counters are attention-based: select it and read "Changeling," notice it has no selection circle or ignores orders, force-attack it, or hit it with long-range or cloaked units. The notes read this as the scout's cost being paid by the opponent's attention (ESTIMATED). Wargame treats recon as a unit: a recon guide says "a tank will only be able to cover his vision and not his range," and competitive play needs 5-6 recon assets to deny the opponent information dominance ([Troublmaker](https://troublmaker.wordpress.com/2014/04/01/recon-and-counter-recon-wargame-red-dragon-strategies-and-tactics/); VERIFIED, community blog). They Are Billions scouting facts and AoE IV scout counters were not found.

On design commentary the notes are explicit that the cupboard is nearly bare. Brandon Casteel's general RTS argument is that "the player themselves have a limited supply of an implicit resource - time" and that "either player is capable of forcing their opponent to misallocate or waste time to their own advantage" ([Wayward Strategy](https://waywardstrategy.com/2014/12/18/random-thoughts-on-resource-management-in-rts/); VERIFIED), but that post does not discuss scouting, so applying it to scan or ping design is the note-writer's extension (ESTIMATED). No GDC talk, developer interview or caster commentary (Day[9], Artosis, Husky) on scan skill expression or on active-vision "busywork" was found.

## Sensing that gives the sensor away outranges the thing it senses

The real-world rule that games copy is that emitting reveals you farther away than you can see. Wikipedia's active sonar article states that an active pulse "will reveal a submarine's position at twice the maximum distance that the submarine can itself detect a contact," and calls active sonar "normally considered a backup to passive sonar" ([Wikipedia, Active sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_sonar); VERIFIED as Wikipedia states it, the "twice" being a rule of thumb). For radar, echo energy "drops with the fourth power of the distance" while an interceptor sees the pulse drop "only as the square of distance," so the receiver "is always at an advantage over the radar in terms of range," and low-probability-of-intercept techniques (frequency agility, pulse compression, long low-power pulses, low sidelobes) narrow the gap ([Wikipedia, LPI radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-probability-of-intercept_radar); VERIFIED). Electronic support measures platforms "can remain electronically silent and detect and analyze RADAR transmissions beyond the RADAR detection range" ([Wikipedia, ESM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_warfare_support_measures); VERIFIED). The notes derive that the intercept-to-detection ratio depends on target cross-section, receiver sensitivity and transmitter power, so "2x" is not fixed (ESTIMATED). Emission control, as defined in a glossary summarizing naval doctrine, aims "to prevent an enemy from detecting, identifying, and locating friendly forces," with graduated conditions from complete silence to minimal limits ([Windward EMCON glossary](https://windward.ai/glossary/what-is-emcon/); REPORTED, USNI pages returned 403).

Naval simulations carry this as an information trade, and the evidence is mostly player-sourced. In Cold Waters a player says you hear pings "FAR before you can be detected by them" because active sound travels out and back ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1290691937729379755/); REPORTED); the pinged player gets "a yellow line briefly pointing away from your submarine on the map in the bearing of the active source," bearing only, no range ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/2921102177257146984/); REPORTED); and a player on pinging describes going active as trading bearing secrecy for fast range and bearing: "The moment I go active they launch on me. Within about 30 seconds" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1368380934295009433/); REPORTED), with active sonar useful at about 2.5 km or less. The exact detection threshold on the signature screen is AMBIGUOUS between a "+10" reading and a "up to 10 dB" reading, and one poster complains that submarines are detected within about 1,000 yards regardless of stealth measures (REPORTED). Dangerous Waters tips say "AI subs fire on Active sonar bearing easily most of the time" and "If you begin a mission by pinging away on active sonar, you will have already given away your presence as an ASW threat" ([Subsim tips](https://subsim.com/tips/tip_dangerwaters.html); VERIFIED, tips page text). Sea Power flags a contact with "E" and "R" for emission and radar detection, and a player concluded "EMCON will get you killed" against IR-guided sea-skimmers that are visible only at about 4 nm ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1286220/discussions/0/597386590728244241/); REPORTED); a guide describes the design as "strategic trade-offs between using active sensors to detect enemies versus remaining silent" (REPORTED, secondary framing, not the developer). Command: Modern Operations lets sensors be toggled per group or per sensor, added "intermittent emissions," and made self-defense override EMCON by default (Build 522) after players complained units "spontaneously light up their radars" ([Matrix Games forum](https://forums.matrixgames.com/viewtopic.php?t=256722); REPORTED).

Space and fleet games separate emission from detection more finely. Nebulous: Fleet Command players state the core trade as "It's either Stealth or detection," with radar off leaking nothing electronic but leaving optical and wake signatures, and jamming hiding identity but leaking bearing ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/887570/discussions/0/3189115186362121110/); REPORTED). ELINT "says 'There is an active RADAR transceiver in this direction'" and "however far a given radar can see, ELINT can see that radar from farther" (REPORTED); the community missile wiki documents that radar-off blinds ACT, SARH, ARAD and HOJ seekers while datalink-guided CMD still works, so the stealth switch is also a missile defense ([NEBFLTCOM wiki](http://nebfltcom.wikidot.com/mechanics:missiles); VERIFIED, may lag the current patch). HighFleet treats emission discipline as graduated actions (continuous sweep, single sweep, sector search, off): radar off makes you undetectable by enemy radar, ELINT picks up radars at roughly **2x** radar range for the MP-404 and **1.4x** for the MP-21, and IRST is a short-range passive sensor that "cannot be detected" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1434950/discussions/0/4345408777045049710/), [gameplay.tips guide](https://gameplay.tips/guides/11966-highfleet.html); REPORTED, Fandom wiki blocked). Starsector is the cleanest documented example of a short, player-triggered active sensing burst with a stated cost. Detection range is sensor strength plus the other fleet's sensor profile; the Active Sensor Burst gives **+3000 strength and +5000 profile**, takes 0.2 days to activate, lasts 0.1 days, has a 1 day cooldown, limits the fleet to go-slow speed, and has no resource cost listed; "hostile fleets within range will then start going towards the location/direction of the sensor reading to investigate," and players use it to bait patrols away, then slip through while dark ([Starsector Wiki, Active Sensor Burst](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Active_Sensor_Burst), [Avoiding combat](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Avoiding_combat), [Sensors](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Sensors); VERIFIED, community wiki). Go Dark gives -50% sensor profile (-75% with a skill), at a speed cost, and pursuers who lose track head for the last detected position. The developer's 2015 sensors post, as summarized by a search result, describes tiers of information by range (a blip with fleet size, then composition, then everything) (REPORTED, page returned 403).

EVE Online shows the opposite arrangement, in which the free action is the one that does not announce. The directional scanner reaches up to 14.3 AU with a cone of 5 to 360 degrees and a 2 s reset, "does not show distance or coordinates," and does not alert the target; combat probes (0.5 AU base range, about 1 h 6 m lifetime) can be seen on d-scan, so "if you see combat probes on D-Scan, assume you're being probed down NOW" ([EVE University: Directional scanning](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Directional_scanning), [Probe scanning](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Probe_scanning); VERIFIED; the warning advice itself from a search summary, REPORTED). Ship and cargo scans give the target no notification beyond a "very subtle" faint beam ([EVE University: Cargo scanning](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Cargo_scanning); VERIFIED). Elite Dangerous silent running trades shields and cooling for a low heat signature: below about 15% heat a ship is nearly undetectable on most radars, but "within 499m ... you become instantly visible" ([Elite Dangerous wiki](https://elite-dangerous.fandom.com/wiki/Silent_Running); REPORTED, page returned 402). The notes catalog at least four ways a scan can warn its target (a bearing line in Cold Waters, an emission flag in Sea Power, a visible probe object in EVE, an AI investigation response in Starsector and the pinged naval AIs), and note that only EVE lets the target tell a scan is coming before it resolves, via an artifact on the prober's side (ESTIMATED). No developer interview or GDC-style commentary on the design choice of warning the target was found.

Supreme Commander and Beyond All Reason split intel into separate channels, which is the nearest documented model of "denying" sensing without a trigger. Supreme Commander has vision, radar, sonar and omni; radar yields unidentified blips and "Vision is the only Intel type that will identify units," stealth hides from radar but not line of sight or omni, cloak hides from sight but not radar, and a unit with both needs omni ([Supcom wiki, Intel](https://supcom.fandom.com/wiki/Intel); REPORTED, pages returned 402, numbers from search snippets). Beyond All Reason documents the same split in primary guide text: shooting at radar dots "is inaccurate" and "will miss most of the shots," targeting facilities restore accuracy (+50%, 75%, then most of the penalty), "cloaked units still have a radar signature," cloaked units cannot shoot, build or reclaim without decloaking and drain significant energy, and the Intrusion Countermeasure System shows cloaked units as blinking red circles only when they move ([BAR advanced mechanics guide](https://www.beyondallreason.info/guide/important-knowledge-on-advanced-mechanics); VERIFIED). A BAR radar tower costs 60 metal and 630 energy with radar 2100 and sight 680, and the Veil jammer costs 125 metal and 19,000 energy with 125 energy/s upkeep ([BAR Radar Tower](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/armrad), [Veil](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/armveil); VERIFIED). The notes read these as passive-coverage structures whose cost shows up as upkeep and exposure of the structure (the Juno missile targets radars and jammers), not as a per-use ping (ESTIMATED). Whether Supreme Commander or BAR radar emissions are detectable was not found, and absence was not confirmed.

## Layers that mattered carried an explicit rule, cost or counter

The strongest cases of layers changing play are submarine thermal layers. In Cold Waters, one player advises "If you are trying to hide from a sub that is below the layer, you should go above it. And if you are trying to hide from a surface ship, you should go below it," while another calls the layer "dynamic," a "sound refractor," not a mirror, and says a towed array deployed below it detects across it better than hull sonar "though the game apparently doesn't model this mechanic perfectly" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/2119355556476149147/); VERIFIED as player statements). Another poster says "it doesn't seem to matter what depth you are at, only which side of the layer you are on" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1470840994965474248/); VERIFIED as player statements). The documented cost of depth is cavitation: "Cavitation depth = 20 * SpeedInKnots - 100" ([gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/653-cold-waters.html); VERIFIED), and torpedoes can be set to run above or below the layer (REPORTED). Layer depth figures conflict (a real-world 600-1200 ft against an in-game roughly 100-300 ft in search summaries), likely real versus in-game but the sources do not say so, and the Cold Waters manual could not be read (AMBIGUOUS). Whether the towed array crosses the layer is AMBIGUOUS between two summaries of the same thread, both player opinion. Sea Power guides describe a "shadow zone" where you "can't hear anything below the layer, even with active sonar," with variable depth sonar and towed arrays able to listen and ping below it, and the real-world towed-array tactic is to keep the array on the other side of the layer from the hull ([Sea Power Thermal Layer page](https://sea-power.pmctactical.org/thermal-layer.php), [PMC Thermocline](https://naval-warfare.pmctactical.org/thermocline.php); VERIFIED for the quotes, which the latter page frames as real-world "extremely simple terms," not game rules). Advance Wars, a turn-based precedent, makes diving an explicit action: submarines dive on any water tile, become invisible unless an enemy is adjacent, burn 5 fuel per turn submerged, and only Cruisers, other submarines and Missile Silo missiles can hit them ([Advance Wars wiki](https://advancewars.fandom.com/wiki/Submarine); REPORTED, page returned 402).

Supreme Commander and Beyond All Reason have the most layers by engine definition. Supreme Commander uses five: Seabed, Sub, Water, Land and Air, with each weapon's `FireTargetLayerCapsTable` mapping the layer it fires from to the layers it may hit ([FAF wiki Weapon](https://wiki.faforever.com/en/Development/Modding/Weapon); REPORTED, direct fetch blocked). Counterplay clusters in three shapes the notes identify (ESTIMATED): layer-crossers such as torpedo bombers and amphibious tanks, units sitting where most weapons cannot target them (hover versus torpedo, submarine versus most guns), and surfacing conditions on weapons (the Yathsou's anti-air only works surfaced). In BAR, submarines "can only be seen with sonar and targeted by torpedoes, depth charges, or the Commander laser," hovercraft cannot be torpedoed, and sonar is "included on fewer units and at a lower range than normal vision" ([BAR sea warfare](https://www.beyondallreason.info/guide/basics-on-sea-warfare); VERIFIED). A review of Forged Alliance records both the virtue and the failure of the water layer: torpedo bombers could kill submarines and amphibious tanks could engage ships, but "Mass Deposits were not found in the water which made naval play contain less contention and harassment opportunities," which the FAF community later patched with underwater extractors ([GameCloud](https://gamecloud.net.au/features/opinion/why-supreme-commander-forged-alliance-is-a-masterpiece); VERIFIED). Planetary Annihilation's orbital layer split critics. Quarter to Three called orbit "a shell around each planet where satellites can provide radar coverage, where geosynchronous turrets can defend certain areas, and where death lasers can pummel the ground below," yet found the planet-to-planet transport loading "goddamnable" ([Quarter to Three](https://www.quartertothree.com/fp/2014/09/12/wont-live-happily-ever-planetary-annihilation/); VERIFIED, one reviewer's opinion). Players on Steam said units "pop in and out of the orbital layer with no transition or warning," and a beta change made arriving orbital units unable to act for several seconds, "That's your opportunity to react" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/386070/discussions/0/141136086913610961/); VERIFIED as player statements). Search summaries of other reviews call orbital units "forced" ("if you don't build them, you will lose control of your orbital area") and also call orbit "probably the best gameplay system" (REPORTED); the notes mark this AMBIGUOUS, since reviewers differ on whether obligation is a virtue.

StarCraft II's high ground and Wargame's helicopter altitude are single-boundary layers tied to sight. In StarCraft II "ground units cannot see up cliffs," air units ignore cliffs, a low-ground army "can be ambushed and cannot counterattack unless vision is gained of the high ground" by an air unit, Colossus, Xel'Naga Tower, Scan or a unit on the ledge, and an attacker firing from above reveals itself ([Liquipedia High Ground](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/High_Ground_and_Low_Ground); REPORTED, search snippet of a page that returned 429). Burrowed units can be targeted only under detection or Fungal Growth (REPORTED). In Wargame: Red Dragon, helicopters have three altitude states: at high altitude they are "easier to spot and engage, but can see and shoot over cover," and they "duck to low altitude to hide behind terrain" ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails?id=247884292); REPORTED). A player complaint documents a micromanagement tax: helicopters descend automatically after moves or attacks, losing sight and wasting ordnance, with no way to chain orders to stay high ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/251060/discussions/0/1813170373216272928/); VERIFIED as a player statement). Wargame spotting depends on optics, distance, veterancy and a stealth stat, with poor optics seeing 2.1 km and medium 2.8 km against unconcealed targets (REPORTED). BSG Deadlock ties altitude to armor and arcs, with a player saying "a major part of the current high end meta is based squarely around diving the fastest" and "most ships have more guns above them" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/544610/discussions/0/1488866180590481117/); VERIFIED as a player statement), while the same thread calls the z axis "minimal to say the least." Ground Control gives height an accuracy bonus and top-armor targeting and has shadows that conceal units (REPORTED, search summary). Sins of a Solar Empire is reported to allow vertical movement that "can be abused in PvP games and may be difficult to use" (REPORTED, forum 403). Harpoon-lineage and Command: Modern Operations tie detection to altitude, mast height and the radar horizon, with low-altitude approaches below the horizon a standard anti-ship tactic (REPORTED); exact band rules were not retrieved. Tabletop: Attack Vector: Tactical, which uses tilt blocks, stacking tiles and the AVID and RALT lookup tools, describes itself as "one of the few wargames that attempts true three dimensional movement" with "most have failed in playability" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_Vector:_Tactical); VERIFIED).

The notes' summary of when layer changes are meaningful actions (ESTIMATED across citations) is that each cited case where changing layer is a deliberate act also carries a stated cost (fuel, cavitation, reveal on attack, auto-descent, an action delay on arrival) and a limited set of counters (named weapons or sensors). The notes find no source measuring how often players actually perform these changes.

## Three-dimensional space was judged a gimmick for four recurring reasons

The documented complaints about layered or 3D battlefields cluster into four reasons, which the notes label ESTIMATED because each rests on a handful of opinion citations. First, the camera and AI absorb the axis. TheGamer's Homeworld 3 review lists the three-dimensional movement as the first notable change from most RTS games, then says "As liberating as this system sounds, it seldom comes up in actual gameplay," noting that unit AI handles cover adequately and the camera makes precise three-dimensional directions difficult ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/homeworld-3-review/); VERIFIED). Aftermath says terrain "rarely feels like it gives the player what the designers were hoping for" ([Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/homeworld-3-pc-review-impressions-blackbird-steam-rts/); VERIFIED, lines as returned by a summarizer). A Homeworld 3 player says ships "will not actually peek out to take shots," and another says "Sneaking up on the enemy is often the wrong choice because of how targeting works," though others report using cover with small strikecraft groups or to keep a carrier alive ([Steam cover thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1840080/discussions/0/4357873056153571844/); VERIFIED as player statements, from an audience skewed negative; Steam rating Mostly Negative against Metacritic 75 per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeworld_3)). ## What the notes leave unanswered

The evidence base is lopsided in several ways that bear directly on the stated question. There is no quantitative evidence, in any game, on whether a mandatory action to use a layer or reveal raises or lowers strategic depth; the notes' strongest statements are reviewer and forum opinions, and the notes explicitly say the retrieved patch history is not a systematic survey. No documented case was found of a shipped game removing an active reveal ability outright, and none where a developer said an active scouting ability was busywork. Developer rationale is missing for nearly every change cited: Scanner Sweep's cost and duration, the MULE removal and reversal, Revelation and Sensor Tower retuning, Dota's Scan cooldown and charges, Homeworld Remastered's ping conversion, and the LoL trinket scaling. The Homeworld Remastered ping numbers rest on forum posts (cost 1000, 5-10 s, with a 10 s cooldown and 500 RU scout cost from one unverified summary), and the Brood War Comsat duration conflicts between sources. Several sources were blocked or paywalled (all Fandom wikis, hoodedhorse Nebulous wiki, Fractal Softworks, USNI, several Steam guides, the Cold Waters manual, Liquipedia direct pages), so many REPORTED items rest on search summaries. The notes did not find Steel Division or WARNO material, Halo Wars reveal powers (a negative search result), They Are Billions scouting, the Zerg Overlord's vision and cost, Nebulous's exact signature formulas, any SupCom unit-by-unit layer tables, Harpoon altitude-band rules, or telemetry on how often players change layer. No source in the notes describes a game with exactly five vertical layers, a per-layer targeted vision burst, or free scouts that exist only in eras lacking the technology.

## Conclusion

The record separates three things that are easy to run together. Priced, timed reveals are common and have exact numbers, but their reception data is a few community verdicts: a recon sweep called "lackluster," an aerial recon "mostly regarded as a waste of points," a ping read by one player as a downgrade from a passive array. Those verdicts share one cause the notes name (a short reveal costing a scarce resource that a persistent scout undercuts), yet the notes treat them as opinions on specific options, not findings. Sensing that gives the sensor away is the best-supported mechanic in terms of rule clarity, with the real-world physics (emission outranging detection) matching the games' models, but its depth claims are player framings ("It's either Stealth or detection," "EMCON will get you killed"), and every game in the set implements the warning differently.

On layers, the cited cases where a layer mattered each pair a position-dependent rule with a named counter and a stated cost, and the cases judged gimmicks are described as having an automated, optional or uncontested axis. Whether that pairing is cause or coincidence is not established by the notes: they contain no usage telemetry, no experiment and no postmortem. The question of whether a player-triggered action is what makes a layer distinctive therefore rests on analogies between games that differ in genre, layer count and reveal mechanic, and the closest matches to the designer's sketch (Homeworld Remastered's select-and-ping, Starsector's burst, Dota's Scan) each have only forum-level or wiki-level reception evidence.
