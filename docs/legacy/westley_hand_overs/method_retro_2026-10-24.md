# Is the method the bottleneck? Retro 2026-10-24, for Westley

Your question (2026-10-24): "I expected with this level of compute we'd be able to accomplish a simple beta in a 2 week span ... I'm second guessing the method I've been enforcing."

Short answer: yes, the method as practised is the bottleneck, but not the part you suspected. The rules that make the game buildable at all (frozen contracts, one owner per file, fenced tests on the sim, a gate with a playthrough) are the rules every documented success uses. What is eating the compute is the ceremony around each piece and the coordinators that run it. Six weeks in, of which the first 34 days also built the harness, product commits fell from 397 in the week of 09-21 to 36 and then 18 in the last two weeks (the last one partial, and partly a shift to lab and records work), and 8 of 240 stories have been seen. The single-session games you heard about are real, but they are monolithic browser games with no determinism, no pipe and no multiplayer; the three research reports found none with our architecture.

Sources: every number below names its file. Measured half: `docs/northstar/plans/retro_2026-10-24/method_cost.md` (a Sonnet engineer over the records, 2026-10-24). Research half: the three Gemini Deep Research reports you ran, in the same folder. A Sonnet auditor read this page against those sources and tried to refute its three load-bearing claims; its corrections are applied and noted at the end. Nothing in the method changes before you have read this and said which of the three lists below you want.

## What the research says (three findings that matter here)

1. The single-session games are not what they sound like. Of the cases with a repository (research_single_session_games.md, table), the best is a 55,000-line Three.js shooter from 60 to 100 hours of an unattended builder-critic loop; blind testers scored it 3.6 to 5.05 of 10. All skipped determinism, saving and multiplayer; most skipped tests, the best case had a smoke test and a pixel diff. None used two processes or two languages. The Godot pipeline author abandoned GDScript for C# because unattended runs could not catch type errors before runtime, a problem our gate meets by running the client (the playthrough and `reach_lint` stages) rather than by trusting the parser.
2. The practice that works is ours minus the ceremony. research_combining_and_failures.md, synthesis: the documented successes share four invariants, leaf-inward order with thin shims, human-partitioned state schemas, translate first then redesign, and test oracles locked outside the agent's write permission; the failures let the agent own the architecture and hit the "Three-Month Wall" (the Metroidvania postmortem died at two months). research_solo_agent_method.md: healthy test-to-code is 1:1 to 1.5:1, heavy spec frameworks on small tasks are "a sledgehammer to crack a nut", local gates over 5 to 10 minutes get abandoned, and in one Unreal project 87k lines of tests over 68k lines of game passed while a 19-minute controller playtest found 11 bugs (one project, grade B).
3. Vendor guidance now says one primary agent loop with sub-agents for exploration, disjoint parallel work and adversarial review, not a role per step (research_solo_agent_method.md, section 2). Our cost data splits two ways: on the credit pipeline the expense follows the model tier per piece (Fable drafting and review $150 of $197, Sonnet builders $3.60), and on the subscription side it is the coordinators ($1,130 of $1,487 in the programme; 638 of 1456 Dispatcher calls above 400k). Neither is the small builders.

## Recommendation

Drop (needs your word, each is a rule of yours or a Westley-approved ruling):
- Tests first by a separate writer for client and look pieces. 348 rows classed test-defect in five weeks. The auditor's split of the about 70 client findings of the last two weeks: about 25 found by a frame, a play or your look, about 10 by a test, 11 by both, 24 by neither (scouts, audits, spec reads). Mostly not the tests. Keep tests-first for the sim and the pipe, where one fenced test caught a real save/load defect before main (ledger row 1360, a unit field missing from the save row) and where determinism cannot be seen in a frame.
- The per-piece records ceremony: a ledger row per named draft, the provenance lint's quote-in-assert rule (it stopped two landings this week, TD-HARNESS-wt-cap-1 and TD-HARNESS-context-guard-1, over wording), attach flip plus lane row plus page row for each landing. One ledger row per landing, written by the landing clerk.
- Fable as per-piece drafter and reviewer on the credit pipeline ($150 of $197). Specs and rulings stay Fable; the per-piece spec paragraph and the review of a built piece go to Sonnet, with Fable only on a contract piece.
- The gate under QA load with a 600 s suite timeout. The gate script already holds the 30B play lock (DEC-gate-holds-agora-lock-2026-10-24); raise the suite timeout to the 2400 s the landing template exports (merge_gate.sh defaults to 600), or the gate rejects nothing but its own clock.

Keep:
- Frozen contracts (header, pipe frames), one owner per file, new code in new files, wiring as its own piece. The research's four invariants.
- Fenced tests on the sim and the pipe, the gate's playthrough and checksum trace. These are the determinism contract.
- Westley sees the frame before "done". Green tests did not see backwards sprites or death-reset bugs in the two game projects the research found.
- The worktree cap, the context guard, the no-polls rule. Landed this week; they are the fix for hypothesis 5.

Try (one experiment, separate go, separate budget, as in the plan):
- One player-visible story end to end (a mission start to a win screen), built two ways on API credit, same clock, same dollar cap. A: the method above with the drops applied. B: one Sonnet 5.5 session on max effort with the bible and the frame as its brief, the 30B playtesting, no fenced tests, determinism checked after by the gate. You see both frames; the gate runs on both; the cost is recorded. The result decides how far the loosening goes.

What a two-week beta needs that none of the above supplies: a scoped list of the stories a player must see, in order, with the loop closed (start a match, give orders, see consequence, win or lose). The records have 240 stories and no such list. That is an Architect deliverable for your approval before any build, and it costs no credit.
