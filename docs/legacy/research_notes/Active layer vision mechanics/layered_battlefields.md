# Layered Battlefields in Strategy and Tactics Games: Rules, Player Actions, and Reception

Scope: shipped games and tabletop precedents with more than one layer, plane, altitude, or depth in the same battle space. Focus is on what the player does with the layers (move between, see across, attack across, hide in). No design proposals.

Confidence tags: VERIFIED = statement read on the cited page in this session (fetched through a summarizer tool, so wording is as returned; player-forum quotes are verified as "player said X", not as "X is true"). REPORTED = secondary source or search-result snippet only, page not read in full. ESTIMATED = my derivation, shown. AMBIGUOUS = sources disagree, stated how.

## Homeworld series (full 3D): vertical-axis use, formations, and whether 3D mattered tactically; Homeworld 3 terrain and cover

### Takeaway
Reviewers and players split: Homeworld 3 reviewers say full 3D "seldom comes up in actual gameplay" and that terrain "rarely feels like it gives the player what the designers were hoping for," while older Homeworld players say vertical flanking is real in multiplayer but "by no means CRITICAL." Homeworld 3's cover is a line-of-sight blocker for direct-fire weapons, mostly handled by unit AI, and players report not using it in missions.

### Cited Findings
- TheGamer's Homeworld 3 review: "The first notable change from most RTS games is the three dimensions of movement—units aren't locked to a 2D plane." It lists navigating moving asteroids, finding cover behind debris, and positioning "above or below other starships to create unique formations." Then: "As liberating as this system sounds, it seldom comes up in actual gameplay"; unit AI handles cover adequately without player input and the camera makes precise 3D directions difficult during combat. — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/homeworld-3-review/) — VERIFIED
- Aftermath's Homeworld 3 review: terrain can be a target ("you can send your units to them, instead of just to positions in 3D space, and sometimes even attach to them") but "the use of terrain in battles rarely feels like it gives the player what the designers were hoping for." — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/homeworld-3-pc-review-impressions-blackbird-steam-rts/) — VERIFIED (as returned by summarizer; the quoted lines are the only ones the tool surfaced)
- Search summary of the Prima Games and Grogheads Homeworld 3 reviews: a button flips the mothership back to the original Homeworld vertical axis but has no tactical significance; pilots "often ignore granular orders and fly straight into turrets' line of fire"; the camera makes precise directions hard and units can get confused about designated locations. Pages not read directly (Grogheads returned a redirect). — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/homeworld-3-review-drifting-in-space), [Grogheads](https://grogheads.com/review/21339) — REPORTED
- Player thread on the Z axis in Homeworld Remastered: Sentinel-Ghost says it is "good for positioning and preparing ambushes on larger vessels" but "by no means CRITICAL." frag2k4 says it matters "more ... when playing multiplayer" because "a good player will slip a fleet above or below you and come up behind getting free shots off." xstar960: "quite useful for getting better angles with your battlecruisers" and resources sit at varied vertical positions. — [Steam: wondering about the Z](https://steamcommunity.com/app/244160/discussions/0/1836811737984666158/) — VERIFIED (as player statements)
- Homeworld 3 cover: per summaries of interviews and previews, megaliths and debris create walls, tunnels, canyons; "a frigate behind a megalith wall cannot be hit by weapons that require direct line of sight"; small-craft AI hugs surfaces passing nearby; right-clicking debris sends interceptors behind it; cover was inspired by Company of Heroes; pathfinding voxelises the world. — [Search results: The Sixth Axis preview, GamingBolt interview, PC Gamer](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2022/09/13/homeworld-3-preview-hands-on-the-megalithic-space-strategy/) — REPORTED (PC Gamer page was paywalled and not read)
- Player thread "Has anyone actually used cover" (Homeworld 3): "Ships won't fire out of anything unless the target is in their field of vision. At which point they are just sitting ducks. They will not actually peek out to take shots." Kakrafoon: "I try to stay away from solid background objects." A.NooB solved a mission by flying around and all-in instead of using terrain. Mausklickmoerder: "Sneaking up on the enemy is often the wrong choice because of how targeting works." Mastermatta uses cover with small strikecraft groups to close in and with ion frigates to ambush; Marcus Forealius uses it to keep a carrier alive in War Games by breaking line of sight. — [Steam: Has anyone actually used cover](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1840080/discussions/0/4357873056153571844/) — VERIFIED (as player statements; Steam audience skews negative, Steam rating was "Mostly Negative" vs Metacritic 75 per Wikipedia)
- Homeworld 3 Wikipedia: "3D ballistics system"; "huge megaliths and trenches" as "coverage strategies for small spacecraft"; Steam "Mostly Negative", Metacritic 75/100. — [Wikipedia: Homeworld 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeworld_3) — VERIFIED
- Formations (Remastered): Delta (wedge), Wall (2-D rectangle), Claw (focus fire, all damage starts at about the same time), Sphere (ships equidistant around target, fire from all sides), X (vertical X shape). Strike craft in Sphere take a 15% attack penalty, minus 20% burstfire time, burst cooldown x4. Formations were "left in a semi-broken state" in Remastered. — [Search results: homeworld.fandom.com Formations and Steam threads](https://homeworld.fandom.com/wiki/Formations) — REPORTED. Homeworld 3 formation roles (Delta spaces to avoid AOE, Claw focuses fire especially vs strike craft, Sphere circles and concentrates fire on one target) — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/homeworld-3-best-group-formations/) — VERIFIED
- Search-result claim that "vertical flanking is devastatingly effective ... yet most players focus on the horizontal plane" comes from after-strategy.com, an unsourced guide-farm page. — [after-strategy.com](https://after-strategy.com/en/homeworld-3-complete-guide-space-rts-fleet-2026/) — REPORTED, low quality
- Original Homeworld development: concept was a 3D RTS with unrestricted maneuvering on all axes; technical challenges included custom engine and "player disorientation" in the Z axis. Secondary (Grokipedia/Wikipedia derived), no primary developer interview found. — [Wikipedia: Homeworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeworld) — REPORTED
- AMBIGUOUS: whether vertical positioning matters. TheGamer (HW3, "seldom comes up") and Sentinel-Ghost (HW1/2 "not critical") vs frag2k4 (multiplayer flanking). They are different games and different player skill levels; none supplies usage data.

### Inferences
- In both reviews and threads the vertical axis is described as available but optional; the AI absorbs it (cover-seeking, pathing) and the camera makes manual use costly. ESTIMATED from TheGamer + Steam threads.
- HW3 cover works only as a block on direct-fire line of sight, and players report that blocking also stops the sheltered ship from shooting. ESTIMATED from the cover thread.

### Gaps
- No developer postmortem or GDC talk found on why Relic chose full 3D or how often players used vertical flanking; no telemetry on vertical movement.

## Supreme Commander, Planetary Annihilation, Total Annihilation, Beyond All Reason: land, air, surface-sea, underwater, orbital

### Takeaway
Supreme Commander literally has five engine layers (Seabed, Sub, Water, Land, Air) with per-weapon, per-firing-layer target lists, and the layer-crossers (torpedo bombers, amphibious, hover, subs that surface) are where counterplay lives. Planetary Annihilation adds an Orbital layer that reviewers split on: "best gameplay system" vs forced and tedious.

### Cited Findings
- Supreme Commander engine layers: "Seabed", "Sub", "Water", "Land", "Air". A weapon's FireTargetLayerCapsTable maps the layer the firing unit is on to the layers it may target, e.g. `{ Sub = 'Land|Water|Seabed', Water = 'Land|Water|Seabed' }`. — [FAF/Supcom wiki Blueprint/Weapon, via search results](https://supcom.fandom.com/wiki/Blueprint/Weapon) and [wiki.faforever.com Weapon](https://wiki.faforever.com/en/Development/Modding/Weapon) — REPORTED (both pages blocked on direct fetch; search text consistent across both)
- SupCom naval rule summary: submerged units can be damaged only by torpedoes and depth charges (plus strategic missiles and tactical missiles in shallow water, per the Atlantis page snippet); the Yathsou's AA autocannon only works when it surfaces; torpedoes cannot hit hover units. — [supcom.fandom.com Torpedo, Torpedo defense, Seraphim Submarine Hunter, via search results](https://supcom.fandom.com/wiki/Torpedo) — REPORTED
- SupCom amphibious: some amphibious vehicles run on the surface (radar and sonar visible, torpedo-vulnerable like ships), some hover (torpedo-immune), some walk the sea floor (submerged: invisible to surface radar but detectable by sonar and vulnerable to torpedoes). — [supcom.fandom.com Amphibious, via search results](https://supcom.fandom.com/wiki/Amphibious) — REPORTED
- GameCloud on Forged Alliance: "Torpedo Bombers could destroy Submarines while Amphibious Tanks and Gunships could engage ships"; "Naval units weren't completely separated from other unit types"; weakness: "Mass Deposits were not found in the water which made naval play contain less contention and harassment opportunities"; FAF community added underwater mass extractors. — [GameCloud](https://gamecloud.net.au/features/opinion/why-supreme-commander-forged-alliance-is-a-masterpiece) — VERIFIED
- Beyond All Reason sea guide: submarines "move below the water" and "can only be seen with sonar and targeted by torpedoes, depth charges, or the Commander laser"; hovercraft "cannot be targeted by torpedoes and depth charges"; sonar is "included on fewer units and at a lower range than normal vision"; floating radar includes medium-range sonar; subs have medium-range sonar; destroyers carry a depth charge and can fire it and their cannon simultaneously; torpedo launchers cannot target hovercraft; "Submarines, battleships, and flagships can only move in water with a depth of at least 17." — [BAR: Basics on Sea Warfare](https://www.beyondallreason.info/guide/basics-on-sea-warfare) — VERIFIED
- BAR air guide: fighters use homing AA missiles; T2 stealth fighters are invisible to radar ("ambush capability"); bombers hit surface; torpedo bombers drop homing torpedoes "to attack sea targets"; torpedo gunships can attack subs; units have a separate "Air LoS" stat "typically higher"; "The Commander's laser can easily destroy bombers"; transports are unarmed except the Abductor. Search summary adds that seaplanes can land underwater and become targetable by torpedoes. — [BAR: Basics of Air Warfare](https://www.beyondallreason.info/guide/basics-of-air-warfare) — VERIFIED; seaplane detail [BAR search result](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/corplat) — REPORTED
- Planetary Annihilation: engine has world layers including WL_AnySurface and WL_Orbital (spawn layer WL_Orbital for the orbital Titan); the wiki page read does not list all layers or weapon targeting rules. — [PA wiki: Unit Properties](https://planetaryannihilation.wiki.gg/wiki/Unit_Properties) — VERIFIED (partial)
- PA orbital: Umbrella is an anti-orbital structure that "attacks enemies in orbit" and intercepts drop pods; cheap orbital units die to Umbrella or Anchor in one hit; "orbital lasers are practically unstoppable if the enemy doesn't have Umbrellas." — [PA Titans units database / wiki results](https://palobby.com/units/unit/ion_defense) — REPORTED
- Quarter to Three on PA: orbital layer is the strong part: "This is a shell around each planet where satellites can provide radar coverage, where geosynchronous turrets can defend certain areas, and where death lasers can pummel the ground below"; "air cover ... one level higher with its orbital layer." But the planet-to-planet transport loading and waiting is "goddamnable" and tedious; multi-planet maps compromise the design. — [Quarter to Three](https://www.quartertothree.com/fp/2014/09/12/wont-live-happily-ever-planetary-annihilation/) — VERIFIED (opinion of one reviewer)
- PA Titans players: Arkay: "units just pop in and out of the orbital layer with no transition or warning"; Quitch/Blutooth: "Orbital units can't act for several seconds after appearing. That's your opportunity to react" (a cooldown added in beta); Blutooth: "it could be interesting to have more depth to the orbital layer"; Sagi: "This is a game about offense so if you are worried about your defense you are not playing the game right." — [Steam: Will Orbital Transport Ever Improve?](https://steamcommunity.com/app/386070/discussions/0/141136086913610961/) — VERIFIED (player statements)
- Search summaries of PA reviews: orbital units "forced" ("if you don't build them, you will lose control of your orbital area and your enemy will bombard you from above"); orbital ships "limited variety and their only purpose is defending orbital cannons and satellites"; PA orbital called "probably the best gameplay system" by one source. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/planetary-annihilation/planetary-annihilation-review), [SpaceSector](https://www.spacesector.com/blog/2014/10/planetary-annihilation-review/) — REPORTED; AMBIGUOUS between "best system" and "forced layer" (reviewers differ on whether obligation is a virtue)
- Total Annihilation: Chris Taylor says it was "a game I designed for myself", with units that shoot while moving, real physics, and more emergent rule-based behavior. No source found on TA layer design specifically. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/chris-taylor-total-annihilation-was-a-game-i-designed-for-myself/) — REPORTED (search result)

### Inferences
- In SupCom and BAR the same three counterplay shapes recur: a layer-crossing unit that can hit a layer its class normally cannot (torpedo bomber into sub, amphibious tank onto ships), a unit that sits in a layer where most weapons cannot target it (hover vs torpedo, sub vs most guns), and a surfacing/diving condition on weapons (Yathsou AA only when surfaced). ESTIMATED from the BAR and SupCom citations.
- Layer-specific detection exists as a separate sensor type (sonar, Air LoS, anti-orbital structures) rather than by one global vision. ESTIMATED.

### Gaps
- No primary page read for SupCom unit-by-unit weapon-to-layer tables, or PA's full world-layer list and orbital radar rules.
- No developer commentary (Gas Powered Games, Uber, BAR devs) found on how often players use the sub or orbital layer, or on win-rate impact.

## Submarine games: depth as a layer; the thermocline

### Takeaway
In Cold Waters and Sea Power, "the layer" is a depth boundary that sonar crosses poorly: a submarine on the opposite side of the layer from a surface hunter is hard to detect, and a sensor lowered across the layer (towed array, VDS) can listen across it. Cold Waters players say the stated rule is "side of the layer, not depth," but sources disagree on details.

### Cited Findings
- Cold Waters player Hoffmannjames: "If you are trying to hide from a sub that is below the layer, you should go above it. And if you are trying to hide from a surface ship, you should go below it." ramjbjb counters that it is "dynamic": the layer is a "sound refractor," not a mirror; sound still passes with reduced intensity; close contacts penetrate more easily; cavitation at shallow depth limits options. A towed array deployed below the layer detects across it better than hull sonar, "though the game apparently doesn't model this mechanic perfectly." — [Steam: Layer and water pressure/temperature](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/2119355556476149147/) — VERIFIED (player statements)
- Cold Waters player thread on depth effects: "it doesn't seem to matter what depth you are at, only which side of the layer you are on"; with no layer present, Wraith_Magus: "every depth is generally the same so far as sound goes, with the exception of reflection off of the ocean floor"; Restilia believes the full Sound Velocity Profile may be implemented but not shown; in 1984 scenarios the towed array "won't work properly if you're within 200 feet of the floor." — [Steam: Effect of depth](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1470840994965474248/) — VERIFIED (player statements)
- Cold Waters, from search results: the layer may not form in northern or shallow water; layer depth varies from "a little under 100 feet" to "around 300 feet" depending on warmth; torpedoes can be set to run shallow (about 30 ft, above the layer), deep (about 30 ft below the layer), or search at about 350 ft if there is no layer; "Hiding from ships can be done just beneath the layer in the shadow zone"; cavitation formula quoted as "Cavitation depth = 20 * SpeedInKnots – 100" (every 5 kt extra speed means cavitation begins 100 ft deeper... wording in source: "happens 100 feet earlier"). — [Cold Waters wiki Navigation / SV Let's Play / Steam threads via search](https://killerfish-games.fandom.com/wiki/Navigation_(Cold_Waters)) — REPORTED. The cavitation formula alone was also read on [gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/653-cold-waters.html) — VERIFIED
- AMBIGUOUS: layer depth in Cold Waters. ramjbjb says the layer occurs "between roughly 600-1200 feet" (a real-world description) while the other summary says about 100-300 ft in game. These are likely real-world vs in-game figures but the sources do not say so. The Cold Waters operations manual PDF (Steam CDN, v1.07) is the primary and could not be read.
- Sea Power (player/guide statements): the thermal layer "will reflect sonar waves back up towards the sea surface," creating a "shadow zone" in which you "can't hear anything below the layer, even with active sonar"; most ASW vessels carry a variable depth sonar or towed array that can listen and ping below the layer; "Submarines can try to hide easier from surface ships by staying under the thermal layer"; layer depth is shown in-game by hovering over "Keel" in the GUI; game also models convergence zones. — [Steam guide: Sonar in Sea Power](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3654466303), [sea-power.pmctactical.org Thermal Layer](https://sea-power.pmctactical.org/thermal-layer.php) — REPORTED for the guide (direct fetch failed 429); VERIFIED for the PMC Tactical quote and the Keel-hover note.
- PMC Tactical thermocline page: a "proven submarine tactic" is to stay above the thermocline and deploy the towed array beneath it; surface ships can place arrays above or below it. Page says it is "extremely simple terms" only. — [naval-warfare.pmctactical.org Thermocline](https://naval-warfare.pmctactical.org/thermocline.php) — VERIFIED (real-world tactic summary, not a game rule)
- Real-world basis cited in search result: a thermal layer refracts sonar and isolates target from pursuer; a surface ASW platform drops its array tail below the layer to detect a quiet contact; a sub floats its array tail above the layer to monitor surface ships while lurking below. — [Wikipedia: Towed array sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towed_array_sonar) — REPORTED
- Cold Waters player note: towed array in Cold Waters "doesn't seem to" detect across the layer even though forums say it should (search summary of the same Steam thread) versus the detail in the fetched thread that it detects "more effectively" than hull sonar but is not modeled perfectly. — [Steam thread above](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/2119355556476149147/) — AMBIGUOUS between the two summaries; both are player opinion.
- Advance Wars (turn-based surface/sub layer precedent): submarines "can dive while on any water tile," become invisible unless an enemy unit is adjacent, burn 5 fuel per turn submerged; only Cruisers, other Submarines, and Missile Silo missiles can damage a submerged sub. — [Advance Wars wiki Submarine, via search results](https://advancewars.fandom.com/wiki/Submarine) — REPORTED (page 402 on fetch); [AWBW Sub](https://awbw.fandom.com/wiki/Sub) cited by same search.

### Inferences
- The stated rule across Cold Waters and Sea Power is binary and positional: which side of the layer each party is on determines detection quality, with specific counter-equipment (towed array, VDS) that crosses it. Depth beyond that mostly feeds other costs (cavitation, floor clearance). ESTIMATED from player statements.
- In Advance Wars, the dive is an explicit player action with an upkeep cost (fuel) and a narrow list of units allowed to hit the diver. ESTIMATED from the REPORTED rule text.

### Gaps
- Cold Waters Operations Manual (primary) not read; layer numbers and exact sonar penalty not verified.
- No developer interview on how much players actually use the layer; only player threads.
- Dangerous Waters, Silent Hunter, and Command: Modern Operations sub-depth rules not researched in depth.

## StarCraft II: air vs ground, high-ground vision, Colossus, cliff-walking, burrow

### Takeaway
SC2 gives two attack-target classes (air, ground) and a vision rule where ground units cannot see up a cliff; flyers and Colossi can reveal the high ground, and a unit attacking from the high ground is briefly revealed. Burrow is a hidden state that is only targetable under detection.

### Cited Findings
- Liquipedia (High Ground and Low Ground, via search snippet): "Air Units ignore Cliffs"; "Ground units cannot see up cliffs"; low-ground units "can be ambushed and cannot counterattack unless vision is gained of the high ground by moving an air unit or Colossus into range, utilizing a Xel'Naga Tower or a Scan with vision of the high ground, or by moving a friendly ground unit onto the high ground"; flyers attacking from over a cliff "immediately give away their position." — [Liquipedia: High Ground and Low Ground](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/High_Ground_and_Low_Ground) — REPORTED (direct fetch 429; text from search snippet of the same page, also referenced by [Liquipedia Terrain Features](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Terrain_Features))
- Colossus (via search summary of Liquipedia): has Cliff Walk; Blizzard confirmed it "counts as both a ground and air unit for the purpose of what attacks are able to hit it," so Vikings and Corruptors can hit it; if a Colossus on high ground attacks low-ground units, the enemy gains temporary vision of it and can return fire. — [Liquipedia: Colossus (LotV)](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Colossus_(Legacy_of_the_Void)) — REPORTED
- Burrow (via search summary of Liquipedia): burrowed units "can only be targeted while in the presence of an enemy Detector or while affected by Fungal Growth"; burrowed units cannot attack (mobile burrowers differ); keen players can spot a shadow-like disturbance, and moving burrowed Roaches/Infestors cast a shadow. — [Liquipedia: Burrow](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/Burrow) — REPORTED
- A 2010-era TeamLiquid thread, "bug? flying units give high ground vision," shows flyer-reveals-high-ground was noticed as an effect by players at launch (text not read). — [TL.net](https://tl.net/forum/starcraft-2/122647-bug-flying-units-give-high-ground-vision) — REPORTED (title only)

### Inferences
- The SC2 high ground is a vision layer not a movement layer: the same ground plane, but a cliff boundary that blocks sight from below and reveals the attacker's position on firing. ESTIMATED from the Liquipedia text.
- The things that cross the sight boundary are specific named items (air unit, Colossus, tower, scan, a unit on the ledge), and an attack from the hidden side has a reveal cost. ESTIMATED.

### Gaps
- Liquipedia pages not fetched directly (429), so exact wording (e.g. whether a duration for reveal is stated) is not verified.
- No miss-chance rule for high-ground attacks verified (SC2 has none in the sources seen; BW had one in other games, not checked).

## Battlestar Galactica Deadlock, Nexus, Empire at War, Sins 2, Ground Control, Battlezone, Ancient Space, EVE, Wargame: what altitude does mechanically

### Takeaway
Most games with a stack of planes or altitude bands use it for one or two narrow things: facing and armor sides (BSG Deadlock), line of sight and detection (Wargame helicopters, Ground Control height), or purely cosmetic (Empire at War, Ancient Space auto-altitude). Reviewers repeatedly call the 3D "minimal" or "automatic."

### Cited Findings
- BSG Deadlock (Steam thread "3D graphics but 2D gameplay"): "the z axis is minimal to say the least"; ships can move up and down and stack; ships have top and bottom armor; ships cannot turn in all directions; Mitchverr: "a major part of the current high end meta is based squarely around diving the fastest" and players must "keep very good control over the 'plain' you fight on as most ships have more guns above them"; Giga: full 3D would be "impractical in an extreme way," given multiple firing arcs, armor and hardpoint sections in turn-based play. — [Steam: 3D graphics but 2D gameplay](https://steamcommunity.com/app/544610/discussions/0/1488866180590481117/) — VERIFIED (player statements)
- BSG Deadlock reviews (search summary): ships start on a horizontal plane and can move to higher or lower planes; different ships have different firing arcs and armor on six sides; "consensus ... the 3D part feels unimportant." — [Stately Play](https://statelyplay.com/2018/06/21/better-stately-than-never-battlestar-galactica-deadlock/), [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/review/battlestar-galactica-deadlock/) — REPORTED
- Wargame: Red Dragon helicopters have three altitude states (High, Low, Land): at high altitude they are "easier to spot and engage, but can see and shoot over cover"; they "duck to low altitude to hide behind terrain"; AA needs line of sight on approach; planes cannot attack what they cannot see; nap-of-the-earth flying avoids aircraft radar. Player complaint: helicopters descend automatically after moves or attacks, losing sight of targets and wasting ordnance, with no way to chain orders to stay high ("move them forward hit stop than hit change alt button"). — [Steam: How to stop helicopters to change altitude](https://steamcommunity.com/app/251060/discussions/0/1813170373216272928/) — VERIFIED (player statements); mechanic summary [Red Dragon guide / Steam threads via search](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails?id=247884292) — REPORTED
- Ground Control (Massive, 2000), search summary: free-floating camera; terrain impedes line of sight; "high ground from cliffs and hills gives an accuracy bonus to direct-fire units attacking lower targets, and allows them to attack the thinner top-armor of vehicles"; shadows of cliffs and trees conceal units; hiding under trees protects ground units against aerodyne attacks and artillery "to an extent." — [Wikipedia: Ground Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Control_(video_game)), [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/GroundControl) — REPORTED
- Sins of a Solar Empire (original and II), search summary: combat space is 3D; the Z axis "can be abused in PvP games and may be difficult to use"; ships can move over starbases that cannot fire upward; ships ordered across a gravity well may travel vertically over a star if faster. Strikecraft are hosted by capitals and can be launched from anywhere in the gravity well. — [StrategyWiki: Sins II Combat](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Sins_of_a_Solar_Empire_II/Combat), [Sins forum 3D game?](https://forums.sinsofasolarempire.com/376174/3d-game) — REPORTED (forum returned 403)
- Empire at War, search summary: space combat is restricted to a 2D plane even though ships are 3D; ship classes sit at different heights (corvettes higher than battleships) as presentation. — [TV Tropes: 2-D Space](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TwoDSpace), [Macworld review](https://www.macworld.com/article/204432/starwars_empireatwar.html) — REPORTED (low depth of evidence)
- EVE Online, search summary: fully 3D space; the "orbit" command circles a target to maximize transversal and angular velocity and reduce radial velocity so turrets cannot track. Vertical motion appears as emergent, not a layer. — [EVE University: Advanced piloting](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Advanced_piloting_techniques) — REPORTED
- Ancient Space, search summary: ships move in 3D but "only ever ... to reach a target in a base ring"; you "can't actually manually move units ... to any height," units auto-select the height to engage; reviewer calls this a missed opportunity. — [SpaceSector: Ancient Space Review](https://www.spacesector.com/blog/2014/11/ancient-space-review-2/) — REPORTED
- Battlezone (1998): RTS-FPS hybrid, hover tanks; no altitude-layer mechanic found in sources. — [Wikipedia: Battlezone (1998)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlezone_(1998_video_game)) — REPORTED (absence of evidence only)
- Nexus: The Jupiter Incident: full 3D camera and tactical pause; no altitude mechanic found in the sources read; reviewers praise navigation and camera. — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/nexus-the-jupiter-incident-review/1900-6120086/) — REPORTED (search summary only)
- Falling Frontier developer StutterFox and posters (Steam): "ships might move in 3 Dimensions, but it doesn't really matter that they do, because it does not give the game any strategic depth"; "2D often serves the strategy-part ... much better than actual 3D would because you can actually work with the map and set up chokepoints"; "for most of the time you don't even zoom in that close." — [Steam: Falling Frontier thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1280190/discussions/0/5079478487790520592) — VERIFIED (player/dev statements)

### Inferences
- Where altitude does something specific it is attached to a rule: armor side and firing arc (Deadlock), sight/cover (Wargame, Ground Control), launch location (Sins strikecraft). Where it is only a coordinate, players describe it as ignorable. ESTIMATED.
- Several games (Ancient Space, Empire at War, parts of Deadlock) auto-pick or fix altitude, and reviews single that out as lost depth or leave it unmentioned. ESTIMATED.

### Gaps
- Nexus altitude mechanics unverified; Sins II Z-axis rules rely on search summaries; Battlezone yielded nothing on layers.
- No primary rule text read for Empire at War.

## Board and tabletop precedents with stacked levels

### Takeaway
Tabletop games handle depth by bands or abstract markers: Attack Vector: Tactical keeps a 2D hex map with altitude tiles and tilt blocks; Star Trek tri-level chess lets a piece change level mid-move to bypass blockers; Harpoon-lineage games use altitude and depth bands tied to radar horizon and sensor type. I did not retrieve exact rule text for Harpoon bands or Star Fleet Battles.

### Cited Findings
- Attack Vector: Tactical (Ad Astra): "one of the few wargames that attempts true three dimensional movement"; "most have failed in playability"; tools: AVID (Attitude/Vector Information Display, a "color-coded polar projection of a sphere fixed relative to the hexmap" for sighting angles and ship orientation), RALT (Range/Angle Lookup Table, a color-coded Pythagorean table), tilt blocks, box miniatures, stacking tiles for altitude; fuel mass loss changes acceleration. 2004 Origins Award for Best Miniatures Game. — [Wikipedia: Attack Vector: Tactical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_Vector:_Tactical) — VERIFIED
- Star Trek tri-dimensional chess (Franz Joseph Schnaubelt, Star Fleet Technical Manual): seven levels, three fixed 4x4 and four movable 2x2 attack boards; "if the next square is on a different level, a piece may continue moving on the new level, bypassing blocking pieces"; original rules "were far from comprehensive." — [Search results: ChessVariants and Screen Rant](https://www.chessvariants.com/3d.dir/startrek.html) — REPORTED (direct fetch 403)
- Harpoon: in the wargame rules "all detection ranges are limited by the radar horizon"; detection ranges are based on "altitude/depth, distance, and size"; VLow counts as part of Low for radar; low-altitude approach below the radar horizon is a standard anti-ship tactic (P-3 approaches below the horizon to within about 60 nm of Harpoon range). Larry Bond's Harpoon4 radar rules PDF was unreadable. — [Larry Bond, Smarter Radars in Harpoon4 (search result)](http://www.admiraltytrilogy.com/cic/Harpoon/Smarter_Radars_for_Hpn.pdf), [Radar horizon, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_horizon) — REPORTED
- Command: Modern Operations: detection against surface or low-altitude contacts is limited by the horizon computed from the unit's mast height; the game shows it as a yellow range ring; sensors model weather, clutter, jamming, line of sight and horizon. — [Steam: surface sensor rings and radar detection range](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1076160/discussions/0/5367692919513483812/) — REPORTED
- Star Fleet Battles: search returned only generalities (400+ pages of rules); no 3D or above/below rules found. — [Star Fleet Games](http://www.starfleetgames.com/starfleetbattles.shtml) — REPORTED (absence of evidence)

### Inferences
- The tabletop pattern for fire between bands is to put a table or marker between altitude and range (AVID/RALT) or to tie detection to band and horizon (Harpoon); these are real-simulation inheritors. ESTIMATED from the above, partially unverified.

### Gaps
- Exact altitude band names and fire/detection modifiers for Harpoon, Star Fleet Battles 3D rules, BoardGameGeek rule summaries, and Attack Vector range/angle firing rules are not retrieved. BGG pages returned 403.

## Cases where a layered or 3D battlefield was judged a failure or gimmick

### Takeaway
Complaints cluster around four reasons: the camera makes the extra axis hard to command, the AI or auto-pathing handles it so the player does not have to, there is no reason to change layer, and a forced layer feels like a tax. Defenders say the axis matters for ambushes and flanks at high level.

### Cited Findings
- Camera and AI absorbing the axis (Homeworld 3): "As liberating as this system sounds, it seldom comes up in actual gameplay ... unit AI handles cover adequately ... camera makes precise three-dimensional directions difficult." — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/homeworld-3-review/) — VERIFIED
- No strategic depth from the axis (Falling Frontier): "it doesn't really matter that they do [move in 3D], because it does not give the game any strategic depth." — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1280190/discussions/0/5079478487790520592) — VERIFIED
- Z minimal (BSG Deadlock): "the z axis is minimal to say the least" and a developer-side reason: full 3D "impractical in an extreme way" for turn-based multi-arc ships. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/544610/discussions/0/1488866180590481117/) — VERIFIED (player statements)
- Auto-altitude removes the choice (Ancient Space): units pick height themselves; reviewer: missed opportunity. — [SpaceSector](https://www.spacesector.com/blog/2014/11/ancient-space-review-2/) — REPORTED
- Forced or tedious layer (PA): "if you don't build [orbital units], you will lose control of your orbital area"; transports between planets "goddamnable"; Arkay: units "pop in and out of the orbital layer with no transition or warning." — [Quarter to Three](https://www.quartertothree.com/fp/2014/09/12/wont-live-happily-ever-planetary-annihilation/), [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/386070/discussions/0/141136086913610961/) — VERIFIED (opinions)
- Layer with no reason to contest (SupCom naval): "Mass Deposits were not found in the water which made naval play contain less contention and harassment opportunities"; FAF later added underwater extractors. — [GameCloud](https://gamecloud.net.au/features/opinion/why-supreme-commander-forged-alliance-is-a-masterpiece) — VERIFIED
- Cover that does not change decisions (HW3): "They will not actually peek out to take shots"; terrain "rarely feels like it gives the player what the designers were hoping for." — [Steam cover thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1840080/discussions/0/4357873056153571844/), [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/homeworld-3-pc-review-impressions-blackbird-steam-rts/) — VERIFIED
- Micromanagement tax from an altitude toggle (Wargame helis): helicopters auto-descend and lose sight; no way to keep them high without manual "stop then change alt." — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/251060/discussions/0/1813170373216272928/) — VERIFIED
- Tabletop: Attack Vector's own description says most 3D wargames "have failed in playability." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_Vector:_Tactical) — VERIFIED
- AMBIGUOUS: same Steam audience (Homeworld) gives both "not critical" and "good player will slip a fleet above or below" (see first section). Neither is quantified.

### Inferences
- The recurring failure factors in these sources are (a) no command cost-benefit in using the layer, (b) automation hiding the layer, (c) layers with nothing to contest, (d) a layer-entry cost the reviewer experiences as tedium. ESTIMATED; each rests on a handful of opinion citations, not data.

### Gaps
- No telemetry, no balanced sample of players; mostly forum threads and individual reviewers.
- No postmortem found for any of these games explaining a layer decision.

## Mechanics where changing layer is itself the meaningful action

### Takeaway
The best-documented layer-change actions are: diving (Advance Wars sub, Cold Waters going under the layer, SupCom sub), going low (Wargame helicopter ducking behind terrain, Harpoon low approach), surfacing to use a weapon (Yathsou), moving above the layer with the towed array below (real-world and Sea Power), and orbital drops (PA). Each has an explicit cost or exposure rule in the sources.

### Cited Findings
- Dive to hide: Advance Wars subs dive on any water tile; invisible unless an enemy is adjacent; 5 fuel per turn submerged; only Cruisers, other subs, and Missile Silo missiles can hit them. — [Advance Wars wiki, via search results](https://advancewars.fandom.com/wiki/Submarine) — REPORTED
- Dive across a detection boundary: Cold Waters players recommend going below the layer to hide from surface ships and above it to hide from deep subs (Hoffmannjames); "Hovering on or near thermal layer makes Soviets blind if the layer is between you and them." Cost: deeper is slower-safe, but cavitation depth = 20 * knots - 100. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/2119355556476149147/), [gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/653-cold-waters.html) — VERIFIED (first); VERIFIED (formula only); "Hovering on or near thermal layer" quote — [search snippet of Cold Waters threads](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1470840994965474248/) — REPORTED
- Sea Power: towed array / VDS depth can be changed to listen above or below the layer; ASW vessels use this to cross the layer. — [Steam guide, via search](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3654466303) — REPORTED
- Torpedo run-depth setting: Cold Waters torpedoes can be set to run about 30 ft above the layer, about 30 ft below it, or about 350 ft if no layer. — [Cold Waters wiki/threads via search](https://killerfish-games.fandom.com/wiki/Navigation_(Cold_Waters)) — REPORTED
- Climb for sight/range: Wargame helicopters at high altitude "can see and shoot over cover" but are "easier to spot and engage"; they duck low behind terrain to hide. Cost: automatic descent after orders. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/251060/discussions/0/1813170373216272928/), [Red Dragon guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails?id=247884292) — VERIFIED (descent complaint); REPORTED (heli rule summary)
- Climb for sight (ground): SC2: a unit moved onto the ledge, an air unit, a Colossus, a tower, or Scan reveals the high ground. Cost for the attacker on the ledge: it is revealed when it attacks. — [Liquipedia](https://liquipedia.net/starcraft2/High_Ground_and_Low_Ground) — REPORTED
- Drop onto the enemy / layer change: PA orbital units arriving by drop or transport "can't act for several seconds after appearing" (a defender reaction window), and Umbrella structures intercept drop pods. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/386070/discussions/0/141136086913610961/) — VERIFIED; Umbrella — [palobby](https://palobby.com/units/unit/ion_defense) — REPORTED
- Surface to use a weapon: SupCom Yathsou's AA autocannon is usable only on the surface. — [supcom.fandom.com via search](https://supcom.fandom.com/wiki/Naval_Supremacy) — REPORTED
- Seaplanes in BAR can land underwater and thereby become torpedo-targetable. — [BAR seaplane platform](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/corplat) — REPORTED
- Layer-specific weapon / layer denial: BAR AA, torpedoes, depth charges, and the Commander laser each hit only some layers (see section 2); PA Umbrella/Anchor deny orbit; SC2 detectors and Fungal Growth deny burrow; hover units deny torpedoes by being above the target layer. — sources as above — VERIFIED/REPORTED per item
- Dive meta in BSG Deadlock: "a major part of the current high end meta is based squarely around diving the fastest" and keeping control of the "plain" you fight on because "most ships have more guns above them." — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/544610/discussions/0/1488866180590481117/) — VERIFIED (player statement)
- Layer-crossing via movement: Star Trek chess pieces continue onto a different level to bypass blockers. — [ChessVariants via search](https://www.chessvariants.com/3d.dir/startrek.html) — REPORTED

### Inferences
- In the cited cases where changing layer is a deliberate act, the sources also give a cost (fuel, cavitation, reveal on attack, auto-descent, action-delay on arrival) and a limited set of counters (named weapons or sensors). ESTIMATED across citations.
- The sub-with-layer pattern pairs a hiding benefit on one side with a sensing penalty on the other and with equipment that can cross the layer; this is stated by players for Cold Waters/Sea Power and is not documented in a primary rule text read in this session. ESTIMATED.

### Gaps
- No source found that measures how often players perform these layer changes.
- Cold Waters manual, SupCom full layer tables, Liquipedia full text, and Harpoon altitude-band rules not read in primary form.
