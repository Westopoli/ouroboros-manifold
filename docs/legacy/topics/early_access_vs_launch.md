# Early Access vs Full Launch

## What this brief is

Survey of Early Access (EA) vs full-1.0 launch shapes for niche-strategy, city-builder, and sim-adjacent PC titles on Steam. Twelve case rounds (RimWorld, Manor Lords, Against the Storm, Sea Power, Caves of Qud, Songs of Syx, Dwarf Fortress Steam, Battle Brothers, Old World, Wildermyth, Frostpunk 2, Mechabellum) cover the modal launch-shape spectrum from "ship EA at $25 to fund five years of dev" to "ship 1.0 at $44.99 to a built audience" to "skip EA entirely on Steam." Cross-axes cover retention cohort differences, wishlist conversion at EA vs 1.0, Pre-Zip Steam-page-30-day gate fit, solo-dev EA-as-funding pattern, niche-strategy EA-tolerance, the 1.0-fakes-a-launch second-spike pattern, and risk shape (ship-early-to-fund vs ship-polished-once).

Feed targets: T-W1 wishlist conversion (cross-validation), T-F1 launch window seasonality (refinement around EA vs 1.0 timing), T-F2 festival roster (EA-launch vs 1.0-launch festival fit), T-D1 demo strategy (EA + demo combo), T-B1 brand positioning capstone, funding-shape decision substrate (solo/very-small-team cash-flow constraint).

Non-recommendation disclaimer: Research Lane surfaces inputs, never picks. Confidence tags inline. No advocated launch shape — only options + tradeoffs surfaced for Design Lane.

## Case: RimWorld (Ludeon Studios, Tynan Sylvester)

**Shape.** Independent EA off-Steam (Patreon / direct sales) starting 2013 → Steam EA July 2016 → Steam 1.0 October 2018. Solo-dev → very-small-team scale. Genre: colony sim, generational-adjacent (multi-day campaigns, dynasty-shape emergent stories), Foundation-tribe-adjacent in narrative depth.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** EA generated reportedly multi-million-unit lifetime by 1.0; trade press reported $20M+ revenue across EA window by 2018. 1.0 launch added a second concurrent-peak spike but EA had already monetized the bulk of the audience. Confidence: REPORTED for unit-sales scale, ESTIMATED for EA-vs-1.0 revenue split (likely 70–85% of pre-DLC revenue in EA, 15–30% in 1.0 spike + tail).

**Mechanics.** EA price ~$30 held through 1.0 with no discount (rare in EA — most titles increase price at 1.0). Genre community (modding-heavy) consumed updates as content drops. Steam Workshop integration during EA expanded the audience-creator loop. Long EA tail (>2 years on Steam) accumulated wishlists and word-of-mouth at a slower-than-1.0-launch rate but persistently.

**Lessons.** EA shape funded multi-year development from a solo-dev start. The audience read EA as "I am buying the game and getting updates" not "I am beta-testing." Modding and emergent-story content carried the genre past EA's typical "feature creep" failure mode.

## Case: Manor Lords (Slavic Magic / Hooded Horse)

**Shape.** Solo-dev (Greg Styczeń) → small-team via Hooded Horse publisher support. Steam EA April 2024. 1.0 not yet shipped as of training-data window. Genre: medieval city-builder with battle layer, niche-strategy adjacent.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** EA launch April 2024 hit ~1M units in first 24 hours; ~2M units in first week; reportedly highest pre-launch Steam wishlist count in city-builder history (~3M wishlists going in). Concurrent peak >170K. EA launch broke into mainstream press cycle (PC Gamer, Eurogamer, Rock Paper Shotgun coverage). Confidence: REPORTED for unit numbers (dev/publisher-disclosed), ESTIMATED for wishlist count (Hooded Horse comms cited "millions").

**Wishlist conversion.** ~3M wishlists → ~2M week-one units implies ~65% week-one wishlist-conversion which is *exceptional* — niche-strategy EA conversion typically lands 10–15% per Chris Zukowski guidance. Manor Lords benefited from years-long pre-launch hype cycle and one solo-dev visible publicly. Treat as outlier — NOT a target benchmark for typical niche EA. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

**Mechanics.** EA launch with $39.99 price (high for EA in this band; most EA strategy titles land $25–35). No demo at EA launch. Long pre-launch wishlist accumulation over multi-year teaser cycle. Solo-dev visible public face (twitter, dev blog).

**Lessons.** EA can absolutely "fake a 1.0" if pre-launch wishlist accumulation is large enough. Manor Lords is the *positive* outlier — solo-dev with publisher support, multi-year tease, genre-defining ambition. Replicability is low. Mostly useful as proof that EA-as-launch is viable, not as a number to target.

## Case: Against the Storm (Eremite Games / Hooded Horse)

**Shape.** Steam EA October 2022 → Steam 1.0 December 2023. **Under 14 months in EA** — among the shorter EA windows for sim titles. Genre: roguelite city-builder, niche-strategy adjacent.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** EA launch reportedly strong but not Manor-Lords-tier. 1.0 launch December 2023 produced a second concurrent-peak spike, with reportedly strong retention going into year 2 post-launch. Trade press (Game Developer, PC Gamer) covered the 1.0 launch as a "best of 2023" candidate. Confidence: REPORTED for launch coverage, ESTIMATED for retention specifics.

**Wishlist conversion at 1.0.** Eremite/Hooded Horse have publicly noted strong 1.0 spike — REPORTED in trade press but no exact wishlist-conversion figure disclosed. ESTIMATED: 1.0 wishlist conversion ~15–25% on the wishlist count that *remained* after EA shipped (EA launch already converted the early-adopter cohort).

**Mechanics.** Tight EA scope (under a year), aggressive content cadence during EA, 1.0 marketed as a distinct launch event with new content + polish + price hold. Retention through 1.0 was strong — short EA windows correlate with smoother 1.0 transitions (less "I already played this years ago" fatigue).

**Lessons.** Short EA (<18 months) can preserve the 1.0-as-launch effect — the audience hasn't burned out and the 1.0 spike functions like a real launch. Long EA (RimWorld-style 27 months on Steam, CoQ-style 9 years) tends to amortize the 1.0 spike across the EA tail. Tradeoff: short EA requires the game to be close to feature-complete at EA launch.

## Case: Sea Power (Triassic Games / MicroProse)

**Shape.** Steam EA November 2024. 1.0 not yet shipped as of training-data window. Genre: cold-war naval simulation, deep-sim wargame, highly niche.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** EA launch November 2024 reportedly strong within the deep-sim wargame niche but not mainstream-press scale. MicroProse publisher relationship provided distribution and marketing leverage. Concurrent peak in the low-five-figure range REPORTED. Confidence: REPORTED for niche-success characterization, ESTIMATED for exact concurrent / unit figures.

**Mechanics.** Premium EA price (~$45) — high for EA in this band. Genre supports premium pricing because the audience (sim-wargame hardcore) treats EA as expected and supports long dev cycles. MicroProse publisher catalog effect — buyers of one MicroProse sim are pre-qualified for another.

**Lessons.** Niche-genre EA at premium price works when the audience expects EA *and* the publisher provides distribution to a pre-qualified buyer pool. Solo-dev-without-publisher comparable would land at lower price points (~$25–30 EA).

## Case: Caves of Qud (Freehold Games / Kitfox)

**Shape.** Steam EA July 2015 → Steam 1.0 December 2024. **~9 years in EA** — among the longest EA windows on Steam. Genre: deep roguelike, sim-adjacent emergent-narrative, niche.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** Long EA tail with modest but persistent revenue across years. 1.0 launch December 2024 produced a strong concurrent-peak spike reportedly *exceeding* anything seen during EA — Kitfox publisher relationship + accumulated wishlist tail + RPGamer/Rock Paper Shotgun "best of 2024" coverage drove a real launch-event effect. Confidence: REPORTED for spike characterization, ESTIMATED for exact figures.

**1.0-fakes-a-launch effect.** Caves of Qud is the *canonical* example of "1.0 spike outperforms EA peak" — the 9-year EA window kept the wishlist accumulating without burning out the audience (deep niche, audience tolerance is high) and the 1.0 launch acted as a discovery surface for players who had wishlisted years prior but never pulled the trigger. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Mechanics.** Modest EA price (~$15 → $20 over EA window) held through 1.0. Patient pace, no marketing pressure during most of EA. Kitfox publisher arrived mid-late EA and ran a real 1.0 launch campaign. Wishlist accumulation across 9 years gave the 1.0 campaign a huge built-in audience.

**Lessons.** Long EA is viable for very-niche titles with deep audience and patient dev pace, *provided* the 1.0 launch is treated as a distinct marketing event with publisher / press support. Solo-dev-without-publisher long-EA tends to amortize the 1.0 spike; with publisher 1.0 launch can outperform EA peak.

## Case: Songs of Syx (Gamatron / Jakub Wasilewski)

**Shape.** Steam EA September 2021. 1.0 not yet shipped as of training-data window. Solo-dev. Genre: colony / city-builder / civilization sim hybrid.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** Steady EA tail revenue, dev-funded entirely by EA sales for years. Concurrent peaks modest (low-thousands), but accumulated lifetime unit sales reportedly into the hundreds-of-thousands. Confidence: REPORTED for "self-funding solo dev" characterization (dev has been public about this on dev posts), ESTIMATED for exact numbers.

**Mechanics.** Solo-dev, modest EA price (~$22 → $25 over EA window), persistent content cadence (multi-year), no demo for most of EA window. Audience tolerates long EA because game is clearly being developed and updates are consistent.

**Lessons.** EA-as-funding is *the* primary case for solo-dev. Songs of Syx is the proof-of-existence that a solo developer with modest EA pricing and consistent updates can sustain multi-year development entirely from EA revenue. No 1.0 spike to evaluate yet — the model is "EA pays the bills indefinitely, 1.0 is a future event."

## Case: Dwarf Fortress Steam (Bay 12 Games / Kitfox)

**Shape.** **NOT EA on Steam.** Paid Steam launch December 2022 of the previously-free classic Dwarf Fortress, with Kitfox publishing. Bay 12 had been developing DF as free software since 2002 — the Steam release was effectively a 20-year-development-into-1.0-launch shape, but Steam-side it shipped as a regular paid release with no EA phase.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** $7M+ in first month per Bay 12 / Kitfox disclosure. ~500K+ units in first month. Concurrent peak >35K. Trade press (Game Developer, PC Gamer, Eurogamer, Rock Paper Shotgun) covered it as a major launch event. Confidence: REPORTED (Bay 12 has published revenue figures publicly).

**Wishlist conversion.** Wishlist count entering launch reportedly very high (decade-plus of accumulated DF interest). 1.0 launch wishlist conversion estimated ~20–30% week-one — strong for niche, but the headline figure is the *absolute* wishlist count, not the conversion rate. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

**Free classic version remained free.** Free legacy DF continued to be available on Bay 12's site post-Steam-launch. This is the canonical "free version as demo surface" case (T-D1 cross-ref `docs/research/topics/demo_strategy_norms.md`).

**Mechanics.** ~$30 price at launch. UI overhaul, Steam Workshop integration, tilesets, sound — all the polish-layer additions that the free version lacked. Publisher (Kitfox) ran a real launch campaign with trade-press outreach.

**Lessons.** EA is not the only path. A free pre-existing product + a paid Steam launch with publisher campaign can produce a single-spike "1.0 launch" with no EA tail to amortize. Requires either pre-existing free product *or* unusually long pre-launch dev window. For Ouroboros: the bible-locked Roman free-tier *could* play this role (T-D1 cross-ref).

## Case: Battle Brothers (Overhype Studios)

**Shape.** Steam EA April 2015 → Steam 1.0 March 2017. **~24 months in EA.** Genre: tactical squad RPG, niche-strategy adjacent.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** Modest EA sales, strong 1.0 spike, persistent long-tail (DLC strategy across 2017–2022 added incremental revenue). Confidence: REPORTED for trajectory characterization, ESTIMATED for revenue split.

**Mechanics.** ~$25–30 EA price, held through 1.0 with modest discounts during sales. Sequential paid DLC strategy post-1.0 (Beasts & Exploration, Warriors of the North, Blazing Deserts, Of Flesh and Faith, Lindwurm). DLC strategy extended the revenue tail well past 1.0.

**Lessons.** Moderate-length EA (~24 months) with 1.0 spike + post-1.0 DLC tail is a *replicable* niche-strategy shape. Less spectacular than Manor Lords / CoQ but reliable. Audience tolerance for ~2-year EA is high in tactical-squad and grand-strategy clusters.

## Case: Old World (Mohawk Games)

**Shape.** **NOT EA on Steam.** Epic Games Store 1-year exclusive starting 2021 → Steam launch May 2022. Genre: 4X grand strategy.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** Strong Epic launch (publisher disclosure modest), strong Steam launch when exclusive ended. No EA on Steam. Confidence: REPORTED for trajectory, ESTIMATED for revenue split.

**Mechanics.** Epic exclusivity was effectively an "off-Steam EA" — funded continued development for a year while the game polished. Steam launch in 2022 was treated as a real launch event (not EA). ~$40 launch price, sequential paid DLC strategy post-launch.

**Lessons.** Off-Steam EA-equivalent (Epic exclusivity, direct sales, Patreon) can substitute for Steam EA. The audience for niche-strategy 4X is Steam-centric — Epic exclusivity created a built-in "second launch" effect when the game arrived on Steam. Less common path; requires platform-level deal.

## Case: Wildermyth (Worldwalker Games)

**Shape.** Steam EA November 2019 → Steam 1.0 June 2021. **~19 months in EA.** Genre: tactical RPG with generational/legacy character system. **Closest-in-shape comp to Ouroboros' generational/legacy axis.**

**Numbers (REPORTED).** Modest EA performance, strong 1.0 spike, sustained long-tail through 2024 with paid DLC and platform expansion (consoles, Game Pass). Confidence: REPORTED for trajectory.

**Mechanics.** ~$25 EA price → $25 at 1.0 (price held). 1.0 launch coincided with Steam Summer Sale visibility and was treated as a real launch event with press outreach. Generational/legacy theme (characters age, pass legacy to next-gen characters, multi-generation campaigns) is *directly* analogous to Ouroboros' Foundation-tribe axis.

**Lessons.** Generational/legacy strategy is *EA-friendly*. The Wildermyth EA → 1.0 → DLC arc produced sustained revenue across 5+ years from a very-small team (Worldwalker is 3-person core). For Ouroboros: this is the strongest single-shape comp.

## Case: Frostpunk 2 (11 bit studios)

**Shape.** **NOT EA.** Full 1.0 launch September 2024 at $44.99. Genre: city-builder / survival-sim sequel.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** Strong 1.0 launch, ~350K units first week per Game Developer coverage. Concurrent peak ~37K. Premium price ($44.99) supported by predecessor's audience and 11 bit's publisher / dev reputation. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Wishlist conversion.** Reportedly strong week-one conversion (~15–20%) on a built-in audience from Frostpunk 1. ESTIMATED.

**Mechanics.** Premium 1.0 launch with no EA. Pre-launch demo (limited-time) drove wishlist accumulation. Trade press covered as a major release. Post-launch content roadmap announced at launch with paid DLC + free updates.

**Lessons.** Established-studio sequels can skip EA entirely and ship 1.0 at premium price *because the audience is already built*. First-game-from-an-unknown-team comparable would land at lower price and benefit from EA-as-funding. Frostpunk 2's shape is not replicable for a solo / very-small-team first title.

## Case: Mechabellum (Game River)

**Shape.** Steam EA September 2023 → Steam 1.0 October 2024. **~13 months in EA.** Genre: auto-battler / tactics, multiplayer-oriented.

**Numbers (REPORTED).** Strong EA launch (publisher-disclosed >1M units across EA window by mid-2024). 1.0 launch produced a second spike. Multiplayer-focused EA shape — different mechanics from sim/strategy EA (live-service-adjacent cadence). Confidence: REPORTED.

**Mechanics.** ~$20 EA price, multiplayer balance updates during EA, 1.0 launch added single-player content and PvE modes. Multiplayer EA shape carries different audience-expectation profile — audience expects ongoing balance changes, treats EA as "live game."

**Lessons.** Multiplayer-leaning EA can hit 1M+ units in <12 months with aggressive content cadence. Not directly analogous to single-player generational/legacy Ouroboros shape, but useful for "EA-can-be-fast" proof-of-existence. Multiplayer-focused EA is a different beast from sim/strategy EA — audience expects different cadence and content shape.

## Cross-axes

### T-CL3 retention shape changes between EA and 1.0

Retention cohort differences between EA-launch and 1.0-launch are meaningful per REPORTED GameDiscoverCo / Chris Zukowski guidance:

- **EA-cohort refund rate** — typically *higher* than 1.0-cohort refund rate. EA buyers know they're buying unfinished software but a fraction (~3–8%) refund anyway when they encounter the unfinished state. 1.0 buyers refund at typical Steam baseline (~2–5%) for niche-strategy. T-CL3 cross-ref `docs/research/topics/retention_curves_niche.md` for full retention curve detail. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **EA-cohort long-term retention** — *higher* than 1.0-cohort retention for the *same* unit. EA buyers are pre-qualified (they bought unfinished software intentionally) and tend to engage more deeply, return more often, write more reviews. 1.0 cohort skews more casual. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **Refund-window behavior at EA launch.** Steam's 2-hour / 14-day refund window applies the same at EA. Niche-strategy EA refund rates land roughly 4–7% per GameDiscoverCo aggregate guidance — slightly above the 1.0 baseline of 2–5%. T-RR cross-ref `docs/research/topics/refund_rate_norms.md`. Confidence: REPORTED-ish.
- **Review-cohort divergence.** EA cohort reviews are written with "this is unfinished" context; 1.0 cohort reviews evaluate the finished product. EA review score can drag if EA was rushed; 1.0 review score reflects the polished state. Manor Lords' EA review score (~88% Mostly Positive at peak) vs hypothetical 1.0 review score — divergence depends on 1.0 polish state.

**Implication for Ouroboros.** EA-launch cohort retention is likely *better* than 1.0-launch cohort retention per unit, but absolute units depend on launch-shape choice. EA-cohort review noise (some negative reviews during EA from "this is unfinished" complaints) is a marketing-surface risk to manage during EA window.

### T-W1 wishlist conversion at EA-launch vs 1.0-launch

REPORTED + ESTIMATED Chris Zukowski / GameDiscoverCo guidance:

- **EA-launch wishlist conversion** typically **10–15% week-one** for niche-strategy. Exceptional cases (Manor Lords, 65%) are outliers driven by years-long pre-launch tease and publisher support.
- **1.0-launch wishlist conversion** typically **15–25% week-one** on the *remaining* wishlist pool (i.e., the wishlist count that wasn't already converted during EA).
- **Critical math.** EA-launch wishlist conversion happens on the *full pre-launch wishlist*. 1.0-launch happens on the *remaining* wishlist after EA — which is smaller because EA already converted the high-intent cohort. So 1.0-launch's higher percentage applies to a smaller base.
- **Total combined conversion** (EA + 1.0 on the same starting wishlist pool) typically lands ~25–40% across the EA → 1.0 arc. Higher than either single-event launch.
- **EA → 1.0 wishlist re-accumulation.** During EA, the wishlist count continues to grow (new players discover the game during EA, wishlist for 1.0). 1.0-launch's wishlist pool is *not* simply "EA-launch wishlist minus EA conversions" — it's "EA-launch wishlist minus EA conversions plus EA-window new wishlists." For long-EA titles (CoQ-style), the 1.0 wishlist pool can exceed the EA-launch wishlist pool.

T-W1 cross-ref `docs/research/topics/wishlist_conversion_benchmarks_2026.md`.

### Pre-Zip Steam-page-minimum-30-day gate fit

Bible-locked Pre-Zip gate: friends-ZIP → Steam $100 → 30-day Steam page → launch (per CLAUDE.md and `docs/priority_queue.md` § Pre-Zip Beta Readiness Gate).

**Critical observation.** EA *is* a launch for Steam page purposes. The 30-day-Steam-page minimum applies *equally* to EA launches and 1.0 launches — Valve does not distinguish. From the Pre-Zip-gate perspective:

- **EA-launch path.** Steam page ships, EA launches 30+ days later. EA functions as the "launch" the gate is gating.
- **1.0-launch-only path.** Steam page ships, 1.0 launches 30+ days later. No EA in between.
- **EA-then-1.0 path.** Steam page ships, EA launches 30+ days later. 1.0 ships X months/years later — 1.0 is *not* gated by Pre-Zip (gate is satisfied at EA).

**Implication.** Pre-Zip gate fit is *identical* for EA-launch vs 1.0-launch. Gate does not constrain the EA-vs-1.0 decision. The decision is independent of the gate.

### Solo-dev / very-small-team EA-as-funding pattern

EA-as-funding is *the* primary case for solo / very-small-team dev cash-flow. Cases:

- **RimWorld** — EA funded multi-year team growth from solo-dev (Tynan) to small-team. Patreon pre-Steam EA was direct funding; Steam EA was scaled funding. Without EA, the project would have needed external investment.
- **Caves of Qud** — 9-year EA funded the Freehold team's continued development. Solo-to-small-team scale.
- **Songs of Syx** — solo dev (Jakub Wasilewski) funded entirely by EA revenue since 2021. Proof-of-existence for the model at the smallest scale.
- **Battle Brothers** — small Overhype team (4-person core) funded multi-year development via EA + post-1.0 DLC strategy.
- **Wildermyth** — 3-person Worldwalker team funded by EA + 1.0 + DLC + platform expansion.

**Pattern.** EA-as-funding works when:
1. Game is playable enough at EA launch to deliver meaningful value (not just a tech demo).
2. Audience expects EA and tolerates incompleteness for the genre.
3. Dev cadence is consistent (visible weekly / monthly updates).
4. Modest pricing ($20–30) keeps audience accessibility high.
5. Pre-launch wishlist accumulation (years of teaser content, social presence) seeded the audience.

**Non-pattern.** EA-as-funding *fails* when:
1. EA launches in a rushed state and review score drags.
2. Dev cadence stalls mid-EA (visible inactivity → audience trust loss).
3. Pricing is too premium for an unfinished product.
4. Genre audience does not tolerate EA (action / narrative-AAA-leaning audiences).

For Ouroboros (solo-dev or very-small team, funding-shape unknown but likely cash-flow constrained), EA-as-funding is the most relevant precedent cluster. Confidence: REPORTED for pattern characterization, INFERRED for Ouroboros applicability.

### Niche-strategy EA-tolerance

Sub-genre EA-tolerance varies significantly:

- **Generational / legacy strategy (Wildermyth-likes, RimWorld-likes, CK3-cluster).** **EA-friendly.** Audience expects multi-year dev cycles, treats EA as "I'm supporting development." Wildermyth and RimWorld evidence directly. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **City-builder / colony-sim (Manor Lords, Against the Storm, Songs of Syx, Workers & Resources).** **EA-friendly.** Genre cluster has a long EA tradition. Audience tolerance high. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Tactical squad RPG (Battle Brothers, Wartales).** **EA-tolerant.** Less aggressive than sim/colony, but EA is accepted. ~24-month EA windows are typical. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Grand strategy / 4X (Old World, CK3 historically).** **EA-mixed.** Paradox-cluster historically ships 1.0 then iterates with paid DLC; Mohawk Games (Old World) went Epic-exclusive instead of Steam EA. Genre is more 1.0-launch leaning. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Operational / hex wargame (Slitherine catalog, Combat Mission).** **EA-uncommon.** Publisher (Slitherine) typically ships 1.0 with iterative patch + paid DLC pattern. EA is rare in this cluster. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Deep roguelike / sim-adjacent (Caves of Qud, Cataclysm: DDA, Stoneshard).** **EA-very-friendly.** Audience is extremely tolerant of long EA windows. CoQ's 9-year EA is normal for this cluster. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Ouroboros placement.** Generational/legacy + Foundation/hard-SF + Run-shape-F (collapse-rebuild) + Lieutenant System tactical layer. Primary niche cluster = generational/legacy strategy (Wildermyth-likes) + sim-adjacent depth (RimWorld-likes / CoQ-likes). Both clusters are *EA-friendly*. Confidence: INFERRED from cluster-fit.

### "1.0 fakes a launch" pattern

Multiple cases show 1.0 launches function as a *second* launch event with its own spike, regardless of EA tail:

- **Caves of Qud 1.0 (Dec 2024)** — strong 1.0 spike after 9-year EA. Publisher (Kitfox) + press coverage + accumulated wishlists. Reportedly the largest single-week sales window in CoQ history. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Wildermyth 1.0 (Jun 2021)** — strong 1.0 spike with summer-sale visibility. Worldwalker has noted publicly that 1.0 launch produced a meaningful revenue boost above EA tail. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **RimWorld 1.0 (Oct 2018)** — second concurrent-peak spike, though smaller relative to RimWorld's already-strong EA performance. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Against the Storm 1.0 (Dec 2023)** — strong 1.0 spike covered as "best of 2023." Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Battle Brothers 1.0 (Mar 2017)** — strong 1.0 spike, post-1.0 DLC strategy compounded. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Mechabellum 1.0 (Oct 2024)** — second spike with new PvE content. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Pattern.** 1.0 is *not* just "the end of EA" — it's a second marketable launch event. Trade press treats 1.0 as a fresh release. Steam algorithm boosts 1.0 launches similar to fresh releases. Wishlist tail accumulated during EA converts at 1.0.

**Conditions for strong 1.0 spike.**
1. EA was *short enough* that audience hasn't burned out (or *long enough + niche enough* that the wishlist tail is huge).
2. 1.0 is marketed as a distinct event — publisher campaign, press outreach, new content beyond EA scope, polish layer.
3. Price hold (no EA-launch discount that anchored audience to lower price). Some titles increase price at 1.0 (~10–25% bump).
4. Festival / sale window timing (1.0 launches during Steam Summer Sale, Autumn Sale, or major festival capture additional visibility — T-F1, T-F2 cross-ref).

**Implication for Ouroboros.** Even if Ouroboros ships EA, a 1.0 launch later remains a marketable event with its own spike. The EA-vs-1.0 decision is *not* "one launch or the other" — it can be "two launches" with appropriate marketing planning.

### Risk shape: ship-EA-early-to-fund vs ship-1.0-once-polished-once

The core fork. Risk profiles:

**Ship EA early (~$25, 12–24 month EA, 1.0 later):**
- **Funding risk: reduced.** EA revenue funds continued dev. Solo-dev cash-flow constraint addressed.
- **Polish risk: increased.** EA launches at lower polish; audience writes "this is unfinished" reviews. Review-score drag during EA.
- **Audience-burnout risk: moderate.** Long EA can fatigue audience. Short EA (Against the Storm, Mechabellum) preserves 1.0 spike.
- **1.0-spike preserved if EA is short and 1.0 is marketed as distinct event.**
- **Marketing-cadence cost.** EA window requires ongoing content updates, dev communication, community engagement. Solo-dev time cost.
- **Two marketing events.** EA launch *and* 1.0 launch each need press outreach, festival fit, capsule updates, trailer cuts.

**Ship 1.0 once at full polish (~$30–40, no EA):**
- **Funding risk: high if solo-dev.** No interim revenue. Dev must self-fund or external-fund through to launch.
- **Polish risk: reduced.** Game launches polished. Review score reflects finished state.
- **Audience-burnout risk: low.** No EA window to fatigue audience.
- **Single launch event.** One marketing campaign, one festival fit, one capsule cycle. Lower marketing-cadence cost.
- **No "second wave" marketing surface.** EA → 1.0 provides two launches; 1.0-only provides one. Less compounding.
- **Wishlist accumulation must reach launch-target on its own.** No "EA-launch funnel" to seed the audience.

**Hybrid (Manor Lords / RimWorld shape):**
- **EA functions as the launch.** Audience treats EA as the real release. 1.0 is a polish-layer event later.
- **Funding mostly addressed at EA-launch.** Subsequent dev funded by ongoing EA sales + DLC tail.
- **Best of both.** Funding from EA, polish at 1.0, two marketing events.
- **Requires.** Strong pre-EA wishlist accumulation, publisher support (or solo-dev hustle equivalent), modest pricing, consistent content cadence.

**Implication for Ouroboros.** Solo-dev cash-flow constraint pushes toward EA-shape. But polish-risk and review-score risk during EA need explicit management. The middle-ground (short EA ~12–18 months at modest price, then 1.0 as distinct marketing event) is the most-replicated successful shape per case round.

## Cross-cutting findings

- **EA-as-funding is the dominant solo / small-team pattern in niche-strategy.** RimWorld, CoQ, Songs of Syx, Battle Brothers, Wildermyth all funded continued development from EA revenue. Without EA, most of these would have required external investment.
- **Niche-strategy audience is EA-tolerant in most sub-clusters.** Generational/legacy, colony-sim, deep-roguelike, tactical-squad. The exception cluster is grand-strategy / 4X, which is more 1.0-launch leaning.
- **Short EA preserves the 1.0 spike better than long EA.** Against the Storm (<14 months), Mechabellum (~13 months) produced strong 1.0 spikes. CoQ (9 years) produced a strong 1.0 spike too — but for opposite reasons (long wishlist tail in a deep niche).
- **1.0 is not just "end of EA" — it's a separate marketable launch event.** Plan accordingly with festival fit, press outreach, polish layer, possible price bump.
- **Pre-Zip gate is launch-shape-independent.** 30-day Steam page minimum applies equally to EA and 1.0. Gate does not constrain the decision.
- **Manor Lords is an outlier, not a target.** ~3M wishlist EA conversion is exceptional. Niche-strategy EA typically converts 10–15% week-one.
- **Wishlist conversion is higher at 1.0 (15–25%) than EA (10–15%) but applies to a smaller remaining pool.** Total combined EA + 1.0 conversion ~25–40% on the same starting wishlist count.
- **EA refund rate (~4–7%) is moderately higher than 1.0 baseline (~2–5%) for niche-strategy.** Manage by setting EA-launch expectations explicitly on Steam page.
- **Generational/legacy strategy specifically is EA-friendly.** Wildermyth + RimWorld direct evidence. Ouroboros' primary niche cluster (generational/legacy + Foundation-tribe + Run-shape-F) lands in this band.
- **Free-tier-as-demo-substitute is a third path.** Dwarf Fortress shape — no EA, no traditional demo, free legacy product as discovery surface, paid Steam launch. Bible-locked Roman free-tier could play this role (T-D1 cross-ref).

## Ouroboros launch-shape implications

Given bible-locked Roman-free-tier + 7-era ladder + Lieutenant System + Run-shape-F + solo / very-small-team scale + likely cash-flow constraint, options surface below. Research Lane does **not** pick. Tradeoffs surfaced for Design Lane:

### Option A — EA launch (~$25), 12–18 month EA, 1.0 later (~$30)

Ship EA at modest price as "soft launch." EA funds continued dev. 1.0 ships 12–18 months later as second marketable event with polish layer + possible price bump.

- **Pros.** Funding addressed via EA revenue. Two marketing events. EA-tolerant niche cluster. Generational/legacy precedent (Wildermyth) directly applicable. Short EA preserves 1.0 spike.
- **Cons.** EA polish risk — review-score drag during EA. Marketing-cadence cost (two campaigns). Roman free-tier interaction unclear (does it ship at EA launch too? before? after?).

### Option B — EA launch (~$25), open-ended EA, no committed 1.0 date

Ship EA, iterate indefinitely, 1.0 declared when feature-complete. RimWorld / CoQ / Songs of Syx shape.

- **Pros.** Maximum solo-dev flexibility. Funding sustained for years. No deadline pressure.
- **Cons.** No "second launch" spike planned. Wishlist tail accumulates but doesn't convert at peak velocity. Audience may fatigue. Long-EA risk profile.

### Option C — 1.0-only launch (~$30–40), no EA

Ship 1.0 at full polish. No EA window. Frostpunk 2 / Old World shape.

- **Pros.** Single polish event. Review score reflects finished product. No EA-window content-cadence cost. Premium pricing supported by full polish.
- **Cons.** No interim funding. Solo-dev cash-flow constraint unaddressed. Single launch event. Higher pre-launch wishlist accumulation requirement.

### Option D — Roman free-tier as "free EA" + paid 1.0 launch

Roman ships as standalone Steam Free product or Demo product (T-D1 cross-ref). Paid full game (all 7 eras) ships at 1.0 with no EA window. Dwarf Fortress shape.

- **Pros.** Free Roman seeds audience and funds nothing but builds wishlist + word-of-mouth. Paid 1.0 launches polished. Single paid launch event.
- **Cons.** No interim revenue from Roman. Solo-dev cash-flow constraint unaddressed. Roman maintenance cost during pre-1.0 dev. Player mental-model risk (Roman-as-the-game anchoring).

### Option E — EA at scenario-slice / Lieutenant-slice scope, expand to full 7-era at 1.0

Ship EA with a subset of the game (e.g., one era, or a constrained Lieutenant System scenario). Use EA revenue to fund expansion to full 7-era ladder at 1.0.

- **Pros.** Reduces EA-launch polish surface area (smaller scope to polish). Funding addressed. 1.0 spike retained.
- **Cons.** EA scope decision is itself a design fork. Audience reading EA as "this is the whole game" risk (perception trap). Era-ladder marketing harder to communicate during EA.

### Option F — Off-Steam EA (Patreon, direct sales, Itch) → Steam 1.0

RimWorld-Patreon-precedent shape. Build EA audience off-Steam, ship Steam 1.0 as fresh launch event.

- **Pros.** Off-Steam EA captures the funding benefit without Steam-side EA polish risk. Steam launch is a 1.0 event with full algorithm boost.
- **Cons.** Off-Steam audience-building is harder in 2026 than 2013 (Patreon-era RimWorld was unusual). Demands direct-sales infrastructure. Smaller audience reach.

Six options surfaced. Not ranked. Design Lane picks.

## Conflict surfaced

**EA-launch funding benefit vs 1.0-launch polish benefit.** The core tension is between cash-flow constraint resolution (favors EA) and review-score / polish risk (favors 1.0). Both are real constraints. Both have niche-strategy precedent. The decision substrate depends on:

- **Funding runway.** Unknown to Research Lane. How long can the project sustain without revenue? If runway is short, EA is forced. If runway is long, 1.0-only is feasible.
- **Pre-launch wishlist accumulation trajectory.** Manor Lords shape (~3M wishlists pre-EA) is exceptional. Typical niche-strategy enters EA with ~30K–100K wishlists. Ouroboros' current wishlist trajectory unknown.
- **Roman free-tier interaction.** Bible-locked Roman is free. Does Roman ship before EA, at EA, or at 1.0? Each timing changes the launch-shape calculus.
- **Lieutenant System polish state.** If Lieutenant System is the marketing-lead differentiator, polish state at EA-launch matters disproportionately. Half-polished Lieutenant System at EA may damage the differentiator's brand.

**Flag explicitly as Design Lane fork.** This is a *blocking* fork for any Steam product structure work, festival roster planning (T-F2), demo strategy (T-D1), and launch window timing (T-F1). Recommend Design Lane resolve funding-shape question first (Westley-input required, not Research-Lane-resolvable) before EA-vs-1.0 lock.

**Secondary conflict — short EA vs long EA within EA-path.** If Option A or B is picked, sub-decision is short EA (Against the Storm / Mechabellum shape) vs long EA (RimWorld / Songs of Syx / CoQ shape). Short EA preserves 1.0 spike better but requires near-feature-complete state at EA launch. Long EA gives more dev runway but amortizes the 1.0 spike. Decision depends on game state at EA-launch readiness moment.
