## Festival-driven spikes

Steam hosts ~12-20 themed festivals per year (Strategy Fest, Sci-Fi & Cyberpunk Fest, City Builder Fest, Visual Novel Fest, etc.) in addition to Next Fest. Each festival creates a 1-week discovery surface for participating titles in the theme.

**Festival wishlist spike magnitudes by tier (ESTIMATED, niche-strategy participation):**

| Festival tier | Typical wishlist gain over week | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Steam Next Fest (Feb/Jun/Oct) | 1k-5k median; 30k-100k+ top decile | REPORTED |
| Major themed fests (Strategy Fest, City Builder Fest) | 500-2k median; 5k-20k top tier | ESTIMATED |
| Minor themed fests (Indie Sale, Festival of New Voices) | 100-500 median; 1k-3k top tier | ESTIMATED |
| Publisher-curated fests (Hooded Horse Showcase, Wholesome Direct) | 500-3k median; depends on showcase reach | ESTIMATED |
| Cross-platform showcases (Day of the Devs, IGN Showcase) | 1k-5k median; varies w/ slot prominence | ESTIMATED |

**Festival spike persistence (decay curve):**

Per Zukowski + GameDiscoverCo commentary, festival-driven wishlist behaves like other wishlist sources w/ a few specific traits:

- **Spike concentrates in week of festival** — ~80% of festival-driven wishlist arrives during the 7-day window, ~15% in following 2 weeks (residual creator coverage + delayed clicks), ~5% over months following from continued discovery-surface placement post-festival. REPORTED-leaning.
- **Post-festival velocity drops sharply** — within 2-3 weeks of festival close, daily wishlist velocity typically returns to pre-festival baseline +/- 10%. ESTIMATED.
- **Conversion rate of festival wishlist** — per T-W1 per-source section, generic festival w/o demo runs ~8-15% launch-week conversion (below median 15%); Next Fest w/ demo runs ~25-35% (well above median). Wide bifurcation: a festival presence without a demo is mediocre-quality wishlist; a Next-Fest-with-demo is premium-quality wishlist. REPORTED-leaning.

**Do festival wishlists convert at base rate?**

No — festival wishlist quality is bifurcated:

- **Festival-w/-demo wishlist** converts ABOVE niche-strategy base rate (15% median). Demo-played-then-wishlisted is the highest-conversion wishlist class observed in T-W1 (30-45% launch-week). REPORTED.
- **Festival-w/o-demo wishlist** converts BELOW niche-strategy base rate. Festival exposure produces wishlists but many are "save for later, may forget" intent. Conversion drops to 8-15% launch-week. ESTIMATED.

This is the strongest argument for demo-readiness before festival participation. A festival appearance without a polished demo wastes the discovery slot — wishlist gain is shallow + low-quality.

**Implications for Ouroboros festival roster (T-F2):**

- Festival participation cadence is high-leverage IF paired w/ demo. Without demo, festivals are mid-to-low ROI.
- 2-3 Next Fest appearances pre-launch (cross-ref T-W1 ## Sale-event multipliers Steam Next Fest section) — but only after demo is presentation-ready.
- Minor themed fests (Strategy Fest, Sci-Fi Fest) — participate when tags align, but expect modest spike + mediocre conversion if demo absent.
- Major showcases (Day of the Devs, Wholesome Direct, Future Games Show) — invite-only / curation-dependent; pursue if reachable but not a velocity baseline.

## Next Fest multiplier

Steam Next Fest is the structural velocity-spike event for niche-strategy pre-launch. Runs 3× yearly (Feb / June / October). Demo + livestream slot + dedicated discovery surface for ~1 week. Free participation; one Next Fest per title (Valve restriction — though some titles have participated 2× w/ EA-then-1.0 split).

**Wishlist gain per Next Fest tier (REPORTED-leaning, Valve aggregate + publisher snippets):**

| Tier | Typical wishlist gain | Multiplier vs baseline week | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top decile participant | 30k-100k+ | 30-100× | REPORTED |
| Strong participant | 10k-30k | 10-30× | REPORTED |
| Median participant | 1k-5k | 5-10× | REPORTED |
| Soft participant | 200-1k | 2-5× | ESTIMATED |
| Bust participant | <200 | <2× | ESTIMATED |

**Baseline weekly wishlist velocity for niche-strategy pre-launch (ESTIMATED reference points):**

- Pre-active-marketing (T-365+): 50-200 wishlists/week — passive Steam page discovery only.
- Early active marketing (T-365 to T-180): 200-800/week — early press, community-building.
- Mid active marketing (T-180 to T-90, post-demo public): 500-1.5k/week — demo + creator + festival exposure.
- Late active marketing (T-90 to T-30): 1k-3k/week — launch trailer + press + ramping coverage.
- Final pre-launch (T-30 to T-0): 2k-5k/week — pre-launch hype + final festival.

A "5-10× baseline weekly" Next Fest multiplier on a T-180 placement = 2.5k-15k Next Fest gain. A "10-30× baseline weekly" on a T-90 placement = 10k-90k Next Fest gain. Late-Next-Fest amplifies absolute gain because baseline is higher AND title is more discoverable.

**What separates tiers (REPORTED-distilled from Valve + Zukowski + GameDiscoverCo):**

1. **Demo polish + first-impression** — demo opens compellingly within first 5 minutes; clear value-prop + niche identification within demo. Soft/bust demos fail this.
2. **Capsule + trailer in discovery surface** — players see hundreds of titles on Next Fest surface; capsule + trailer must communicate niche + hook within 3 seconds. Tag mix must surface title to right discovery sub-surfaces.
3. **Livestream slot performance** — Valve livestream slot + dev livestream produce inbound traffic. Articulate hosts + clear demo gameplay outperforms reading-from-notes formats.
4. **Creator coverage during fest** — top-tier participation requires major creators (Quill18, Splattercat, Wanderbots, SsethTzeentach, ManyaTrueNerd) playing the demo during Next Fest week. This is partly demo-quality-driven (creators pick from Next Fest titles based on demo polish) + partly outreach-driven (pre-fest creator outreach + key-distribution).
5. **Tag accuracy** — tagging that matches niche (Grand Strategy, Generation, Procedural, Sandbox, Sci-Fi) routes title to right Next Fest sub-surfaces. Generic tagging buries title in mass-Indie surface.
6. **Pre-fest existing wishlist pool** — Next Fest performance is partially self-reinforcing; titles entering Next Fest w/ existing 50k+ wishlist accumulate more wishlist during fest (algorithmic surface placement favors momentum). Titles entering Next Fest cold accumulate less.

**Per-fest cadence pattern (REPORTED-leaning):**

Most successful niche-strategy launches participate in Next Fest 1× pre-launch, anchored 3-6 months before launch (so launch is on momentum tail of fest). Some participate 2× — once early (T-180 to T-365, w/ early demo build) + once late (T-90 to T-30, w/ polished launch-ready demo). The second appearance typically requires Valve approval or EA-then-1.0 split.

Wildermyth participated in Next Fest once pre-launch w/ a polished demo of an early chapter. Manor Lords participated in Steam Next Fest Feb 2024 (T-60) w/ pre-EA demo — top-decile performance (>100k wishlist gain in fest week). Tempest Rising participated June 2024 Next Fest pre-launch — strong performance, helped drive 500k T-0 pool.

**Implications for Ouroboros Next Fest planning:**

- Single anchor Next Fest 3-6 months pre-launch (T-90 to T-180) is canonical. Demo must be polished + presentation-ready by Next Fest slot.
- Second earlier Next Fest (T-270 to T-365) is option if early demo build is feasible — gains wishlist pool + creator awareness; risks burning the slot on a not-ready demo.
- Per-fest tier outcome ESTIMATED for Ouroboros at 5-15× baseline weekly = 5k-15k wishlist gain assuming median-to-strong participant tier. ESTIMATED.
- Top-decile (30-100k Next Fest gain) requires demo polish + creator coverage cascade — aspirational target, not floor.

## Trailer-drop spike magnitudes

Trailer drops are publisher/dev-controlled velocity events distinct from Steam-platform-driven festivals. Three canonical trailer-drop categories: announce, gameplay-reveal, launch.

**Announce trailer spike (game-reveal):**

First public trailer announcing title exists. Typically paired w/ Steam page going live. Wishlist begins from zero; spike is bounded by Steam-page activation timing + initial press pickup.

- **Median niche-strategy announce trailer spike (week 1):** 500-2k wishlist. ESTIMATED.
- **Strong announce (paired w/ Showcase placement, e.g., Future Games Show / IGN / Day of the Devs reveal):** 5k-20k wishlist week 1. ESTIMATED.
- **Top-tier announce (Manor Lords pattern — trailer virality + creator pickup):** 30k-100k+ wishlist week 1. REPORTED for Manor Lords specifically.
- **Persistence:** announce trailer wishlist decays within 2-3 weeks to baseline + a slight elevated floor (~50-200/week vs ~50/week pre-announce). ESTIMATED.

**Gameplay-reveal trailer spike (deeper-look or new-trailer-w/-mechanics):**

Second or third trailer drop showing gameplay depth. Typically T-180 to T-90 timeframe.

- **Median niche-strategy gameplay-reveal spike (week 1):** 1k-4k wishlist. ESTIMATED.
- **Strong gameplay-reveal (paired w/ press feature + creator coverage):** 5k-15k wishlist week 1. ESTIMATED.
- **Top-tier gameplay-reveal (viral / breakout mechanic showcase):** 20k-50k+ wishlist week 1. ESTIMATED.
- **Persistence:** gameplay-reveal wishlist holds longer than announce — typically 4-6 week elevated period before returning to baseline. ESTIMATED.

**Launch trailer spike (T-7 to T-1, launch announcement w/ confirmed date):**

Final trailer drop confirming launch + final value-prop.

- **Median niche-strategy launch trailer spike (final pre-launch week):** 2k-8k wishlist. ESTIMATED.
- **Strong launch trailer:** 10k-30k wishlist final pre-launch week. ESTIMATED.
- **Top-tier launch trailer (e.g., CK3 launch hype window):** 50k-200k+ wishlist final pre-launch week. REPORTED-leaning for CK3.
- **Persistence:** launch trailer wishlist converts in same motion (day 0 wishlist = same-day purchase pattern). Effective wishlist-purchase compression. REPORTED.

**Aggregate trailer cadence (niche-strategy norm):**

- **Announce trailer** at T-365 to T-540 (or earlier — multi-year build) — establishes Steam page presence.
- **Gameplay-reveal trailer** at T-180 to T-90 — paired w/ demo + Next Fest typically.
- **Mechanic-deep trailer** at T-90 to T-30 — supports press feature cycle.
- **Launch trailer** at T-7 to T-1 — final hype + date confirmation.

Optional intermediate trailers (story trailer, feature trailer, dev-diary trailer cluster) fill gaps but produce smaller per-event spikes (typically 200-1k wishlist each) — more useful for community-engagement than velocity.

**Cross-cutting trailer pattern (REPORTED-distilled):**

- Trailer spike magnitude correlates w/ pre-trailer momentum. Cold trailer drops onto small Steam page produce smaller spikes than warm trailer drops onto existing momentum.
- Trailer-paired-w/-festival amplifies both (festival surface + trailer attention compound).
- Creator-coverage-cascaded-from-trailer (creator picks up trailer day-of, does breakdown video, drives audience to wishlist) is the breakout mechanism. Not all trailers cascade; trailers that show novel mechanics or strong identity-hooks cascade more.

**Implications for Ouroboros trailer schedule:**

- 4-trailer cadence (announce + gameplay-reveal + mechanic-deep + launch) is niche-strategy norm.
- Spike magnitudes per trailer ESTIMATED at median-tier 1k-5k each = 4k-20k aggregate trailer-driven wishlist. Festival + creator overlap compounds.
- Per-trailer hook design matters — trailers that show generational/legacy mechanic + Foundation-tribe-readable identity will cascade better than generic 4X feature reels. Reaches T-A3 demographics adjacency.

## Daily-velocity vs cumulative — predictor quality

Chris Zukowski's "velocity > cumulative" framing argues daily wishlist velocity is a stronger predictor of launch outcome than cumulative wishlist count. Mechanism:

- **Cumulative count includes aged wishlist** — large cumulative pool may be mostly 2+ year aged, which converts poorly per T-W1 freshness section.
- **Daily velocity reflects current momentum** — current creator coverage + current festival exposure + current organic discovery. High velocity at T-30 means launch-week buyers are actively wishlisting, which is exactly the freshness-premium audience.
- **Algorithmic surface placement is velocity-driven** — Steam's recommendation surface (More Like This, Discovery Queue, Popular Upcoming) is widely understood to weight wishlist velocity over wishlist cumulative when ranking visibility. REPORTED-leaning. Valve does not publish algorithm specifics; framing is publisher-inferred.

**Velocity thresholds (REPORTED-leaning aggregate):**

| Velocity threshold (daily wishlists, T-30 to T-0) | Implied launch outcome | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| <50/day sustained | Soft launch likely — below algorithmic visibility threshold | ESTIMATED |
| 50-200/day | Median niche-strategy launch — 10-20% wishlist conversion likely | ESTIMATED |
| 200-500/day | Strong launch — algorithmic placement favorable, 20-30% conversion likely | ESTIMATED |
| 500-1k/day | Breakout-tier — top-decile placement | ESTIMATED |
| 1k-3k/day | Top-tier — Manor Lords / Tempest Rising shape | REPORTED-leaning |
| >3k/day | Outlier — Manor Lords late pre-EA window | REPORTED for Manor Lords |

**Velocity at T-7 vs T-30 (sprint-shape):**

T-30 velocity baseline + T-7 velocity peak typically run 2-4× T-30 baseline due to launch-hype accumulation. A T-30 velocity of 300/day + T-7 velocity of 1k/day = healthy launch shape. ESTIMATED.

**Velocity sustained vs velocity-spike:**

Single-day high-velocity spikes (trailer drop day = 5k wishlist) are good but not predictive on their own. Sustained 7-day average velocity is the metric — Steam algorithm + buyer-intent both reward sustained signal over single-day spikes. REPORTED.

**Per-niche velocity-vs-cumulative weighting:**

- **Niche-strategy** — velocity premium stronger because tribe is small + active; sustained velocity proves current tribe-engagement.
- **AAA / mass-market** — cumulative count more important because AAA audience is broader + buys-on-launch at high rate regardless of pre-launch momentum.
- **Cult / IP-revival titles** — velocity matters less if cumulative pool is large + IP-halo guarantees conversion (Dwarf Fortress Steam pattern).

**Implications for Ouroboros velocity targeting:**

- Cumulative milestones (T-90, T-30, T-0 targets in section above) are necessary but not sufficient.
- Daily velocity at T-30 should target 200-500/day sustained (strong-launch band) — requires active marketing + creator + festival overlap in final month.
- Daily velocity at T-7 should target 500-1k+/day — launch trailer + final festival + pre-launch hype compound.
- If velocity stalls below 50/day at T-30, course-correct (see stalling section).
- Steam algorithm visibility multiplier (T-S3) downstream depends on velocity, not cumulative — surface this in T-S3 brief.

## Indie-strategy vs AAA

Velocity curve shapes differ structurally between niche-indie-strategy and AAA launches.

**AAA velocity curve (REPORTED-leaning, e.g., Bethesda / EA / Activision launches):**

- Steep accumulation in final 6-12 weeks pre-launch — marketing budget concentrates, ads + influencer + press cycle peaks.
- Wishlist count often jumps from 200k → 2M+ in final 3 months.
- Launch-week conversion often LOWER % than niche-strategy because pool is broader-intent + less tribe-aligned.
- Post-launch decay sharp — AAA buyers convert in first month, then audience moves on.

**Niche-indie-strategy velocity curve (REPORTED-leaning):**

- Slower accumulation over 12-30 months, w/ festival + creator + press spikes punctuating steady baseline.
- Wishlist count grows from ~5k → 200-400k over 18-24 months.
- Launch-week conversion HIGHER % because pool is tribe-aligned + intent-vetted.
- Post-launch tail extends 1-3 years w/ sale-event + DLC + mod-ecosystem driving long-tail conversion.

**Shape difference visualized (descriptive):**

- **AAA shape:** roughly flat through T-540 to T-180, then hockey-stick spike through T-180 to T-0.
- **Niche-strategy shape:** stairstep — flat baseline (50-200/wk) w/ spikes at announce, demo public, Next Fest, gameplay-reveal, launch trailer. Each spike elevates baseline floor slightly. Cumulative grows linearly w/ embedded spike contribution.

**Implications for Ouroboros velocity shape:**

- Ouroboros should expect stairstep shape, not hockey-stick. Resist comparing to AAA trajectories (e.g., Starfield, Diablo IV, Stellar Blade) — different audience capture mechanism.
- Stairstep predictability is an advantage — each milestone (announce, demo, Next Fest, gameplay-reveal, launch trailer) has known spike + persistence pattern. Stack them deliberately.

## Stalling curve + course-correct

Niche-strategy wishlist velocity stalls in recognizable patterns. Course-correct interventions exist at specific windows.

**Stalling-curve signs (REPORTED-leaning, Zukowski + GameDiscoverCo commentary):**

1. **Flat-baseline collapse** — daily velocity drops below 50/day for 2+ weeks in T-180 to T-90 window. Indicates discovery surface drying up + no new momentum source. Course-correct: schedule next trailer drop OR push festival participation OR push demo build.
2. **Post-spike decay too steep** — trailer or festival spike fades within 1 week (vs typical 2-3 week persistence). Indicates trailer/festival was thin — didn't convert curiosity to engagement. Course-correct: investigate per-event analytics + adjust next trailer's hook.
3. **Cumulative target miss at T-90** — cumulative is <50% of target. Indicates compounded velocity miss across multiple months. Course-correct: scope reset on target OR major intervention (publisher partnership, paid creator campaign, accelerated demo release).
4. **Conversion-quality signal — review ratio at demo** — if demo "Want to Play" / "Wishlist" buttons show low conversion ratio vs demo plays, indicates demo hook is failing. Course-correct: demo iteration before next festival.
5. **Tag mismatch signal** — wishlist accrual is steady but conversion-tier indicators (creator-coverage, press features) underperform. Indicates Steam tags are routing title to wrong discovery sub-surfaces. Course-correct: tag audit + niche-tag tightening.
6. **Demographic skew** — international wishlist % rises above 70% w/ US <20%. Indicates US/Western tribe not engaged. Course-correct: investigate US-press + US-creator coverage; revisit capsule + trailer-1 language.
7. **Wishlist-aging penalty** — pool is large but velocity is <100/day at T-90. Indicates pool is aged; launch-week conversion at risk. Course-correct: late-stage demo release + final-trailer-w/-new-mechanic-hook to refresh pool.

**Intervention windows (when course-correct still works):**

| Window | Intervention options | Realistic impact |
|---|---|---|
| T-365 to T-180 | Demo release schedule shift, capsule swap, tagline rework, trailer-1 redo | High — full reset possible |
| T-180 to T-90 | Demo public, Next Fest participation, gameplay-reveal trailer, creator key distribution | Medium-high — most niche-strategy intervention happens here |
| T-90 to T-30 | Additional festival, mechanic-deep trailer, accelerated press feature cycle, paid creator campaign | Medium — pool still growing meaningfully |
| T-30 to T-7 | Launch trailer hook iteration, final festival, last-minute Steam capsule refresh | Low-medium — narrow time, limited upside |
| T-7 to T-0 | Launch-week PR push, day-0 creator embargo lift, day-0 community AMA | Low — launch-shape mostly locked |

**Specific course-correct patterns from training-data window:**

- **Demo delay rescue** — multiple niche-strategy launches delayed launch + released demo first to rebuild momentum. Pattern: cumulative was on-track but velocity stalled; demo release at T-60 to T-30 restored velocity to 200-500/day, launch hit target. ESTIMATED-REPORTED hybrid.
- **Capsule refresh** — Falling Frontier (Hooded Horse) reportedly iterated capsule mid-marketing cycle when velocity stalled. ESTIMATED.
- **Tagline rework** — generic "strategy game" tagline replaced w/ niche-specific tagline ("colony sim w/ stories" for Wildermyth) — example of pre-launch positioning iteration. REPORTED for Wildermyth.
- **Trailer-2 redo** — when gameplay-reveal trailer fails to spike, second gameplay-reveal w/ different hook can recover. Pattern observed in Hooded Horse-published titles; rare but documented. ESTIMATED.

**Specific stalling patterns to flag for Ouroboros:**

- **If T-90 cumulative is below 60% of target** — likely 90% T-0 miss. Major intervention required (publisher partner, demo accelerate, festival overhaul).
- **If T-30 daily velocity is <100/day sustained** — launch-week velocity unlikely to clear 200/day threshold; algorithmic placement weak. Course-correct via launch-trailer hook + final-festival anchor.
