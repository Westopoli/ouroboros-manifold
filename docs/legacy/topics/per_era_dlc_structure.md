## Extreme high-count precedent: Train Simulator Classic

Train Simulator (rebranded TS Classic 2024) is the most-extreme high-count DLC ecosystem in PC gaming. Dovetail Games platform with third-party developer ecosystem.

Catalog size: 1000+ paid DLC SKUs by mid-2020s. Routes, locomotives, rolling stock, scenarios. ESTIMATED 1200-1500 distinct paid items in Steam catalog by 2024-2025. Confidence: REPORTED (catalog-size figure widely commented).

Price dispersion: $1.99 floor (single-locomotive cosmetic SKU) to $39.99 ceiling (premium route DLC with rolling stock pack). Modal route DLC $24.99-$29.99. Locomotive-only DLC $9.99-$19.99. Single-vehicle skin packs $1.99-$4.99. Confidence: REPORTED.

Cumulative full-catalog price: complete-the-collection cost ESTIMATED $15,000-$25,000+ at full sticker. Confidence: ESTIMATED — no buyer is plausibly assumed to own full catalog; the catalog functions as à-la-carte hobby-equipment storefront not as expansion ladder.

Bundle architecture: Train Simulator runs mega-bundles aggressively — "Steam-Era Bundle," "British Routes Pack," "American Steam Locomotives Bundle" — typically 70-90% off the à-la-carte sum. Bundle revenue is the dominant capture mechanism for the long tail. ESTIMATED 50-70% of late-catalog DLC revenue arrives via bundle purchase rather than individual SKU. AMBIGUOUS — Dovetail does not disclose split.

Fatigue signal: Train Simulator's audience reads catalog growth as positive — each new DLC is a new toy in a hobby ecosystem, not a content gate. Fatigue language in Train Sim reviews is rare relative to Paradox-niche reviews. The model works because DLC is **discretionary cosmetic-hobby** not **content-completion mandatory**. Confidence: REPORTED.

Long-tail maintenance: third-party developer ecosystem absorbs maintenance burden. Dovetail does not patch every third-party route on every base-engine update; community-tolerated breakage is a known feature of the model. Long-tail maintenance multiplier ESTIMATED 1.1-1.3x of first-party-only catalog — most of the burden externalized to third parties. AMBIGUOUS — exact split not disclosed.

Ouroboros applicability: very low. Train Sim model assumes (a) discretionary-hobby buyer mindset, (b) third-party ecosystem to absorb maintenance, (c) asset-shaped DLC rather than narrative-arc DLC. Ouroboros' 7-era ladder is content-completion-shaped (each era is a discrete piece of the civilizational arc), not asset-shaped. Train Sim demonstrates the **ceiling** of how many SKUs a platform can carry without revolt (1000+), not a template for how Ouroboros should structure its 7-era ladder. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Extreme high-count precedent: Microsoft Flight Simulator

MSFS 2020 (and 2024 successor) runs a high-count aircraft / scenery / airport DLC ecosystem. Microsoft / Asobo platform with both first-party Marketplace and third-party storefronts.

Catalog size: 400-700+ paid items in MSFS Marketplace by 2024. Aircraft DLC $9.99-$79.99 (study-level simulation aircraft at the ceiling). Airport scenery DLC $14.99-$39.99 per major hub. Scenery region packs $19.99-$49.99. Confidence: REPORTED.

Price stratification: visible four-tier shape. Cosmetic livery packs ($4.99-$9.99), simple aircraft ($14.99-$29.99), study-level aircraft ($39.99-$79.99), airport scenery / region packs ($19.99-$49.99). Bundle SKUs exist but are less aggressive than Train Sim — MSFS buyers more willing to pay à-la-carte premium for study-level aircraft. Confidence: REPORTED.

Bundle architecture: MSFS Premium Deluxe Edition bundles base + 5 aircraft + 5 airports for $119.99 (vs $59.99 standard base). 100% pricing premium for ~$200+ worth of à-la-carte content. Pre-buy-via-bundle is a strong revenue capture. Confidence: REPORTED.

Fatigue signal: similar to Train Sim — discretionary-hobby buyer base, low completionism pressure. MSFS players collect aircraft as a hobby; no expectation that any single buyer owns all aircraft. Fatigue language rare. Confidence: REPORTED.

Long-tail maintenance: Marketplace third-party developers absorb most aircraft / scenery maintenance. Asobo patches the base engine; third parties patch their own products. Some third-party abandonment is community-tolerated. Confidence: REPORTED.

Ouroboros applicability: low. Same hobby-discretionary-vs-arc-completion mismatch as Train Sim. MSFS does demonstrate that a four-tier price stratification ($5 cosmetic / $25 standard / $50 mid-premium / $80 study-level) is tolerable in a hobby niche — but again the buyer mindset is collector-hobby not narrative-completion. Confidence: REPORTED.

## ETS2 / ATS long-tail map DLC pattern

Euro Truck Simulator 2 and American Truck Simulator (both SCS Software) ship a long-tail map DLC pattern most directly applicable to Ouroboros' 7-era expansion shape — discrete geographically-scoped expansion packs delivered on multi-year cadence.

ETS2 map DLC (chronological): Going East! (2013 $11.99), Scandinavia (2015 $17.99), Vive la France! (2016 $17.99), Italia (2017 $17.99), Beyond the Baltic Sea (2018 $17.99), Road to the Black Sea (2019 $17.99), Iberia (2021 $17.99), West Balkans (2023 $17.99), Greece (2024 $17.99 ESTIMATED), Nordic Horizons (Iceland announced) likely 2025-2026. Approximately 9-10 map DLCs over 11+ years of post-launch support. Confidence: REPORTED.

ATS map DLC: similar cadence — Arizona (2016 free with base), New Mexico (2017 $11.99), Oregon (2018 $11.99), Washington (2019 $11.99), Utah (2019 $11.99), Idaho (2020 $11.99), Colorado (2020 $11.99), Wyoming (2020 $11.99), Montana (2022 $11.99), Texas (2022 $24.99 — larger map, price-tier step-up), Oklahoma (2023 $14.99), Kansas (2024 $11.99 ESTIMATED), Nebraska / Arkansas in pipeline. Approximately 12-13 map DLCs. Confidence: REPORTED.

Cadence: 1-2 map DLCs per year on average per title, sustained for 10+ years. SCS Software is medium-team (estimated 50-100 employees company-wide across both titles). Confidence: ESTIMATED on team size.

Price discipline: flat $17.99 across ETS2 map DLCs from 2015-2023 (Texas $24.99 in ATS the explicit exception, justified by 2x geographical footprint). Almost identical to RimWorld's flat-$24.99 discipline — flat-pricing-as-quality-signal pattern. Confidence: REPORTED.

Bundle architecture: SCS runs "Map Booster Pack" bundles routinely. Going East! + Scandinavia + Vive la France! at 50-60% off à-la-carte sum during sales. Pre-buy on individual DLCs the dominant launch capture; bundles capture late-attach buyers. Confidence: REPORTED.

Long-tail attach decay: ESTIMATED 60-80% attach on first map DLC (Going East!) for active 2013-era buyers; ESTIMATED 30-50% attach on most-recent map DLC for active buyers at that release window. Older DLCs accumulate attach over time as new buyers join base, hitting eventual lifetime-attach ESTIMATED 40-60% across the catalog. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

Fatigue signal: very low. ETS2 / ATS community celebrates each new map as a new expansion of the hobby world. No documented "DLC fatigue" review-bombing in either title. Confidence: REPORTED.

Ouroboros applicability: **moderate-to-high**. Most-direct precedent in this brief for Ouroboros' shape — geographically-scoped (era-scoped) expansion packs at flat-pricing on multi-year cadence with publisher-bundle architecture. Differences: ETS2/ATS buyer is hobby-driver not narrative-completionist; ETS2/ATS base price is lower ($19.99) than Ouroboros' planned $25-35; SCS team size is 50-100 vs Ouroboros' solo-dev or very-small team. Cadence implication: SCS at 50-100 person scale ships 1-2 map DLCs per year; Ouroboros at solo-dev scale per T-CL1 ships at 1-3 year-per-DLC cadence, which yields 7-21 years to complete 7-era ladder. Confidence: REPORTED. AMBIGUOUS on team-size scaling factor.

## DCS World aircraft modules

Eagle Dynamics' DCS World runs a high-count, high-price aircraft module ecosystem. Closest precedent in PC gaming for "premium-priced individual content unlocks in a niche hardcore audience."

Catalog size: 30+ paid aircraft modules by 2024, plus 8-10 paid map modules (Caucasus free / Persian Gulf $49.99 / Syria $59.99 / Marianas $39.99 / South Atlantic $49.99 / Sinai $59.99 / Afghanistan $49.99 / Kola Peninsula $59.99 ESTIMATED). Confidence: REPORTED.

Per-module pricing: aircraft modules $39.99-$79.99 range. F/A-18C Hornet $79.99, F-16C Viper $79.99, A-10C II $79.99 (or upgrade-from-original $19.99), F-14B Tomcat $79.99, AH-64D Apache $79.99, F-4E Phantom $79.99 (2024). Lower-tier modules $39.99-$59.99. Map modules $39.99-$59.99. Confidence: REPORTED.

Cumulative full-catalog price: aircraft modules + map modules sum ESTIMATED $2500-$3500 at full sticker. DCS World base is free; full catalog is the entire revenue surface. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

Bundle architecture: Eagle Dynamics runs sale-event bundles but no permanent-discount mega-bundle SKU. Sale discounts on individual modules 30-50% during major sales. "Flaming Cliffs 3" bundle is the historical low-fidelity entry bundle ($39.99 for 7 aircraft at lower simulation fidelity). Confidence: REPORTED.

Fatigue signal: DCS community accepts $80-per-aircraft pricing because each module is study-level (3-5 year development cycle per aircraft, deep-sim manuals 600+ pages). Pricing is **quality signal at extreme** — players read $80 as "this is a real simulation, not a casual aircraft skin." Cumulative-catalog fatigue exists but is mitigated by free base, à-la-carte selection (no completionism pressure), and explicit hobby-spend framing. Confidence: REPORTED.

Long-tail maintenance: Eagle Dynamics first-party plus third-party developer partnerships (Heatblur, Razbam, Magnitude 3, Polychop). Each developer maintains their own modules. Some abandonment / hand-off incidents have caused community tension (notably Magnitude 3 controversies). Confidence: REPORTED.

Ouroboros applicability: low-to-moderate. DCS demonstrates the **ceiling** of per-SKU pricing tolerance in a hardcore niche ($80 per aircraft) — relevant to T-P3 ceiling validation. Demonstrates that **free base + premium paid content** model can sustain a 10+ year revenue surface in a hardcore niche, directly relevant to Ouroboros' Roman-free + paid-eras model. But: DCS is study-level simulation, Ouroboros is generational-arc strategy — buyer-mindset and per-SKU value calculation differ. The "free base as discovery surface, paid content as the actual product" structural shape transfers; per-SKU pricing ceiling does not. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Paradox 4-tier flavor-pack accretion (Stellaris, CK3)

Already deeply surveyed in `dlc_pricing_ladders.md` (T-P3). Pulling the high-count-stress-test angle here specifically.

Stellaris catalog 30+ paid SKUs by early 2026. CK3 catalog 12+ paid SKUs by early 2026 (younger catalog). EU4 catalog 30+ paid SKUs over 11-year DLC run before EU5. HOI4 catalog 15-20 paid SKUs by early 2026.

Tier discipline: three-tier ladder ($6.99 / $14.99 / $29.99) inside each Paradox title is the cleanest example of intentional accretion. Confidence: REPORTED.

Cumulative catalog price: Stellaris full catalog $200+, CK3 full catalog $150+, EU4 $300+, HOI4 $200+. The niche "DLC fatigue" language appears in Stellaris reviews around 15-20 paid items (per `dlc_pricing_ladders.md` line 200). Confidence: REPORTED.

Fatigue threshold marker (key calibration for T-M2): **15-20 SKU ceiling before "padding" / "fatigue" review language becomes prevalent in niche-strategy audiences.** Stellaris hit it; CK3 has not (catalog too young); EU4 hit it earlier and accepted it as a flagship-franchise tax. Confidence: REPORTED. ESTIMATED on the exact threshold — community sentiment is non-linear, not a hard line.

Ouroboros 7-era ladder cumulative at hypothetical $19.99 per era × 6 paid eras = $119.94. At $24.99 per era × 6 paid = $149.94. At $19.99 per era × 7 paid (Roman-free + 7 paid) = $139.93. At $24.99 × 7 = $174.93. **All four configurations sit below the Stellaris fatigue-onset $200 ceiling**. Confidence: REPORTED.

If Ouroboros adds flavor-pack tier accretion atop the 7 main era DLCs (a Paradox-shape moment), per-era flavor packs at $6.99 each could add 2-3 SKUs per era × 7 eras = 14-21 additional SKUs, pushing total catalog to 21-28 SKUs and total catalog price into Stellaris-fatigue territory. Confidence: ESTIMATED. AMBIGUOUS whether the bible's 7-era lock implies any flavor-pack tier or whether 7 is the hard SKU ceiling.

## Total War Warhammer trilogy + Lord/Race Packs

Creative Assembly's Total War: Warhammer (2016), Warhammer II (2017), Warhammer III (2022) is the strongest cumulative-DLC stress-test inside a tiered-strategy precedent. Three base titles plus 30+ DLC packs spread across the trilogy.

Per-title catalog: TWW1 received approximately 15-18 DLC packs (Lord Packs, Race Packs, Campaign Packs). TWW2 received approximately 10-15 DLC packs. TWW3 has received approximately 8-12 DLC packs through 2024-2025. Confidence: REPORTED. ESTIMATED on exact per-title counts.

Tier shape: Lord Packs ($8.99-$9.99 for 2 Legendary Lords + race units), Race Packs ($18.99-$24.99 for 1 new race with full roster), Campaign Packs ($14.99-$19.99 for new sandbox modes), DLC race FLC (free unit / lord drops between paid DLC). Three-tier-plus-FLC pattern. Confidence: REPORTED.

Mortal Empires + Immortal Empires combined-map design: TWW2's Mortal Empires (free with TWW1 ownership) combined TWW1 + TWW2 maps into one mega-campaign; TWW3's Immortal Empires extends this to all three titles' content. Cumulative-ownership unlock is a **structural anti-fatigue mechanism** — owning more DLC enriches the mega-campaign rather than fragmenting into separate playlists. Confidence: REPORTED.

Fatigue signal: TWW3 launched in February 2022 to mixed reception driven partly by Realm of Chaos campaign design and partly by DLC fatigue residue from TWW2. Champions of Chaos DLC (August 2022 $24.99) was the first major paid pack post-launch and received polarized reception. Subsequent DLC has stabilized reception but TWW3 cumulative-catalog fatigue is documented in community discussion through 2023-2024. Confidence: REPORTED.

Cumulative full trilogy price: base TWW1 + TWW2 + TWW3 ($60 + $60 + $60 = $180) plus 30+ DLC ($200-$400 depending on which packs) = $380-$580+ at full sticker. Mortal/Immortal Empires structural design partially offsets the sticker-shock perception by making cumulative ownership feel additive rather than fragmenting. Confidence: REPORTED.

Bundle architecture: Creative Assembly bundles aggressively post-launch. Steam-sale-driven mega-bundles, occasional publisher-bundles. No subscription-style pass. Confidence: REPORTED.

"Era 1-2 carries franchise" pattern: TWW1 + TWW2 sold dramatically more than TWW3 lifetime (ESTIMATED 6-8M units each for TWW1/TWW2 vs 3-4M for TWW3 at comparable post-launch windows). Late-trilogy attach decay is visible at the title level. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

Ouroboros applicability: **moderate**. Demonstrates that a 30+ SKU catalog inside a tiered-strategy precedent is **achievable but stress-tested** at the upper end of audience tolerance. The Mortal/Immortal Empires structural design is a strong **anti-fragmentation pattern** — Ouroboros' 7-era ladder could borrow this by making each new era DLC enrich the cumulative civilizational arc rather than gate a discrete content silo. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Civilization VI New Frontier Pass (6-pack staggered $40 model)

Firaxis' Civ VI New Frontier Pass (May 2020 - March 2021) is a structural precedent for staggered-multi-pack pricing inside a strategy AAA precedent.

Structure: 6 DLC packs released approximately bi-monthly over 11 months. $39.99 total for the season pass; à-la-carte purchase available per pack at $4.99-$9.99 per pack. New leaders, new civilizations, new game modes (Apocalypse, Heroes & Legends, Zombie Defense, Secret Societies, Barbarian Clans, Monopolies & Corporations). Confidence: VERIFIED.

Pre-buy capture: NFP was an explicit pre-buy season-pass SKU — buyer commits at start of season for the full 6-pack at discount vs sum of à-la-carte. Pre-buy revenue captures interested buyers at single decision moment. Confidence: REPORTED.

Reception: mixed-to-positive overall. Game modes received more polarized reception than civilization additions — Zombie Defense and Apocalypse mode polarized hardcore players; Secret Societies and Monopolies & Corporations received better reception. Confidence: REPORTED.

Attach: ESTIMATED 25-40% of active Civ VI base purchased the New Frontier Pass during its release window. Lifetime attach against base owners likely ESTIMATED 15-25%. Confidence: ESTIMATED — Firaxis does not disclose attach.

Civ VI subsequent: Leader Pass (November 2022 - March 2023) followed NFP structure — 6 packs of new leaders at $4.99 each or $19.99 season-pass bundle. Smaller scope per pack than NFP, lower price ceiling. Anthology editions bundle all Civ VI DLC plus base at $89.99-$129.99. Confidence: REPORTED.

Ouroboros applicability: **moderate-to-high** for season-pass-bundle architecture, low for cadence. NFP demonstrates that a 6-pack-staggered season pass at $40 total works in a strategy AAA niche — directly informs Ouroboros' "Eras Pass" bundle-SKU pick (pre-buy capture for all 6 or 7 paid eras at discount vs sum). NFP cadence (bi-monthly across 11 months) is impossibly fast for solo-dev scale per T-CL1. The **structural pre-buy season-pass concept** transfers cleanly; the **bi-monthly delivery cadence** does not. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Civilization VII era/age DLC plan (closest direct analog)

Civ VII (released February 2025) ships an era / age structural model — game divided into three Ages (Antiquity, Exploration, Modern) at launch, with future Age DLC planned. This is the **closest direct narrative-arc analog to Ouroboros' 7-era ladder** in surveyed precedents.

Launch structure: base Civ VII at $69.99 includes 3 Ages (Antiquity, Exploration, Modern). Deluxe Edition $99.99 / Founders Edition $129.99 with cosmetic and pre-order bonus content. Confidence: VERIFIED.

Post-launch Age DLC plan: Firaxis announced post-launch Age expansions during pre-release marketing — additional Ages (potentially future eras beyond Modern, or alternate-history Age branches) planned at $19.99-$29.99 per Age. As of mid-2026 the exact post-launch cadence and pricing has not been fully published. Confidence: REPORTED. AMBIGUOUS on specific post-launch Age pricing and cadence given Civ VII's youth.

Reception at launch: Civ VII launched to polarized reception — Age transition system received mixed reviews, civilization-change-per-Age mechanic received polarized hardcore-player feedback. Patches have stabilized reception through 2025-2026. Confidence: REPORTED.

Ouroboros parallels:
- Era-as-structural-unit (Civ VII Ages, Ouroboros eras) is the same structural concept.
- Civ VII ships 3 Ages at launch; Ouroboros plans 1 free + 6-7 paid (1 launch-tier era + 6-7 DLC eras) — Ouroboros' base-game scope is dramatically narrower than Civ VII at launch.
- Civ VII's $69.99 base is **2-3x Ouroboros' planned $25-35 base**. Ouroboros' price-to-scope ratio is closer to Paradox base prices than to Civ VII base price.
- Post-launch Age cadence in Civ VII expected at Firaxis scale (200+ employees) — solo-dev scale is dramatically below this. Civ VII cadence demonstrates **what is possible at AAA scale**, not what is achievable at solo-dev scale.

Ouroboros applicability: **high for structural model, low for execution scale**. Civ VII's era/age plan demonstrates that the niche audience accepts era-as-paid-expansion structure — that's a load-bearing signal for Ouroboros' bible-locked model. Per-Age pricing range ($19.99-$29.99 estimated) cross-validates T-P3 mid-tier. Cadence implications inverse — Civ VII can deliver post-launch Ages at Firaxis cadence (1-2 per year); Ouroboros at solo-dev scale per T-CL1 cannot. Confidence: REPORTED.

Risk surface: Civ VII's mixed reception at launch is a **directional caution signal** — the era / age structural concept is novel enough in 4X strategy that even Firaxis at AAA scale received polarized reception. Ouroboros at solo-dev scale will have fewer iteration cycles to recover from polarized reception on era-structural-model. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

## Endless Legend / Endless Space DLC pattern

Amplitude Studios' Endless Legend (2014) and Endless Space 2 (2017) ship a moderate-count DLC ecosystem — fewer SKUs than Paradox but more structured than indie titles.

Endless Legend DLC: Guardians (2015 free major update), Shadows (2015 $9.99), Forges of Creation (2016 $4.99), Echoes of Auriga (2016 $4.99), Tempest (2016 $9.99), Shifters (2017 $9.99), Inferno (2017 $9.99), Symbiosis (2019 $9.99), Monstrous Tales (2020 $4.99). Approximately 8 paid DLCs across 6-year post-launch window. Confidence: REPORTED.

Endless Space 2 DLC: Vaulters (2017 $7.99), Supremacy (2018 $9.99), Penumbra (2018 $12.99), Untold Tales (2019 $9.99), Awakening (2019 $14.99), Dark Matter (2024 $14.99 ESTIMATED). Approximately 6 paid DLCs across 7-year post-launch window. Confidence: REPORTED.

Cadence: 1-2 DLCs per year on average. Amplitude is medium-team (estimated 50-80 employees). Confidence: ESTIMATED on team size.

Price discipline: $4.99-$14.99 range, similar to Paradox flavor-pack / mid-tier band. No major-expansion-tier ($29.99) DLC in Amplitude catalog — Amplitude stays inside the smaller-scope tier. Confidence: REPORTED.

Bundle architecture: Amplitude Definitive Editions bundle base + key DLCs at ~$29.99-$39.99 (vs sum of à-la-carte $50-$80). Sega's acquisition of Amplitude in 2016 has not visibly shifted bundle strategy. Confidence: REPORTED.

Fatigue signal: low. Endless Legend / Endless Space community reception of DLC has been broadly positive across catalog. Smaller catalog (6-8 DLCs vs Paradox's 15-30) stays below fatigue threshold. Confidence: REPORTED.

Long-tail attach: ESTIMATED 30-50% attach on first major DLC for active base; ESTIMATED 15-30% on later DLCs. Decay curve similar to Paradox but at smaller absolute revenue per item due to lower per-SKU pricing. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

"Era 1-2 carries franchise" pattern: Endless Legend Shadows + Tempest carried more attach than later DLCs. Endless Space 2 Penumbra + Awakening carried more than Untold Tales / Dark Matter. Decay curve consistent with broader niche pattern. Confidence: REPORTED.

Ouroboros applicability: **moderate-to-high for cadence and pricing**. Amplitude at 50-80 person scale ships 1-2 DLCs per year at $9.99-$14.99 modal price. Ouroboros at solo-dev scale per T-CL1 ships 1-3 year-per-DLC at higher per-SKU price point ($19.99-$24.99 likely). Amplitude's catalog-size discipline (6-8 DLCs total over 6-7 years) keeps cumulative catalog below $80 — well below Stellaris fatigue ceiling, similar to RimWorld's full catalog cost. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Cross-axes

### Fatigue thresholds

- **CK3 community fatigue language post-Royal Court:** Royal Court (February 2022 $29.99) was the first CK3 expansion at the $29.99 price ceiling. Community reception was mixed at launch — 3D throne room rendering polarized players before deeper grandeur mechanics landed in patches. Pricing-vs-value tension documented but no broad-catalog fatigue language yet at CK3's 12-SKU catalog size. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **EU4 "padding" criticism:** EU4 community discussion of "padding" / "feature stripping" patterns visible from approximately Common Sense (2015) onwards, intensifying through Mandate of Heaven (2017) and Leviathan (2021 bug-driven review-bombing). EU4 catalog hit 15+ items by 2017, hitting the niche fatigue threshold from above. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Stellaris cosmetic-fatigue signals:** Species packs (Lithoids, Necroids, Aquatics, Humanoids v2) received community language characterizing the lower tier as "flavor packs exist for revenue" — extracted from `stellaris_dlc_ladder.md` line 79. Catalog-fatigue language intensified around 15-20 SKU mark. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Total War Warhammer III fatigue:** TWW3 launched into accumulated trilogy-fatigue from TWW1 + TWW2 catalog cumulative. Champions of Chaos DLC (August 2022) received polarized reception driven partly by this fatigue. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Calibrated threshold:** **15-20 paid SKUs is the niche fatigue-onset zone** across the surveyed strategy precedents. Ouroboros at 7 paid eras stays well below this; 7 paid eras + flavor-pack accretion could approach it. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

### Bundle SKU design

### Long-tail SKU maintenance burden

Each additional SKU adds a maintenance multiplier across patching, regression testing, localization, customer support.

- **Train Simulator approach:** externalize maintenance to third-party developer ecosystem. ESTIMATED 1.1-1.3x maintenance multiplier on first-party-side; community-tolerated breakage on third-party-side. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **MSFS approach:** similar to Train Sim — Asobo patches base engine; Marketplace third parties patch their own products. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Paradox approach:** first-party maintenance of full catalog. Each new patch on base game requires regression testing across all DLC content. ESTIMATED multiplier 1.05-1.10x per active DLC SKU (compounding). Paradox dedicates internal QA resources to this. Stellaris at 30+ SKUs implies ESTIMATED 2-3x multiplier on patching time vs 0-SKU baseline. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **RimWorld approach:** Tynan Sylvester maintains 4 DLCs first-party plus base game. Maintenance burden tractable at this catalog size. RimWorld's mod-API extensibility allows community to absorb edge-case feature requests rather than expanding DLC catalog. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Total War approach:** Creative Assembly maintains 30+ DLC across trilogy. AAA-scale QA resources. Mortal/Immortal Empires unified-campaign design adds maintenance complexity (interactions between all DLCs must be tested cumulatively, not in isolation). Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Solo-dev implications:** at solo-dev or very-small team scale, maintenance multiplier compounds aggressively. Adding the 7th era DLC requires regression testing against the 6 previously-shipped era DLCs. ESTIMATED 1.5-2.5x effective maintenance multiplier by SKU 7 vs SKU 1. Confidence: ESTIMATED. Engineering scope implication: 7-era pipeline requires upfront architectural investment in regression-test automation, era-isolation boundaries, save-format versioning, content-pack loading discipline. Confidence: REPORTED.

T-CL2 attach-decay finding: first-expansion attach 40-60% of active base; second-expansion attach 30-50%; third-expansion attach 20-40%; fourth+ attach 15-30%. Decay curve sloped but not collapsed — sustained per-DLC revenue floor across the catalog. Confidence: REPORTED.

Per-precedent re-statement:

- **CK3:** Royal Court 30-50%; Roads to Power and later DLC ESTIMATED 20-35%. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **RimWorld:** Royalty 30-40%, Ideology 40-60%, Biotech 35-55%, Anomaly 30-45%. Decay-curve flatter than Paradox due to flat-pricing + smaller catalog. Confidence: ESTIMATED (per `dlc_pricing_ladders.md` lines 106-110).
- **Total War Warhammer trilogy:** TWW1 + TWW2 carried more lifetime DLC attach than TWW3 at comparable post-launch windows. Title-level decay (separate dynamic from per-DLC-within-title decay). Confidence: REPORTED.

Revenue model substrate: don't budget eras 5-7 on first-DLC-equivalent attach; budget them at 15-30% of active base attach. Confidence: REPORTED.

### Internal-bundle vs publisher-bundle leverage

- **Internal-bundle leverage:** publisher controls the bundle SKU, captures pre-buy revenue, sets discount tier, controls when bundle SKU goes live. High leverage. Examples: Paradox Chapter Pass, Civ VI New Frontier Pass, MSFS Premium Deluxe, Amplitude Definitive Editions.
- **Publisher-platform-bundle (Steam-sale-driven):** Steam holds bundle UI surface; publisher participates in Steam sale event bundles at discount tier. Lower leverage on timing and discount depth. Examples: RimWorld bundles, Train Sim sale bundles, Total War Warhammer Steam bundles.
- **Subscription-pass-bundle:** Paradox Plus rotating-access subscription. Different revenue mechanic (recurring subscription vs one-time bundle pre-buy). Mixed leverage — captures monthly recurring revenue but cannibalizes per-DLC purchase at some rate. Per `dlc_pricing_ladders.md` line 166-168.
- **Ouroboros leverage options:** at solo-dev or very-small team scale, internal-bundle SKU requires storefront management overhead; publisher-platform-bundle is lower-overhead but lower-leverage. Steam season-pass mechanism is a midpoint. Confidence: REPORTED.

### Completionism cost vs entry-tier perception

Buyer-perception axis: "How much does completion cost me from the entry tier?" maps to fatigue likelihood.

- **Low completionism cost (entry tier + 1-3 SKUs total):** RimWorld $34.99 base + 4 × $24.99 = $134.95 full catalog. Endless Legend $30 base + 8 DLCs at $4.99-$9.99 ≈ $90 full catalog. Wildermyth $24.99 base + Omenroad $9.99 = $34.98 full catalog. Buyer-perception: "I can own everything." Low fatigue. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Medium completionism cost (entry tier + 6-10 SKUs total):** Civ VI base $59.99 + NFP $39.99 + Leader Pass $19.99 + smaller DLC ≈ $140-$180. CK3 base $49.99 + 12 DLCs at varying tiers ≈ $150-$200. Buyer-perception: "Completion is a real spend but achievable." Moderate fatigue tolerance. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **High completionism cost (entry tier + 15-30 SKUs total):** Stellaris base $39.99 + 30+ DLCs ≈ $250-$350. EU4 base $39.99 + 30+ DLCs ≈ $300-$400. Total War Warhammer trilogy + 30+ DLCs ≈ $400-$600. Buyer-perception: "Completion is fundamentally not the right purchase model." High fatigue, drives subscription / bundle / partial-buy strategies. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Hobby-discretionary completionism (no realistic completion):** Train Sim $20 base + 1000+ DLCs at $15,000-$25,000+. MSFS base $59.99 + 400-700 DLCs. DCS free base + 30+ aircraft at $80 each. Buyer-perception explicitly hobby-mode: "I buy what I want, completion is impossible by design." Lowest fatigue at the catalog level. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Ouroboros at 6-7 paid eras stays in the low-to-medium completionism cost band.** Cumulative ladder at $19.99-$24.99 per era yields $120-$175 full catalog at 6-7 paid eras. **Buyer-perception likely "I can own everything" or "Completion is real but achievable" — low-to-moderate fatigue zone.** Confidence: REPORTED.

If flavor-pack tier added atop 7 era-DLCs, completionism cost rises into medium-to-high band. Confidence: ESTIMATED. AMBIGUOUS on whether bible's 7-era lock implies hard SKU ceiling or accretion atop the 7.
