## Day of the Devs

Day of the Devs is a Double Fine + iam8bit-curated indie showcase. Two events per year typically: a Summer Game Fest co-located edition (early June) and a Game Awards-adjacent edition (December). Highly curated — Day of the Devs hand-picks 20-40 titles per event from hundreds-to-thousands of submissions. REPORTED.

**Application window 2026.** Summer 2026 edition: submission open approximately January-February 2026, close approximately March 2026, event approximately June 2026. ESTIMATED from prior-year cadence. December 2026 edition: submission open approximately July-August 2026, close approximately September 2026, event approximately December 2026. ESTIMATED.

**Selection criteria.** Curatorial. Day of the Devs favors strong art direction, distinct voice, narrative angle, and "showcase-able" trailer-friendly footage. Hard-SF strategy is not a typical fit unless visuals carry — Day of the Devs leans toward visually striking indies (Stray, Sable, Cocoon, Tunic). Generational-strategy with chronicle visuals could fit if the trailer reads narrative-first. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Application success rate.** Submissions are in the thousands; selections are 20-40 per event. Implied acceptance rate: 1-4%. ESTIMATED, FLOOR.

**Wishlist delta.** Selected titles report 10,000-50,000+ wishlist gain in the week of showcase, driven by livestream surface and YouTube clip propagation. ESTIMATED, REPORTED at upper bound (multiple post-mortems on indie blogs cite the 20,000-40,000 band).

**Required assets.** 60-90 second showcase trailer in Day-of-the-Devs editorial format (no logos in first 3 seconds, cinematic-first); Steam page live; press kit; key art; willingness to license clip footage to Day of the Devs. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Lead time.** Application 3-4 months before event; trailer must be near-final at application time. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Tiny Teams (Yogscast)

Yogscast Tiny Teams is a one-week Steam-page-front + Yogscast-streamed event for solo-developer or very-small-team games (typically ≤3 developers). Runs once per year, typically late July or early August. REPORTED.

**Application window 2026.** Typically opens March-April 2026, closes May-June 2026, event late July-early August 2026. ESTIMATED.

**Selection criteria.** Team size: ≤3 developers is the documented floor (Yogscast has flexed to ~5 in some years but the brand is "tiny"). Steam page must be live with demo or near-launch build. Yogscast favors quirky, distinctly-voiced indies that stream well — humor, charm, surprise factor. REPORTED.

**Application success rate.** Yogscast accepts approximately 60-100 titles per Tiny Teams event from submissions in the hundreds. Implied acceptance rate: 10-30%. ESTIMATED.

**Wishlist delta.** Selected titles report 2,000-15,000+ wishlist gain during the week, with Yogscast-stream-featured titles seeing the upper band. Strong tail effect — Yogscast YouTube clips drive 2-4 weeks of post-event traffic. ESTIMATED, REPORTED at middle of band.

**Required assets.** Steam page; demo or near-launch build; willingness for Yogscast streamers to record; press kit; ideally one streamable hook (a moment that reads on camera in <30 seconds). Confidence: REPORTED.

**Niche-strategy fit.** Generational-strategy with chronicle hooks is plausible if team size qualifies. Ouroboros team-size status check needed before application. The pitch must read in 30 seconds of stream footage. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

**Lead time.** Application 2-3 months before event. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Steam Strategy Fest

Steam Strategy Fest is a Valve-run themed sale + discovery event focused on strategy and tactics games. Runs typically once per year, August-September. Some years Valve has run a secondary strategy-themed event in winter; cadence is not perfectly consistent. REPORTED.

**Application window 2026.** Strategy Fest 2026 ESTIMATED for late August-early September 2026. Opt-in via Steamworks event dashboard, typically opens 6-8 weeks before event and closes 2-3 weeks before event. REPORTED on mechanism, ESTIMATED on exact dates.

**Selection criteria.** Largely opt-in for games tagged Strategy or with strategy-adjacent tags. Valve does not curate Strategy Fest the way Day of the Devs is curated — most strategy games on Steam can participate. Discount commitment typically required (10%+ discount during fest is the norm). REPORTED.

**Application success rate.** Effectively 100% for tagged strategy games meeting discount requirement. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Wishlist delta.** Variable. Front-page surfacing during Strategy Fest is the leverage; titles featured on front page report 5,000-30,000 wishlist gain. Titles deep in the listing report 500-3,000. Featured slot is the asymmetric win. ESTIMATED, FLOOR.

**Pre-launch participation.** Pre-launch games (no live store discount possible) can participate via the "Upcoming Strategy Games" surface but generally see lower deltas than launched-with-discount titles. ESTIMATED.

**Required assets.** Steam page live; tags accurately reflecting Strategy; capsule and screenshots optimized for the fest's grid view; trailer that reads in the fest carousel; discount commitment (if launched). Confidence: REPORTED.

**Lead time.** 2-3 months from decision to event. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Big Adventure / Cerebral / niche dev showcases

A constellation of smaller curated showcases run by indie collectives or individual developers. Each is small audience but high signal — buyers attending these are pre-filtered for the showcase's theme.

**Big Adventure Event.** Indie showcase historically focused on adventure games and narrative indies. Runs roughly twice yearly, dates vary. Application is curated, free, modest in scale (~30-50 titles featured per event). Wishlist delta: 1,000-5,000 typical, occasionally higher for trailer-hits. ESTIMATED. Niche-strategy fit: marginal — Big Adventure leans adventure-genre. A generational-strategy with strong narrative angle could fit if framed narrative-first.

**Cerebral Showcase.** Indie showcase focused on thinky / brain-burner games — puzzle, strategy, simulation, narrative-heavy. Runs once or twice yearly. Application is curated, ~30-60 titles per event. Wishlist delta: 2,000-10,000 typical for strategy/sim titles. REPORTED at band level, ESTIMATED on magnitude. Niche-strategy fit: strong — Cerebral's audience overlaps the primary Ouroboros tribe (generational-strategy, hard-SF, simulation-depth buyers). Application dates ESTIMATED for Spring 2026 and Fall 2026 submission windows; check live site.

**Other niche showcases worth mentioning (REPORTED existence, ESTIMATED 2026 cadence).** Wholesome Direct (June), Future of Play Direct (June), Realms Deep (gamescom-adjacent, August), Triple-i Initiative (April), Devolver Direct (June, invitation-only), Black Voices in Gaming, Latinx in Gaming, IGN Live, OTK Games Expo. Most are invitation-only or have idiosyncratic curation. Niche-strategy fit varies. Wishlist delta for selected titles 3,000-30,000 ESTIMATED. The takeaway: the showcase landscape is fragmented and a strong indie can credibly target 4-8 small showcases per year.

**Application success rate (small showcases).** 5-25% acceptance typical. Curatorial fit matters more than polish. ESTIMATED.

**Required assets.** Trailer (30-90 seconds), Steam page, key art, press kit. Same baseline as Day of the Devs but lower production bar acceptable. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Lead time.** 2-4 months from application to event. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Hooded Horse Showcase

Hooded Horse is a strategy/grand-strategy publisher (Old World, Falling Frontier, Manor Lords pre-launch, Terra Invicta) and runs its own publisher showcase typically once or twice yearly, often gamescom-adjacent in August. REPORTED.

**Inclusion criteria.** Hooded Horse Showcase is publisher-curated. A game must be either (a) a Hooded Horse-published title, or (b) invited as a guest by Hooded Horse editorial. Submissions are not publicly open in the way Steam Next Fest is open. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Implications for Ouroboros.** Direct inclusion would require either signing with Hooded Horse as publisher (a strategic decision well outside Research Lane scope, with major implications for revenue split, marketing budget, and editorial control) or being invited as a guest title (rare, but does happen for strategy indies that align with Hooded Horse's brand). The Hooded Horse audience overlap with primary Ouroboros tribe is high — 60-80% ESTIMATED based on tag overlap with Old World, Falling Frontier, Terra Invicta wishlists.

**Wishlist delta.** Hooded Horse Showcase titles report 5,000-50,000+ wishlist gain in the showcase week, with the higher band tied to Hooded Horse's own audience push (newsletter, social, YouTube). ESTIMATED, REPORTED at band level.

**Required assets.** Whatever Hooded Horse editorial requests, typically a 60-90 second trailer in their cut style, Steam page live, key art. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Lead time.** Publisher conversations are 6-12+ months. Guest invitation lead time 3-6 months. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

**Status for Ouroboros.** No Hooded Horse relationship surfaced in Research Lane scope. Surface only — Westley/Design Lane decides whether to pursue publisher outreach.

PC Gaming Show (Future / PCGamer) and Future Games Show (GamesRadar) are press-curated showcases broadcast during major industry windows — Summer Game Fest (June) and gamescom (August-September) and occasionally a Spring event. Each curates approximately 40-60 trailer slots per event. REPORTED.

PC Gaming Show gamescom 2026: submissions May-June 2026, close July 2026, event August-September 2026. **Selection criteria.** Editorial curation. Both shows favor trailers with clear hook in first 5 seconds, strong art direction, identifiable genre signal, and willingness to license clip footage. Paid slots exist (publishers can buy guaranteed slots); curated free slots also exist. Confidence: REPORTED.

## gamescom / PAX / GDC

Three major industry events with festival surfaces of varying applicability to niche-strategy indies.

**gamescom (Cologne, late August).** Largest gaming trade show in the world. For a small indie, gamescom participation is via (a) Indie Arena Booth (curated indie exhibitor area, application typically February-April), (b) publisher-shared booth, (c) attending as press/business pass without booth, or (d) gamescom Opening Night Live trailer slot (invitation-only, publisher-mediated). Indie Arena Booth fees historically €1,000-3,000 per developer for a shared booth slot. REPORTED.

**Application window gamescom 2026.** Indie Arena Booth typically opens January-February 2026, closes April-May 2026, event August 2026. ESTIMATED.

**Selection criteria (Indie Arena Booth).** Curated. Favors visually distinct indies, demos-ready, willing to staff booth for 3-5 days. REPORTED.

**Wishlist delta.** Highly variable — gamescom booth traffic is significant (hundreds of thousands of attendees) but conversion to wishlists is modest for niche-strategy because the on-floor audience skews mainstream. Indies report 1,000-10,000 wishlist gain across the event week, with B2B / press meetings often being the higher-leverage outcome (publisher conversations, influencer meetings, press coverage). ESTIMATED.

**Required assets.** Demo build playable on tradeshow PCs; pull-up banner; key art; business cards; willingness to staff booth physically; travel + lodging budget. Total cost €5,000-15,000 ESTIMATED for a single-developer attendance. Confidence: REPORTED.

**PAX (multiple events per year — PAX East, PAX West, PAX Australia, PAX Unplugged).** Indie Megabooth and PAX Rising are the indie-focused booth surfaces. Indie Megabooth has had on-again-off-again history; PAX Rising is the curated indie selection program. Application timelines vary by event. REPORTED.

**Wishlist delta.** Similar pattern to gamescom — modest direct wishlist, higher value in press and influencer meetings. 500-5,000 wishlist gain typical. ESTIMATED.

**Cost.** Booth fees + travel typically $3,000-10,000 USD per event for a single developer. REPORTED.

**GDC (San Francisco, March).** Game Developers Conference is industry-facing — business, press, publishers, fellow developers. Almost no consumer audience. Festival surface for consumers is minimal. Value is networking, publisher conversations, press meetings, IGF awards if nominated. REPORTED.

**Application window GDC 2026.** Conference pass purchase ongoing through March 2026. IGF submissions typically open August 2025-October 2025 for the 2026 awards. Schedule speaker slots application closes ~6 months before conference. REPORTED.

**IGF nomination wishlist delta.** Nomination announcement (typically January before the March conference) drives 5,000-30,000 wishlists for nominated indies. ESTIMATED, REPORTED at upper band.

**Cost.** $2,000-3,000 conference pass + travel. $5,000-10,000 total ESTIMATED. REPORTED.

**Status for Ouroboros.** Industry-event participation is a strategic-budget decision well outside Research Lane scope. Surface only.

## Stacking patterns

Sequences observed across indie post-mortems where festival participation compounds.

**Sequence A: small-showcase warm-up → Next Fest → launch.** Apply to 2-3 small curated showcases (Cerebral, Big Adventure, similar) in the 3-6 months before Next Fest. Each small showcase drives 1,000-5,000 wishlists. Cumulative pre-Next-Fest wishlist floor reaches 5,000-15,000 — crosses the algorithmic visibility threshold for Next Fest (see T-D2). Next Fest then drives 10,000-30,000 additional. Launch follows 30-60 days after Next Fest. ESTIMATED, REPORTED in multiple indie post-mortems (Cliffski, Zukowski cites Manor Lords-adjacent pattern).

**Sequence B: PC Gaming Show/FGS trailer reveal → Next Fest → launch.** Trailer reveal at PC Gaming Show or Future Games Show drives 5,000-20,000 wishlist spike. Next Fest 3-4 months later compounds on top of that base. Launch follows. Pattern requires getting a free curated slot, which is the bottleneck. ESTIMATED.

**Sequence C: Tiny Teams → Steam Strategy Fest → launch.** Tiny Teams in late July drives modest wishlist gain plus Yogscast YouTube tail. Strategy Fest in late August catches the tail. Launch in Q4 catches Q4 wishlist conversion peak. Pattern works for solo/tiny-team developers who qualify for Tiny Teams. ESTIMATED.

**Sequence D: Day of the Devs trailer reveal → Next Fest demo drop → launch within 3-4 months.** Day of the Devs in December (Game Awards adjacency) drives 10,000-50,000 wishlists. February Next Fest cohort follows ~8-10 weeks later. Launch follows February Next Fest by 30-60 days, landing in spring. Pattern requires getting into Day of the Devs (1-4% acceptance) — high reward but high gate. ESTIMATED.

**Sequence E: announcement trailer (Future Games Show Spring) → Next Fest June or October → launch Q4.** Spring trailer reveal builds initial wishlist base. Summer or Fall Next Fest compounds. Q4 launch catches holiday wishlist conversion. ESTIMATED.

**Stacking pattern (common denominator).** The pattern that recurs is "trailer reveal at a curated showcase + Next Fest demo drop + launch within 60-90 days of Next Fest." Each surface compounds on the prior surface's wishlist base. Skipping the warm-up showcase reduces Next Fest leverage. Skipping Next Fest reduces launch-week visibility. The three-surface stack is the indie consensus baseline.

**What doesn't stack well.** Multiple major trailer reveals in a single quarter cannibalize — buyers and press have a limited memory window, and a second trailer 4 weeks after the first reads as repetition rather than new news. Two showcases per quarter is the practical ceiling for trailer-fresh signal. ESTIMATED.

## Collision patterns

Festivals that overlap or conflict in ways that reduce combined leverage.

**Collision 1: June Next Fest vs Summer Game Fest vs PC Gaming Show Summer vs Future Games Show Summer.** All four anchor to early-to-mid June. Indie signal is drowned by AAA reveals and publisher showcases the same week. A niche-strategy demo in June Next Fest competes for attention with 100+ AAA trailers. Pattern: June is the contested cohort. Cross-ref T-D2.

**Collision 2: Steam Strategy Fest (August-September) vs gamescom (late August).** Both anchor late summer. Buyers attending gamescom are not browsing Steam Strategy Fest in parallel. Strategy Fest still works because Steam's audience is far larger than gamescom's, but the timing overlap means a developer can't optimally do both (and especially can't physically attend gamescom while also running a Strategy Fest discount campaign).

**Collision 3: Day of the Devs December vs Game Awards vs Winter Sale.** December is a content-saturated month. Day of the Devs December edition airs the same week as Game Awards. Trailers shown in both windows compete with each other and with Winter Sale buyer mindset (which is "spend my wishlist down," not "add to wishlist"). Net effect: December reveals get strong opening-day spike but flatten faster than other cohorts.

**Collision 4: February Next Fest vs Lunar Sale vs Spring Sale.** Less severe than the June collision but still present. Lunar Sale typically late January through early February overlaps with February Next Fest application window. Demo build crunch + sale-week marketing competing for developer attention. ESTIMATED.

**Collision 5: October Next Fest vs Autumn Sale.** Autumn Sale typically late October. October Next Fest cohort runs early-to-mid October. Buyers' wishlist-building behavior in early October feeds Autumn Sale spending in late October. Pattern is actually complementary rather than collision-y — October Next Fest is well-timed for pre-Autumn-Sale wishlist farming. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Collision 6: Tiny Teams late July vs Summer Sale late June-July.** Summer Sale draws buyer attention to existing wishlists; Tiny Teams targets wishlist addition. Buyers who just spent down their wishlist during Summer Sale have budget hangover. Tiny Teams typically lands just after Summer Sale closes, which mitigates but does not eliminate the budget-hangover effect. ESTIMATED.

**Collision 7: Once-per-game rules.** Steam Next Fest enforces a strict once-per-game rule. A demo that participates in February 2026 Next Fest cannot participate in any subsequent Next Fest cohort. This forces a single-cohort pick and surfaces the cohort-choice decision as load-bearing. Cross-ref T-D2. REPORTED.

## Cross-cutting findings

- **Trailer is the universal asset.** A 60-second master trailer + 30-second and 90-second cutdowns covers ~80% of festival submission requirements. Producing the master trailer first is the highest-leverage asset decision. The same trailer's first 3 seconds carry disproportionate weight in editorial curation for Day of the Devs, PC Gaming Show, Future Games Show.
- **Steam page lead time is the binding constraint.** Festival applications require live Steam page weeks-to-months before application close. Pre-Zip gate's 30-day minimum page-live window is below the 60-90+ day floor most curated festivals require. Festival schedule must be planned backward from the latest-required page-live date among target festivals. Surfaced in `## Conflict surfaced`.
- **Acceptance-rate vs delta is inversely correlated.** Hardest-to-get-into festivals (Day of the Devs, PC Gaming Show, Future Games Show) drive largest deltas. Easiest (Next Fest, Strategy Fest) drive consistent but page-quality-bound deltas.
- **The three-surface stack is the indie baseline.** Curated showcase trailer reveal + Next Fest demo + launch within 60-90 days. Each surface compounds on the prior. Skipping a surface reduces the next surface's leverage.
- **June is contested; October is the niche-strategy sweet spot.** Cohort multipliers for niche-strategy: October 1.0× / February 0.7-0.9× / June 0.6-0.8× (from T-D2). Festival roster should anchor around October Next Fest unless other constraints dominate.
- **Once-per-game rules force commitment.** Next Fest cohort is a one-shot decision. Picking the wrong cohort is recoverable only by accepting the loss and skipping Next Fest entirely — there is no second-Next-Fest fallback.

## Conflict surfaced

Pre-Zip Beta Readiness Gate per CLAUDE.md requires Steam page live 30+ days before any external surface. Festival application lead times are 60-90+ days from page-live to application close for most curated festivals (Day of the Devs, PC Gaming Show, Future Games Show, Hooded Horse Showcase) and 60+ days for Steam Next Fest cohort participation. The 30-day Pre-Zip gate is below the festival-floor lead time.

Implication: planning the festival schedule must start from the latest-required page-live date among target festivals, not from the Pre-Zip gate's 30-day minimum. If targeting October 2026 Next Fest (page must be live by approximately late July or early August 2026 to clear the 60-90 day pre-fest wishlist accumulation window), the Pre-Zip gate must close at least 60-90 days before that, not 30 days. The 30-day gate is necessary but not sufficient for a festival-aware launch.

Surface for resolution. Two options for the Design Lane: (a) extend Pre-Zip gate functionally — set page-live target 60-90+ days before earliest festival surface, treat 30-day gate as a floor not a target; (b) plan a no-festival launch that respects only the 30-day gate and accepts the lower visibility ceiling. Westley/Design Lane decides.
