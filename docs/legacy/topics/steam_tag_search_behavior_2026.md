# Steam Tag Search Behavior 2026 (Design Lane Commercial Stream T-S1)

## What this brief is

A characterization of the Steam tag landscape relevant to Ouroboros' two niche-target tribes (generational/legacy strategy + Foundation/hard-SF) in 2026, with explicit attention to the tag-list contradiction between `docs/bible_history/game_bible_1_23.md (formerly bible_monetization.md, folded into the hub 2026-05-31)` L288 (locked April 2026) and `docs/bible/game_bible.md ## In Flux` 2026-05-16 RTS-framing kill. The brief catalogs which tags carry signal to which audience, which tags are over-fished kill-zones, which tags are under-fished blue oceans, and which tags have changed status since the bible_monetization tag list was authored. It does not pick a tag slate. The Design Lane decides; this brief surfaces the landscape that informs the decision.

This brief feeds the lock of Ouroboros' Steam tag slate. The slate is the primary discovery surface controllable from the Steam page side — wishlist conversion downstream of search depends on the tag pick. Mis-tagging surfaces the page to the wrong tribe (kill-zone tag pull) and starves the right tribe (under-fished tag absence).

## Method

Source-scanned: Steam store tag landscape circa 2024–2026 from training data; tag co-occurrence patterns observed in genre-adjacent commercial hits (CK3, Wildermyth, RimWorld, Old World, Battle Brothers, Stellaris, Manor Lords, Frostpunk 2, Helldivers 2, Caves of Qud, Sins of a Solar Empire II); existing Research Lane briefs `rts_market_kill_zone_2026.md` and `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md` for tribe-discovery surface data; bible monetization L288 + game_bible In Flux 2026-05-16 verbatim for the conflict surface. WebFetch and WebSearch were unavailable for this capture — current Steam algorithm behavior (specifically the 2026 first-three-tag weighting and any new-tag emergence since training data) is REPORTED from training data and ESTIMATED for post-2024 algorithm shifts. Confidence floor for algorithm specifics is ESTIMATED; tag co-occurrence patterns in adjacent games are REPORTED; the conflict-flag surface itself is VERIFIED against the cited bible lines.

## Tag landscape — by tribe

### Primary tribe (generational/legacy strategy intersection)

The lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief identifies the primary tribe as the CK3 + Wildermyth + Battle Brothers + Field of Glory: Empires intersection, with discovery surfaces r/CrusaderKings, r/wildermyth, r/grandstrategy, and YouTube creators SsethTzeentach, Quill18, Many a True Nerd. The Steam tag surfaces this tribe browses, in REPORTED order of pull strength:

- **Grand Strategy** — load-bearing. CK3, EU4, HOI4, Stellaris, Imperator, Old World, Field of Glory: Empires all carry this tag in their first-five. The tag is the strongest single signal of the generational/legacy intersection. Confidence: REPORTED from co-occurrence pattern.
- **Strategy** — universal. Carried by every commercial hit in the cluster. Necessary but not differentiating — the tag is too broad to surface a tribe-target page on its own.
- **Singleplayer** — load-bearing for the niche specifically. The tribe is multiplayer-skeptical; Singleplayer-tagged pages get a positive bias from this audience. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Story Rich** — strong pull for the Wildermyth and Battle Brothers subset specifically. Carried by Wildermyth, RimWorld, Caves of Qud, Disco Elysium-adjacent narrative-strategy. Differentiates from pure-mechanics strategy. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Procedural Generation** — strong for the RimWorld and Battle Brothers subset. Less load-bearing for CK3 buyers (CK3 has procedural generation but the audience does not pattern-match on the tag). Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Historical** — strong pull for the CK3, EU4, FoG: Empires subset. Carries the inverse-IP signal — "real history, not licensed IP." Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Replay Value** — moderate pull. Used in adjacent-genre marketing more than discovery. Tribe-aware buyers cite it positively in reviews but the search-tag pull is weaker than the review-keyword pull. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **Sandbox** — moderate pull. Strong for the RimWorld subset; weak for the CK3 subset. The tag carries an "open-ended" connotation that aligns with the tribe but does not differentiate. Confidence: REPORTED.

The primary tribe does **not** browse on RTS, Real-Time, Competitive, Esports, or MOBA. These tags actively repel — the tribe perceives them as the failed-classic-RTS cluster the In Flux entry kills off. Per the kill-zone brief, these tags carry negative-yield discovery for this niche specifically. The lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief's "open question 4" (Steam tag search-share for RTS vs. Strategy vs. Grand Strategy) is partially resolved here: RTS carries the kill-zone load; Grand Strategy carries the load-bearing tribe signal; Strategy carries the necessary-but-not-sufficient broad-genre signal.

### Foundation/hard-SF tribe (thematic differentiator)

The In Flux entry sizes this tribe at ~20k–80k reachable with halo from Apple TV Foundation viewership, discovery surfaces r/asimov, r/printSF, r/IsaacArthur. The Steam tag surfaces this tribe browses, in REPORTED order of pull strength:

- **Sci-fi** — load-bearing. Universal across the SF cluster (Stellaris, Caves of Qud, HighFleet, Nebulous, Sins II). The tag is necessary for Foundation-tribe surfacing.
- **Atmospheric** — strong for the Foundation literary subset specifically. Carries the "feels like a serious work, not a power fantasy" signal that distinguishes Asimov-reader expectations from Warhammer-reader expectations. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Story Rich** — strong overlap with primary tribe; load-bearing for the Foundation tribe as well. The tribe expects authored narrative, not pure systems.
- **Space** — moderate pull for the Isaac Arthur viewer subset; weaker for the Asimov-reader subset (whose canon includes terrestrial Foundation Earth and pre-Empire sequences). The bible monetization L288 explicitly excludes "Space" as a primary tag for era-lock reasons — the brief surfaces this exclusion as a separate decision point: the Foundation tribe's discovery surface partially indexes on Space, but Ouroboros' multi-era catalog argues against locking the primary slate to a single era's tag.
- **Cyberpunk / Post-apocalyptic / Dystopian** — Foundation-adjacent but tribe-imperfect. Carries the "civilizational collapse" connotation that maps onto Ouroboros' F-territory run shape (per `blue_ocean_run_shape.md`) but the named tribes do not browse these as a primary surface. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

The Foundation tribe does not browse heavily on Strategy, Grand Strategy, or Historical — these are the primary-tribe surfaces. Tag overlap between the two tribes is narrow: Sci-fi and Story Rich are the cross-tribe surfaces; Strategy and Atmospheric are within-tribe surfaces.

### Tribes actively avoided

The In Flux entry names four niches Ouroboros explicitly does not target. Tag implications:

- **SC2 / classic-RTS PvP** — exclude RTS, Real-Time Strategy, Competitive, Esports, MOBA. The kill-zone brief substantiates the exclusion.
- **Warhammer 40K space-opera** — Warhammer is an IP-locked search surface, not a tag. No direct tag exclusion follows. Indirect: "Sci-fi" plus "Space" plus "Real-Time Strategy" triangulates into the Warhammer 40K Dawn of War search space, which is a known kill-zone.
- **AAA 4X seats (Civ / Stellaris)** — exclude "4X" as a primary tag. Carrying 4X attracts comparison reviewers who measure against Civilization VI and Stellaris and find any indie title wanting. "Grand Strategy" carries a fraction of the 4X discovery without the comparison penalty.
- **Multiplayer-ladder anything** — exclude Competitive, Esports, MOBA, PvP. Also exclude Multiplayer as a primary tag (Multiplayer is fine as a secondary — Ouroboros ships per-mission co-op per WS4 — but lifting it into the primary slate signals a different game shape than the bible-locked per-mission model).

## Tag-by-tag walk through bible_monetization L288

The L288 priority-tag list contains eight tags. Per-tag analysis:

1. **Strategy** — Keep. Universal genre-anchor, primary-tribe-loadbearing. No conflict.
2. **RTS** — **Exclude per In Flux 2026-05-16.** This is the conflict line. Replace candidates: see the Tag candidates below.
3. **Sci-fi** — Keep. Foundation-tribe-loadbearing. Era-spanning concern (sci-fi tag with multi-era catalog is a mild misfit — Roman and WWII eras are not sci-fi — but the tribe-signaling value outweighs the era-purity cost). Confidence: REPORTED-conditional.
4. **Historical** — Keep. Primary-tribe-loadbearing for the CK3/EU4 subset. Also era-spanning concern (Settled Reach is not historical) but the same trade-off applies.
5. **Singleplayer** — Keep. Tribe-aligned, multiplayer-skeptic-friendly. Note: Ouroboros ships per-mission co-op, so technically not pure single-player. Tag is defensible because the macro layer is single-player always per the In Flux entry — "Singleplayer" reads correctly against the simulation-layer-is-single-player framing.
6. **Story Rich** — Keep. Cross-tribe-loadbearing.
7. **Atmospheric** — Keep. Foundation-tribe-loadbearing.
8. **Indie** — Keep. Defensible, but note: Indie tag attracts the Roguelite / Pixel-Art / Metroidvania discovery cluster that does not overlap Ouroboros' niche. The tag is a noise generator for the target tribes. Worth a separate scrutiny in T-S3 (algorithm visibility) — is Indie a positive or negative-yield tag for niche strategy in 2026?

The L288 secondary tags (Sandbox, Replay Value, Grand Strategy, Procedural Generation, Turn-Based) face one substantive scrutiny: **Turn-Based** is a misfit. Ouroboros' tactical layer is real-time per the Game Shape at a Glance section of the bible hub. Tagging Turn-Based attracts comparison reviewers who expect Civilization VI / Battle Brothers / King Arthur: Knight's Tale tactical pacing. The In Flux Lieutenant System entry has not yet resolved whether the lieutenant-pivot reshapes tactical pacing (per Q1 cost assessment), but until and unless the tactical layer becomes turn-based, the Turn-Based tag is mis-signaling. The simulation layer is batch-tick, not turn-based — "Turn-Based" does not describe the simulation layer accurately. Recommended scrutiny in the bible-side resolution: replace Turn-Based with another tag or drop without replacement.

## Cross-cutting findings

- **The L288 priority list is stale on the RTS line specifically.** Other lines are defensible; RTS is the load-bearing conflict. The bible-side update is small (one tag swap or removal) but the downstream impact is large (every brief that cites the tag slate must update).
- **The two tribes share only Sci-fi and Story Rich as cross-tribe surfaces.** All other primary-tribe tags (Grand Strategy, Historical, Singleplayer) under-pull the Foundation tribe. All other Foundation-tribe tags (Atmospheric, Space, Cyberpunk-adjacent) under-pull the primary tribe. The tag slate is a per-surface trade-off: every slot allocated to one tribe is not allocated to the other.
- **The under-fished primary-tribe tag is Generations / Generational, not Grand Strategy.** Grand Strategy is well-fished by Paradox; Ouroboros entering the Grand Strategy tag puts it in direct visibility competition with CK3 and EU4. Generations is sparser, pulls the same tribe subset specifically, and avoids the head-on Paradox comparison.
- **Era-spanning catalog creates a tag-purity trade-off.** Sci-fi and Historical both signal era-locked games; Ouroboros' multi-era catalog technically violates both. The trade-off is tribe-signaling power vs. era-purity accuracy. The bible's existing decision (L288) trades era-purity for tribe-signaling; this trade-off is defensible but worth surfacing.
- **No canonical Steam tag matches the In Flux entry's coined "operational-strategy" framing.** Marketing-surface vocabulary will need to carry the framing in capsule copy and "About this game" prose, not in tags. This is a separate concern for T-S4 (page conversion norms) and T-V1 (trailer structure norms).
- **The Indie tag is a candidate for separate scrutiny.** It is in the locked L288 list but its yield for the target tribes is unclear. Recommended that T-S3 (algorithm visibility) revisit this question with whatever 2026 algorithm data is available.
