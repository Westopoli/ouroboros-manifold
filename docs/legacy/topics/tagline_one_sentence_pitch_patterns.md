# Tagline / One-Sentence Pitch Patterns

## What this brief is

Pattern scan of one-sentence pitches and taglines used by adjacent strategy / sim titles, especially the passionate-niche success precedents and failed-RTS counter-examples surveyed in `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md`. Identifies success-pattern shapes ("X is a Y generator," "dieselpunk fleet survival," "your colony has a story"), failure-pattern shapes ("X but better," "the next StarCraft"), and stress-tests Ouroboros-bible-locked tagline candidates + Cluster-A-union-pitch-language candidates surfaced by T-A4 against those patterns.

Brief feeds: Steam page short description (the 90-character + 300-character tier descriptions Steam exposes), capsule visual + text composition, trailer opening line + closing line, festival pitch deck title slide, press release headline, creator-pitch subject line, social-post pinned-bio one-liner, paid-ad headline, Discord welcome-message hook. Cross-references T-B1 (`brand_positioning_relative.md`) capsule register pick and T-B2 (`author_voice_signalling.md`) author-voice mode pick.

**Does NOT recommend a pick.** Research Lane surfaces tagline shapes, success / failure patterns, and stress-test results. Design Lane + Westley pick the actual tagline. No "Ouroboros' tagline should be X" statements below.

## Success-pattern shapes

Six recurring tagline-shape patterns recur across the passionate-niche success cluster + primary-tribe anchors:

### Shape 1 — "X is a [Y] generator"

**Pattern.** Names the emergent-narrative property as the product. Subject = the game; verb = "is"; object = the system the player gets.

**Anchors.** RimWorld — "a story generator" (Tynan's own framing; entered the lexicon for the whole emergent-story sim genre). CK3 — "a story generator" (RPS + community-adopted framing; not Paradox's official tagline but the recognised audience-language). Dwarf Fortress — implicitly "a history generator" across press + community framing. Wildermyth — "a procedurally generated tactical RPG with myths and legends" (less crisp; community-shortens to "Wildermyth is a story generator for a fantasy campaign").

**Why it works.** Names the player's gain as a product property. Promises emergence without committing to specific content. Audience-validating — players who already have RimWorld-shape mental model immediately understand.

**Risk.** Heavy use across passionate-niche cluster means the pattern is somewhat-saturated. Ouroboros using "[Y] generator" framing competes with established RimWorld / CK3 mental-models; differentiation requires the [Y] noun to do work.

**Variants for Ouroboros stress-test (TENTATIVE):**

- "Ouroboros is a civilization generator." [Y = civilization] — strong differentiation vs RimWorld (colony) / CK3 (dynasty), neutral register (works heroic or archival), fits Cluster A + B + C intersection per T-A4.
- "Ouroboros is a history generator." [Y = history] — Foundation-register-leaning; Dwarf Fortress overlap; fits Cluster B emergent-story register; loses the "civilization-scale" specificity.
- "Ouroboros is a chronicle generator." [Y = chronicle] — strongest Foundation-register pull, aligns with bible's locked Chronicle system per `foundation_vocabulary.md`; risk: "chronicle" is jargon that requires unpacking for non-Foundation-tribe audience.
- "Ouroboros is a cycle generator." [Y = cycle] — most Foundation-register, aligns with collapse-rebuild Tier-1 run shape, novel framing; risk: high abstraction, audience might not parse "cycle" without context.

Pattern fits Ouroboros. Confidence TENTATIVE — specific [Y] noun pick is downstream of T-B1 capsule register pick. REPORTED on pattern shape.

### Shape 2 — "[Aesthetic adjective] [genre noun]" or "[adjective + noun] survival/strategy"

**Pattern.** Single visual-or-thematic adjective compresses the entire pitch. Two- or three-word capsule.

**Anchors.** HighFleet — "dieselpunk fleet survival" or "Crimson Skies plus Dune" (Koshutin's framing per training-recall; either form). Caves of Qud — "science-fantasy roguelike" + community framing "deepest procedural lore in the genre." Songs of Syx — "city-builder grand strategy with battles" (recall paraphrase). Sea Power — "Cold War naval simulation." Nebulous: Fleet Command — "real-radar fleet command."

**Why it works.** Single signature adjective compresses authorship. "Dieselpunk" = HighFleet aesthetic in one word. The audience that wants the aesthetic immediately recognises; the audience that doesn't filters out.

**Risk.** Requires Ouroboros to have a single signature adjective that compresses the project. Per T-B1: Ouroboros' positioning slot is multi-cluster intersection — no single adjective compresses cleanly. The signature adjective candidates are awkward ("sci-fi generational" / "Foundation-flavored" / "civilizational collapse" / "cycle-based") and not as crisp as "dieselpunk."

**Variants for Ouroboros stress-test (TENTATIVE):**

- "Generational sci-fi strategy" — neutral, descriptive, weak. Reads like a Steam tag list.
- "Civilizational collapse-rebuild strategy" — descriptive of mechanic, weak as adjective.
- "Foundation-coded generational strategy" — strongest secondary-tribe signal; risk: invokes Asimov IP comparison too directly (Foundation TV-show halo lift but legal-IP framing risk).
- "Hard-SF generational strategy" — register-clearer, less IP-collision-risk than "Foundation-coded"; "hard-SF" reads to Foundation tribe per `foundation_vocabulary.md` Isaac Arthur audience.
- "Cycle-based civilization strategy" — Foundation-register, novel, requires context; medium risk.

Pattern partially fits Ouroboros. The multi-cluster intersection positioning makes single-adjective compression difficult. Mode is workable but stronger Shape-1 fit. REPORTED.

### Shape 3 — "Your [X] across [time-scale]"

**Pattern.** "Your" + [stake-noun] + temporal expansion. Audience hooked by ownership + scale-promise.

**Anchors.** CK3 — "Your dynasty across centuries" (community-adopted framing; Paradox official capsule paraphrases). Wildermyth — "Your heroes become legend" (capsule + trailer-adjacent recall). BB — "Your mercenary company, your story" (recalled). Stellaris — "Your empire among the stars" (capsule paraphrase). Old World — "Your dynasty leads an empire across generations" (recall paraphrase).

**Why it works.** "Your" claims ownership; the noun specifies stake; the time-scale promises scale. Triple-load in one sentence.

**Risk.** Genre-saturated. Most Cluster A + C anchors use "Your dynasty" / "Your empire" framing. Ouroboros must differentiate the [X] noun + time-scale to avoid blending in.

**Variants for Ouroboros stress-test (TENTATIVE):**

- "Your civilization across centuries." Direct ownership claim; "civilization" differentiates from CK3 "dynasty" / Stellaris "empire" / RimWorld "colony"; "centuries" common ground with CK3 / Stellaris.
- "Your civilization across cycles." Foundation-register-substitution of "cycles" for "centuries"; aligns with collapse-rebuild; novel but parses harder.
- "Your lieutenants across centuries." Lieutenant-pivot-anchored; foregrounds Ouroboros' differentiation per `docs/bible/game_bible.md ## In Flux`; risk: "lieutenants" requires context.
- "Your civilization rises, falls, rises again." Promise-structure variant; foregrounds collapse-rebuild Tier-1 run shape; foundation-register; risk: doesn't specify scale, reader might not infer centuries.
- "Your commander, your civilization, centuries." Triple-load via comma-list; "commander" replaces "king" per bible's "commander not king" framing in `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md`; risk: telegraphic-cadence might read as fragmented.

Pattern fits Ouroboros. "Your civilization" + scale-promise is direct fit for Cluster A union per T-A4. Confidence TENTATIVE on best variant. REPORTED on pattern.

### Shape 4 — "[The world / The civilization / History] [verb]s [around / without / despite] you"

**Pattern.** Inverts agency — the world acts, the player observes / influences. Foundation-register-anchored.

**Anchors.** CK3 — community-adopted "the world keeps moving" (not Paradox official). Field of Glory: Empires — "empires actually decay" (community framing). RimWorld — "the world doesn't care" (Tynan-framing-adjacent). Caves of Qud — "the world remembers" (community + Freehold-adjacent framing per `foundation_vocabulary.md`).

**Why it works.** Inverts agency — player is one actor among many. Foundation-register-aligned. Per T-A4 + T-A1: "world keeps moving" is the single most-unifying primary-tribe phrase per multiple independent angles.

**Risk.** Less product-claim language and more thematic-claim language. Capsule conversion may underperform Shape 1 / 3 because reader does not get a clear "what is this" signal.

**Variants for Ouroboros stress-test (TENTATIVE):**

- "The civilization keeps moving." Variant of CK3-community-phrase; replaces "world" with "civilization" for differentiation; risk: subtle, may read as paraphrase.
- "History persists. You arrive, you act, you depart." Triple-verb cadence; Foundation-register; promises chronicle persistence + lieutenant transience; risk: literary cadence may be too high-register for capsule.
- "Civilizations rise and fall. You shape one cycle." Promise-cadence + role-clarification; aligns with collapse-rebuild + chronicle; risk: high abstraction.
- "The world keeps moving — your lieutenants don't outlive it." Variant that pairs unifying-phrase with lieutenant-pivot mortality; foreground stakes; risk: phrasing may be too dark for first-touch capsule.
- "Centuries pass. Names persist." Two-clause cadence; Foundation-register; chronicle-anchored; risk: more poetic than commercial.

Pattern fits Ouroboros' Foundation-register option (T-B1 archival-dominant capsule) and partial fit for heroic-dominant. Confidence TENTATIVE. REPORTED on pattern + on cross-anchor unification of "world keeps moving" phrase.

### Shape 5 — "[Anchor game X] meets [Anchor game Y]"

**Pattern.** Two-anchor-cross product framing. "Crusader Kings meets RimWorld."

**Anchors.** Old World — "Civilization meets Crusader Kings" (Mohawk Games + community framing). King Arthur: Legion IX — "Total War meets RTS-RPG" (community). Solium Infernum — "Risk meets demon politics" (recalled paraphrase). Many indie pitches use this pattern.

**Why it works.** Compresses positioning into two known mental models. Reader instantly understands genre + scope. Press loves it; festival-pitch loves it.

**Risk.** Per `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md` passionate-niche-success-pattern paragraph: "If the pitch is 'X but better,' the title dies." "X meets Y" is a softer version of "X but better" — close enough to risk being read as derivative if not handled with care. Songs of Syx, HighFleet, Caves of Qud, RimWorld all explicitly DID NOT use "X meets Y" framing — their authorship-led positioning rejected the derivative shape.

**Variants for Ouroboros stress-test (TENTATIVE):**

- "Crusader Kings meets RimWorld in space." Captures Cluster A + B + sci-fi axis; loses Wildermyth + Old World + lieutenant pivot; risk: derivative-framing per passionate-niche failure pattern.
- "Old World meets Wildermyth with named lieutenants." More accurate but reads festival-pitch-dense; primary tribe might appreciate, casual audience less.
- "Foundation meets Crusader Kings." Strongest IP-evocation; Apple TV Foundation halo lift but legal-IP framing risk (Asimov estate + Apple TV); primary tribe + Foundation tribe both immediately understand; risk: IP-collision.
- "Wildermyth at civilization scale." Single-anchor-with-scale-shift; less derivative than X-meets-Y; risk: Wildermyth audience may expect chapter-bounded format.

Pattern fits Ouroboros for festival-pitch / press-release / creator-pitch contexts where compression-to-known-mental-models matters more than authorial-identity. **Does NOT fit for capsule / authorial-voice primary-surface contexts** — derivative-framing failure pattern is the warning. REPORTED.

### Shape 6 — "[Action verb] [object]" imperative

**Pattern.** "Forge an empire," "Build a kingdom," "Command a fleet." Imperative-mood single-clause.

**Anchors.** CK3 (heroic-register variant) — "Forge a medieval dynasty." Old World — "Lead a dynasty through history." Stellaris — "Explore the stars." Many AAA + mid-tier strategy use this. Per `foundation_vocabulary.md`: imperative-mood is heroic-register-defining.

**Why it works.** Direct call-to-action. Heroic-register-anchored. Primary-tribe heroic-register-tolerant audience accepts.

**Risk.** Per T-B1 + T-A4: imperative heroic-register signals "yet another empire-builder" to Foundation tribe; misses secondary-tribe signal. Per `foundation_vocabulary.md`: "Heroic achievement names will read as 'yet another empire-builder' to the Foundation tribe regardless of how Foundation-coded the rest of the game is." Same applies to imperative-mood taglines.

**Variants for Ouroboros stress-test (TENTATIVE):**

- "Forge a civilization across centuries." Heroic-register; primary-tribe-tolerant; loses Foundation-tribe signal; loses lieutenant differentiation.
- "Command a civilization." Heroic-register; "commander not king" framing-adjacent per `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md`; brief; weak on time-scale.
- "Shape a civilization. Outlive your lieutenants." Two-clause imperative + paradox; promises lieutenant mortality + civilization persistence; risk: brief but loaded.
- "Build, lose, rebuild." Three-verb cadence; promises collapse-rebuild shape; novel; risk: doesn't specify scale or stake.

Pattern fits Ouroboros for heroic-register-dominant capsule (T-B1 Option A). Does NOT fit for archival-register-dominant capsule (T-B1 Option B). Confidence TENTATIVE. REPORTED on pattern + heroic-register collision warning.

## Failure-pattern shapes

Three failure patterns recur across the failed-RTS cluster + cross-genre fusion failures:

### Failure 1 — "X but better"

**Pattern.** Explicit improve-on-incumbent framing. "The new StarCraft," "the next CK3."

**Failure cases.** Stormgate — community + press framing of "the new StarCraft 2 / Warcraft 3 by ex-Blizzard devs" (per training-recall) underperformed; per `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md`: dropped from 4,854 peak CCU to ~24 CCU. Tempest Rising — community + press "the new Command & Conquer" framing; collapsed from 9,400 peak to ~187 CCU. Homeworld 3 — "Homeworld 1 but modern" implicit framing; dropped to ~16 CCU; roadmap cancelled. Per `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md`: "If the pitch is 'X but better,' the title dies."

**Why it fails.** Audience compares directly to incumbent; switching cost (per `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md` failed-RTS-mechanism-4 incumbent-stickiness paragraph) is too high. The incumbent audience does not want a new X; they want their X with more of it.

**Risk for Ouroboros.** "CK3 but in space" / "RimWorld at civilization scale" / "Wildermyth but longer" / "Stellaris but human-scale" — all close-to-failure-pattern. Per T-B1: Ouroboros' positioning slot is adjacent-not-cloning to all six primary-tribe anchors simultaneously. "X but better" framing is exactly the framing T-B1 surfaces as wrong-for-positioning. **AVOID.**

REPORTED with high confidence (failed-RTS pattern is documented in `rts_market_kill_zone_2026.md` + `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md`).

### Failure 2 — "the next [genre]"

**Pattern.** Generic genre-revival framing. "The next great RTS," "the next 4X."

**Failure cases.** ZeroSpace — community + press "the next StarCraft for a new generation" framing; dropped to ~0-1 CCU per `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md`. Immortals: Gates of Pyre — "the next RTS" framing; collapsed similarly per same source. Per `rts_market_kill_zone_2026.md`: this framing signals "another entry into a dying genre" to genre-fatigued audience.

**Why it fails.** Audience reads "the next X" as "an entry, not the differentiator." Genre-fatigue audiences specifically tune out "the next X" because they have heard it before and were burned.

**Risk for Ouroboros.** "The next great grand strategy game" / "the next CK3-killer" — close-to-failure-pattern. Per T-B1: adjacent-not-cloning positioning explicitly rejects this framing.

**AVOID.** REPORTED.

### Failure 3 — "An ambitious indie [genre] with [vague differentiator]"

**Pattern.** Self-aware ambition-flagging + vague-differentiator. "An ambitious indie 4X with deep diplomacy." "A hardcore indie strategy game with emergent stories."

**Failure cases.** Difficult to pin specific failures by tagline alone, but per `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md` passionate-niche success-pattern paragraph #2: "the pitch is one sentence and incompatible with anything else on the market" — successful pitches are specific + uncompromising. "Ambitious indie" framing signals lack of confidence in single-sentence specificity. Pitch-language inversion: confident pitches make claims, ambitious-framing makes apologies.

**Risk for Ouroboros.** "An ambitious indie generational strategy game with named lieutenants and Foundation-flavored sci-fi" — close-to-failure-pattern. Reader sees "ambitious indie" + "with X" + "with Y" as list-of-features rather than single-confident-claim.

**AVOID.** REPORTED.

## Per-surface implementation cascade

Tagline pick cascades differently per surface:

- **Steam page 90-char short description.** Strict character cap forces compression. Primary surface; conversion-critical. Shape 1 / 3 / 4 fit; Shape 2 / 5 / 6 conditional. Per T-A4 + T-S1: short description must include genre-tag-keywords (Grand Strategy + Sci-fi + Strategy + Generations) implicit or explicit for Steam discovery.
- **Steam page about-the-game opening line.** First-paragraph hook; less strict cap. Any successful shape works. Cascade from short-description language.
- **Trailer opening line + closing line.** Read out loud — cadence + memorability matter more than character count. Shape 4 inversion ("Centuries pass. Names persist.") reads well; Shape 1 generator-framing reads less well aloud.
- **Festival pitch deck title slide.** Compression + immediate-recognizability matter. Shape 5 X-meets-Y tempts here (festival audiences appreciate compression) but Failure 1 / 2 / 3 patterns are risks. Shape 2 single-adjective compression works if signature adjective exists.
- **Press release headline.** Format: "Ouroboros — [tagline] — launches [date]." Tagline doubles as press-shorthand. Shape 2 / 3 / 4 fit; Shape 5 fits if press will adopt the comparison (risky).
- **Creator-pitch subject line.** Per T-A4 + T-C1: Quill18 / Splattercat / SsethTzeentach pitches benefit from a single-line product-claim. Shape 1 generator-framing + Shape 3 "your civilization" + Shape 4 "world keeps moving" all fit creator-pitch context.
- **Discord welcome-message hook + pinned-bio.** Casual + community-facing. Any shape works. Less conversion-load.
- **Paid-ad headline (Reddit / Meta / YouTube ads).** Per `paid_ad_channels_roi.md` (T-C7): Reddit Promoted is the highest-leverage paid surface for primary tribe. Conversion-critical; per T-A4 + T-A1: primary-tribe ads benefit from "world keeps moving" / "your civilization" / "story generator" Shape 1 + 3 + 4 framings; banned-bundle keywords (RTS / Action / Free-to-play / Multiplayer) must NOT appear.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **Shape 1 ("[Y] generator") is the highest-leverage success pattern for emergent-narrative strategy.** RimWorld + CK3 + Dwarf Fortress all use this framing in community-adopted form. Ouroboros' [Y] noun choice (civilization / history / chronicle / cycle) is downstream of T-B1 capsule register pick. **High recommended-shape — but Research Lane does not pick.** REPORTED.

2. **Shape 3 ("Your [X] across [time-scale]") is the most genre-saturated success pattern.** CK3 + Wildermyth + BB + Stellaris + Old World all use variants. Ouroboros differentiation depends on the [X] noun (civilization differentiates from dynasty / colony / empire) + time-scale (centuries common, cycles novel). REPORTED.

3. **Shape 4 ("[World/civilization] [verb]s [around/without] you") is the Foundation-tribe-aligned success pattern + cross-cluster-union pattern.** Per T-A4 cross-cutting-finding #5: "world keeps moving" unifies all six primary-tribe-relevant anchors. **Highest cross-tribe-leverage shape.** REPORTED.

4. **Shape 5 ("X meets Y") works for press + festival contexts but fails for capsule + authorial-voice contexts.** Surface-specific shape; not the headline tagline. Per failure-pattern-1 X-but-better proximity: use with care. REPORTED.

5. **Shape 6 ("[verb] [object]" imperative) is heroic-register-anchored and signals empire-builder genre to Foundation tribe.** Per `foundation_vocabulary.md` cross-cutting-finding #2: heroic register signals "yet another empire-builder" to Foundation tribe. If Ouroboros wants Foundation-tribe signal in primary tagline, Shape 6 is wrong-mode. REPORTED.

6. **Three failure patterns are explicit AVOID.** "X but better" (Stormgate / Tempest Rising / Homeworld 3 failure mode). "The next [genre]" (ZeroSpace / Immortals failure mode). "An ambitious indie [genre] with [vague differentiator]" (apology-framing failure mode). REPORTED with high confidence.

7. **Tagline length matters per-surface.** 90-char Steam short description is the strict cap. 30-50 character compression for ad headlines. 5-10 word festival title-slide. 1-2 sentence press-release tagline. Single shape variant typically only fits 2-3 of these length contexts — multiple tagline-variants will exist per surface. REPORTED.

8. **The Cluster-A-union "world keeps moving" phrase is the single most cross-validated tagline anchor.** Confirmed by T-A1 (review themes), T-A4 (tag + creator + curator overlap), `foundation_vocabulary.md` (archival register fit), `lieutenant_pivot_niche_brief.md` (rare-mechanic paragraph). Multiple independent angles converge on this phrase as Ouroboros' highest-leverage anchor language. REPORTED with high cross-source confidence.
