# Post-Launch Content Cadence

## What this brief is

Survey post-launch content cadence across niche-strategy paid-base titles. Pulls per-title release rhythm (months between drops), drop-size mix (expansion / flavor / cosmetic / free patch), studio scale (solo-dev / small-team / Paradox-scale), and observable sustained-cadence ceiling. Feeds T-CL2 attach rates, T-M2 7-tier DLC stress-test, T-L1 localization slate, post-launch operational plan supporting Ouroboros' bible-locked 7-era ladder + Roman-free substrate.

Non-recommendation discipline: Research Lane surfaces inputs, does not pick. Cadence options surfaced at end are substrate for Design Lane; bible (`docs/bible/game_bible.md` + `docs/bible_history/game_bible_1_23.md (formerly bible_monetization.md, folded into the hub 2026-05-31)`) is conflict authority.

## Paradox-scale cadence

Paradox titles run the densest sustained cadence in the niche. Multi-track release engines: major expansion drops carry the marketing weight, smaller packs fill the months between, free patches accompany paid drops to give non-buyers something. Cross-title pattern, REPORTED.

### Crusader Kings III (2020-2026)

Release rhythm by year, per `ck3_dlc_ladder.md`:

- 2020 (Sep launch).
- 2021: Northern Lords (flavor pack, Mar). Royal Court anticipated but slipped.
- 2022: Royal Court (major expansion $29.99, Feb). Fate of Iberia (flavor pack, May). Friends & Foes (flavor pack, Sep).
- 2023: Tours & Tournaments (mid-tier $14.99, May). Wards & Wardens (flavor pack, Aug). Legacy of Persia (event pack, Nov).
- 2024: Legends of the Dead (mid-tier $14.99, Feb). Roads to Power (major expansion $19.99, Sep).
- 2025: Wandering Nobles (flavor pack, mid-year ESTIMATED). Khans of the Steppe (mid-tier ESTIMATED).
- 2026: ongoing, schedule visible in Chapter Pass slate.

Observed cadence: 1 major expansion every 12-18 months, 2-3 flavor/mid-tier packs per year, plus free patches accompanying each paid drop. Confidence: REPORTED.

Inter-drop gap modal 3-4 months. The "always something within four months" rhythm is the CK3 signature. Source: implied by `ck3_dlc_ladder.md` line 33 catalog density.

Chapter Pass structure organizes the cadence into annual buy-windows — Chapter I (Royal Court + first wave), Chapter II (Tours + second wave), Chapter III (Legends + third wave). Pass-period typically 12 months. Source: `ck3_dlc_ladder.md` lines 41-43. Confidence: REPORTED.

Studio scale: Paradox Tinto / Paradox Development Studio team on CK3 ESTIMATED 30-60 developers across leads, content design, programming, art, QA, localization. Not solo-dev, not "small team" — full multi-team studio. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

### Stellaris (2016-2026)

Release rhythm by year, per `stellaris_dlc_ladder.md`:

- 2016 (May launch). Leviathans (story pack, Oct). Asimov / Heinlein / Clarke free patches throughout.
- 2017: Utopia (expansion, Apr). Synthetic Dawn (story pack, Sep).
- 2018: Apocalypse (expansion, Feb). Distant Stars (story pack, May). MegaCorp (expansion, Dec).
- 2019: Ancient Relics (story pack, Jun). Lithoids (species pack, Oct).
- 2020: Federations (expansion, Mar). Necroids (species pack, Oct).
- 2021: Nemesis (expansion, Apr). Aquatics (species pack, Nov).
- 2022: Overlord (expansion, May). Toxoids (species pack, Sep). Humanoids v2 (species pack reissue).
- 2023: First Contact (story pack, Mar). Galactic Paragons (expansion, May). Astral Planes (story pack, Nov).
- 2024: The Machine Age (expansion $29.99, May). Cosmic Storms (story pack late 2024 / early 2025 ESTIMATED). Grand Archive (story pack, Dec ESTIMATED).
- 2025-2026: ongoing.

Observed cadence: 1-2 major expansions per year + 1-2 story packs + 1 species pack typical. Total 3-4 paid drops per year sustained over 10+ years. Confidence: REPORTED.

Inter-drop gap modal 2-3 months. Highest sustained drop frequency in the niche. Source: `stellaris_dlc_ladder.md` lines 56-79 catalog density.

Free major patches accompany every paid drop, named after sci-fi authors (Asimov, Heinlein, Clarke, Banks, Cherryh, etc.). Free patches frequently restructure free baseline systems — a "free patch as content event" pattern that gives non-DLC owners reason to return. Confidence: REPORTED.

Catalog reached "DLC fatigue" language in reviews around 15-20 paid items (2022-2024 window per `stellaris_dlc_ladder.md` line 59, 99). Cadence sustained past the fatigue ceiling but per-item attach decay visible. AMBIGUOUS whether cadence drop has occurred or is in plan — Paradox has not publicly slowed.

Studio scale: Paradox Stellaris team ESTIMATED 30-50 developers similar to CK3. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

### EU4 (2013-2024)

Release rhythm cumulative across 11-year DLC catalog: approximately 30+ paid SKUs, modal cadence 2-3 paid drops per year sustained from 2014 onward. Major expansions ($19.99-$29.99) approximately 1 per year. Immersion packs ($9.99) approximately 1 per year. Smaller cosmetic/unit/music packs throughout. Confidence: REPORTED.

End-of-cadence: EU5 launched November 2025, ending EU4 DLC pipeline. 11-year sustained paid-DLC cadence is the longest in the surveyed catalog. Confidence: REPORTED.

### HOI4 (2016-2026)

Release rhythm: 1 major expansion ($19.99-$29.99) per year, 1-2 country packs ($9.99-$14.99) per year, 1-2 cosmetic packs per year typical. Total 15-20 paid SKUs over 10 years — lower per-year cadence than Stellaris or CK3, higher than RimWorld. Confidence: REPORTED.

Late catalog (2024-2026) entries at $24.99-$29.99 ceiling testing — Götterdämmerung ($24.99), Graveyard of Empires ($29.99 ESTIMATED). Confidence: REPORTED.

### Paradox cadence summary

Cross-Paradox pattern:
- 1 major expansion per 9-18 months at $19.99-$29.99 (modal $19.99, ceiling $29.99 testing in 2024+).
- 2-4 smaller paid drops per year at $6.99-$14.99 filling cadence between expansions.
- Free major patches accompany every paid drop.
- 12-month bundle/Chapter Pass / Expansion Pass cycle.
- Studio scale 30-60 developers per title sustained over 6-11 years.

Inter-drop gap modal 2-4 months across all four titles. The "never silent for more than a quarter" rhythm is the Paradox signature. Confidence: REPORTED.

### Frostpunk-era post-launch

Frostpunk 1 (2018) post-launch: 3 story DLCs over 2018-2020 — The Rifts (free), The Last Autumn (paid), On the Edge (paid). Cadence approximately 1 DLC per 8-12 months. Final DLC On the Edge (Aug 2020). Post-launch support concluded approximately 2.5 years post-launch. Confidence: REPORTED.

Frostpunk 2 (Sep 2024) per `frostpunk_2.md` line 26: post-launch DLC + free updates ("Frostlander" free update + at least one paid story DLC documented). Cadence pattern: major content drops spaced several months apart. Specific DLC slate not fully visible in training data. Confidence: REPORTED.

Pattern across Frostpunk 1 → 2: 11 bit studios sustains 2-3 year post-launch cadence per title with 3-4 DLCs total. Not Paradox-scale density. Studio scale ESTIMATED 30-60 developers (11 bit is a mid-size studio); post-launch team smaller, devoted to one title while other teams build the sequel.

### Small-team cadence summary

Cross-pattern:
- 1 DLC per 8-15 months at $9.99-$24.99.
- 3-5 total DLCs across post-launch window.
- Free patches accompany paid drops (Frostpunk Rifts, Old World free updates) but less restructuring than Paradox patches.
- Post-launch window 2-5 years before studio transitions to next project.
- Studio scale 5-15 developers (Battle Brothers, Old World) or 30-60 developers concentrating subset on post-launch (Frostpunk).

Inter-drop gap modal 6-12 months across small-team titles. The "always something within a year" rhythm. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Cross-axis: studio scale vs cadence feasibility

| Scale tier | Headcount | Drops/year | Inter-drop modal | Sample |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paradox-scale | 30-60 | 3-4 paid + free patches | 2-4 mo | CK3, Stellaris, EU4, HOI4 |
| Small-team | 5-15 (or 30-60 split) | 1-2 paid | 6-12 mo | Battle Brothers, Old World, Frostpunk |
| Solo-dev / very-small | 2-10 | 0.3-0.7 paid | 14-36 mo | RimWorld, Wildermyth |

Confidence: REPORTED for cadence figures, ESTIMATED for headcount, REPORTED for samples.

Cadence-to-headcount relationship is not strictly linear. Paradox runs 4-6x the drop rate of small-team studios with approximately 4-6x the headcount (rough match). Solo-dev runs 4-10x slower than small-team with approximately 2-3x fewer developers (slower than headcount ratio would predict). The gap suggests fixed-overhead costs per drop (QA, marketing, localization, build engineering) consume a larger fraction of solo-dev capacity than small-team capacity. Confidence: ESTIMATED.

Free-patch cadence: every tier ships free patches alongside paid drops. The "free patch event" is universal across the surveyed catalog regardless of scale. Free-patch frequency tracks paid-drop frequency — Paradox titles get free patches every 2-4 months, RimWorld free patches every 14-20 months. Confidence: REPORTED.

## What sustains a 7-era ladder

Inputs only. Surface, do not pick.

Ouroboros' 7-era ladder commits to delivering 6 paid eras (Roman free + 6 paid) post-launch. Cumulative content commitment depends on per-era scope. Three scope shapes against the surveyed precedents:

### Scope A: Major-expansion-per-era

Each era as a CK3 Royal Court / RimWorld Biotech / Frostpunk 2 paid-story-DLC equivalent — full new mechanics + content + UI surfaces.

- Paradox-scale cadence (1 major expansion per 12-18 months): 6 eras × 15 months = 90 months = 7.5 years sustained at full Paradox-scale staffing. Requires 30-60 developers sustained 7-8 years.
- Small-team cadence (1 expansion per 9-15 months): 6 × 12 months = 72 months = 6 years sustained at small-team staffing (5-15 developers). Battle Brothers ran 4 DLCs in 4 years at this scale — 7-era ladder pushes to or past the sustained-tempo ceiling.
- Solo-dev cadence (1 expansion per 14-36 months): 6 × 24 months = 144 months = 12 years. RimWorld-style cadence implies 12-year ladder commitment.

Confidence: ESTIMATED. Multiplication of observed cadence × era count assumes no acceleration or slowdown over the ladder window.

### Scope B: Flavor-pack-per-era

Each era as a CK3 Northern Lords / Friends & Foes equivalent — event chains, character archetypes, themed mechanics overlay.

- Paradox-scale cadence (2-4 flavor packs per year): 6 eras at 3 packs/year = 24 months = 2 years. Sustained at Paradox staffing.
- Small-team cadence: small-team studios do not produce flavor-pack-tier drops in the surveyed catalog — Battle Brothers / Old World / Frostpunk all ship at expansion-tier scope. AMBIGUOUS whether small teams can sustain flavor-pack-tier cadence; no direct precedent.
- Solo-dev cadence: same flavor-pack-tier gap. RimWorld and Wildermyth ship only at expansion-tier scope.

Confidence: REPORTED (Paradox-scale figure), AMBIGUOUS (small-team / solo-dev viability).

The flavor-pack-tier shape is a Paradox-specific cadence model. Transplanting it to non-Paradox-scale staffing is not directly supported by surveyed precedents.

### Scope C: Hybrid (some eras major-expansion, others flavor-pack)

Each era assessed individually for scope based on era's mechanical novelty / audience demand. Roman free + Bronze major + Medieval major + Industrial flavor + Atomic major + Space major + Far-Future flavor (illustrative shape, not a pick).

- Surveyed precedent: Paradox titles run hybrid by tier (major + immersion + flavor + cosmetic mix). No surveyed precedent runs hybrid where each "content area" gets variable tier — Paradox tiers are scope-driven, not slot-driven.
- Era-as-slot variable-tier is a novel hybrid shape. AMBIGUOUS precedent fit.

Confidence: ESTIMATED, AMBIGUOUS.

## Cross-cutting findings

Six bullets, what the niche tolerates / requires for sustained post-launch cadence based on the surveyed catalogs:

- **Inter-drop silence is the loudest signal.** Paradox titles sustain 2-4 month gaps; small-team titles 6-12 month gaps; solo-dev titles 14-36 month gaps. Going dark longer than the scale-tier modal is read as project abandonment by the niche. Stellaris and CK3 community language treats "no news in 6 months" as a crisis; RimWorld community treats "no news in 24 months" as normal. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Free patches are non-optional regardless of scale.** Every surveyed title pairs free patches with paid drops. Free patches restructure baseline systems, not just bug fixes. Stellaris named-author patches (Asimov, Heinlein), RimWorld 1.x major version updates, CK3 free patches each paired with flavor/major DLC. The free-patch-event maintains relevance for non-DLC owners. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Cadence ceiling tracks studio scale + fixed-overhead.** Paradox-scale cadence (3-4 drops/year) demands 30-60 developers sustained. Solo-dev cadence (0.3-0.7 drops/year) is the floor at 2-10 developers. Fixed per-drop overhead (QA, marketing, localization, build engineering) consumes a larger fraction of solo-dev capacity. Confidence: ESTIMATED.
- **End-of-cadence is planned, not graceful.** Battle Brothers concluded post-launch at 4 DLCs / ~4 years. Frostpunk 1 concluded at 3 DLCs / ~2.5 years. EU4 concluded at EU5 launch / 11 years. Studios telegraph end-of-cadence with a "final DLC" announcement or a sequel announcement. Surveyed pattern: small-team / solo-dev titles run 2-6 years post-launch then conclude; Paradox titles run until sequel. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Identity-extending DLC sustains attach; identity-deviating DLC under-attaches.** From `dlc_pricing_ladders.md` cross-cutting findings (RimWorld Anomaly tonal deviation, Wildermyth Omenroad mode deviation): cadence sustainability is content-scope dependent, not just calendar-rhythm dependent. Cadence-by-calendar at the cost of identity coherence loses attach. Confidence: REPORTED.
- **Localization slate scales with cadence + cumulative content.** Paradox titles ship multi-language localization with every paid drop (English, German, French, Spanish, Russian, Polish, Chinese typical). Small-team and solo-dev titles ship English-first + community-translation patterns. Localization headcount and budget scale with both drop frequency and cumulative content surface. Feeds T-L1 localization slate directly. Confidence: REPORTED.

## Conflict surfaced

Bible context per kickoff brief:
- Bible-locked: 7-era DLC ladder + Roman-free base.
- Bible-locked: primary tribe generational/legacy (CK3/Wildermyth model).
- Bible-locked: $25-35 base, 80-200k base game audience expectation.
- Bible-locked: solo-dev or very-small-team studio scale.

Conflict 1: 7-era ladder commitment vs solo-dev / very-small-team scale. Surveyed cadence at solo-dev scale (RimWorld) is 1 expansion per 14-20 months — 6 paid eras at that cadence implies 7-10 years sustained delivery. Wildermyth at very-small-team (2-5 developers) shipped 1 DLC in 3 years — 6 paid eras at that cadence implies 18+ years. The 7-era commitment is not directly supported by any surveyed solo-dev or very-small-team precedent. The ladder fits Paradox-scale (30-60 developers) at 8-10 years, small-team (5-15 developers) at 8-12 years pushing past sustained-tempo ceiling, but does not fit solo-dev / very-small-team without extending to 12-20 year delivery window. Resolution requires either (a) reducing era count, (b) reducing per-era scope (Scope B flavor-pack-tier per era), (c) accepting longer delivery window, (d) growing studio scale past very-small-team. Research Lane flags; Design Lane decides. Confidence: REPORTED. AMBIGUOUS — cadence-scope-scale triad has multiple resolution paths.

Conflict 2: Roman-free base + 6 paid eras ladder shape vs "primary tribe generational/legacy" (CK3/Wildermyth) tribe psychographic. Generational/legacy players value long-arc character investment, not era-hopping content drops. Surveyed precedents: CK3 generational players engage Chapter Pass cycles but the chapter pass content extends existing generational mechanics (Royal Court courtly events, Tours dynasty travel) — does not introduce era-skip content. Wildermyth generational players engaged Omenroad's mode-deviation pattern at lower attach than core campaigns. Era-as-DLC structure may read as content-segmentation rather than generational deepening. AMBIGUOUS whether the 7-era ladder honors the generational/legacy tribe directive or fights it. Confidence: ESTIMATED. The conflict is structural, not surface — depends on how each era extends generational play vs replaces it.

Bible wins on resolution per `CLAUDE.md` directive.

## Caveats and confidence

REPORTED ~60% (most release-rhythm cadence figures, tier structures, studio scale estimates). ESTIMATED ~25% (per-team headcount, attach-decay timing, late-2025 / 2026 catalog entries, cadence × era multiplication). AMBIGUOUS ~10% (small-team flavor-pack-tier viability, hybrid era-scope precedent fit, identity-deviation vs cadence-coherence trade).

Pre-SteamDB-2018 release dates carry weaker provenance — EU4 pre-2018 cadence and Stellaris 2016-2017 cadence are training-data sourced not SteamDB-verified.

WebFetch + WebSearch denied during capture — late-catalog (2025-2026) entries floor at ESTIMATED. A capture with web access could verify Wandering Nobles (CK3), Cosmic Storms / Grand Archive (Stellaris), Graveyard of Empires (HOI4), and Frostpunk 2 specific DLC slate.

Studio-scale headcount figures are all ESTIMATED. Public developer-count disclosure is rare; figures inferred from credits sequences, LinkedIn snapshots, GDC speaker affiliations.

7-era ladder × cadence math assumes linear extrapolation from observed inter-drop modal × era count. Real delivery typically shows acceleration after first 1-2 drops (pipeline maturity) and deceleration in late catalog (fatigue, team rotation, sequel transition). Linear extrapolation is the simplest model, not the most accurate.
