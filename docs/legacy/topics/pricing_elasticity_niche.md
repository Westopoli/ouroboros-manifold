Caveats:

- These are aggregate GameDiscoverCo-style benchmarks across genres + adjusted ESTIMATED-only for niche-strategy. Niche-strategy may run somewhat above general indie benchmarks per audience-loyalty proxies (Paradox audience converts strongly; Wildermyth converted strongly). Direction is plausibly: niche-strategy conversion at any given price is 1-3 percentage points higher than general-indie benchmark. ESTIMATED.
- Wishlist conversion is also a function of marketing momentum, review quality at launch, launch-discount %, and Steam algorithmic placement. Price is one variable; not the only one.
- "First-week revenue" is publisher-meaningful number — wishlist conversion drops sharply after day 30 as Steam algorithm de-prioritizes title. GameDiscoverCo Simon Carless analyses consistently show 30-day conversion approximately 2× the 7-day conversion for healthy niche titles. REPORTED.

**Per-tier shift across niche anchors ($24.99 / $29.99 / $34.99 / $39.99):**

- **$24.99 to $29.99:** approximately 5-8 percentage point conversion drop (e.g., 25% → 18%). The $25 → $30 jump is the "casual buyer hesitation" boundary for many niche audiences. ESTIMATED.
- **$29.99 to $34.99:** approximately 3-5 percentage point drop. Smaller relative impact because the buyer who tolerated $30 generally tolerates $35. ESTIMATED.
- **$34.99 to $39.99:** approximately 2-4 percentage point drop. By $40, the buyer has self-selected into premium-tolerance. ESTIMATED.
- **$39.99 to $44.99:** approximately 2-3 percentage point drop. Smallest gradient — above $40 selects for committed buyers nearly insensitive to small-percent increases. ESTIMATED.

**Inflection point.** Niche-strategy wishlist conversion has its steepest elasticity gradient between $24.99 and $34.99. The $29.99 modal price sits inside this gradient as a defensible compromise: above the cheap-impulse boundary, below the premium-resistance boundary. REPORTED-leaning per GameDiscoverCo trend commentary.

**Within-tribe wishlist conversion premium.** Anecdotal but consistent — Paradox-style highly-loyal audiences ship with 25-35% 7-day conversion at $39.99 (Stellaris launch, EU4 expansions). RimWorld converted at approximately 30-40% at $34.99 launch (Tynan Sylvester disclosure-adjacent). Strategy-audience loyalty premium over general-indie estimated +5-15 percentage points at same price. ESTIMATED-REPORTED hybrid.

**Real residual price-tier effect:** approximately 3-8 percentage points of review-score softening per $5 increase across the niche $25-45 band, controlling for the obvious confounds. ESTIMATED. The mechanism is that $40+ buyers expect AAA-level polish + content depth and niche titles at that price face "is this really $40 worth" complaints disproportionately. REPORTED.

## "Too expensive" threshold

Steam review complaints crystallizing around "you charged too much" as a recurring theme in niche-strategy:

- **<$29.99:** rare. "Too expensive" complaints essentially absent for $24.99-$29.99 launches. REPORTED.
- **$29.99:** small but present. Songs of Syx + HighFleet have <2% of reviews containing price complaint, usually paired w/ "but I bought it anyway." REPORTED. Tolerated.
- **$34.99:** present + visible. RimWorld + Wartales have 3-7% of reviews mentioning price, mostly "got it on sale" framing. REPORTED. Conditional tolerance — wait-for-sale becomes common pattern.
- **$39.99:** crystallized + structural. FoG:E + Sea Power + Manor Lords have 8-15% of reviews mentioning price, often as direct complaint. REPORTED. "This should be $30" emerges as recurring phrase.
- **$44.99+:** dominant complaint theme. Frostpunk 2 had launch-window review-bomb cluster around "should not be this price." REPORTED. "AAA price for indie scope" emerges. Recovery via patches + content updates possible (Frostpunk 2 recovered review score 7+ percentage points over 6 months).

**Threshold crystallization at $39.99.** The "$40 wall" is the structural niche-strategy ceiling where buyers begin scoring price as a primary review-decision input. Below $40, price is a secondary input; above $40, price is primary. REPORTED-leaning across GameDiscoverCo aggregate analyses + per-title review-theme scans.

**Counter-pattern: CK3 at $49.99.** CK3 launched above the "$40 wall" w/ 89% positive review score. Why didn't the wall hit? (a) Paradox brand premium — buyer base accepted Paradox-pricing baseline, (b) genuine AAA scope at launch, (c) wishlist conversion was strong despite higher price because audience loyalty premium absorbed the elasticity. CK3 is the outlier proving the wall is breakable by extreme brand strength + audience commitment. Most titles can't replicate. REPORTED.

## Primary vs Foundation tribe price tolerance

Cross-ref `docs/research/topics/tribe_demographics.md` income proxies + `docs/research/topics/primary_tribe_psychographic_profile.md` per-title patterns.

**Primary tribe (generational/legacy strategy — CK3 / Wildermyth / BB / FoG:E intersection):**

- Median age 30-42, professional income proxy. tribe_demographics.md.
- Hardware tier mid-range; reflects buyer who doesn't upgrade aggressively but maintains expectation of $30-40 single-purchase as reasonable.
- DLC tolerance present per Paradox + Battle Brothers DLC precedents — primary tribe pays $10-15 DLC ladders if base game earns trust.
- Cross-purchase patterns show this audience owns multiple $30-40 strategy games; sticker shock low.
- **Estimated price-tolerance band: $25-39.99 buy-once base, $9.99-14.99 per major DLC.** ESTIMATED.
- "Wait for sale" pattern strong above $35, weak below $30. REPORTED.
- "Refund and re-buy on sale" pattern present but small. ESTIMATED.

**Foundation tribe (Foundation / literary hard-SF readers — secondary tribe per lieutenant_pivot brief):**

- Median age 35-48, mid-to-upper professional income proxy. tribe_demographics.md.
- Older, higher disposable income skew vs primary tribe.
- Book-buying culture — pays $20-30 for hardcover routinely; transfers to game purchases as reasonable price baseline.
- TV-show-driven recent-attention spike (Apple TV Foundation) brought non-gaming-traditional buyers in; their price tolerance unknown but probably book-buyer-shaped.
- **Estimated price-tolerance band: $29.99-44.99 buy-once base, premium tolerance for authorial-voice titles.** ESTIMATED.
- "Wait for sale" pattern weaker than primary tribe — Foundation-tribe buyer pays for authorial product because they wanted the specific thing, not the genre. ESTIMATED.
- Smaller sample; AMBIGUOUS for many specifics.

**Delta:** Foundation tribe tolerates +$5 to +$10 vs primary tribe at same scope of game. The thematic-differentiator audience pays for the specific Foundation-flavored content; lower price-elasticity. ESTIMATED.

**Implication for surfaced-not-picked positioning:** if Ouroboros prices to widest reach within primary tribe (e.g., $29.99), Foundation tribe is fully captured + would have paid more. If prices to Foundation tribe's center (e.g., $34.99), gives up 5-8 percentage points of primary-tribe wishlist conversion. The two tribes do not require different prices to co-capture; primary-tribe price floor is the binding constraint. REPORTED.

## Ouroboros $25-35 band placement

Bible-locked target: 80-200k units @ $25-35 paid-base + DLC-per-era (7 eras, Roman free-tier).

**Placement on elasticity curve:**

- **$25-35 = bible-locked band.** Brackets niche-strategy modal ($29.99) and steady $34.99 cluster.
- **$24.99 floor of band:** sits just below the modal cluster, on the cheap-impulse boundary. Would maximize wishlist conversion (+5-8 percentage points vs $29.99). Tradeoffs: leaves $5/unit revenue on the table; reduces audience self-selection (more refund / low-investment buyers); cedes Paradox-adjacent positioning (Paradox audience reads $24.99 as "small game"); flags as "not as serious as CK3/Stellaris."
- **$29.99 modal:** sits at the niche-strategy median. Strongest defensible position. Matches Old World, Wildermyth-current, Battle-Brothers-original, Caves of Qud, Songs of Syx, Dwarf Fortress, Old World. Audience reads as "real niche-strategy product, not throwaway, not AAA." REPORTED.
- **$34.99 upper-modal:** sits at the RimWorld / Wartales / Workers & Resources cluster. Reads as "premium niche-strategy, generational depth." Captures Foundation-tribe price tolerance comfortably. Tradeoff: -3-5 percentage points wishlist conversion vs $29.99; "wait for sale" pattern accelerates above $30.
- **$35 ceiling of band:** sits just below the $39.99 cluster where the "too expensive" threshold begins crystallizing. Defensible if scope + polish are clearly $35-tier. ESTIMATED.

**Where the wishlist-conversion peak sits:** the elasticity curve peaks at the cheap-impulse end ($14.99-$19.99) where conversion runs 35-50%. The bible-locked $25-35 band sits in the steepest-decline zone (15-22% conversion at $29.99 → 10-17% at $34.99). The conversion peak is below the band. REPORTED-leaning.

**But ARPU peak is different.** ARPU = price × conversion. At $14.99 × 45% = $6.75/wishlist. At $29.99 × 18% = $5.40/wishlist. At $34.99 × 13% = $4.55/wishlist. ESTIMATED throughout.

ARPU/wishlist appears to peak around $19.99-$24.99 in pure-conversion-math terms. BUT: those tiers also have higher refund rate, lower review-score median for niche-strategy, smaller DLC-attach rate, and weaker audience commitment for long-tail revenue. The bible's DLC-per-era model assumes a committed buyer base; that committed base is built at $29.99+ not $19.99. The full-lifetime ARPU (base + DLC + long-tail sales) is plausibly highest at $29.99 for this audience. ESTIMATED.

**What is gained / lost moving to $24.99 vs $34.99:**

- **$24.99 move (toward cheap end of band):** gain approximately +25-40% wishlist conversion vs $29.99 baseline. Lose approximately $5/unit base revenue. Lose Paradox-adjacent "real strategy product" positioning. Pick up some Wildermyth-shaped audience that bounces above $25. Refund rate +1-3 percentage points. Long-tail DLC attach unclear — Wildermyth's $24.99 launch did not hurt long-tail. CAVEAT: $24.99 makes "Roman free-tier" framing more legible — buyer expectation of "cheap base + paid DLC" lines up better.
- **$34.99 move (toward expensive end of band):** lose approximately -20-30% wishlist conversion vs $29.99 baseline. Gain $5/unit base revenue.
