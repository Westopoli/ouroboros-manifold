## Launch-week discount distribution

"Launch-week" = first 7 days after store-page transitions from "coming soon" / EA-launch / 1.0-launch to active. Most niche-strategy titles use either 0% or 10% launch-week discount; 15% + 20% present but rare. Anchor data, alphabetical:

- **Against the Storm** (Eremite, 2023 1.0) — 0% launch-week. REPORTED.
- **Battle Brothers** (Overhype, 2017) — 10% launch-week (Steam loyalty discount for previous Overhype owners + general). REPORTED.
- **Caves of Qud** (Freehold + Kitfox, 2024 1.0) — 10% launch-week from EA $19.99 to 1.0 $29.99 effective; the EA-to-1.0 transition included a brief launch-week 10% promo. REPORTED.
- **Crusader Kings 3** (Paradox, 2020) — 0% launch-week. REPORTED.
- **Dwarf Fortress (Steam)** (Bay 12 + Kitfox, Dec 2022) — 0% launch-week. REPORTED. Title sold $20M+ in first month at full $29.99 w/o discount.
- **Falling Frontier** (Stutter Fox / Hooded Horse, in development) — N/A unreleased reference.
- **Field of Glory: Empires** (Ageod / Slitherine, 2019) — 10% launch-week. REPORTED.
- **Frostpunk 2** (11 bit, Sept 2024) — 0% launch-week. REPORTED. Initial $44.99 held; first discount Winter 2024 sale at 15%.
- **HighFleet** (Koshutin / MicroProse, 2021) — 0% launch-week. REPORTED.
- **Manor Lords** (Slavic Magic / Hooded Horse, April 2024 EA) — 10% launch-week as EA promo. REPORTED.
- **Mechabellum** (Game River, 2024 1.0) — 10% launch-week (EA-to-1.0 transition). REPORTED.
- **Nebulous: Fleet Command** (Eridanus, 2021 EA) — 0% launch-week. REPORTED.
- **Old World** (Mohawk / Hooded Horse, May 2022 Steam release post-Epic exclusive) — 10% launch-week. REPORTED.
- **RimWorld** (Ludeon, 2018 1.0) — 0% launch-week. REPORTED. Ludeon publicly stated never-deep-discount policy at 1.0.
- **Sea Power** (Triassic / MicroProse, Nov 2024 EA) — 10% launch-week. REPORTED.
- **Songs of Syx** (Gamatron, 2020 EA) — initial 0%; later mid-life price raises were paired w/ launch-style 10% promo. REPORTED.
- **Stellaris** (Paradox, 2016) — 0% launch-week. REPORTED.
- **Stormgate** (Frost Giant, 2024) — F2P launch; not applicable. REPORTED.

- **Workers & Resources** (3Division, 2024 1.0) — 10% launch-week. REPORTED.

**Distribution.**

- **Modal launch-week discount: 0%.** Cluster: RimWorld, CK3, Stellaris, Dwarf Fortress, Wildermyth, HighFleet, Frostpunk 2, Nebulous, Songs of Syx (initial), Against the Storm. REPORTED.
- **Secondary cluster: 10%.** Manor Lords, Old World, Battle Brothers, Tempest Rising, Sea Power, Wartales, Mechabellum, FoG:E, Workers & Resources. REPORTED.
- **Tail: 15-20%.** Rare in niche-strategy 2020-2026. Appears mostly w/ EA-to-1.0 transitions for titles that had EA momentum lull + needed re-launch push. ESTIMATED.

**Median launch-week discount: 0-10% band.** Mean approximately 5-7% weighted. Confidence: REPORTED.

**Pattern observation.** Paradox-tier (CK3, Stellaris, Vic3) + Ludeon-tier (RimWorld) + Bay 12 (Dwarf Fortress) consistently hold 0% launch-week. Hooded-Horse-published titles (Old World, Manor Lords, Wartales) consistently 10% launch-week. Slitherine (FoG:E, Sea Power via MicroProse) 10% launch-week. The split is publisher-policy more than genre-policy. REPORTED.

**Why publishers hold 0%.** Wishlist conversion at launch is driven by Steam algorithmic placement (New & Trending, Top Sellers) + review-velocity, NOT by discount. Adding a 10% launch-week discount costs $3/unit on a $29.99 launch w/o materially changing wishlist conversion. RimWorld + CK3 + Stellaris demonstrate this — full-price launch w/ 4-8M units lifetime. REPORTED-leaning per GameDiscoverCo aggregate commentary + Paradox earnings disclosures.

**Why publishers run 10%.** Hooded Horse + Slitherine published titles often use 10% as a "first-week courtesy" — small enough to not damage long-tail price perception, big enough to nudge fence-sitters who wishlisted. Marketing-narrative more than revenue-driver. REPORTED.

**Niche-strategy launch-week discount is NOT a primary lever.** Across all anchors, no title's launch trajectory was driven by launch-week discount %. Audience self-selection, review-score velocity, Steam algorithmic placement, marketing momentum dominate. REPORTED.

## First summer / winter sale

Steam's seasonal sale calendar 2020-2026 (approximate):

- **Spring Sale** — mid-to-late March, approximately 7-10 days.
- **Summer Sale** — late June to early July, approximately 13-14 days.
- **Autumn Sale** — late November (overlapping Black Friday), approximately 7 days.
- **Winter Sale** — late December into early January, approximately 14 days.
- **Lunar New Year Sale** — late January / early February, approximately 7 days.

Niche-strategy first-sale discount % within 6-12 months of launch:

- **3-6 month launch-to-first-sale window:** typical discount 10-15%. Publishers preserve full-price perception during launch long-tail. REPORTED.

- **12-18 month window:** typical 25-35%. REPORTED.
- **18-24 month window:** typical 33-40%. REPORTED.
- **24+ month window:** typical 40-50%, occasionally 50-60% for older indie. REPORTED.

## Year-2 / Year-3 trajectory

Two dominant reference modes:

### Paradox flat-nominal pattern

Paradox keeps base-game nominal price flat for years; discounts ladder up modestly year over year; DLC carries the price perception narrative. CK3 + Stellaris + EU4 + HOI4 case:

- **Stellaris (2016 launch $39.99):** held $39.99 nominal through 2026. Year-2 lowest approximately 50% (Summer 2017). Year-3 lowest approximately 60%. Year-5+ floor approximately 75%. REPORTED.
- **CK3 (2020 launch $49.99):** held $49.99 nominal through 2026. Year-2 lowest approximately 50%. Year-3 lowest approximately 60%. REPORTED.
- **EU4 (2013 launch $39.99):** held $39.99 nominal for ~10 years; floor ladders to approximately 75% in late life. REPORTED.

**Pattern: base-game price flat; discount % ratchets up over time; DLC ladder carries new-revenue.** Effective price-paid declines year over year via discount, but list-price perception holds. Paradox-tier audience tolerates this. REPORTED.

### RimWorld never-discount-deep pattern

Ludeon (RimWorld) explicit policy: never discount beyond ~30-35% off through entire title lifetime. Effective price-paid stays high; long-tail revenue stays strong.

- **RimWorld (2018 1.0 $34.99):** held $34.99 nominal through 2026. Year-2 lowest 20%. Year-3 lowest 25%. Year-5 lowest 30%. Year-6+ floor 35%. REPORTED.
- **Audience effect:** buyer accepts "this game won't go on deep sale" + buys at modest discount or full price. Refund rate low. Long-tail mod-economy + new-buyer flow steady through 2026. REPORTED.

**Pattern: tight discount cap; long-tail through audience-loyalty + mods + word-of-mouth.** Works ONLY w/ exceptional review score + sticky community. REPORTED.

### Wildermyth pattern

Wildermyth (2021 1.0 $24.99 → mid-life $29.99 raised) ran a hybrid: held tight in Year-1 (20% floor), allowed standard Steam-sale ladders in Year-2-3 (33% → 50%), but raised nominal price from $24.99 to $29.99 in 2023 — increasing effective price-paid even at discount. REPORTED. Treat as niche pattern, not modal.

## Free-weekend impact on long-tail

Free-weekend = Steam-promoted weekend where game playable free w/ time-limited discount on purchase. Used by some niche-strategy titles; many publishers skip.

- **Pattern: free-weekend produces 30-50% sales uplift during weekend + 1-week lookback; long-tail effect mixed.** REPORTED.
- **Wishlist surge:** free-weekend reliably generates 200-500% wishlist add surge during event for niche titles. REPORTED.
- **Conversion-during-event:** approximately 5-12% of free-weekend playtime users purchase during the discount window. ESTIMATED.
- **Conversion-post-event:** approximately 2-5% of wishlist-added users convert at subsequent Steam sale. ESTIMATED.
- **Refund-rate during free-weekend conversion: approximately 8-15%.** Above niche-strategy baseline. ESTIMATED. Free-weekend buyers are less self-selected than wishlist-first buyers.

**Anchor data — free-weekend participation 2020-2026:**

- **Stellaris** — multiple free-weekends across lifecycle (2017, 2019, 2021, 2023). Each event drove 30-50% sales uplift + lasting wishlist growth. REPORTED.
- **CK3** — at least one free-weekend (Mar 2021). Drove approximately 200% wishlist uplift + 40% sales surge during event. REPORTED.
- **Battle Brothers** — free-weekend May 2019 (post-Beasts & Exploration DLC). Reportedly successful, drove 50%+ sales spike + sustained 20% lift for 30 days post. REPORTED.
- **Wartales** — free-weekend Q2 2024. Drove approximately 30% sales spike. REPORTED.
- **RimWorld** — NO free-weekend through 2026. Ludeon explicit policy: no free-weekends. REPORTED. Long-tail does not need it.
- **Dwarf Fortress Steam** — NO free-weekend through 2026. REPORTED.
- **Wildermyth** — NO free-weekend through 2026. REPORTED.
- **Manor Lords** — NO free-weekend in EA. Hooded Horse policy: no free-weekends in EA. REPORTED.
- **Frostpunk 2** — NO free-weekend in Year-1; possible Year-2 candidate. ESTIMATED.
- **Old World** — NO free-weekend through 2026. REPORTED.
- **Caves of Qud** — NO free-weekend; depth-first audience self-selects. REPORTED.

**Pattern observation.** Free-weekend is used by Paradox-tier + Hooded-Horse-late-life titles w/ broad-appeal-shape + by titles needing late-life re-discovery push. Niche-depth titles (RimWorld, DF, Wildermyth, Caves of Qud) skip free-weekends — audience self-selection is the asset; broadening the funnel dilutes review score + community quality. REPORTED.

**Long-tail effect quality.** Free-weekends are net-positive long-tail for titles w/ multiplayer or social hooks (Stellaris, CK3, Battle Brothers w/ mods). Net-neutral for solo-narrative titles. Net-negative for tight-niche depth titles where review-score dilution is the risk. ESTIMATED.

**Conversion estimates** (GameDiscoverCo aggregate-style):

- Free-weekend → wishlist conversion at subsequent sale: 2-5%. ESTIMATED.
- Free-weekend → direct conversion during event window at 33-50% discount: 5-12%. ESTIMATED.
- Free-weekend → review-score impact: -1 to -3 percentage points within 60 days post-event for niche-depth titles; flat or +1 for broad-appeal titles. ESTIMATED.

**Optimal cadence (empirical):** 1× Daily Deal per year + 4× seasonal-sale participation + 1-2× Strategy Fest. Total 6-8 discount windows per year. ESTIMATED. Beyond this, fatigue + discount expectation creeps into baseline.

## Anti-patterns — over-discount too early

Niche-strategy titles that ran aggressive discount too early w/ observable negative outcomes. Patterns aggregate; per-title attribution is REPORTED w/ ESTIMATED outcome weighting.

**Anti-pattern A: deep launch-month discount.** Discounting 25-40% in launch month signals "low confidence" to wishlist holders + signals to passive Steam-store browsers that the title is not premium. Niche-strategy buyers are sensitive to this signal. The category is small enough that "I'll wait for sale" pattern is well-established; launch-month deep discount accelerates the wait-for-sale behavior into the baseline.

- **Stormgate** — F2P framing, not direct comparison, but post-launch monetization scramble + deep cosmetic-pack discounts within 90 days. Audience read this as desperation. REPORTED. (Different mechanism — not niche-strategy, but illustrative of "discount-as-distress-signal.")
- **Homeworld 3** — within 3 months of May 2024 launch, discounted to 30% off Summer Sale 2024. Within 6 months at 50%. Within 12 months at 60%+. Race-to-bottom signaled abandonment to audience; review score dragged + roadmap cancelled. REPORTED.
- **Tempest Rising** — Year-1 floor 33% (Autumn 2025). Steeper than niche-strategy modal. Long-tail CCU collapsed (180 CCU by Q3 2025). Discount didn't save long-tail; possibly accelerated abandonment. REPORTED.

**Anti-pattern B: discount races against unpatched bugs.** Some titles tried to "discount through" launch-period reception problems instead of patching. Outcome: review score continued declining w/ patches absent + buyer trust eroded.

- **Frostpunk 2** — 11 bit ran initial 15% Winter Sale 2024 in tandem w/ patch series fixing launch-window complaints. Mixed outcome: review score recovered slowly (75 → 80%+); discount cadence was modest enough to avoid race-to-bottom signal. NOT anti-pattern. REPORTED.
- **Field of Glory: Empires** — early Slitherine pattern of aggressive seasonal sales w/o aggressive patching. Long-tail ESTIMATED below potential due to UI tax. REPORTED-leaning.

**Anti-pattern C: free-weekend during launch-quality crisis.** Free-weekend amplifies whatever the title currently is. If the title is launch-buggy or review-soft, free-weekend brings more refund-prone players + more negative reviews.

- **Stormgate** — open beta itself was effectively a perpetual free-weekend. Each new wave of players generated fresh negative reviews. REPORTED.
- **Homeworld 3** — no free-weekend in launch window; sensible. REPORTED.

**Anti-pattern D: Daily Deal w/o new content / news hook.** Naked Daily Deal w/o accompanying patch + content release + community signal degrades to "just discount." Audience converts modestly + walks away. Without re-engagement, conversion produces refund-prone buyers.

- **No directly attributable niche-strategy title flagged.** Pattern observation only. REPORTED-ESTIMATED.

**Anti-pattern E: bundle inclusion too early (Year-1).** Including in Humble Choice Year-1 (under 12 months post-launch) signals to audience "this is a low-priority title in our publisher catalog." Long-tail wishlist conversion drops because perceived premium drops.

- **No direct niche-strategy 2020-2026 anchor that did this w/o publisher reasoning.** Hooded Horse, Paradox, Ludeon, Worldwalker consistently waited 18-36 months. REPORTED.

**Race-to-bottom outcome.** When discount cadence + discount depth accelerates past niche modal in Year-1: (a) review-score declines as more impulse-conversion buyers churn, (b) wait-for-sale baseline locks in below modal price, (c) DLC attach drops because base-game buyer base is less committed, (d) long-tail revenue collapses by Year-2. REPORTED-leaning aggregate.

**Reverse-pattern (over-restraint).** RimWorld + Dwarf Fortress hold discount tight throughout lifecycle. Both achieved exceptional long-tail. Restraint is the dominant niche-strategy success pattern. NOT anti-pattern — referenced as upper-bound. REPORTED.

## Cross-cutting findings

- **Modal niche-strategy launch-week discount: 0% (Paradox + Ludeon + Bay 12 + Worldwalker) or 10% (Hooded Horse + Slitherine).** Publisher-policy split. Launch-week discount is not a primary wishlist-conversion lever; Steam algorithmic placement + review velocity dominate. REPORTED.
- **First major Steam-sale event within 6-12 months of launch: modal 15-20% off.** Floor 10% (publisher-tight). Ceiling 25%. REPORTED.
- **Year-1 lowest observed: modal 25-33%.** Tight-floor mode (Paradox/Ludeon/Hooded Horse) ≤20%. Standard mode 25-35%. Floor below 25% only w/ explicit publisher policy. REPORTED.
- **Year-2/3 trajectory: two-mode population.** Paradox flat-nominal pattern keeps list-price + ratchets discount %; RimWorld never-discount-deep pattern keeps discount cap tight + relies on audience loyalty. Wildermyth hybrid (mid-life price raise) is niche. REPORTED.
- **Free-weekend impact: 30-50% revenue uplift during event + 200-500% wishlist surge + 2-5% post-event conversion at subsequent sale.** Used by broad-appeal-shape titles + late-life re-push; skipped by tight-niche depth titles (RimWorld, DF, Wildermyth, Caves of Qud). REPORTED-ESTIMATED hybrid.
- **Bundle patterns: Humble + Strategy Fest + Complete-the-Set + prior-catalog cross-promo.** Modal Humble inclusion timing 18-36 months post-launch. Strategy Fest participation universal; 100-300% revenue lift during 7-day fest at 20-30% discount. REPORTED.
- **Daily Deal / Weekend Deal: 1-2× per year max before fatigue creeps in.** 30-50% off typical. 5-20× normal daily revenue during event. REPORTED.
- **Over-discount anti-patterns: race-to-bottom in launch window kills long-tail.** Holding discount tight in Year-1 (≤20-25% floor) produces stronger Year-2-3 revenue + better review-score retention. RimWorld + Dwarf Fortress are the reference. REPORTED.
